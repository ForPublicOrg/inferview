## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As a public interview guide phrases it">
"How would you red-team an LLM feature before launch, and what counts as a finding?"
</Callout>

The feature is a customer-facing assistant that answers product questions, can look up the signed-in customer's orders, and can open a support ticket on their behalf.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>What are you trying to make the system do that it should not? Write the policy before the attacks.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>A finding has to be reproducible and measurable. How do you record one from a non-deterministic model?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Which of your attacks become permanent tests?</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Start from a policy: what must the assistant never do (reveal another customer's orders, open tickets without the customer, follow instructions in retrieved content, emit another customer's PII). Attack each line with the known families (role-play, encoding, translation, payload splitting, many-shot, Best-of-N, indirect injection through anything it reads), running tools against staging. A finding is any input that violates the policy, recorded with the exact input, the model and prompt versions, the outcome, and how often it reproduces across samples. Automate with a probe suite such as garak, keep every finding as a regression test, and gate launch on severity, not on a clean static run.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p>Define targets first. Confidentiality: no cross-customer data, no system prompt or endpoint leakage. Integrity: no ticket opened without the customer, no false claims about their orders. Availability and cost: no single request that runs away on tokens or tool calls.</p>
<p>Pick attackers. Humans for creativity, a probe suite for coverage (garak has families such as promptinject, encoding, leakreplay and xss, driven by its current <code>--target_type</code> and <code>--spec probes.&lt;family&gt;</code> flags), and automated adaptive attacks such as Best-of-N where you can afford them, because The Attacker Moves Second showed static sets overstate defences.</p>
<p>Cover the input paths, not just the chat box: the order data, any retrieved help articles, and the ticket text, since an injected instruction can ride in through any of them.</p>
<p>Define a finding. It is a reproducible policy violation. Record the input verbatim, the model and prompt versions, the tool calls made, the outcome, and the success rate over N samples, because a model that complies one time in ten is still vulnerable. Severity comes from impact (data of another customer is higher than a rude reply) and from how easy the attack is.</p>
<p>Close the loop. Every finding becomes a regression test with an expected outcome, run on every prompt, model and tool change. Triage by severity; fix the high-impact ones with architecture (scope the order lookup to the signed-in customer in code, require the customer's identity for ticket creation), and track the rest. Launch is gated on severity and on the regression suite passing, not on a classifier's static score.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>How large a red-team set is enough?</em> There is no number; track whether new human findings already fail against your suite. If most new findings are new families, the suite is behind.</li>
<li><em>A finding reproduces 1 in 20 times. Ship or block?</em> Block if the impact is cross-customer data; 1 in 20 at scale is many incidents. Rate and impact together set the call.</li>
<li><em>Who signs off?</em> Security owns the policy and severity; product owns the launch decision against that policy.</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

## Follow-ups

- *How do you keep this running after launch?* The regression suite runs in CI, and production monitoring alerts on the same policy violations seen live.
- *What is not a finding?* A refusal of a borderline-but-allowed request is a quality issue, not a security finding; track it separately.

## Common mistakes

- Red-teaming only the chat box and ignoring retrieved content and tool text.
- Recording a single successful attempt without a reproduction rate.
- Treating a clean probe-suite run as proof of safety rather than proof your probes failed.
- Fixing findings in the prompt instead of in the architecture.
