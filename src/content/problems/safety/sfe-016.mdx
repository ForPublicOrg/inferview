## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As a public question list phrases it">
"Your RAG system is hallucinating despite having the right context. How do you fix it?"
</Callout>

An HR assistant (scenario) answers employees from the policy library, using the Claude Messages API with each retrieved chunk sent as a plain-text document with citations enabled. A ticket from an employee:

```text
question   How many weeks of paid leave do birth parents get?
answer     Birth parents get 12 weeks of paid parental leave. [1]
citation   [1] HR-214 Parental leave policy
           cited_text: "Eligible employees may take paid parental leave after the birth,
                        adoption or foster placement of a child."
```

The trace store for this request:

```text
retrieval (top 8; text as stored in the index)
  rank  chunk       score  effective    chars  content
  1     HR-214#3    0.83   2026-01-01   1,604  parental leave: eligibility, then pay, then the leave
                                                lengths for birth and non-birth parents in the last
                                                paragraph ("Birth parents receive 16 weeks at full pay...")
  2     HR-214#4    0.79   2026-01-01     980  notice periods and how to apply
  3     HR-198#2    0.77   2023-04-01   1,150  previous policy: "Birth parents receive 10 weeks..."
  4     BEN-031#1   0.71   2025-07-01   1,420  adding a newborn to health cover
  5-8   (four chunks on sick leave, holidays and payroll dates)

model call
  system         "You are the HR assistant. Answer the employee's question using the
                  documents provided and your general knowledge of HR practice. Be
                  concise and cite the policy."
  input tokens   5,912      output tokens  31      stop_reason  end_turn
  (the documents as sent are not logged)
```

The last change to the code between the index and the API call, merged nine days ago:

```diff
-TOP_K = 5
+TOP_K = 8                      # recall@k on the eval set: 0.81 -> 0.88
+CHUNK_CHARS = 1200
 def build_documents(chunks):
     docs = []
-    for c in chunks:
+    for c in sorted(chunks, key=lambda c: c.score):
         docs.append({
             "type": "document",
-            "source": {"type": "text", "media_type": "text/plain", "data": c.text},
+            "source": {"type": "text", "media_type": "text/plain", "data": c.text[:CHUNK_CHARS]},
             "title": c.doc_title,
+            "context": f"effective {c.effective_date}",
             "citations": {"enabled": True},
         })
     return docs
```

Retrieval found the current policy. Which defect explains this answer? Then rank the others, say what you would collect that the trace does not have, and decide what changes.

## Answer

The correct choice is the truncation. HR-214#3 is 1,604 characters and its leave lengths sit in the last paragraph; `c.text[:CHUNK_CHARS]` sends the first 1,200. The model received a chunk that says parental leave exists and is paid, cited the one sentence that supports that much, and supplied a length from nowhere in the context: 12 is in no retrieved chunk. The retrieval log shows the index's text, which is why "the right context" looked present. The citation is valid in exactly the sense the API promises: the cited text is a real span of a document that was sent. It does not support the number in the sentence it is attached to.

**What to collect, and what to do without it.** The trace does not hold the documents as sent, so the first step is to rebuild this request from the logged chunk ids with the deployed `build_documents` and confirm the last paragraph is missing. Then count, over a week of traffic, how many retrieved chunks are longer than 1,200 characters and how often the answer-bearing sentence of a labelled question sits past the cut; that is the size of the regression, which the recall@k comment on the merge could not show, because recall is measured on the index before the cut. Without any of that, the chunk length and the position of the leave lengths are enough to act: put the old behaviour back behind a flag today.

**Ranking the rest.**

1. The truncation explains this answer.
2. The system prompt's "your general knowledge of HR practice" turned a missing sentence into a confident number. Changing it to "answer only from the documents; if they do not state it, say so and link the policy" makes this failure an honest non-answer. On its own it would have answered the employee with "the policy excerpt does not say", for a question the policy answers.
3. The 2023 policy is retrieved with no effective-date filter. The new `context` line labels it, which leaves the choice to the model. Filter superseded versions at retrieval.
4. The interface shows a citation badge as if it were support. A cheap deterministic check would have caught this one: every number in an answer sentence must appear in that sentence's `cited_text`. It misses numbers written differently ("four months"), so it is a tripwire, not a support check; a judge reading claim and span together is the general version and costs a call per answer.
5. Ascending sort puts the top chunk last, next to the question. It is worth knowing it changed, not worth fixing first.

**The decision.** The merge was trying to fit more chunks into the same prompt, and two ways keep that goal without cutting sentences:

- Return to 5 whole chunks. Costs the recall gain the merge measured, 0.88 back to 0.81, and nothing else changes.
- Keep 8, and re-chunk at ingestion to at most 1,200 characters on sentence or paragraph boundaries. Keeps the recall gain and the prompt size, at the cost of re-embedding the library, and of re-running the recall eval, because smaller chunks change what ranks first.

The quantity to defend is the chunk size, and the eval that defends it measures answer correctness on labelled questions with the documents as sent, not recall on the index. I would re-chunk, with the 5-chunk revert as the change that ships today.

**Where the re-chunk fails.** Splitting at 1,200 characters on paragraph boundaries can put "Birth parents receive 16 weeks at full pay" in one chunk and the heading that says which policy and which country it applies to in another. Retrieval then returns a number without its scope. Prefix each chunk with its document title, section heading and effective date at ingestion, and check the labelled questions whose answers span a split.

## Follow-ups

- *The truncation shipped with an eval that went up. What eval would have failed it?* Answer correctness on labelled questions, scored from the request as sent, plus the number-in-cited-text check across the eval set; both run on the prompt, not on the index.
- *Policy owners write long sections and will not change them. Does that change your chunk size?* It changes where the split goes: on headings and list items inside the section, with the section heading repeated in every chunk, rather than a larger cap that pushes the prompt over budget.
- *Legal wants every answer to carry structured fields for an audit log. What breaks?* The Citations docs say citations and structured outputs cannot be combined in one request, so the audit record is built from the response's citation blocks after the call, or a second call produces it.

## Common mistakes

- Reading the retrieval log as the prompt.
- Lowering temperature, which makes the same unsupported number repeatable.
- Fixing only the prompt, which converts a wrong answer into a refusal on a question the policy answers.
- Treating a valid citation pointer as evidence that the cited text supports the claim.
- Judging a context-assembly change by recall@k, which is measured before the assembly runs.
