## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Reported by a public interview guide as an Anthropic question (candidate report)">
"A deployed conversational model gives confident but factually wrong answers in high-risk contexts."
</Callout>

The interviewer adds a case (scenario). A health insurer's member assistant answers about 40,000 questions a week: plan coverage 45%, claim status 30% (answered from the claims system through a tool), prior authorisation 15%, drug formulary 10%. Compliance escalated three member complaints this month: the assistant said a procedure needed no prior authorisation, and the claim was later denied.

Two benefits specialists labelled 200 recent answers per topic:

```text
topic                 wrong   notes from the labellers
plan coverage         6/200
claim status          1/200
prior authorisation  19/200   15 of the 19 carried a citation; in 11 of those 15 the cited
                              passage was from a different plan's document than the member's
drug formulary       11/200   8 of the 11 were drugs whose tier changed after the quarterly
                              document snapshot the assistant retrieves from
all 37 wrong answers were stated without a hedge
```

What else is true:

- The prior-authorisation list per plan lives in the utilisation-management system, which has a read-only lookup by plan and procedure code. The formulary has an API updated weekly. The claims system cannot be changed.
- Four engineers own the assistant. Open enrolment starts in three weeks.
- Twelve benefits specialists can take about 1,500 escalated chats a week on top of their phone work.
- The compliance officer wants prior-authorisation and formulary answers switched off until fixed. The product VP wants them kept, because the phone queue is already long.

Design the workflow from investigation to what ships on the enrolment date: the steps, what state each answer carries, when it escalates and to whom, the rollout and its rollback triggers, and the memo compliance signs. Then tick the checklist.

## Answer

### Size it before fixing it

Prior authorisation: 19 / 200 = 9.5% wrong. The 95% interval is ±1.96 × √(0.095 × 0.905 / 200) = ±4.1 points, so 5.4% to 13.6%, which at 6,000 questions a week is about 325 to 815 wrong answers a week, every one stated without a hedge. Formulary: 11 / 200 = 5.5%, about 220 a week on 4,000. Coverage at 3% and claim status at 0.5% are a different order of problem. An overall error rate for the assistant, 37 / 800 = 4.6%, would have hidden the slice that produced the complaints.

The labellers' notes already split the causes, and none of them is the model making things up from nothing:

- **Wrong plan.** 11 of the 15 cited prior-authorisation errors cite another plan's document. Retrieval does not filter by the member's plan, and the citation is valid (the API guarantees a real pointer) while answering for the wrong contract.
- **Stale snapshot.** 8 of the 11 formulary errors are tier changes after the quarterly snapshot. The model repeated its source faithfully; the source was out of date.
- **Generation on correct context.** The remainder, a handful per topic. This is the slice where a verification pass, such as Chain-of-Verification's independent answering of check questions, earns its extra calls.

### The steps

1. **Classify** the question by topic and extract the member's plan and plan year from the session, never from the question text.
2. **Prior authorisation:** map the question to a procedure code, then call the utilisation-management lookup with plan and code. The model phrases the result and links the record. If the code mapping is ambiguous or the lookup has no entry, the answer is an escalation, not a guess.
3. **Formulary:** the same pattern against the formulary API. The quarterly documents are no longer a source for tiers.
4. **Coverage:** retrieval filtered by plan and plan year in the query, citations on, and a support check that the cited span states what the sentence claims.
5. **Escalate** with the conversation, the plan, the lookup result and the reason attached, as one case per conversation keyed on the conversation id, so a retry does not open a second case.
6. **Record** per answer: plan, plan year, document ids and versions, lookup request and response, cited spans, support-check result, escalation id. This is health data: same access controls and retention as claims. The escalation record is built from the response after the call, because the Citations docs say citations and structured outputs cannot be combined in one request.

### The escalation trigger, and the direction of the error

The two errors are not symmetric. A wrong "authorisation needed" costs the member a phone call. A wrong "not needed" costs a denied claim, and the operator answers for what its assistant said: the B.C. tribunal ordered Air Canada to pay over its chatbot's wrong fare advice and rejected the argument that the airline was not liable for it.

Using Kalai and colleagues' rule, an answer is worth giving when the chance it is right exceeds t = c / (1 + c), where c is the cost of a wrong answer in units of the value of a right one. The cost is the quantity to choose. If compliance puts a wrong "not needed" at 20 times the value of a right one, t = 20 / 21 = 0.95: the answer may be given only when the error rate is under 5%. Today's prior-authorisation pipeline is at 5.4% to 13.6%, so no confidence threshold on it qualifies; only the lookup does. For "authorisation needed", c near 1 gives t = 0.5, and the lookup link is enough. From c = 18 upwards (t = 0.947, under 5.3% wrong) even the low end of the interval fails. At c = 10 (t = 0.91) the point estimate fails and the low end passes, so the sample cannot clear the pipeline either. A reader who picks c = 2 is pricing a denied claim like a phone call.

Capacity: if both high-risk topics escalated everything, that is 10,000 chats a week against 1,500, before enrolment raises volume. The design only works if the lookups resolve most questions; the shadow week measures that share, and the escalation rate is a rollback trigger.

### Two defensible answers for the enrolment date

| | Switch the topics off | Ship them lookup-backed |
|---|---|---|
| What members get | Prior-authorisation and formulary questions answered with a link to the lookup page and the phone number | A definitive answer from the system of record, or an escalation |
| What it costs | 10,000 questions a week moved to the phone queue or the web page during enrolment | About three weeks of the four engineers; the code-mapping step is a new model step that can be wrong |
| What it protects | No model-produced prior-authorisation answer at all | Answers that are only as wrong as the code mapping and the lookup |

I would ship formulary lookup-backed on the date, because the API exists and the mapping from a drug name is close to exact. For prior authorisation I would switch "not needed" answers off on the date and ship "needed" answers with the lookup link, then turn on lookup-backed "not needed" answers only after a shadow week shows the code mapping agrees with specialists. Compliance gets their switch-off on the dangerous direction; the VP keeps most of the containment.

**What you give up.** Chat containment for every prior-authorisation question whose honest answer is "not needed" during enrolment, which is the answer members most want. Three weeks of the team on lookups instead of the enrolment features on the roadmap.

**Where this fails.** The procedure-code mapping. A member who writes "knee scope" means one of several codes; if the mapper picks one confidently, the lookup returns a definitive answer for the wrong procedure, and the answer looks more authoritative than before because it cites the system of record. The mapper needs its own labelled sample, its own abstention when candidates are close, and the member's confirmation of the procedure before a "not needed" answer.

### Rollout and rollback

- Week 1: plan filter on coverage retrieval; formulary lookup in shadow, compared with specialists on 200 answers.
- Week 2: formulary lookup live at 25% of traffic, then all; prior-authorisation mapper in shadow.
- Week 3: "not needed" answers off; "needed" answers with the lookup link live.

Rollback triggers: any "not needed" answer later contradicted by the utilisation-management system returns that topic to switched off. Formulary disagreement with specialists above 1% on the weekly sample returns formulary to the link-only answer. Escalations above 1,500 in a week is also a trigger, and the response is specialist hours or a callback queue, never a looser escalation rule, because loosening the rule under load is how the original errors come back.

### The memo compliance signs

The memo states the measured error per topic with its interval, which answers are switched off and which are lookup-backed, the residual risk (wrong code mapping, a lookup that is itself out of date), the weekly labelled sample and who reviews it, the rollback triggers, and that the compliance officer and the product VP both sign. The number it does not claim is zero.

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: the lookups and the support check cost three times what the assistant cost to build. Which half do you cut, and what risk do you accept in writing?* Cut the support check on coverage answers that state no amounts, keep the lookups; the accepted risk is a misread document on coverage questions, sized from the 3% coverage slice.
- *Reversal: what would you need to see in production to switch prior-authorisation "not needed" answers back on?* A month of shadow mapping with every "not needed" answer checked against the lookup by a specialist and no contradiction, and at least 60 of them, because zero errors in n checked answers bounds the rate below about 3 / n at 95% confidence, and 3 / 60 is the 5% the trigger needs.
- *The compliance officer and the product VP disagree after the memo. How is it decided without deciding by rank?* On the measured weekly cost of each option in the same units: wrong "not needed" answers times the cost compliance assigned, against phone-queue minutes during enrolment.
- *The model vendor ships a new version mid-enrolment and retires yours in sixty days. What do you re-run before switching?* The weekly labelled sample per topic and the mapper's sample, on the new model, before any traffic moves.

## Common mistakes

- Reporting one accuracy figure for the assistant, which hides the 9.5% slice behind 4.6%.
- Raising an abstention threshold on a pipeline whose error comes from the wrong plan's documents.
- Counting a valid citation as support for the claim.
- Grounding faithfully in a stale snapshot and calling the formulary errors hallucinations.
- Treating both directions of a prior-authorisation error as the same cost.
- An escalation path with no capacity check, which gets loosened under load.
