## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As an interview question list phrases it">
"What is jailbreaking, and what are common jailbreak techniques?"
</Callout>

Jailbreaking is crafting input that makes a model ignore its safety training. Three of the statements below describe real techniques correctly. One is false. Pick the false one.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Think about what each attack varies: the sampling, or the input text.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Would making the model deterministic stop an attack that changes the prompt each time?</p></details>

## Answer

The false statement is A. Best-of-N jailbreaking resamples augmentations of the input, such as random capitalisation and character noise, and sends each until one gets through. It attacks the input, not the sampling, so a model at temperature 0 still produces a different output for each different input, and some of those outputs comply. OWASP's cheat sheet notes directly that temperature 0 did not stop jailbreaks in the study it cites.

The other three are accurate. Many-shot (B) fills a long context with faux dialogues and gets stronger with more shots. Encoding (C) exploits mismatched generalization: the model's capabilities cover more of the input space than its safety training. Role-play (D) exploits competing objectives: being helpful is set against refusing. The two failure modes in C and D come from the 2023 analysis of why safety training fails.

## Follow-ups

- *Which of these is hardest to stop with a filter?* Best-of-N and payload splitting, because they change the surface form every time.
- *Does a bigger model help?* It can refuse more reliably on simple attacks and is also better at decoding the clever ones.

## Common mistakes

- Believing low temperature is a jailbreak defence.
- Confusing many-shot (many examples in one prompt) with multi-turn (many messages over time).
- Treating "jailbreak" and "prompt injection" as the same thing; a jailbreak targets the provider's policy, an injection targets your application.
