checklist:
  - Runs a broad per-model eval suite on every model the prompt reaches, not only the set the change was developed against
  - Measures each model's noise band with a repeat run of the current prompt and sets the regression threshold from it before reading any delta
  - Ablates line by line, at least the lines that constrain output length, effort or reasoning, on every model each line applies to
  - Gates the model-specific line to its model in code, with a test that fails if the line reaches another model
  - Soaks the exact public build with internal users for a stated period, with the metrics to watch named in advance
  - Rolls out in stated stages by stable hash at session start and pins the prompt version for the life of a session
  - Serves old and new prompt versions side by side until in-flight long sessions end, so no running session changes behaviour or loses its prompt cache
  - Writes rollback triggers with metric, threshold and window before rollout, and names who can roll back without a meeting
  - Watches cost and cache signals (tokens per task, cache hit rate) in the first week as well as quality signals
  - Says what it gives up, such as the launch-day token savings or ablation coverage on some lines
  - "Judgement call: shipping a line that regresses one model is acceptable if it is gated off for that model and the gain on the others is outside their noise band"
  - "Judgement call: ablating on a smaller suite is acceptable if that suite's own noise band is measured and the final combination still runs on the broad suite"

## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"You own the system prompt of a coding agent that runs on three models. Walk me through shipping a five-line change to it, from the pull request to the day you call it done."
</Callout>

The team, the models and the quotas are a scenario. A coding-agent CLI runs on three models: L, a new flagship that launches in nine days; M, the current flagship; and S, a small model on a cheaper plan. The pull request adds five lines to the system prompt:

```text
1. Keep text between tool calls short: report what you found, not what you plan.
2. Before editing, run the narrowest test that covers the code you will change.
3. Prefer targeted edits to rewriting whole files.
4. Before context is compacted, write the task's state to NOTES.md.
5. Do not restate the user's request before acting.        (written for L, which restates)
```

The author developed it on L against the team's 120-task internal set, where output tokens per task fell and the pass rate did not move. The product manager wants it in L's launch build, mostly for line 1: S users are on the cheaper plan and output tokens are their largest cost.

Constraints:

- The broad eval suite takes a day per run, and the quota allows two runs at once.
- Three engineers own the harness.
- The client reads a server-side flag at session start to pick the prompt version. That mechanism cannot change before launch. Sessions run for hours.

Design the release: what you evaluate, in what order, how it rolls out, what rolls it back, and what you write down.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Decouple the prompt change from the launch. L launches with the current prompt plus line 5, gated to L in code and evaluated on L's broad suite. Lines 1 to 4 go through a per-model broad eval against a measured noise band, line-by-line ablation of line 1 on all three models, a soak on the exact public build, and a staged rollout by account hash with the version pinned per session; rollback triggers are written before the first stage. You give up the launch-day token savings for S users. The trade is deliberate: line 1 is the same kind of change as the verbosity line Anthropic shipped on 16 April 2026 after weeks of internal testing found no regression, and that one eval in a broader ablation suite later showed cost 3% on two models.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Why not ship all five at launch.</b> The change was tested on one model and one eval set, which is how Anthropic's verbosity line passed: weeks of internal testing showed no regressions on the eval set, and one eval in a broader ablation suite later showed a 3% drop for both Opus 4.6 and 4.7. Anthropic's commitments after that postmortem are this release process: a broad per-model eval for every system-prompt change, line-by-line ablations, model-specific changes gated to their model, and soak periods with gradual rollouts for changes that could trade off against intelligence.</p>
<p><b>What the quota allows.</b> Nine days at two runs a day is 18 suite runs. Everything costs more than that: a repeat run of the current prompt on each model to measure its noise band (3 baselines plus 3 repeats, 6 runs), the full change on each model (3), and leave-one-out ablations for lines 1 to 4 on three models plus line 5 on L (13): 22 runs. So something gives.</p>
<p><b>The decision.</b> Two plans are defensible.</p>
<ul>
<li><em>Decouple (recommended).</em> Launch week: L's baseline and repeat (2 runs), L with line 5 (1 run). Then, after launch: the full change on all three models (3), leave-one-out of line 1 on all three (3) and of line 5 on L (already done), plus a rerun of whatever combination survives (3). Lines 2 to 4 are not ablated one by one; they ship only if the combination is inside each model's noise band. Cost: S users wait about two more weeks for the savings, and an interaction between lines 2 to 4 shows only as a combined regression.</li>
<li><em>Ship at launch with reduced ablation.</em> Run the 6 baseline runs, the full change (3) and leave-one-out for lines 1 and 5 only (4): 13 runs in seven days, leaving room for one rerun of the final combination on all three models. Cost: the soak overlaps the ablations, so the build that soaks is not final, and a problem found late blocks the model launch, which is the larger risk to the business.</li>
</ul>
<p><b>The threshold you choose.</b> For each model, the regression threshold is the difference between its baseline and repeat runs, rounded up, and never less than the band a binomial interval on that suite's size gives. A line that moves a model by less than its band is "no measured effect", not "safe". A reasonable bar for line 1 on S: ship if S's pass rate is inside its band and output tokens per task fall by more than their own run-to-run spread; if S regresses beyond the band and L and M do not, gate line 1 off for S, since the savings were for S and a regression there is the outcome the product manager would least want.</p>
<p><b>Gating line 5.</b> The prompt builder adds line 5 only when the model is L, with a unit test that renders the prompt for every supported model and fails if the line appears for M or S. A comment in a shared prompt file is not a gate. Anthropic's commitment was guidance in its CLAUDE.md that model-specific changes be gated to their model; a test that fails the build is the stronger form of the same rule.</p>
<p><b>Soak.</b> Staff use the exact public build with the new prompt for a period you state; five working days covers a full week of normal work. Watch the metrics the rollout will use, so the soak also tests the dashboards. Anthropic's thinking-clearing change passed review, tests and dogfooding because other changes hid it in most sessions; soak on the build users get, not an internal one.</p>
<p><b>Rollout.</b> The existing flag picks the prompt at session start, so roll out by stable hash of the account: 1%, 10%, 50%, 100%, each stage held long enough to cover a weekday and a weekend day of sessions. Sessions on the old prompt in the same days are the control, so time-of-day and API conditions cancel. The version is pinned for the life of a session, and both versions stay served until sessions on the old one end. Changing the system prompt under a running session invalidates its system and message caches, so the next call rewrites the whole prefix at the cache-write price, and the session's behaviour changes mid-task.</p>
<p><b>Rollback triggers</b>, written before stage one, per model, new cohort against control in the same window: share of sessions ending with the agent's tests passing and the diff kept, down by more than its control-to-control spread; output tokens per completed task up rather than down; prompt cache hit rate down by more than a point (the thinking-clearing bug showed up as cache misses and usage drain); interrupts per session up. Any trigger flips the flag for new sessions; the on-call engineer pulls it without a meeting and writes it up after. Running sessions finish on their pinned version unless the regression is harmful, in which case they are moved and the cache misses are accepted.</p>
<p><b>How this plan fails.</b> L is new, so in the first week it has the fewest sessions of the three, and its triggers are the least able to see a regression; its launch week is exactly when its numbers are thinnest. Hold L's general-line rollout at the 10% stage until its cohort is large enough for the trigger to detect the regression size you chose. The "tests pass and diff kept" proxy can also be pushed up by line 2 itself (running the narrowest test makes a pass more likely without making the change more correct), so read a sample of transcripts from both cohorts before calling the rollout done.</p>
<p><b>What is written down.</b> A rollout note: the lines, the model each applies to, each model's noise band, the ablation results, the stages and dates, the triggers with thresholds, the owner, and the sentence "lines 2 to 4 were evaluated together, not one by one".</p>

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: the eval quota is halved and the product manager will not move the launch. Which runs do you drop, and what risk do you accept in writing?* Keep the noise-band repeats and the line 1 ablation on S, because that is the line with the business case and the documented failure mode; drop the full-change runs on M. Write down that M gets the change only through the staged rollout, with its triggers as the only check.
- *Reversal: what would you need to see in production to remove line 1 after it shipped everywhere?* S's kept-diff share falling below control by more than its spread for two consecutive weeks while its output tokens fell, or transcript reviews showing the agent skipping explanations users needed. The savings do not justify a measured quality loss on the plan they were meant to help.
- *Operating: M is retired in sixty days and two of the five lines regress on L. Which ship degraded, and who signs off?* Gate the two lines off for L and ship the rest; M's users move to L with the gated prompt. The harness lead signs the gating; the product manager signs that S keeps a line L does not.
- *Two senior engineers disagree about decoupling the change from the launch. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Write down the cost of each failure: a regression found after launch on a new model's first week against two weeks of delayed savings for S. Price the savings from S's token volume, and decide on that number.

## Common mistakes

- Evaluating the change on the one model and the one set it was written for.
- Reading a delta before knowing the run-to-run spread of the suite on that model.
- Gating a model-specific line with a comment instead of in the code that builds the prompt.
- Switching running sessions to the new prompt, which changes behaviour mid-task and invalidates their caches.
- Choosing the rollback thresholds after the numbers come in.
