## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"A vendor wants us to replace our coding-agent harness with theirs, and their sales deck has this slide. Procurement decides on Friday. Review it."
</Callout>

The vendor, its harness and every number on the slide are a scenario. Terminal-Bench 2.0 and the published results cited in the answer are real.

```text
Forge Agent leads Terminal-Bench 2.0

                      score     cost per task
Forge Agent           61.4%     $0.42
OpenRig (open source) 48.3%     $1.10
Lead                  +13.1 points, 62% cheaper

Methodology
- Forge Agent ran on Model X Pro in our managed sandbox fleet: 8 vCPU and 32 GB per task, no limits enforced.
- OpenRig's score is its public leaderboard entry (Model Y), published five months ago.
- We excluded 6 tasks that need network access our sandbox blocks for security; Forge's score is over 83 tasks.
- Each task ran up to 3 attempts; Forge's built-in verifier picked the attempt to submit.
- Trials that hit sandbox errors were retried until they completed.
- The per-task time limit was raised to 2 hours so the agent could finish.
- Cost uses our contracted cache-read rate for Forge and list prices on the leaderboard's token counts for OpenRig.
- One run. Results are deterministic because we sample at temperature 0.
```

List every flaw, ranked by how much it could move the 13.1 points, before opening the checklist. Then say what single rerun you would ask the vendor for before Friday.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>For each row of the methodology, ask whether the OpenRig number was produced the same way.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask what the verifier that picks among the three attempts is allowed to run.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask the vendor for two runs of the same configuration a day apart, and look at how far apart they land.</p></details>

## Answer

**1. Two models.** The slide compares Forge on Model X Pro with OpenRig on Model Y, so it measures model and harness together, and the headline credits the harness with all of it. Published results show how much either can move alone: in the Terminal-Bench 2.0 paper, Claude Opus 4.5 scores 57.8% with Terminus 2 and 52.1% with Claude Code, and GPT-5 scores 49.6% with Codex CLI and 35.2% with Terminus 2. A 13-point gap across different models and harnesses cannot be split between them from this table.

**2. Uncapped resources against the task spec.** Forge ran with 8 vCPU and 32 GB per task and no limits enforced. Anthropic ran one model on Terminal-Bench 2.0 under six resource configurations and found uncapped scored 6 points above strict 1x enforcement, with infra errors falling from 5.8% to 0.5%. The leaderboard entry's configuration is not on the slide; ask for both.

**3. A different metric.** "Up to 3 attempts, the verifier picked one" is a best-of-3 with a selector, not the mean over trials a leaderboard reports. If the verifier is perfect it approaches pass@3, which is always at least pass@1 and, for an inconsistent agent, far above it. Worse, if the verifier runs the task's own tests, the grader is leaking into the agent's choice, and the score measures the benchmark's tests as much as the agent. This is the flaw most reviewers read past, because "a built-in verifier" sounds like a product feature.

**4. A smaller benchmark.** 61.4% of 83 tasks is 0.614 × 83 = 51 tasks. Counted over all 89 with the 6 excluded tasks as failures, that is 51 / 89 = 57.3%, 4.1 points lower before anything else is questioned. Tasks that need the network are not a random sample of the 89.

**5. "Deterministic at temperature 0".** Temperature 0 does not make an agent run deterministic: tool timings, sandbox state and API conditions all vary between runs, and Anthropic reports pass rates moving with time of day. The Terminal-Bench paper runs each model and agent pair at least five times and reports 95% intervals between ±1.4 and ±4.6 points. One run has no interval at all. This is the second flaw a senior reviewer tends to accept, because temperature 0 is often treated as a reproducibility switch.

**6. A longer time limit.** Each Terminal-Bench task has its own time limit. Raising it to two hours changes the task, and helps exactly the harness that is slow.

**7. Infra errors removed on one side.** Retrying sandbox errors until completion turns every infra error into another attempt for Forge. Whether OpenRig's leaderboard run did the same is not stated. Ask for each side's infra-error rate.

**8. Five months apart.** The two runs differ in date, API conditions, and possibly task fixes in the benchmark itself. That is a confound even with everything else matched.

**9. The cost row.** Forge's cost uses a contracted cache-read rate; OpenRig's uses list prices. Up to three attempts plus a verifier run per task is not reflected in a cost per task unless it says so. Ask for tokens by type per attempt, priced on one price sheet.

**The rerun to ask for: a decision.** The vendor offers one rerun before Friday. Two choices are defensible.

- *Forge and OpenRig on your production model,* all 89 tasks, the same resource configuration at the task spec, the benchmark's time limits, one attempt per trial, five trials per task, interleaved, infra errors reported per side. This answers the question procurement is actually asking: does Forge's harness do better with the model you will run. It costs the vendor's best case: Forge may be tuned for Model X Pro, and the rerun will understate what Forge plus that model would give you.
- *Forge on Model X Pro against OpenRig on Model X Pro,* same controls. This answers whether Forge's harness beats OpenRig on the vendor's preferred model. It costs relevance if you will not buy Model X Pro, and it needs OpenRig set up by someone who is not the vendor.

Choose the first. How it fails: five trials per task gives a paired interval that may still include zero for a gap of a few points, and the honest result on Friday may be "no measured difference". Say in advance that this result means buying on other grounds (price, support, security model), not buying on the slide.

**The two judgement calls.** Comparing against a public leaderboard entry is acceptable if model, task set, resource configuration, time limits and trials match and are stated; Anthropic's recommendation is to document and match setups before trusting small gaps. Retrying infra errors is acceptable if both sides use the same policy and each side's infra-error rate is published, because then the retry measures the agent and the rate shows what was removed.

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: the vendor says a rerun on your model takes two weeks and procurement will not wait. What do you sign off on, and what risk do you accept in writing?* A pilot contract with an exit clause, conditioned on the rerun; the written risk is that the purchase rests on a vendor's own unmatched benchmark for two weeks.
- *Reversal: the rerun shows Forge 5 points ahead with a paired interval of ±3. What would make you still not switch?* Infra-error rates that differ between the two sides, a verifier that turns out to see task tests, or a cost per kept change on your own repositories that is higher. A benchmark lead on terminal tasks does not settle performance on your codebase.
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one says benchmarks are marketing and wants a two-week trial on your repositories; the other wants the matched rerun. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Ask what each result would change. The rerun is cheap and settles whether the slide's gap is real; the trial measures what you buy. Run the rerun first, and run the trial only if the rerun shows a gap.
- *The vendor offers you their verifier as a feature. How do you evaluate it separately?* Report the agent's mean over trials and the verifier's selection accuracy separately: how often the verifier picks a passing attempt when one exists, measured with tests the verifier cannot see.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the table as a harness comparison when the models differ.
- Accepting best of 3 with a verifier as the same metric as a mean over trials.
- Accepting "temperature 0" as a reason to run once.
- Arguing about the resource configuration and missing the 6 excluded tasks, which move the headline 4 points on their own.
- Asking for "a fair rerun" without saying which model it runs on and why.
