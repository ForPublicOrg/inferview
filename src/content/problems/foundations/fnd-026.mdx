## Question

A short-video platform has a text-image embedding model in production. Its image tower embeds video keyframes into an index of 2 billion items, and the search and recommendation teams query it with the text tower. Product wants audio in the same space: search for a sound by description, find videos that reuse a sound, and find audio that fits an image.

Constraints, all scenario inputs: four ML engineers and one quarter. The 2-billion-item image index cannot be re-embedded this quarter; the platform team has given you the budget for one new audio index. You have about 300 million videos with audio and keyframes, 20 million with text captions, and a few thousand audio clips with human-written descriptions.

Design the training: architecture, supervision and losses, evaluation, and deployment. State what you give up and write down the rollout plan.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Freeze the existing image and text towers and train an audio tower into their space, using the 300 million audio and keyframe pairs with a symmetric contrastive loss against the frozen image embeddings, plus the small described-audio set against the frozen text tower. Image is the hub, as in ImageBind, so audio-to-text works without many audio-text pairs. The existing index keeps working unchanged. What I give up is a better joint space: audio can only be as close to text as the frozen image space allows.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>The decision.</strong> Two designs are defensible. Freezing the image and text towers and training only an audio tower keeps every existing vector valid and needs one new index; the ceiling is the frozen space, which was never shaped by audio. Training all three towers jointly gives a better space and requires re-embedding 2 billion keyframes and migrating every consumer, which the constraint rules out this quarter. I take the frozen design now and record the joint retrain as the next-year option, with the measurements that would justify it.</p>
<p><strong>Architecture.</strong> A pretrained audio encoder over log-mel features, a projection head to the existing dimension, and output normalisation matching the existing towers. If consumers want smaller audio vectors, a Matryoshka loss on the head lets them truncate, as long as the image side is truncated the same way at query time.</p>
<p><strong>Supervision and losses.</strong> Audio and keyframe pairs from the same video are the bulk signal: a symmetric InfoNCE loss between the audio embedding and the frozen keyframe embedding, with a learned temperature clipped as CLIP clips it. The described-audio clips add an audio and text loss, upweighted, because they are the only direct signal for the product's main query direction. Batches are mixed by pair type with a fixed ratio, and the ratio is a tuned hyperparameter. CLIP trained with batches of 32,768; the audio encoder will not fit that in memory, so use gradient caching (encode sub-batches without gradients, compute the loss on all embeddings, then re-encode each sub-batch with gradients), which costs an extra forward pass, or gather embeddings across devices, which costs communication. The frozen image side helps here: its embeddings can be precomputed once, so only the audio tower runs in the loop.</p>
<p><strong>False negatives.</strong> On a video platform, many videos reuse the same sound. Two videos with the same track in one batch make each other's audio a negative for an image it genuinely matches. Deduplicate by audio fingerprint before batching, or mask positives that share a fingerprint.</p>
<p><strong>Evaluation.</strong> Recall@k per direction on held-out videos deduplicated against training data by fingerprint and by near-duplicate keyframe: audio to image, image to audio, text to audio, audio to audio (same-sound retrieval). Text to audio has little training supervision, so its held-out set is the one that decides launch. Add a regression check that text-to-image recall on the existing benchmark is unchanged, which it must be if the towers are truly frozen; a change means someone retrained a shared layer.</p>
<p><strong>Deployment.</strong> A new audio index with the model and head version on every vector. Backfill audio for the 300 million videos, newest first, while new uploads are embedded at ingest. Query paths: text and image queries embed with the existing towers and search the audio index; audio queries embed with the new tower and can search either index.</p>
</Fragment>

**What you give up, in writing.** Audio lives in a space shaped by images and captions. Sounds with no visual correlate (a voice-over that does not match the picture, a background track chosen at random) will embed near whatever images they co-occurred with, and text queries about the sound itself will find them only through the described-audio set. The joint retrain fixes this and costs a full re-embed of the image index and a migration of every consumer.

**Rollout plan.**

1. Offline gate: held-out recall@10 per direction above agreed floors, and existing text-to-image recall unchanged.
2. Shadow: audio queries logged and answered from the new index without being shown, for a week, to measure latency and empty-result rate.
3. Launch sound search to a small share of users behind a flag. Rollback triggers: query success rate below control by more than an agreed margin for a day, p95 latency over budget, or any change in text-to-image metrics.
4. Backfill continues during the launch; recall is reported separately for backfilled and freshly ingested items, since a gap there means the ingest path differs from the backfill path.

The failure mode of this design is coupling. Freezing makes the audio tower depend on the exact weights and preprocessing of the image and text towers. If another team retrains or quantises either of them, the audio index silently drifts out of alignment. Pin the tower versions in the audio index metadata and fail the query path if they disagree.

**Self-check.** Tick what your design covered:

- States which existing towers are frozen, which are trained, and what the choice gives up in quality of the shared space
- Picks a hub modality for pairing, and names which modality pairs have no direct supervision and how they are evaluated
- Uses a symmetric contrastive loss per pair type with a learned, clipped temperature, and says how pair types are mixed in a batch
- Sizes the batch and says how it is reached in memory (gradient caching or cross-device gathering) with the cost of each
- Handles duplicates and near-duplicates (reposts, re-uploads, shared audio tracks) as false negatives
- Defines evaluation per direction (text to audio, audio to image and so on) on held-out items deduplicated against training data
- Includes an index-compatibility check that existing text-image vectors still retrieve as well as before
- Gives a rollout with rollback triggers and states how audio vectors are backfilled and versioned
- Writes down what is given up, for example a frozen space that caps cross-modal quality

## Follow-ups

- Legal says the audio of some videos may not be used for training. How do you remove them from the training set and the index, and how do you prove it?
- Two staff engineers disagree: one wants a single model for all modalities now, the other wants per-pair models. How do you decide without deciding by rank?
- What changes if the product adds video as a fourth modality next year?

## Common mistakes

- Proposing the joint retrain without pricing the re-embed of the existing index and the consumer migration.
- Evaluating only the directions with training pairs and assuming the rest work.
- Treating videos that share a sound as independent pairs in a batch.
- Calling the existing index "unchanged" without a regression check that proves the towers stayed frozen.
