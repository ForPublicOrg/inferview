## Question

"Document classification with a short justification moves to a reasoning model because it gains on our eval. The route has a p95 budget of 6 seconds end to end. Measured on our traffic (scenario numbers): time to first token 0.5 s, decode 100 tokens/s, visible output 150 tokens. Which requests get the reasoning model, at what effort, with what output cap, and what do you measure to keep the decision honest?"

Pick the output cap as a number and defend it.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>How many output tokens in total fit inside the p95 budget at the measured decode rate?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>What does the API return when the cap is reached before the visible answer, and what does your client do with it today?</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>The budget fits (6 − 0.5) × 100 = 550 output tokens, so 400 tokens of thinking after the 150-token answer. Thinking is billed as output and decoded sequentially, so effort is a latency setting. I measure thinking tokens and score per effort level on this route, send only the class of documents where the eval gain lives, pick the lowest effort whose p95 thinking fits 400, and set the cap at 550 with an alert on incomplete responses. If no effort fits, this route does not get a reasoning model synchronously.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>What changes.</b> Anthropic's docs: thinking tokens are billed as output even when not returned, count toward max_tokens, and summarized thinking is charged at the full thinking count. OpenAI's: reasoning tokens are invisible, occupy context and are billed as output. Output length now depends on effort and input difficulty, so latency and cost per request grow a tail that the visible answer does not show.</p>
<p><b>The cap.</b> 550 is the latency-derived ceiling, and choosing it has a cost: any request that would think past 400 tokens returns incomplete, on OpenAI with status incomplete and reason max_output_tokens, possibly with no visible text. Two defensible settings. Cap at 550: the budget holds by construction, and the incomplete rate becomes the quality cost, handled by a fallback call to the non-reasoning model. Cap above the measured p99 at the chosen effort: fewer truncations, but the p95 budget holds only if the effort setting keeps the distribution inside it. Unreasonable: a cap that leaves no room for the 150-token answer after typical thinking, or OpenAI's starting reservation of 25,000 tokens, which is advice for experiments, not for a 6-second route.</p>
<p><b>Which requests.</b> Snell et al. found test-time compute paying off on problems the model already had some success on, and the effect depending on difficulty. So the gain is probably concentrated in one class of documents; find it on the eval and route only that class.</p>
<p><b>What to measure.</b> Per effort level: eval score, p50 and p95 thinking tokens, end-to-end p95, incomplete rate. In production: the same, plus the share routed and cost per request, re-measured on every model version change. Not the thinking text: Anthropic found Claude 3.7 Sonnet mentioning a hint it used 25% of the time, so the reasoning is not a record to audit against.</p>
<p><b>Failure mode.</b> The router's difficulty signal drifts as new document types arrive, the routed class grows, and p95 breaches without any change to the model. The routed share is an alerting metric.</p>
</Fragment>

<li>What would you need to see to move the whole route to the reasoning model?</li>

## Follow-ups

- Parallel samples with a vote instead of a longer chain: what does it do to latency, cost and your rate limits on this route?
- Compliance asks for the model's reasoning to be stored with each classification. What do you store instead?

## Common mistakes

- Pricing the route on visible output tokens.
- Setting a tight output cap without handling the incomplete response, and shipping empty justifications.
- One effort setting for every route and every document class.
- Treating thinking text as an audit trail.
