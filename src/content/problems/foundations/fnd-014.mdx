## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design, 30 minutes">
Scenario: you classify incoming support messages into 12 categories, 1,000,000 a day. Each request is about 500 input tokens, of which about 400 are fixed instructions and the label list, and the visible answer is a label of about 5 tokens. Three categories (fraud, outage, legal threat) route to urgent queues. Today a frontier model scores 96% on a 300-message labelled set. Finance caps the feature at $4,000 a month; product sets a floor of 95% overall accuracy and 90% recall on each urgent category. Two engineers own it, there is no GPU platform team, and the switch must land in six weeks. Choose the model setup and design the bake-off that justifies it.
</Callout>

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask how many output tokens each candidate actually bills per request at its lowest accepted effort, not how many the label needs.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask how many of the 300 labelled messages belong to each urgent category.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask whether a label can arrive minutes late for any category.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>The frontier model is out on cost alone: 500M input and 5M output tokens a day is about $2,100 a day on Opus 5.5. Haiku 5.5 with the 400-token prefix cached and about 100 billed thinking tokens per request is about $66.50 a day, roughly $2,000 a month, which leaves room to escalate about 3% of traffic to Sonnet 5.5 under the cap. The bake-off is a paired comparison on a set rebuilt so each urgent category has enough examples to measure 90% recall, and the memo records what we give up: frontier accuracy on the 97% of traffic that never escalates.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Price the candidates</b> (Claude list prices, checked 28 October 2026). Volume: 500M input and 5M visible output tokens a day. Opus 5.5 at $4 / $20 is 500 × 4 + 5 × 20 = $2,100 a day. Sonnet 5.5 at $2 / $10 is $1,050 a day. Haiku 5.5 at $0.10 / $0.50 is $52.50 a day, but that assumes no thinking. Haiku 5.5 thinks adaptively at default effort medium, and thinking is billed as output: at 100 thinking tokens per request (a number to measure, not assume) output becomes 105M tokens and the day costs $102.50, or $3,075 a month, most of the cap before any escalation.</p>
<p><b>Cut the input bill.</b> The 400-token prefix is identical on every request. Cached at Haiku's $0.01 per million for hits, the input becomes 400M × $0.01 + 100M × $0.10 = $14 a day instead of $50, so the day is about $66.50 and the month about $1,995. The remaining $2,005 a month, $67 a day, pays for escalations: Sonnet 5.5 at 500 input and 105 output tokens costs $0.00205 per message, so about 32,600 messages a day, 3.3% of traffic. If labels for non-urgent categories can wait, the Batch API halves the price of those requests and roughly doubles the escalation budget.</p>
<p><b>Self-hosting</b> an open-weight model fixes the cost to GPU-hours and removes per-token billing, but with two engineers, no platform team and six weeks, operating an inference stack is the larger risk. I would leave it out of this round and say so in the memo.</p>
<p><b>Design the bake-off.</b> The 300-message set has about 25 messages per category on average and likely fewer in the urgent ones; at 25 examples a 90% recall estimate has a standard error of 6 points, too wide to check a floor. I would add labelled messages until each urgent category has at least 100 (standard error about 3 points), keep the set paired across candidates, run each message three times to measure label instability, and report overall accuracy, per-category recall with intervals, billed tokens per request and cost per correct label. Escalation rules (low label confidence, an urgent label, a parse failure) are part of the system under test, so the bake-off scores the routed system, not Haiku alone.</p>
<p><b>Write down</b> a one-page memo: the chosen setup, the measured numbers against the floors, the cap arithmetic, and what we give up. Failure mode: the escalation rate is set on today's traffic; a new product launch that shifts messages toward ambiguous categories raises escalations and breaks the cap, so the escalation rate is an alert with a threshold, and above it the memo says which urgent rule is relaxed first.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- *Would a fine-tuned encoder classifier beat this setup? What would you need to measure, and who maintains it?*
- *The vendor retires Haiku 5.5 next year. What in this design makes the migration cheap?*

## Common mistakes

- Pricing the label's 5 tokens and forgetting billed thinking tokens, which can double the bill.
- Comparing candidates on overall accuracy only, which hides an urgent category below its recall floor.
- Scoring the small model alone when the shipped system is the small model plus escalation rules.
- Treating a 300-message set as enough to check a per-category floor.
