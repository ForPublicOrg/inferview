    correct: false
    why: The scaling is applied to the scores QKᵀ, before the softmax. V is never divided by anything.
  - text: To stop gradients exploding in the feed-forward layer that follows attention
    correct: false
    why: The residual connections and normalisation layers handle the scale of activations between sub-layers. The paper's stated reason is the softmax, and the problem is gradients that vanish, not explode.

## Question

In scaled dot-product attention, softmax(QKᵀ / √d_k) V, why are the scores divided by the square root of the key dimension d_k?

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>What is the variance of a sum of d_k independent products, each with variance 1?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>What does softmax look like when its inputs are spread over a range of plus or minus 30?</p></details>

## Answer

If the components of q and k are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, each product q_i k_i has variance 1, and the sum of d_k of them has variance d_k. At d_k = 64, the head size in the original base model, the scores have a standard deviation of √64 = 8. Softmax over inputs that far apart is nearly one-hot: one weight close to 1 and the rest close to 0, and the gradient through a saturated softmax is close to zero, so training slows or stalls. Dividing by √d_k brings the standard deviation back to 1.

Why the wrong answers are tempting. "Rows sum to 1" describes softmax, which sits right next to the scaling in the formula. "Normalise V" sounds plausible because the output should keep its scale, but the division happens on the scores. "Exploding gradients" names the right family of problem with the wrong direction and the wrong place.

## Follow-ups

- *What happens if you forget it?* The spread of the scores grows with d_k, so the larger the head, the more saturated the softmax. Turn the scaling off in the attention lesson's widget and the weights sharpen even at d_k = 2.
- *Where else does the same idea appear?* Temperature in sampling divides logits by a constant before softmax for the same reason: it controls how peaked the distribution is.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the scaling makes the weights a probability distribution.
- Dividing by d_k instead of √d_k in an implementation.
- Applying the scale after the softmax.
