## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Estimate, 25 minutes">
Scenario: an invoice-extraction route sends each invoice (about 3,000 input tokens) to a model that returns structured fields. A validator (schema, line items summing to the total) decides success; a failed attempt is retried with the same input, at most 3 attempts in total because of a latency budget, and an invoice that fails all three goes to a human queue. Your bake-off on 1,000 production invoices measured, per model:

| Model | First-attempt success | Success on a retry after a failure | Output tokens per attempt, thinking included |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opus 5.5 | 94% | 60% | 1,200 |
| Sonnet 5.5 | 90% | 45% | 900 |
| Haiku 5.5 | 78% | 20% | 600 |

Prices (Claude list, checked 28 October 2026): Opus 5.5 $4 / $20, Sonnet 5.5 $2 / $10, Haiku 5.5 $0.10 / $0.50 per million input and output tokens. Compute the model cost per successful invoice for each, then the total cost per invoice including the human queue. Choose a handling cost per failed invoice for your company and name the winner. What is the handling cost at which Opus 5.5 overtakes Sonnet 5.5?
</Callout>

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether a retry of the same invoice succeeds as often as a first attempt did, and check the table before assuming it.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Per attempt: Opus $0.036, Sonnet $0.015, Haiku $0.0006. With the measured retry rates, expected attempts are 1.084, 1.155 and 1.396, and the share failing all three is 0.96%, 3.0% and 14.1%. Model cost per successful invoice: about $0.039, $0.018 and $0.001. Total per invoice is model cost plus failure share times the handling cost H: Haiku wins below about $0.15, Sonnet between $0.15 and about $1.05, Opus above $1.05. The human queue is the dominant term at any realistic H, and the assumption that flips the result is retry independence.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Cost per attempt.</b> Opus: 3,000 × $4 / 10⁶ + 1,200 × $20 / 10⁶ = $0.012 + $0.024 = $0.036. Sonnet: $0.006 + $0.009 = $0.015. Haiku: $0.0003 + $0.0003 = $0.0006. Output dominates for the two larger models because thinking is billed as output.</p>
<p><b>Attempts and residual failures</b>, using the measured retry success rate r after a first-attempt failure rate f: expected attempts = 1 + f + f(1 − r); residual failure = f(1 − r)². Opus: 1 + 0.06 + 0.024 = 1.084 attempts, 0.06 × 0.4² = 0.96% failing. Sonnet: 1 + 0.10 + 0.055 = 1.155, 0.10 × 0.55² = 3.03%. Haiku: 1 + 0.22 + 0.176 = 1.396, 0.22 × 0.8² = 14.08%.</p>
<p><b>Model cost per successful invoice</b> = cost per attempt × attempts / (1 − residual failure). Opus: $0.0390 / 0.9904 = $0.0394. Sonnet: $0.0173 / 0.9698 = $0.0179. Haiku: $0.00084 / 0.8592 = $0.00098. On this number alone Haiku wins by a factor of about 18 over Sonnet.</p>
<p><b>Total per invoice</b> = model cost per invoice + residual failure × H. Haiku: 0.00084 + 0.1408H. Sonnet: 0.01733 + 0.0303H. Opus: 0.03902 + 0.0096H. Sonnet overtakes Haiku where 0.1105H = 0.0165, H ≈ $0.15. Opus overtakes Sonnet where 0.0207H = 0.0217, <b>H ≈ $1.05</b>. I choose H = $4 (a few minutes of an operations clerk plus the follow-up with the supplier): Haiku $0.564, Sonnet $0.139, Opus $0.077 per invoice, so Opus wins, and at that H the queue term (0.0096 × $4 = $0.038) is about the same size as Opus's model cost. Any H above about $1.05 gives the same winner; a team whose failures are handled by a cheap automated fallback could reasonably pick H below $1 and choose Sonnet.</p>
<p><b>Which choices are unreasonable.</b> Treating retries as independent draws at the first-attempt rate gives residual failures of 0.02%, 0.1% and 1.1% and moves the Opus break-even to about $28 per failed invoice, a conclusion the bake-off's own retry column contradicts: failures cluster on hard invoices. Ignoring retries entirely (one attempt, failures to the queue) gives a break-even of about $0.53, which ignores money the route actually spends. Both answers fall outside the band; adding a few hundred tokens of validator feedback to each retry stays inside it.</p>
<p><b>Failure mode of the recommendation.</b> The validator defines success. An extraction that passes the schema and the total check with a wrong supplier ID counts as a success here and costs more than a queued failure later, so the rate of validator-passing errors, measured by sampling passed invoices, has to be added per model before the decision is final; a model with fewer queue failures but more silent errors can lose.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- *How would the Batch API change the comparison for invoices that can wait a day?*

## Common mistakes

- Comparing price per token or cost per attempt and stopping there.
- Assuming retries succeed at the first-attempt rate when the bake-off measured otherwise.
- Leaving the human queue out of the total, which makes the cheapest model look best at any accuracy.
- Treating validator success as correctness without sampling passed outputs.
