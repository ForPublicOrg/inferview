## Question

You are fully fine-tuning a model with the Llama 3.1 8B shape (hidden 4,096, intermediate 14,336, 32 layers, 8 key-value heads of 128, vocabulary 128,256, untied embeddings) on one node of 8 × 80 GB GPUs (scenario). Training uses bf16 with Adam, ZeRO stage 2 data parallelism across the 8 GPUs, sequences of 4,096 tokens, micro-batch 1 per GPU and full activation checkpointing.

Estimate the peak memory on one GPU in GB, model states and activations together. State each assumption you choose. Then say whether you would run this job as specified, move to ZeRO stage 3, or switch to LoRA or QLoRA, with the memory figure for the option you pick.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Does your training framework keep a separate fp32 buffer for accumulating gradients, and in what precision does it compute the loss?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Which of the memory terms are partitioned across the 8 GPUs at this stage, and which does every GPU hold in full?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>What would one profiled training step at 4,096 tokens on this hardware report as the peak?</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Parameters: 8.03B from the shapes. Model states at 16 bytes per parameter are 128.5 GB unsharded; ZeRO stage 2 keeps the 2-byte weights on every GPU and shards the other 14 bytes: 16.1 + 14.1 = 30.1 GB per GPU. Activations are not sharded by data parallelism: about 1.1 GB of checkpointed layer inputs, 0.6 GB for recomputing one layer, and 2.1 GB of fp32 logits plus 2.1 GB for their gradient, about 5.9 GB. Peak about 36 GB, or about 40 GB if the stack keeps an fp32 gradient accumulation buffer (20 bytes). It fits with headroom, so I run stage 2 as specified.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Parameter count.</b> Per layer: attention 2 × 4096² + 2 × 4096 × 1024 = 41,943,040; MLP 3 × 4096 × 14336 = 176,160,768; two norms 8,192; total 218,112,000. 32 layers: 6,979,584,000. Embeddings in and out: 2 × 128,256 × 4,096 = 1,050,673,152. Final norm 4,096. Total 8,030,261,248.</p>
<p><b>Model states.</b> The ZeRO accounting: 2 bytes of half-precision weights, 2 of gradients, 12 of fp32 weights, momentum and variance. Unsharded that is 128.5 GB. Stage 2 partitions gradients and optimizer state: 2 × 8.03 + 14 × 8.03 / 8 = 30.1 GB per GPU. If the framework also keeps an fp32 gradient accumulation buffer (a reasonable assumption to choose), the sharded part is 18 bytes: 16.1 + 18 × 8.03 / 8 = 34.1 GB.</p>
<p><b>Activations.</b> Each GPU runs its own micro-batch, so activations are per GPU, unsharded. With full checkpointing the saved layer inputs are 4,096 × 4,096 × 2 bytes × 32 = 1.07 GB. Recomputing one layer in the backward pass needs its full working set: per token, about 6 × 4,096 + 2 × 1,024 + 3 × 14,336 = 69,632 bf16 values, about 0.57 GB at 4,096 tokens, assuming fused attention that never materialises the 4,096 × 4,096 score matrix. The output layer is the term people miss: fp32 logits are 4,096 × 128,256 × 4 = 2.1 GB, and their gradient is the same size. Total about 5.9 GB.</p>
<p><b>Peak.</b> 30.1 + 5.9 = 36 GB at 16 bytes; 34.1 + 5.9 = 40 GB at 20 bytes. The accepted band, 33 to 46 GB, admits either byte count, bf16 or fp32 logits, and an allowance of a few GB for communication buckets and allocator fragmentation. Unreasonable: quoting 128.5 GB (ignores the sharding the question specifies), quoting 30 GB (no activations), or counting activations without checkpointing.</p>
<p><b>The dominant term</b> is the replicated 16.1 GB of bf16 weights plus the sharded optimizer state; activations are small here only because micro-batch is 1 and checkpointing is on. <b>The assumption that flips the conclusion</b> is sequence length with fp32 logits: at 32K tokens the logits and their gradient alone are 2 × 32,768 × 128,256 × 4 bytes = 33.6 GB, and stage 2 no longer has room.</p>
<p><b>The decision.</b> Two defensible answers. Run stage 2 as specified: about 40 GB of headroom and data-parallel communication volume, which the ZeRO paper reports for stages 1 and 2. Or switch to LoRA on all layers at rank 16: 41.9M trainable parameters, 16.1 GB frozen weights plus 0.67 GB adapter states, about 22.7 GB with the same activations; QLoRA brings the frozen weights to about 4.0 GB plus quantization constants. LoRA saves memory, not much compute: LoRA Without Regret counts slightly more than 2/3 of the full fine-tuning FLOPs per pass, so it does not finish three times faster. It is the better answer when the dataset is small or medium and the goal is behaviour, or when the node is shared; full fine-tuning is the better answer when the dataset is large enough that adapter capacity becomes the limit.</p>
<p><b>Failure mode of the recommended stage 2 run.</b> The estimate holds at 4,096 tokens and fails silently when someone raises the sequence length for a long-document slice of the data: the logits term grows linearly and the run dies with an out-of-memory error hours in, on the first long batch. The plan caps sequence length in the data loader, or computes the loss in chunks so full logits never exist, and profiles one step at the longest sequence in the dataset, not the median.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- The 8-bit optimizer in your library stores momentum and variance in 1 byte each. Recompute the stage 2 figure and say which term now dominates.
- You need 32K-token sequences for a contract-review slice of the data. Which change to the loss computation makes that fit, and what does it cost?
- What changes in this estimate when the same model is served after training, and which parts of the 36 GB disappear?

## Common mistakes

- Sharding activations by 8 as if they were model states; data parallelism gives each GPU its own activations.
- Forgetting the replicated bf16 weights at stage 2, which are the largest single term per GPU.
- Ignoring the output layer: with a 128K vocabulary, fp32 logits at 4,096 tokens are as large as all 32 layers' checkpointed inputs combined, twice over.
- Treating LoRA as a compute saving of the same size as its memory saving.
- Quoting the model-state figure as the GPU requirement without profiling one step at the longest sequence.
