## Question

You want to fully fine-tune a 7B-parameter model with Adam in mixed precision (bf16 weights and gradients, fp32 master weights and optimizer moments). Estimate the GPU memory for the model states alone: weights, gradients and optimizer state, ignoring activations and framework overhead. Answer in GB.

Then estimate the same figure for LoRA and for QLoRA, and say what the estimate leaves out.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Count bytes per parameter: half-precision weights, half-precision gradients, and the fp32 copy plus two Adam moments.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>With LoRA, only the adapter parameters need gradients and optimizer state; the frozen base still has to be stored.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Mixed-precision Adam needs 16 bytes per trained parameter: 2 for bf16 weights, 2 for bf16 gradients, 12 for fp32 master weights and the two Adam moments. 7 billion × 16 bytes = 112 GB before activations. LoRA keeps the frozen base at 2 bytes per parameter, 14 GB, plus well under 1 GB for the adapters and their optimizer state. QLoRA stores the base in 4 bits, about 3.5 GB plus quantization constants, and the adapters on top.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p>Full fine-tuning. The ZeRO paper's accounting: bf16 weights (2 bytes) and gradients (2 bytes) for the forward and backward pass, and an fp32 master copy plus Adam's first and second moments (4 + 4 + 4 = 12 bytes) for the update. 16 bytes × 7 × 10^9 = 112 GB. The accepted band is 100 to 125 GB, which covers rounding the parameter count.</p>
<p>LoRA. Take a Llama 3.1 8B-shaped model as the worked example, since its config is public. Adapters of rank r on every linear layer add r × (d_in + d_out) parameters per matrix. Per layer that is q and o at 4,096 + 4,096 each, k and v at 4,096 + 1,024 each, gate and up at 4,096 + 14,336 each, and down at 14,336 + 4,096: 81,920 × r. Over 32 layers at r = 16 that is 41,943,040 parameters, about 0.52% of the 8.03 billion. Their model states are 41.9M × 16 bytes ≈ 0.67 GB. The frozen base in bf16 is 8.03B × 2 ≈ 16.1 GB. Total about 16.7 GB instead of about 128.5 GB.</p>
<p>QLoRA. The frozen base in 4-bit NormalFloat is 0.5 bytes per parameter, about 4 GB for 8.03B, plus the quantization constants, which double quantization keeps small; adapters stay in higher precision. The paper fine-tuned a 65B model on one 48 GB GPU.</p>
<p>What the estimate leaves out. Activations, which grow with batch size and sequence length and can dominate at long context (gradient checkpointing trades them for recompute); temporary buffers; and allocator fragmentation. Run one training step at your real sequence length before quoting a GPU count.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>How would you fit full fine-tuning of 7B on smaller GPUs?</em> Shard the optimizer state, gradients and weights across GPUs (ZeRO stages 1 to 3), or offload optimizer state to CPU memory.</li>
<li><em>Does LoRA lose quality?</em> The 2025 LoRA Without Regret results found it matches full fine-tuning on small and medium SFT datasets when applied to all layers including the MLP, with about a 10 times higher learning rate.</li>
<li><em>What does inference cost after LoRA?</em> Nothing extra once BA is merged into the weights; keep adapters separate only if you want to swap them per customer.</li>
</ul>

## Follow-ups

- *Why 12 bytes for the optimizer and not 8?* The fp32 master copy of the weights is kept alongside the two moments, because bf16 cannot represent small updates accurately.
- *What changes with an 8-bit optimizer?* The moments shrink, so the per-parameter cost drops below 16 bytes; the weights and gradients do not change.

## Common mistakes

- Counting only the weights (14 GB) and forgetting gradients and optimizer state.
- Counting 4 bytes per parameter for Adam state instead of 12 with the master copy.
- Quoting the model-state figure as the total GPU memory, with no word about activations.
