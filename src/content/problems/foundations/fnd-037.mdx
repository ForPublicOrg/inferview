## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Multiple choice">
Scenario: a consumer app has had about 900M monthly active users for two years. The team plans to fine-tune Llama 4 Scout on its own data and serve it in a new feature, rolling out to 5% of users first. Legal reviews the Llama 4 Community License. Which clause changes the plan?
</Callout>

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask legal whose users the clause counts, and as of which date.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask what rights the licensee has while a request to Meta is pending.</p></details>

## Answer

Section 2 of the Llama 4 Community License sets a threshold of 700 million monthly active users, counted across all products and services of the licensee and its affiliates, in the calendar month before the Llama 4 version release date (5 April 2025). A licensee above it must request a licence from Meta, which Meta may grant at its sole discretion, and has no rights under the agreement until Meta grants them. An app that has been near 900M for two years was above the threshold on that date, so it cannot ship on Llama 4 under the community licence at all, at any rollout percentage. The plan changes from "fine-tune and launch" to "request a licence, with no date, or choose another model".

Counting only the feature's users is tempting because rate limits and quotas are usually scoped per feature; the licence scopes by licensee and affiliates. Reading the clause as a growth trigger is tempting because many SaaS terms work that way; this one is a one-time test at the release date, which also means a company that crosses 700M later is not caught by it. The naming clause is a real duty for a distributed derived model and annoys brand teams, but it is downstream of having rights.

## Follow-ups

- *The same company crossed 700M users in June 2025, after the release date. What does section 2 say then, and what would you still ask legal before relying on that reading?*
- *Meta has not answered the licence request after eight weeks and the feature has a launch date. What do you put in the decision memo?*

## Common mistakes

- Treating "open weights" as "permissively licensed" and skipping the licence review until after engineering work.
- Reading the user threshold as a per-product or per-feature count.
- Assuming a licence request lets work proceed while Meta decides.
