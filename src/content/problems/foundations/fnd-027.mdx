## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design round">
You own the serving plan for a contract-review product that needs 128K-token context. The budget is one node of 8 GPUs with 80 GB each, for the next two quarters. Pick the open model, and therefore the attention layout, you would serve, and defend the KV bytes per token and the quality evidence you would require before launch.
</Callout>

The scenario. The product team wants as many concurrent sessions as the node can hold. Most sessions are short; a minority load whole contract bundles near the limit. The serving engine your team runs in production has kernels you trust for grouped-query and sliding-window attention; it also lists multi-head latent attention as supported, but nobody on the team has operated it. Legal has asked for a written note of what the model can and cannot be relied on to read at the far end of the window.

Candidates, from their published configs (checked 2026-10-28): Llama 3.1 70B (GQA, 8 KV heads), DeepSeek-V3 (MLA), Gemma 3 27B (five 1,024-token sliding-window layers per global layer) and gpt-oss-120b (alternating 128-token windows and full attention, 8 KV heads). The configs are in the sources; read them as you would in the meeting.

Choose your own session mix and concurrency target and state it.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask for the distribution of session context lengths, not the maximum.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>For each candidate, ask whether the weights fit the node before you compare caches.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask the engine owners whether windowed layers are allocated at the window or at the full context.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>KV bytes per token in bf16, from the configs: Llama 3.1 70B 320 KiB; DeepSeek-V3 68.6 KiB; Gemma 3 27B 80 KiB for its 10 global layers plus a fixed 416 MiB per sequence for the local ones; gpt-oss-120b 36 KiB for its 18 full layers plus 4.5 MiB. A 128K sequence costs 40, 8.6, 10.4 and 4.5 GiB. DeepSeek-V3's FP8 weights are at least 671 GB and do not fit a 640 GB node, so it is out on budget, not on layout. I would serve gpt-oss-120b if it passes a long-context evaluation on our own contracts, with Llama 3.1 70B as the fallback whose layout carries no windowing risk, at roughly a tenth of the concurrency.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>The formula per layer type.</b> Full attention with per-head keys and values: 2 × KV heads × head_dim × bytes per layer per token. A sliding-window layer is the same per token but stops growing at the window. MLA caches one latent of kv_lora_rank values plus one shared rotary key of qk_rope_head_dim values per layer per token.</p>
<p><b>The numbers.</b> Llama 3.1 70B: 2 × 80 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 327,680 bytes per token, 40 GiB per 131,072-token sequence. DeepSeek-V3: 61 × (512 + 64) × 2 = 70,272 bytes per token, 8.58 GiB per sequence. Gemma 3 27B: every sixth of its 62 layers is global, so 10 global and 52 local; each layer costs 2 × 16 × 128 × 2 = 8,192 bytes per token, so the global layers cost 81,920 bytes per token, 10 GiB per sequence, and the local layers 52 × 8,192 × 1,024 bytes = 416 MiB, 10.41 GiB in all. gpt-oss-120b: each layer costs 2 × 8 × 64 × 2 = 2,048 bytes per token; 18 full layers give 36,864 bytes per token, 4.5 GiB per sequence, and 18 windowed layers 4.5 MiB.</p>
<p><b>Fit on the node.</b> 8 × 80 GB is 596 GiB. Ignoring activations and the engine's reserve, which a real plan cannot ignore: Llama 3.1 70B in bf16 is about 140 GB of weights (70 billion × 2 bytes), 130 GiB, leaving about 466 GiB, 11 sequences at full length. Gemma 3 27B is about 54 GB, 50 GiB, leaving about 546 GiB, 52 sequences. gpt-oss-120b's checkpoint is 60.8 GiB, leaving about 535 GiB, 118 sequences. My scenario mix: 80% of sessions at 16K and 20% at 128K. Average cost per session is then 0.8 × 16,384 + 0.2 × 131,072 = 39,322 tokens, which costs 12.0 GiB on Llama and 1.35 GiB on gpt-oss, so Llama holds about 38 sessions of that mix and gpt-oss about 395, before compute limits, which will bind first for gpt-oss.</p>
<p><b>The decision.</b> Two answers are defensible. gpt-oss-120b gives roughly ten times the sessions per node and runs on kernels the team trusts. Its cost: half its layers see only 128 tokens, so everything a token learns from the far end of a contract passes through 18 full layers, and the evidence that this holds on our documents does not exist yet. Llama 3.1 70B costs ten times the memory per session and puts every layer on the full context; its cost is concurrency, and with it the price per session. Gemma 3 27B sits between them on memory, and its report's quality evidence for the window is perplexity, which says little about whether clause 212 is read correctly against clause 3. DeepSeek-V3 has the best ratio of cache to model size here and is ruled out by its weights; on a two-node budget it would be the candidate to evaluate, with the unoperated MLA path as the risk.</p>
<p><b>What you give up.</b> Choosing gpt-oss gives up the certainty that every layer sees every token, and accepts a model whose per-session cost depends on the engine allocating windowed layers at the window.</p>
<p><b>Failure mode of the recommendation.</b> If the engine allocates the windowed layers at full context, which some engines do for simplicity, the cache doubles to 9 GiB per 128K sequence, and the capacity plan is wrong by a factor of two on the day of launch. Check the engine's allocator with a measured peak at 128K before signing the plan.</p>
<p><b>The written artefact.</b> A decision memo: the chosen model; the per-token and per-session numbers above; the evaluation gate (accuracy on a held-out set of our own contracts with questions whose evidence sits in the first, middle and last quarter of a 100K-token bundle, against Llama 3.1 70B on the same set); and the reversal trigger: if gpt-oss trails on last-quarter questions beyond the agreed margin, or the measured cache at 128K exceeds 5 GiB per sequence, switch to Llama and cut the concurrency target in writing with the product team.</p>

The arithmetic in one table, bf16 cache, 131,072-token sequence:

| Model | Layout | KV bytes per token (growing part) | Fixed per sequence | Per 128K sequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Llama 3.1 70B | GQA, 80 full layers | 327,680 | 0 | 40 GiB |
| DeepSeek-V3 | MLA, 61 layers of 576 values | 70,272 | 0 | 8.58 GiB |
| Gemma 3 27B | 10 global, 52 local (1,024) | 81,920 | 416 MiB | 10.41 GiB |
| gpt-oss-120b | 18 full, 18 windowed (128) | 36,864 | 4.5 MiB | 4.50 GiB |

Reasonable choices of mix move the session counts, not the ranking: any mix where long sessions dominate the token count makes the per-token term the whole decision, which is why the memo states the mix it assumed.

## Follow-ups

- *The engine team says MLA decode is supported but slower per token than GQA on your GPUs. How much slower would it have to be before DeepSeek-V3 loses on a two-node budget, and what do you measure to find out?*
- *Legal asks for a sentence on what the model can be relied on to read at 128K. What do you write, and which evaluation result is it based on?*
- *A Gemma 3 advocate cites the report's perplexity ablation. What would you need to see before accepting it as evidence for contract review?*

## Common mistakes

- Comparing models by maximum context in the config instead of KV bytes per token.
- Applying the full-attention formula to every layer of a hybrid model, which overstates gpt-oss by a factor of two and Gemma 3 by six.
- Forgetting that the weights have to fit first.
- Treating a perplexity ablation as evidence of retrieval at the far end of the window.
- Leaving out what would reverse the decision.
