  - text: Cosine, dot product and L2 always produce the same ranking, so the choice only affects speed
    correct: false

  - text: They produce the same ranking when all vectors are normalised to length 1; on unnormalised vectors the dot product favours long vectors and L2 penalises them
    correct: true
    why: For unit vectors, cosine equals the dot product and squared L2 distance equals 2 minus 2 times the cosine, so all three sort identically. Without normalisation, length leaks into the dot product and into L2.
  - text: L2 is always the safest choice because it is a true distance metric
    correct: false
    why: Being a metric does not make it measure meaning. On unnormalised vectors L2 ranks the same-direction long document below an unrelated one.
  - text: The dot product is wrong for text embeddings because it ignores the angle between vectors
    correct: false
    why: The dot product depends on the angle and the lengths. On unit vectors it is exactly the cosine, which is why normalised providers recommend it as the faster equivalent.

## Question

A query vector q = [0.9, 0.1, 0.3, 0.2] is compared with three stored vectors:

| Document | Vector | Cosine | Dot product | L2 distance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| short | [0.9, 0.1, 0.3, 0.2] | 1.000 | 0.95 | 0.000 |
| long | [2.7, 0.3, 0.9, 0.6] | 1.000 | 2.85 | 1.949 |
| other | [0.1, 0.9, 0.2, 0.4] | 0.325 | 0.32 | 1.153 |

"long" is "short" multiplied by 3. Which statement about choosing a similarity metric for the vector index is correct?

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Which column ranks "long" first, which ranks it last, and which cannot tell it from "short"?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Expand |a - b|² for two vectors of length 1.</p></details>

## Answer

The correct statement is the conditional one. For unit-length vectors, |a - b|² = |a|² + |b|² - 2 a·b = 2 - 2 cos(a, b), and a·b = cos(a, b), so the three metrics are monotonic functions of each other and sort documents identically. OpenAI's embeddings guide relies on exactly this: its embeddings are normalised, so you can use the cheaper dot product and get the cosine ranking.

The table shows what happens without normalisation. Cosine says "short" and "long" are equally good matches. The dot product ranks "long" first because it is longer. L2 ranks "long" below "other", an unrelated document, because it is far away in absolute terms.

Why the wrong answers are tempting. "Always the same ranking" is the normalised case stated without its condition. "L2 is a true metric" is correct mathematics and irrelevant to retrieval quality. "Dot product ignores the angle" is backwards: it is the angle times the lengths.

## Follow-ups

- *How do you check whether your vectors are normalised?* Compute the norm of a sample of stored vectors; if they are not all 1 within float error, normalise at write time or use cosine.
- *What breaks normalisation silently?* Truncating a Matryoshka embedding to fewer dimensions. Problem fnd-006 is that bug.
- *Which metric would you configure?* The one the model card names. If the vectors are normalised, use the dot product: it ranks like cosine and skips the division.

## Common mistakes

- Saying "cosine and dot product are the same" without the unit-length condition.
- Normalising the documents but not the query, or the reverse, and then mixing metrics between index and re-ranker.
- Choosing L2 because a vector database defaults to it, without checking how the embeddings were trained.
