## Question

Draw RLHF with PPO, DPO and GRPO with verifiable rewards as data flows on one whiteboard: which models sit in memory, where samples come from, and where the reward comes from. For each, name one published hyperparameter choice and the failure mode that choice or the method is exposed to.

Then pick a group size for a GRPO run of your own: 4,000 maths prompts, a rollout budget of 64,000 samples per epoch, and a measured pass rate of the starting policy that is above 0.9 on 30% of prompts (scenario numbers). Defend the number.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>For each method, which of these exist: a learned reward model, a value model, fresh samples from the current policy during training?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>For the group size: what fraction of groups would you expect to have identical rewards on the easy 30%, and what does a group like that contribute?</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>PPO-RLHF: policy, reference, learned reward model and value model, with fresh samples scored online; exposed to reward hacking of the learned reward and to instability. DPO: policy and frozen reference on a fixed set of chosen and rejected pairs, no sampling; Llama 3 used beta 0.1 and found formatting tokens in the loss caused tail repetition. GRPO: policy and reference, fresh groups scored by a checker, the group mean as baseline; R1-Zero used 16 samples per question and KL 0.001, and the objective has a length bias. For my run, 16 per prompt: 64,000 / 16 = 4,000 prompts, one epoch, and I filter the easy 30% because 18.5% of their groups at p = 0.9 carry no signal.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>PPO-RLHF.</b> InstructGPT's pipeline: SFT on demonstrations, a reward model trained on human rankings, then PPO against it with a value model for the baseline and a KL penalty to the reference. Four networks in the loop, sampling on every step. Exposure: the policy finds what the learned reward model over-scores, and the method is sensitive to tuning. Llama 3 reports trying PPO and choosing DPO because DPO needed less compute at large scale and did better on instruction following.</p>
<p><b>DPO.</b> The reward model is folded into a classification loss on preference pairs: raise the chosen response's log-probability relative to the frozen reference, lower the rejected one's. Offline, no sampling, two networks. Llama 3's settings: learning rate 1e-5, beta 0.1, plus an NLL term on chosen responses with coefficient 0.2. Exposure: it learns only from the pairs collected, and token-level side effects appear in the output; Llama 3 masked formatting tokens out of the loss because they caused tail repetition and abrupt termination tokens.</p>
<p><b>GRPO with verifiable rewards.</b> Sample a group per prompt, score each with a checker (tests, exact match), and use each sample's reward relative to the group mean as its advantage; no value model, and DeepSeekMath adds the KL to the loss instead of the reward. DeepSeekMath used 64 samples per question, lr 1e-6, KL 0.04; R1-Zero used 16, lr 3e-6, KL 0.001. Exposure: the checker is the whole reward, so gaps in it are exploited; Dr. GRPO reports an optimisation bias that inflates response length, strongest on incorrect outputs; and DeepSeekMath reports RL improving Maj@K but not Pass@K, so gains are mostly sharpening.</p>
<p><b>The group size.</b> 64,000 samples over 4,000 prompts is 16 per prompt for one pass. A group is wasted when all samples get the same reward: at p = 0.9 and G = 16 that is 0.9^16 + 0.1^16 = 18.5%; at G = 8 it is 43%. Larger groups waste fewer groups but cover fewer prompts per epoch: G = 32 gives 2,000 prompts. Reasonable answers are 8 to 32 with a stated trade-off; 16 with the easy 30% filtered or down-weighted, and those rollouts given to the harder prompts, spends the budget where advantages are non-zero. Failure mode of the filter: pass rates move during training, so a filter computed at step 0 goes stale; recompute it from logged rewards every few hundred steps.</p>
</Fragment>

## Common mistakes

- Saying DPO has no reward; it has an implicit one defined by the policy and the reference.
- Treating a verifiable reward as hack-proof; tests that pass on wrong code are a reward model with blind spots.
- Choosing the group size from the paper you read last instead of from the pass-rate distribution and the rollout budget.
- Quoting a majority-vote score as pass@1.
