## Question

"Explain RLHF with PPO, DPO and GRPO. Why did DPO become popular, and why are reasoning models trained with GRPO-style RL instead?"

You have a whiteboard. Draw the data flow of each and name what each one needs that the others do not.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>For each method, ask: is there a separate reward model, a value model, and does it sample from the policy during training?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>What kind of reward can you compute without a human or a learned model?</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>RLHF with PPO trains a reward model on human preference rankings, then optimises the policy against it online with PPO, which also needs a value model. DPO skips both: it trains directly on chosen and rejected pairs with a classification-style loss against a frozen reference, so it is cheaper and more stable, but offline. GRPO keeps online RL but drops the value model by scoring a group of samples per prompt and using each sample's score relative to the group. Paired with verifiable rewards such as unit tests or exact answers, that is how reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1 were trained.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p>RLHF (InstructGPT). Three stages: SFT on demonstrations, a reward model trained on human rankings of outputs, and PPO against that reward model. Online: the policy generates, the reward model scores, the policy updates, with a value model estimating the baseline. It worked well (labellers preferred a 1.3B InstructGPT over 175B GPT-3 on their prompts) and is heavy: four models in memory and an unstable optimisation.</p>
<p>DPO. The paper shows the optimal policy for the RLHF objective can be written in closed form, so the reward model can be folded into the loss. Training is a classification loss on preference pairs, raising the chosen answer's log-probability relative to a frozen reference and lowering the rejected one's. No sampling during training, no reward model, no value model. The cost is that it learns only from the pairs you collected; it cannot explore beyond them. That simplicity is why open recipes such as Tulu 3 use it as a stage.</p>
<p>GRPO and RLVR. GRPO samples a group of answers per prompt, scores each, and normalises the rewards within the group (subtract the mean, divide by the standard deviation) to get advantages, so no learned value model is needed. With a verifiable reward, such as tests passing or the final number matching, there is no learned reward model either, and nothing to reward-hack except the checker. DeepSeek-R1-Zero applied this directly to a base model and AIME 2024 pass@1 went from 15.6% to 77.9%.</p>
<p>Limits worth naming. A group where every answer gets the same reward contributes zero advantage, so prompts must be hard enough to split the group. Dr. GRPO showed a length bias in the original objective. Yue et al. found that base models reach higher pass@k than their RL-trained versions at large k, which suggests RLVR mostly sharpens what the base model could already do.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>When is online RL still better than DPO?</em> When you can score new samples cheaply, as with verifiable rewards, because exploration finds answers no static preference set contains.</li>
<li><em>Where does reward hacking come from?</em> A learned reward model has blind spots the policy finds; a verifier only has the gaps in its checks, such as tests that pass on wrong code.</li>
<li><em>What is the importance ratio in these objectives?</em> New policy probability over the policy that generated the sample, clipped to stay near 1. Problem fnd-013 debugs it.</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

The whiteboard version: PPO-RLHF is policy, reference, reward model and value model in a loop; DPO is policy and reference on a fixed dataset; GRPO is policy, reference and a scoring function on fresh groups of samples.

## Follow-ups

- *Which would you use to make a support model follow a house style?* SFT, then DPO on preference pairs from reviewers. There is no verifiable reward for tone.
- *Which for a model that writes SQL?* SFT, then RL with execution-based rewards, because whether the query returns the right rows is checkable.

## Common mistakes

- Saying DPO has no reward at all; it has an implicit one defined by the policy and the reference.
- Quoting R1-Zero's 86.7% (majority vote over 64 samples) as its pass@1.
- Claiming RL teaches new reasoning abilities without the pass@k caveat.
