## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Critique, 30 minutes">
A senior engineer on your team wrote the memo below and asks for sign-off this week. List every flaw you would raise, ranked by what it costs if missed, then decide what you would approve. Write your list before opening the checklist.
</Callout>

> **Model selection for Data Room Review (memo dated 20 October 2026; scenario)**
>
> **Product.** Analysts ask questions over data-room documents during due diligence. Measured on last quarter's uploads: median document 180K tokens, p95 600K tokens. Launch on 15 November.
>
> **Candidates.** Claude Sonnet 4.5 (`claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`), Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`), Llama 4 Scout self-hosted on our existing inference cluster.
>
> **Evidence.**
> 1. On the public chat leaderboard this month, Sonnet 5.5 ranks 3rd overall, Sonnet 4.5 9th and Scout 21st.
> 2. Needle-in-a-haystack: all three retrieve the needle at every depth we tested up to 1M tokens. Scout supports 10M tokens, which gives us 15x headroom over our p95 document.
> 3. Reasoning: Sonnet 4.5's vendor-reported SWE-bench Verified score is the strongest of the three, which matches the multi-step reasoning analysts need.
> 4. Internal eval: 200 analyst questions over 50 data-room documents, each document truncated to its first 128K tokens to fit the eval harness. Accuracy: Sonnet 4.5 87%, Sonnet 5.5 85%, Scout 79%.
> 5. Cost: Sonnet 5.5 is 33% cheaper per token than Sonnet 4.5. Scout runs on GPUs we already own, so its marginal cost is zero.
>
> **Decision.** Sonnet 4.5 for all traffic, pinned to its dated snapshot ID for stability. Scout as the fallback for documents above Claude's 1M window. Full rollout on 15 November; we will monitor user feedback.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>I would not approve it. The decisive flaw is operational: the chosen snapshot retires on 30 November 2026, fifteen days after launch, and requests to it will fail. The evidence then cannot rank the remaining candidates: leaderboard rank and a needle chart are not long-document evidence, the internal eval truncates to 128K when the median document is 180K, and a 2-point gap on 200 questions clustered in 50 documents is within noise. I would approve a launch on Sonnet 5.5 behind a canary, conditional on a rerun of the eval at production lengths with clustered, paired errors before the canary widens.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>The flaw that costs most if missed.</b> <code>claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929</code> was deprecated on 30 September 2026 and retires on 30 November 2026. Pinning it is correct practice applied to the wrong model: on 30 November every request fails. A reviewer reading for evidence quality misses this because the memo's reasoning about pinning sounds careful.</p>
<p><b>Evidence that does not bear on the task.</b> A chat leaderboard rank measures preference on other people's prompts. The needle chart shows that retrieval of a planted string works at 1M; HELMET reports that needle results do not reliably predict downstream long-context performance. Scout's 10M figure is an inference capability; Meta states it was pre-trained and post-trained at 256K, so the memo's p95 of 600K is beyond the trained length and the "15x headroom" is extrapolation. The SWE-bench Verified figure is off-task (code repair, not document reasoning), and its sponsor stopped reporting the benchmark in February 2026 for contamination and flawed tests.</p>
<p><b>Evidence that is on-task but cannot decide.</b> The internal eval is the right kind of evidence, measured wrongly. Truncating to the first 128K tokens removes most of a median document, including every clause in the middle where long-context models degrade most, so it scores an easier task than production. The 200 questions sit in 50 documents; questions about the same document fail together, so the effective sample is closer to 50 than 200, and even unclustered, a single accuracy near 86% on 200 questions has a standard error of about 2.5 points. An 87% against 85% gap with no interval does not rank the two Sonnet models.</p>
<p><b>Cost and licence.</b> Sonnet 5.5's price is two thirds of Sonnet 4.5's per token, but it uses the newer tokenizer, which produces about 30% more tokens for the same text: per request the ratio is about 0.67 × 1.3 ≈ 0.87, before thinking length. Scout's GPUs are not free: 109B total parameters occupy them, and the existing cluster's other workloads lose that capacity. The memo has no licence review for Scout under the Llama 4 Community License.</p>
<p><b>Two defensible decisions.</b> Delay launch two to three weeks to rerun the eval at production lengths on Sonnet 5.5 (and Scout, if legal clears it), with clustered and paired errors: costs the launch date and analysts' expectations. Or launch on 15 November on Sonnet 5.5 behind a 10% canary, with the rerun as the gate to widen it: keeps the date and accepts that up to a tenth of analysts use an unproven configuration for a fortnight. I would take the canary, because the alternative model is gone in either case and the rerun is the same work. Failure mode: analysts in the canary trust an answer on a 600K-token document that the rerun later shows is unreliable at that length; the canary therefore routes documents above the eval's tested length to a "partial coverage" notice until the rerun covers them.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- *How would you build the eval so it covers p95 documents without paying 600K tokens per question per model per run?*
- *What goes in the rollout section that "monitor user feedback" does not cover?*

## Common mistakes

- Critiquing the benchmark evidence thoroughly and never checking the chosen model's retirement date.
- Treating 200 questions as 200 independent observations.
- Accepting truncation as a harness detail rather than as a change of task.
- Comparing per-token prices across a tokenizer change.
