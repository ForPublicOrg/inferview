## Question

A colleague posts this plan for review. It has one approval already. List every problem you would raise, rank them, and say which ones block the run.

```yaml
# Plan: claims-triage adapter, Llama 3.1 8B (bf16), owner: applied-ml
data:
  train: claims_sft_v3.jsonl        # 120,000 examples, mean 1,500 tokens (about 180M tokens)
  eval: 2% random split of claims_sft_v3
method: lora
lora:
  r: 8
  alpha: 16
  target_modules: [q_proj, v_proj]  # standard choice, keeps the adapter small
  dropout: 0.05
optim:
  learning_rate: 2.0e-5             # same as our last full fine-tune, known stable
  global_batch_sequences: 1024      # fills the node, best throughput
  epochs: 1
  schedule: cosine, 3% warmup
budget:
  gpu_hours: 10% of the full fine-tune estimate   # LoRA trains about 0.04% of the weights
phase_2:
  context: 32768                    # long claims files
  method: qlora                     # 4-bit base, so 32K fits on our 24 GB inference cards
  rank_sweep: [8, 32, 64], keep alpha at 16 so the tuned learning rate stays valid
fallback: if eval loss is worse than the full fine-tune, fine-tune a hosted model through OpenAI's
  self-serve fine-tuning API (we have not used it before)
success: eval loss below the full fine-tune's eval loss
```

## Answer

**Blocking.** Four problems change whether the run produces a usable adapter.

Target modules. Adapters on q_proj and v_proj only. LoRA Without Regret reports attention-only LoRA significantly underperforming MLP-only LoRA, and attention at rank 256 (0.25B parameters) underperforming MLP at rank 128 (0.24B) on Llama-3.1-8B, so the gap is placement, not size. The comment "keeps the adapter small" defends the wrong variable: at r = 8 this adapter is 32 × 8 × (8,192 + 5,120) = 3.4M parameters, and all seven projections at the same rank would be 21.0M, still about 0.26% of the model.

Learning rate. 2e-5 is the full fine-tuning rate. The same post finds the optimal LoRA rate consistently about 10x the full rate (a fitted multiplier of 9.8) and about 15x for runs under roughly 100 steps. At this rate the adapter barely moves, and the eval comparison will read as "LoRA is worse".

Steps and batch. 120,000 examples at 1,024 sequences per step is 117 steps for the epoch. That is close to the short-run regime where the multiplier rises, and the batch is the kind LoRA tolerates worse than full fine-tuning: the post reports the penalty growing with batch size, independent of rank. "Best throughput" optimises the wrong quantity; a smaller batch and more steps cost wall time and buy loss.

Fallback. OpenAI's self-serve fine-tuning has been unavailable since 7 May 2026 to organisations that had not run it before, which this team says it has not; and active existing customers lose new jobs on 6 January 2027. The fallback does not exist.

**Wrong but not blocking.** The budget: "trains 0.04% of the weights" (3.4M of 8.03B) is about memory. LoRA still runs the full forward and backward through every frozen matrix; the post counts slightly more than 2/3 of full fine-tuning FLOPs per pass. Budget about two thirds of the full run, not a tenth. Phase 2: a 4-bit base takes the 8.03B weights from 16.1 GB to about 4.0 GB plus constants, but activations are untouched. fp32 logits alone at 32,768 tokens are 32,768 × 128,256 × 4 bytes = 16.8 GB, the same again for their gradient, before any layer activation. 32K does not fit on a 24 GB card without chunked loss computation and checkpointing, and the plan names inference cards for training. Evaluation: a 2% random split of the training file shares templates and near-duplicates with the training set, and "eval loss below the full fine-tune" has no prompted baseline; the base model with a good prompt and a few examples is the number the adapter has to beat.

**Acceptable, with a condition.** Rank 8 is fine for behaviour and format if a short comparison shows no gap to a higher rank or a full fine-tune on a proper held-out set; at 180M tokens, capacity is a real question, so the comparison is not optional. Keeping alpha at 16 across the rank sweep is acceptable: with alpha/r scaling the post finds the optimal rate roughly rank-independent, so the colleague's reasoning is right even though the base rate is wrong.

**The decision the review forces.** Two defensible plans. Fix and rerun LoRA: all layers, about 2e-4, a batch of 64 to 128 sequences, a held-out set drawn by claim type or date, and a one-day comparison against a short full fine-tune. Cost: about two thirds of a full run's compute for the main run plus the comparison. Or go straight to full fine-tuning with ZeRO: removes the capacity question at 180M tokens, costs a full-model artefact per experiment and gives up per-tenant adapter swapping. With no working fallback and a capacity question open, I would run the fixed LoRA with the comparison built in. Its failure mode: the comparison runs too short to show a capacity gap that appears late in training, and the team ships an adapter that plateaus. The written rule says what gap on the held-out set, measured at the end of the comparison run, switches the plan to full fine-tuning, and who signs off.

## Follow-ups

- The author replies that attention-only worked on the last project. What evidence from that project would make you accept it here, and what would you ask them to rerun?
- The fixed run costs two thirds of a full fine-tune and the quarter's GPU budget covers one. Do you cut the comparison run or the main run, and what risk do you accept in writing?
- What result in the held-out comparison would reverse the decision and send the team to prompting and retrieval instead of any fine-tune?

## Common mistakes

- Approving the plan because each line is a common default; the defaults were set for full fine-tuning.
- Treating QLoRA as a context-length fix.
- Accepting a training-file split as an eval set.
- Leaving an external fallback unchecked against the provider's deprecation page.
