## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Estimate, 20 minutes">
A travel assistant runs as an orchestrator that hands a flight change to a booking worker and a payments worker. The eval harness ran one task, a same-day flight change with a fare difference, 8 times against a simulated airline backend; 6 runs ended in the correct database state. Product expects a customer to make this change about four times a month, and the proposed launch gate is pass^4 of at least 0.5 on every critical task. Those figures are a scenario.
</Callout>

1. Estimate pass^4 for this task from the 8 runs, and say whether those runs show that the task fails the gate.
2. How many runs of this task, in total, would you need for a 95% interval on its pass^4 of about plus or minus 0.10? Say which per-run success rate you planned at, and why.

The graded number is the total run count in part 2.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether the 8 runs used fresh backend state, different user-simulator seeds and the production temperature, or replayed cached tool responses.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Request the per-run success rates of the other critical tasks, so you have something besides 6 of 8 to plan at.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask whether the gate applies to each task or to the average over the critical suite, and how many tasks that suite holds.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>tau-bench's estimator gives C(6, 4) / C(8, 4) = 15 / 70 = 0.21, so the point estimate is well under the gate. The 8 runs still cannot show that the task fails it: the Wilson 95% interval on the per-run rate is 0.41 to 0.93, which puts pass^4 anywhere from 0.03 to 0.74, and 0.5 is inside that. Pinning pass^4 to plus or minus 0.10 takes about 205 runs if you plan at the observed rate of 0.75, and about 300 at the worst case. The dominant term is the factor 4p³ by which pass^4 magnifies any error in p.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>The point estimate.</strong> From n runs with c successes, C(c, k) / C(n, k) is the chance that k runs drawn without replacement from the n are all successes: 15 / 70 = 0.214. Raising the observed rate to the fourth power, 0.75⁴ = 0.316, is the plug-in estimate, and with 8 runs it is biased upward. pass@4 for the same data is 1, which is why it is the wrong number for a customer who repeats the task.</p>
<p><strong>What 8 runs rule out.</strong> The Wilson 95% interval for 6 of 8 is 0.409 to 0.929. Raised to the fourth power, that is 0.028 to 0.743. The gate of 0.5 corresponds to a per-run rate of 0.5^(1/4) = 0.841, which sits inside the interval. Eight runs say the task is probably below the gate; they do not show it.</p>
<p><strong>The run count.</strong> To first order, the standard error of p⁴ is 4p³ times the standard error of p, so for a 95% half-width h:</p>
<p>n = (1.96 / h)² × 16p⁶ × p(1 − p) = 384.2 × 16 × p⁷(1 − p) at h = 0.10.</p>
<p>At p = 0.75: p⁷(1 − p) = 0.1335 × 0.25 = 0.0334, so n = 384.2 × 16 × 0.0334 = 205. At p = 0.68, the rate implied by the point estimate (0.214^(1/4)), n = 132. The factor p⁷(1 − p) peaks at p = 7/8, where n = 302, so 302 runs suffice whatever the true rate. Any plan from about 120 to 310 runs, with its p stated, is in the band.</p>
<p><strong>Choices that fall outside it.</strong> Planning at p = 0.5 because p(1 − p) peaks there gives 24 runs; that rule belongs to estimating p itself, and here the variance factor peaks at 0.875. Asking for plus or minus 0.10 on p rather than on pass^4 gives 384.2 × 0.1875 = 72 runs, and leaves pass^4 about 1.7 times less certain than intended at p = 0.75.</p>
</Fragment>

<li><em>Finance asks why the eval bill for one task is 25 times the old one. Which tasks get 200 runs, and what do you accept in writing for the rest?</em></li>

### The cost of the answer, and the assumption that flips it

Suppose each run of this multi-agent task takes 4 minutes and the harness can run 20 at a time (scenario figures). Two hundred runs per task across 30 critical tasks is 6,000 runs, or 6,000 × 4 / 20 = 1,200 minutes, 20 hours of wall-clock. That cannot gate a pull request. Two answers are defensible.

- *A per-task gate on the few tasks with irreversible steps.* Run about 200 runs nightly on the tasks that move money or cancel bookings, where one customer's bad month matters. The cost is a nightly job, and a gate that reports a day late.
- *A suite-level gate on every pull request.* Keep 8 to 16 runs per task and gate on the suite average, whose interval is already near plus or minus 0.10 at 30 tasks. The cost is that a single task can sit well below 0.5 while the average passes.

The assumption that flips the conclusion is whether the gate is per task or on the suite average: per task, 8 runs are about 25 times too few; on the average over 30 tasks, they are enough. I would run both tiers: suite-level per pull request, per-task nightly on the irreversible tasks.

**Where this fails.** Every formula above treats runs as independent. If the simulated backend returns the same flaky error to every run in a window, or the user simulator replays one seed, failures correlate and the true interval is wider than the computed one. The customer's four attempts are not independent either: a customer whose first change failed retries against a booking in a different state, which no fresh-state eval run reproduces.

A short check of both numbers:

```python
from math import comb
import random

print(comb(6, 4) / comb(8, 4))                      # 0.214
for p in (0.68, 0.75, 0.875):
    print(p, round(384.16 * 16 * p**7 * (1 - p)))    # 132, 205, 302

random.seed(1)
est = sorted(comb(sum(random.random() < 0.75 for _ in range(205)), 4) / comb(205, 4)
             for _ in range(5000))
print(est[125], est[4875])   # about 0.22 and 0.42 around a true 0.316
```

## Follow-ups

- *Two senior engineers disagree: one wants 200 runs on 5 tasks, the other 16 runs on 60 tasks, for the same budget. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Ask which failure the launch must not have, one bad irreversible task or a broad regression, and read the last release's incident history for which one happened.
- *Your nightly per-task job shows pass^4 of 0.38 with half-width 0.10 on the payment task, two weeks before launch. Do you ship, and who signs off?*
- *The orchestrator and both workers move to a cheaper model. Which of your run counts must be recomputed, and which still hold?* The counts depend only on the per-run rate, so every count planned at the old rate is suspect until the new rate is measured.

## Common mistakes

- Reporting pass@4, or the plug-in 0.75⁴, as the customer's experience.
- Reading 0.21 from 8 runs as proof the task fails the gate.
- Planning the sample size at p = 0.5 out of habit.
- Treating runs that share a backend, a seed or a cached tool response as independent.
- Applying a per-task precision target to the whole suite, or the reverse, without saying which gate is meant.
