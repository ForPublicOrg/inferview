## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Estimate, 20 minutes">
"Legal wants a number in the risk register for the gap you found: our CV-screening model has never been tested against adversarial inputs, which Article 15(5) asks for. Give me the ceiling under each tier of Article 99 at today's turnover, then the one number that goes in the register for a finding made in 2029. Tell me what would change it by an order of magnitude."
</Callout>

Scenario inputs, not measurements:

- The company is the provider of a CV-screening system sold in the EU. Legal has classified it under Annex III point 4(a).
- Financial year equals the calendar year. FY2027: turnover EUR 42 million, balance sheet total EUR 31 million, 190 staff.
- Board plan: turnover grows between 15 and 35 percent a year; balance sheet total stays between EUR 34 and 38 million through 2029; headcount reaches 230 by the end of 2028.
- The board is also discussing the acquisition of a US company with 120 staff. No decision has been made.
- A market surveillance check in 2029 is the scenario for the finding. When in 2029 or later a decision would be taken is yours to assume.

Published inputs are in Article 99 (see sources, checked 2026-10-28). Give the register number in EUR million.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>The Article 15 gap is a provider obligation through Article 16(a), so it sits in paragraph 4: EUR 15 million or 3 percent of turnover, whichever is higher, and for an SME whichever is lower. The company stays an SME through 2029 even when turnover passes EUR 50 million, because the balance sheet test stays under EUR 43 million and staff stay under 250. So the register carries 3 percent of the preceding year's turnover: EUR 1.45 to 1.70 million on FY2028, or 1.67 to 2.30 million if the decision lands in 2030 and uses FY2029. The dominant term is SME status, not growth. The acquisition is the assumption that flips it: if the group's staff count, the company is no longer an SME and the ceiling becomes EUR 15 million.</p>
</Fragment>

<p>The fixed amounts win for a large company until turnover reaches 35 / 0.07 = 500 million for paragraph 3, 15 / 0.03 = 500 million for paragraph 4 and 7.5 / 0.01 = 750 million for paragraph 5. Below those crossovers, a non-SME's ceiling does not depend on its turnover at all, and an SME's depends on nothing else.</p>
<p><strong>Which paragraph.</strong> Article 15 is a requirement in Section 2 of Chapter III. Article 16(a) obliges the provider to ensure the system complies with Section 2, and paragraph 4(a) fines breaches of Article 16. So the finding is paragraph 4, not paragraph 3.</p>
<p><strong>SME status in 2028 and 2029.</strong> The Commission's test is staff under 250 and either turnover up to EUR 50 million or balance sheet up to EUR 43 million. Staff reach 230; the balance sheet stays at 34 to 38 million. Turnover passes 50 million on most of the growth range, but the balance sheet alternative keeps the company an SME.</p>
<p><strong>The register number.</strong> Choose the decision timing and the growth rate.</p>
<ul>
<li>Decision in 2029, turnover of FY2028: 42 × 1.15 = 48.3 million, ceiling 0.03 × 48.3 = EUR 1.45 million; 42 × 1.35 = 56.7 million, ceiling EUR 1.70 million.</li>
<li>Decision slips to 2030, turnover of FY2029: 42 × 1.15² = 55.5 million, ceiling EUR 1.67 million; 42 × 1.35² = 76.5 million, ceiling EUR 2.30 million.</li>
</ul>
<p>Any figure from about 1.4 to 2.3 follows from choices the plan allows. I would enter EUR 1.7 million, the top of the 2029 case and the bottom of the 2030 case, with both assumptions written next to it.</p>
<p><strong>What changes it by an order of magnitude.</strong> The acquisition. The Commission page warns that a firm in a group may have to include the group's staff, turnover and balance sheet. With 120 more staff the headcount is 350, the company is no longer an SME, and paragraph 4 becomes the higher of 15 million and 3 percent of turnover: EUR 15 million for any turnover under 500 million. That is about nine times the SME figure. If the merged company counts as an SMC, paragraph 6a restores "whichever is lower" for paragraphs 4 and 5, and the figure falls back to 3 percent of turnover, but not for paragraph 3. Whether it qualifies is a legal determination this problem does not make; the register should carry both values until it is made.</p>
<p><strong>The constraint conflict.</strong> Finance wants one number to weigh against the cost of the adversarial-testing work; the deadline is fixed at 2 December 2027 whatever the number is. A ceiling of 1.7 million can make the work look optional next to its cost. It is not: the obligation applies at any company size, Article 99(1) speaks of penalties and other enforcement measures, and Annex IV point 2(h) asks the technical documentation to describe the cybersecurity measures put in place, so the same work is read at conformity assessment.</p>
<p><strong>Failure mode of this answer.</strong> The ceiling is not the expected fine. Article 99(1) leaves the penalty rules to Member States and paragraph 7 lists what an authority weighs: gravity, duration, size, cooperation, prior fines, mitigation. A register that reads "EUR 1.7 million" without "ceiling" will be summed with other ceilings and treated as an expected loss.</p>

### Choices that are not reasonable

- EUR 15 million for the company as it is. That reads paragraph 4 without paragraph 6, the "whichever is lower" rule for SMEs.
- Dropping SME status when turnover passes EUR 50 million. The balance sheet test is an alternative to the turnover test, not an addition.
- EUR 35 million or 7 percent. Paragraph 3 is for Article 5 prohibited practices; a missing Article 15 measure is not one.
- 3 percent of FY2027 turnover, EUR 1.26 million, for a 2029 decision. The base is the financial year preceding the decision.
- Summing the three tiers into one exposure. They are ceilings for different infringements.

## Follow-ups

- *The CFO asks you to drop the adversarial-testing work because the ceiling is less than its cost. What do you write back, and who signs the reply?*
- *The acquisition closes in 2028. Which entries in the risk register change, and which engineering controls change with them?*
- *An authority asks for the Article 15 test logs from 2028 and they are partial. Which paragraph does a misleading answer fall under, and what would you send instead?*

## Common mistakes

- Treating the fixed amount as the cap for every company. For an SME it is a cap on the percentage, not a floor.
- Using the turnover of the year the finding was made instead of the year before the decision.
- Assuming the obligation's size scales with the fine; Article 15 applies the same way at EUR 42 million turnover as at EUR 4 billion.
