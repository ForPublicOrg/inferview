## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design, 45 minutes">
"An employee has asked us to delete all their data from the HR assistant. The DPO wants a design that handles this request and every one after it, inside the legal deadline. Trace every store the request has to reach, including our eval sets and the fine-tuning data, and tell me what we keep and why."
</Callout>

The system (scenario):

- 4,000 employees in the EU. Employees ask the assistant about policy, leave and payroll, and upload documents such as medical notes for leave requests. Managers use it to draft performance review summaries from their own notes. Legal treats the review-drafting feature as high-risk under Annex III point 4(b) and keeps its logs under Article 26(6).
- Stores: a chat database keyed by user id, with nightly backups kept 35 days; per-user memory (extracted facts in a table and a vector index); a semantic answer cache with a 7-day TTL; the application log platform (30 days); an LLM tracing vendor with full prompts and completions (90 days); an analytics warehouse with a daily export of chats; the Article 26 log store for review drafts; a golden eval set of 350 examples sampled from production chats, in git.
- Model: the OpenAI Responses API without zero data retention, with uploaded documents in OpenAI Files and Vector Stores. A LoRA adapter on a self-hosted open-weight model, trained quarterly on resolved HR tickets, drafts the payroll answers.
- Accepted review summaries are copied into the HR system of record, which this team does not own.
- Two engineers, six weeks. The DPO expects 10 to 20 such requests a month.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether "their data" includes what a manager typed about them, and how the system would find it.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask the provider account owner which API objects the integration creates, and whether any of them are kept until deleted.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask legal for a written position on the review-drafting logs before designing their deletion path.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Deletion is a lookup problem first: the user id finds the employee's own chats, but not what managers wrote about them, so the design adds a subject index written at ingestion. Then each store gets one action: delete, let a short TTL expire it, delete through the provider's API, retrain, or keep with a written legal basis. The two hard stores are the adapter and the Article 26 logs. For the adapter I choose a monthly retrain on the batch of the month's deletions, which fits inside the one-month deadline at the cost of a monthly eval and release; the alternative, waiting for the quarterly retrain, costs nothing but needs an extraction test and the DPO's written acceptance. The review logs stay for their retention period in a minimised form, on legal's written position, because Article 26(6) and the erasure right pull in opposite directions there.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>1. Find the data.</strong> The user id reaches the employee's chats, memory, uploads and traces. It does not reach a manager's chat that says "Priya has missed three deadlines", a review draft about her, or an HR ticket she was discussed in. At write time, tag every chat turn, review draft and ticket with the subject ids of employees it mentions, matched against the directory, into a subject-to-record index. Without it, the honest answer to "all their data" is "the data she typed".</p>
<p><strong>2. The data map, one action per store.</strong></p>

<p>The worst case is the backup: 35 days, beyond one month. Article 12(3) allows two further months where necessary, given the complexity and number of requests, with the reasons sent inside the first month; whether a backup cycle meets that test is legal's call, not engineering's. Either way the reply says which stores expire on what date. Shortening backup retention to under a month is the other option, at the cost of a shorter window to recover from corruption found late. With Claude instead of OpenAI, the data map would record where inference runs (<code>inference_geo</code>) separately from where data rests (workspace geo), because they are two settings.</p>
<p><strong>3. Decision: the adapter.</strong> Her resolved tickets are in the training set, so the weights were fitted to them.</p>
<ul>
<li>Option A, carry the deletion to the quarterly retrain. Cost: nothing extra in compute, but for up to three months the deployed adapter was trained on her data. Defensible only with an extraction test (probe the adapter with her ticket prefixes and named details, look for completions) and the DPO's written acceptance, because the EDPB treats anonymity of a model as a case-by-case question and asks that extraction be very unlikely.</li>
<li>Option B, retrain monthly on the batch of the month's deletions. Cost: twelve retrains a year instead of four, each with an eval run and a release. With 10 to 20 requests a month, batching keeps it to one retrain a month and every request completes inside one month.</li>
<li>Option C, stop training on tickets that name people; train on policy documents only. Cost: the payroll answers lose what the tickets taught.</li>
</ul>
<p>I choose B, with the batching window as the quantity I defend: one month, because it is the deadline. A two-week window halves the exposure and doubles the retrains; a quarter misses the deadline unless the DPO signs A.</p>
<p><strong>4. Decision: the tracing vendor.</strong> If it cannot delete one trace, either redact names and identifiers before export (cost: engineers debug from redacted prompts) or cut its retention to 30 days (cost: investigations older than a month lose their traces). I take redaction for chat traces and keep full traces only for the review feature, whose logs are kept anyway.</p>
<p><strong>5. The conflict: Article 26(6) against erasure.</strong> Article 26(6) asks for at least six months of logs "unless provided otherwise" in Union data protection law; Article 17(3)(b) lets a controller refuse erasure where a legal obligation requires processing. Which wins for these logs is legal's call, in writing. Engineering's part is to make the logs hold as little as possible: subject id under a keyed pseudonym, model version, prompt hash, the draft's hash and the manager's decision, with the text in a store that is deleted with the rest.</p>
<p><strong>6. The workflow.</strong> A deletion request becomes a record with one task per store, each idempotent and keyed on the request id, each returning a receipt (rows deleted, API ids deleted, TTL expiry date). A verification task searches every reachable store for her subject id, employee number and email after the deletes. The DPO gets the receipts, the expiry dates and the list of what was kept and why.</p>
<p><strong>What I write down.</strong> The data map with an owner per store, the adapter decision with the extraction-test result, legal's position on the review logs, and the reply template naming each store's completion date.</p>
<p><strong>What I give up.</strong> Full-fidelity traces for chats, eval-score continuity across set versions, four extra adapter releases a year, and two of the six weeks spent on the subject index instead of features.</p>
<p><strong>Failure modes of this design.</strong> The subject index matches names against the directory, so a nickname, a misspelling or "the new analyst in payroll" is missed; the verification search shares the same blind spot. The deletion ledger is itself a list of people who asked to be forgotten; it holds keyed hashes, and the key is a secret whose loss makes restores unsafe. And any new store a team adds, a second cache or a new vendor, is outside the map until someone adds it, so the map needs an owner and a check at design review.</p>
</Fragment>

<li><em>What do you tell her about the provider's abuse logs?</em> That they exist, are held by the provider for up to 30 days by default, and are outside your control unless zero data retention is approved.</li>

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: the subject index costs two of your six weeks and the DPO expects at most 20 requests a month. Which half do you cut, and what risk do you accept in writing?*
- *Reversal: the extraction test on the current adapter finds nothing in 500 probes. Would that move you from monthly retrains to option A, and what would you need to see first?*
- *The eval team says replacing production examples with written ones has made the golden set easier, and pass rates rose 6 points. How do you check that claim, and what do you change?*
- *Legal says the review logs stay for six months in full text. The employee's manager wrote things about her health in a draft. What do you do in the next hour?*

## Common mistakes

- Deleting by user id only, which misses everything other people wrote about her.
- Listing the provider as one store, when the integration creates several with different retention, some kept until deleted.
- Treating the eval set as test code rather than personal data.
- Declaring the adapter clean because its training file was deleted.
- Promising deletion within one month while backups live for 35 days.
