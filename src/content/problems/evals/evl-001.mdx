## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As public interview guides phrase it">
"Your eval shows 95% pass rate but users complain. What is wrong?"
</Callout>

A synthetic version with evidence, so the question has one answer. A dental clinic chain runs a scheduling assistant. Before launch the team wrote an eval suite and it has passed 190 of 200 cases (95%) every week for six weeks. Support tickets say "booked me into the wrong clinic" and "said you are open on Saturday". You pull the evidence:

```text
Eval suite (written before launch from the website FAQ)
  cases:                         200
  single-turn FAQ questions:     186
  cases that reschedule a booking: 0
  cases that name a specific clinic: 0
  grader prompt: "Is this response helpful and polite? Answer PASS or FAIL."
  grader validated against human labels: never

Production, 100 random traces from last week
  multi-turn booking or rescheduling conversations: 58

Complaint traces, 20 pulled from tickets, run through the same grader
  graded PASS: 18

Logs, last six weeks
  gen_ai.prompt.version:   sched-v3 (unchanged)
  gen_ai.response.model:   one dated version (unchanged)
```

Pick the root cause.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Compare what the 200 cases contain with what the 100 production traces contain.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>What does the grader prompt actually ask about, and what does it say about the complaint traces?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Which choices can the logs rule out?</p></details>

## Answer

The suite and the grader both measure something other than what users experience. The cases are single-turn FAQ questions written before launch, while 58 of 100 production traces are multi-turn booking conversations, a kind of conversation the suite never contains. And the grader asks whether a reply is helpful and polite, not whether the booking is right: it passed 18 of the 20 complaint traces, 90%, nearly the same rate as the suite. A fluent, polite answer that books the wrong clinic passes.

Why the wrong picks are tempting. Nondeterminism is a real source of noise, but a pass rate that sits at 95% for six weeks is stable, and noise cannot produce failures of a type the suite does not contain. A silent model update is a real risk, and the right instinct is to check the logs; here they show one prompt version and one resolved model version for the whole period. The "vocal minority" pick assumes the 5% the suite fails are the cases users complain about, but the grader passes almost all of the complaint traces, so those failures sit outside anything the suite counts.

<p>The eval is not measuring the product users use. Its cases come from the FAQ, not from production, and its grader checks politeness, not correctness. I would read a sample of production and complaint traces, write down the failure categories (wrong clinic, wrong opening hours, lost booking context), build cases from those traces, and replace the generic grader with binary checks per failure, validated against labels before trusting the number again.</p>

<p>Confirm the gap. The coverage numbers already show it: no rescheduling cases and no clinic-specific cases, against 58% multi-turn booking traffic. The 18-of-20 grader result shows the grader cannot see the failures even when they are in front of it.</p>
<p>Error analysis. Read at least 100 production traces plus the complaints, note the first failure in each, cluster, and count. Expect categories like wrong clinic, wrong hours, and losing the booking details between turns.</p>
<p>Rebuild the checks. Wrong clinic and wrong hours are mechanical: compare the clinic and slot in the reply with the booking system and the opening-hours table, as code. Lost context across turns is a judge criterion, tested with N-1 replays of real conversations. Each judge gets a held-out labelled set and reports TPR and TNR.</p>
<p>Report differently. Pass rates per failure mode, each with its sample size, and the old 95% retired or relabelled as what it is: a politeness check on FAQ answers.</p>

## Follow-ups

- *How many production traces would you read before trusting your categories?* At least 100, continuing until new traces stop adding categories, and labelling the first 30 yourself before using a model to help.
- *How do you test multi-turn booking conversations repeatably?* Replay the first N-1 turns of real conversations and grade the reply at turn N.
- *What do you log so the next complaint is findable?* Prompt version, resolved model version, the clinic and slot the assistant proposed, and the booking-system result, on the same trace.

## Common mistakes

- Defending the 95% because it is stable. Stable and relevant are different properties.
- Re-running the suite more times. More samples of the wrong measurement make it more precise, not more correct.
- Switching to a stronger judge model with the same "helpful and polite" prompt. The criterion is the problem, not the judge's capability.
- Blaming the users before reading a single complaint trace.
