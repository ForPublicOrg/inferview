## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As a public interview guide phrases it">
"Your eval shows 95% pass rate but users complain. What is wrong?"
</Callout>

A worked version, constructed for this problem. A dental clinic chain runs a booking assistant across its clinics. Its eval suite has passed 190 of 200 cases every week for six weeks. Over the same six weeks, support tickets saying "booked me into the wrong clinic" or "said you are open on Saturday" have grown.

**Part 1, before you open the evidence.** Write the list of what you would pull, and for each item, which explanation it would rule in or out. The operations lead wants a number she can trust for Monday's review, so say which items you would pull first.

**Part 2.** Open the evidence. It is what the team could pull in a day, and it is incomplete.

<details>
<summary>The evidence</summary>

```text
Eval suite, run weekly                                   cases   pass
  written from the website FAQ before launch               136    130
  sampled from production booking chats in week 2           64     60
  grader prompt: "Is this response helpful and polite? Answer PASS or FAIL."
  grader checked against human labels: never

Production sample, 40 traces from last week, not labelled
  booking or rebooking conversations: 23

Tickets about the wrong clinic or wrong hours, by week: 14, 19, 22, 25, 31, 29
  wrong clinic: 88 of 140    wrong hours: 52 of 140
Complaint traces: 20 of the 140, run through the same grader
  graded PASS: 18

Opening-hours sync (nightly job behind the hours tool)
  failed on three nights in week 5, when one clinic changed its Saturday hours

Logs, six weeks
  gen_ai.prompt.version   sched-v3, unchanged
  gen_ai.response.model   one dated model version, unchanged

Not pulled: booking-system records for the complaint conversations; labels on any production trace.
```

</details>

Which defect explains why the suite stayed at 95% while these complaints grew? Then rank the others, and say what you would collect next and what you would tell the operations lead on Monday without it.

## Hints

## Answer

**Part 1.** A list that covers the explanations, in the order that settles them fastest:

1. The grader prompt, and its true positive and true negative rates against human labels. If it was never checked, every pass rate it produced is unvalidated.
2. Complaint traces run through the same grader. If it passes them, it cannot see the failure, and nothing about coverage matters until that is fixed.
3. The suite's pass rate per slice and its mix against a production sample. A total over the wrong mix can hide a weak slice.
4. `gen_ai.prompt.version` and `gen_ai.response.model` over the period. One query rules a silent prompt or model change in or out.
5. Change history of the data the assistant reads: the hours table, the clinic list, the sync jobs.
6. Tickets by week and by type, against the dates of every change in 4 and 5.
7. Booking-system records for the complaint conversations, joined on conversation id: the only ground truth for "wrong clinic".

Items 2 and 4 are an afternoon's work; item 7 needs another team and is the one most likely to be missing on Monday.

**Part 2, the cause.** The grader asks whether a reply is helpful and polite. A reply that books a patient into the wrong clinic is both. The complaint traces show it directly: 18 of 20 pass, a 90% rate, close to the suite's 95%. The suite already contains booking conversations, 64 of them sampled from production, and they pass at 60 of 64. The cases are not the problem the symptom points at; the instrument is.

**Why the others are tempting, and how they rank.** The mix mismatch is real: booking conversations are 32% of the suite and 23 of 40 production traces. But reweighting to production's mix is 0.575 × 0.94 + 0.425 × 0.96 = 0.95, the same number, because the grader passes both slices. Fix it second, after the grader, or the reweighted number is as blind as the old one. The hours sync failure is real and caused some week-5 hours tickets, but tickets grew from week 1 and 88 of 140 are about the wrong clinic, which the hours table cannot cause; it ranks third and needs its own alert on the sync job. The model-alias change is the explanation people reach for first, and the two log fields rule it out.

**What to collect next, and what to do without it.** Join the booking-system records to the complaint conversations and to a random sample of production bookings; the check "the clinic in the confirmed booking is the clinic the patient named" is a few lines of code once the join exists. Label production traces for error analysis: at least 100, the FAQ's floor, not the 40 on hand, which are enough to see the mix and too few to rank failure types. Until the join exists, Monday's message is that the 95% is a politeness rate on a mix that underweights booking, that it will be retired, and that the replacement number arrives with its sample size once the code check runs. Reporting no number is the cost; reporting 95% again is worse, because the review will treat it as a quality claim.

**Where the recommended fix fails.** A code check against booking records is only as right as the records. Reception staff rebook patients by phone, so a conversation that booked the right clinic can be flagged after a manual change, and a check that compares against the latest record rather than the record at the end of the conversation will count those as failures. The check also sees clinic and slot only: a reply that books the right clinic but tells the patient the wrong address passes it. Keep a binary judge for what code cannot see, validated against labels before it reports anything.

## Follow-ups

- *The operations lead says the old number was fine for six weeks and asks why it needs replacing before you have the new one. What do you put in writing?* That the 95% measured politeness, the 18-of-20 result, and the date the per-failure numbers will exist; the risk accepted until then is shipping prompt changes with no booking-correctness check, so prompt changes freeze or go out with a manual review of 20 booking traces.
- *The join to booking records will take three weeks. What would you build in the meantime, and what does it miss?* A judge with a binary question about clinic correctness given the clinic list, checked against 50 labelled traces; it misses manual rebookings and any clinic the list spells differently.
- *What would you need to see to trust the reweighted total again as a release gate?* A grader with measured true positive and true negative rates on held-out labels, per-slice pass rates published next to the total, and a suite mix that is compared with production on a schedule.

## Common mistakes

- Defending the 95% because it is stable. Stable and valid are different properties.
- Fixing coverage first. More booking cases graded by the same prompt pass at the same rate.
- Swapping in a stronger judge model with the same "helpful and polite" prompt. The criterion is wrong, not the model.
- Treating the hours sync as the cause because it is the only incident with a date.
- Pulling only what the team already logs. Ground truth for a wrong booking lives in another system.
