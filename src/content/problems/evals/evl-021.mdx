## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Critique, 30 minutes">
A team's error-analysis memo for a travel booking assistant goes to quarterly planning on Thursday, where it will set the eval budget for the quarter. You are the reviewer. List what is wrong with it, then say what you would send back and what it should say instead by Thursday.
</Callout>

The memo, constructed for this problem:

```text
FAILURE TAXONOMY v1: travel booking assistant

Method
  Categories: the eval vendor's template plus a team brainstorm, agreed before review.
  Sample: the 40 most recent thumbs-down conversations.
  A model scored every trace on every category, 1 (none) to 5 (severe).
  Engineers spot-read 10 of the 40 to check the scores looked reasonable.

Categories (27), mean severity over the 40 traces
  Hallucination         3.1    Factual error          2.9    Unsupported claim      2.7
  Helpfulness           3.4    Completeness           3.0    Relevance              2.2
  Coherence             1.8    Fluency                1.2    Conciseness            2.6
  Tone                  1.9    Empathy                2.4    Politeness             1.3
  Retrieval miss        2.8    Context issue          2.5    Wrong tool             2.1
  Tool error            1.7    Bad arguments          2.3    Instruction following  2.9
  Safety                1.1    Bias                   1.0    PII leakage            1.0
  Format error          1.6    Latency                2.0    Reasoning error        2.7
  Policy violation      1.9    User frustration       3.6    Other                  2.4

Findings
  Overall severity 2.2 / 5. Top issue: user frustration. No safety or PII problems found.

Plan for the quarter
  1. One LLM judge per category (27), 1 to 5 scale, run on every pull request.
  2. Target: mean severity below 2.0 on every category by the end of the quarter.
  3. Repeat this analysis next quarter.
```

## Hints

## Answer

The flaws, roughly in the order a reviewer finds them; the later ones are the ones a first read misses.

**Categories before reading.** The list came from a vendor template and a brainstorm, agreed before anyone looked at a trace. Shankar et al. found that criteria change as people grade outputs, so a list fixed in advance misses whatever this product does wrong that a template did not anticipate. Here that lands in Other, which scores 2.4, higher than fourteen of the named categories.

**Overlap with no definitions.** Hallucination, factual error and unsupported claim are three names for one failure; so are helpfulness, completeness and relevance, and tone, empathy and politeness. Wrong tool, tool error and bad arguments overlap depending on whether the tool or the model is blamed. No category has a pass/fail rule, so two labellers, or the same model on two days, would not apply them the same way, and 27 is far beyond what the 40 traces can support.

**Means of a 1 to 5 scale, and no counts.** A mean severity of 3.1 can be every trace mildly affected or a quarter of them badly. It mixes frequency with impact, so the memo cannot say which failure to fix first, which is the only question an error analysis has to answer. The fix is a count of traces per category, with a binary label per trace.

**Every category on every trace.** Nothing identifies the first upstream failure. A retrieval miss that leads to an invented fare scores under retrieval miss, hallucination, factual error, unsupported claim, helpfulness and user frustration, so one cause is counted six times and the outcome categories absorb it. User frustration tops the list because every thumbs-down conversation is one; it describes the sample, not a failure anyone can fix.

**The sample.** The 40 most recent thumbs-down conversations are a sample of complaints from one week. Every number describes complaints, not production, and 40 is under the at least 100 traces the Husain and Shankar FAQ recommends for a pass, with no sign of checking whether new traces were still adding categories.

**A model did the reading.** The model scored every trace; engineers read 10 and checked that the scores "looked reasonable", which checks the model against the model's own categories. The taxonomy has never been tested against a person open-coding traces.

**Things that are not judgements.** Latency at 2.0 is a model's opinion of a duration that the trace's spans record in milliseconds. PII leakage is a pattern and policy check that code can run on every trace.

**"No safety or PII problems found."** Forty traces with none observed gives an upper bound near 3 / 40 = 7.5% by the rule of three, and these 40 were chosen by users who were unhappy, not by anyone looking for leaks. The sentence should not survive review.

**The plan.** Twenty-seven judges on every pull request is 27 model calls per eval case per run, and validating each against labels, at the 100 to 200 labelled examples per failure mode the FAQ suggests, is thousands of expert labels before any of them can be trusted. The target, mean severity below 2.0, is set on scores from those same unvalidated judges, so the quickest way to hit it is to edit the judge prompts.

**Two judgement calls.** Using a model to propose groupings is acceptable if a person open-coded the first 30 to 50 traces and reviewed every cluster. Starting from thumbs-down traces is acceptable if they are used to find categories and every rate is measured on a random sample.

**What to send back: restart or salvage.** Both are defensible.

- *Restart.* Pull 100 random traces from the last two weeks, have one engineer and one domain expert open-code the first 30 together, group into a handful of categories with pass/fail rules, label the first upstream failure per trace, and count. The cost: about two days of two people, and Thursday's planning gets "results next week" instead of a plan.
- *Salvage.* Re-read the 40 complaint traces by hand this week, label one binary first-upstream category each from a merged list of five to eight categories, report counts as "among complaints", and add 60 random traces before next week's follow-up. The cost: Thursday's numbers still describe complaints only, and categories drawn from 27 pre-set names inherit the template's blind spots.

The conflict is the deadline against a measurement that can bear a budget decision. I would salvage for Thursday under a plain label, "40 complaint traces, re-read by hand, counts not rates", and ask planning to approve only the work that list already justifies: code checks for the categories a lookup can decide, and the restart, with no judges funded until counts from random traffic exist.

**Where the salvage fails.** The merged list is shaped by the 27 names, so a failure the template never named, say a fare quoted in the wrong currency, still has no home, and the 60 random traces may be read through the same categories. The restart has to open-code from scratch, not label against the salvaged list, or the blind spot carries into next quarter.

## Follow-ups

- *Planning asks why it should fund a restart when the team already spent two weeks on this. Which half of the plan do you cut, and what risk do you accept in writing?* Cut all 27 judges and the severity target; keep code checks for latency and PII from now; accept in writing that judgement failures go unmeasured until the restart's counts arrive.
- *What would you need to see in the restart to keep more than eight categories?* Each extra category with a written pass/fail rule, enough traces to count, and agreement between two labellers on whether it applies.
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one wants to keep the vendor template so results compare with other teams, the other wants the product's own categories. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Label 30 random traces under both and compare how many failures each scheme leaves in Other or splits across overlapping names; the scheme that a second labeller applies consistently wins, and cross-team comparison can use a mapping table.

## Common mistakes

- Arguing about which of the 27 categories to drop instead of asking for counts.
- Accepting user frustration as a failure category when it is the reason the trace was sampled.
- Treating "no PII problems found" in 40 complaint traces as evidence of absence.
- Proposing a better judge per category, which keeps every problem in the method and adds cost.
- Rejecting the model's help entirely; proposing groupings after a person has open-coded is fine.
- Sending the memo back with no deliverable for Thursday, which leaves planning to fund the 27 judges anyway.
