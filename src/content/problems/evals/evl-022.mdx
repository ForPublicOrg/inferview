## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Estimate, 25 minutes">
"Our release gate compares the candidate with the current version on the golden set, case by case. Product wants it to catch a three-point drop in pass rate. How many cases does the set need at one run per case, and is growing the set the right way to get there?"
</Callout>

Scenario inputs, measured by the team last quarter:

- The set has 400 cases, graded pass or fail. The current version passes about 82 percent.
- Discordant cases, passing on one version and failing on the other, in the last three release comparisons: 31, 44 and 58 of 400. The 58 was a change of base model version; the other two were prompt changes.
- The current version run twice against itself: 22 of 400 cases flip.
- The 400 cases came from 400 different conversations.
- One case run, candidate call plus judge call, costs about $0.05. Release candidates are compared about twice a week.
- The domain expert labels a new case in about 4 minutes and has 10 hours this month.

Choose the significance level, whether the test is one- or two-sided, the power, and the discordance rate you plan for. Give the number of cases at one run per case per version, then say what you would do instead or as well.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>For a paired binary comparison the per-case difference is −1, 0 or +1, so its variance is the discordance rate f minus δ². Miller's sample-size formula then gives n = (z<sub>α</sub> + z<sub>β</sub>)² × (f − δ²) / δ². With a one-sided 5 percent test, 80 percent power, f = 0.11 and δ = 0.03: 6.18 × 0.1091 / 0.0009 ≈ 750 cases. The dominant terms are δ², which sits squared in the denominator, and f. Reasonable choices span about 500 to 1,700. But half of f is the system disagreeing with itself (22 of 400), and three runs per case cut that half by two thirds: 500 cases at three runs reach the same power, which fits this month's labelling budget where 750 does not. The assumption that flips everything is case independence.</p>
</Fragment>

<p>Cross-check: Miller's worked example uses ω² = 1/9 ≈ 0.111 at the same δ, α and β and gets about 969; the two-sided f = 0.11 cell is 951. My choice: one-sided, because the gate only acts on drops; power 0.8; f = 0.11, the middle of the measured range. That is about 750 cases. At today's 400 the same test detects only about 4.1 points: solving δ = 2.49 × √((0.11 − δ²) / 400) gives δ ≈ 0.041.</p>
<p><strong>Where f comes from.</strong> A run of the current version against itself flips 22 of 400, 5.5 percent. For a binary score that self-disagreement is the run-to-run noise of both arms together, so of the 11 percent discordance between versions, 5.5 points are noise and ω² = 0.11 − 0.0009 − 0.055 = 0.054 is the real per-case difference. With K runs per case per version, the noise term divides by K:</p>

<p><strong>The decision, with two defensible answers.</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Grow to 750 cases, one run each.</strong> 350 new cases at 4 minutes is 1,400 minutes, about 23 hours, against the expert's 10 this month: three months before the gate has its power. Each comparison costs 750 × 2 × $0.05 = $75. The new cases also widen coverage, which resampling never does.</li>
<li><strong>Grow to 500 cases, three runs each.</strong> 100 new cases is about 7 hours, inside this month's budget. Each comparison costs 500 × 3 × 2 × $0.05 = $150, twice the other option, about $300 a week at two comparisons. It buys power only against the noise half of f; the real per-case differences are untouched.</li>
</ul>
<p>The constraint conflict is labelling budget against compute and against coverage. I would do the second now, because it gives the gate its power this month, and keep labelling 150 cases a month toward 750, dropping to two runs per case once the set is large enough that 2 runs give the power.</p>
<p><strong>The assumption that flips the conclusion.</strong> Independence. These 400 cases come from 400 conversations, but new cases written as variants of a seed conversation are correlated: a change that breaks one variant tends to break its siblings. With four variants per seed and an assumed within-seed correlation of the differences of 0.5, the design effect is 1 + 3 × 0.5 = 2.5, and the one-run requirement at f = 0.11 rises from 750 to about 1,870 cases. Miller reports clustered standard errors over three times the naive ones on DROP. So the new cases must be new conversations, and the test must cluster by seed if any are variants.</p>
<p><strong>Where the recommended answer fails.</strong> The f I planned for comes from two prompt changes and one model change. A base model change sat at f = 0.145, where 500 cases at three runs need about 738: the gate is underpowered for exactly the release that changes most. Power on the aggregate also says nothing about slices: a 3-point aggregate drop can be a 25-point drop in a slice that is 12 percent of the set, and a gate on the aggregate is designed to see the 3. And a one-sided 5 percent test blocks one in twenty releases that changed nothing, about one false block every ten weeks at two comparisons a week; someone has to own the rerun policy before the first one happens.</p>

### Choices that are not reasonable

- Planning on f = 0.055, the self-flip rate. That treats every real per-case change as absent and gives about 370 cases, half of what the measured releases need.
- Using an unpaired two-proportion test: (1.96 + 0.84)² × (0.82 × 0.18 + 0.79 × 0.21) / 0.0009 ≈ 2,734 cases per arm. It throws away the pairing that the gate already has.
- Sizing from the standard error of a single pass rate, √(0.82 × 0.18 / 400) ≈ 1.9 points, and concluding 400 cases can see 3 points. That is the precision of one estimate, not the power to detect a difference between two.
- Taking "at least 1,000 questions" as a rule. It is Miller's result for his inputs; the measured f here gives a different number.

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: three runs per case makes each comparison cost twice what the 750-case set would. Which do you cut, the runs or the power, and what risk do you accept in writing?*
- *Reversal: what would you need to see in the next quarter's comparisons to go back to one run per case?*
- *Product now wants to gate on each of five intent slices separately at the same three points. What happens to the case count and the false block rate, and what do you offer instead?*

## Common mistakes

- Treating discordance as only noise or only real change, when the self-flip measurement separates the two.
- Leaving the effect size out of the conversation: halving it from 3 to 1.5 points multiplies the cases by about four.
- Adding cases as paraphrases of existing ones and counting them as independent.
- Running the gate once on a borderline result, rerunning until it passes, and calling that a test.
