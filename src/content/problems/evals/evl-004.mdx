## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As a public interview guide phrases it">
"You have 100K user interactions. How do you build a 500-item eval set from them?"
</Callout>

What you learn when you ask (scenario):

- The interactions are one month of a customer-support assistant's traces, with tool calls. About 6 percent carry a thumbs-down, about 4 percent were escalated to a human, and the two overlap.
- The privacy team allows traces to leave the support org only with names, emails, addresses and order numbers masked.
- One domain expert can label for 20 hours, over one week. You can read traces yourself.
- The set must gate the next release in two weeks.
- The product manager wants the set's pass rate on the quality dashboard as "the" number.

Design the set: what you do before sampling, the strata and how many cases each gets, how cases are labelled and checked, and what goes into the written record. Choose the allocation and the labelling pace and defend them.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Request a breakdown of the month by intent, language and outcome before choosing any allocation.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask the expert to label 20 traces while you time it, before you commit the 20 hours.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask the product manager what decision the dashboard number is meant to drive.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Read about 100 masked traces myself first and code the failures, because those categories become both the strata and the label rubric. Then mask, cluster near-duplicates, tag every conversation with intent, language and outcome, and allocate on purpose: 200 in proportion to traffic, 150 from thumbs-down, escalations and the coded failure categories, 100 rare or high-severity cases, and 50 where the right behaviour is to decline or hand off. The expert gives binary verdicts per failure category at a timed pace; I re-label 50 to measure agreement. Because the set over-samples failures, its raw pass rate is not production's, so the dashboard gets a reweighted estimate and the raw rate stays a regression signal. What I give up: per-slice gating, since 50 cases are too few to gate on, and anything that did not happen this month.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>1. Error analysis before sampling.</strong> I read 100 traces drawn across intents and outcomes and write down the first failure in each, then group the notes into categories. That is my time, not the expert's, about a day. Without it, the strata are guesses and the labels are opinions.</p>
<p><strong>2. Clean.</strong> Mask before anything leaves the support org. Cluster near-duplicates by normalised text plus embedding similarity, and keep one representative per cluster with the cluster size recorded, so 2,000 copies of "where is my order" become one case with a weight, not 40 cases.</p>
<p><strong>3. Allocate.</strong> The allocation is a choice, and this is mine:</p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Stratum</th><th>Cases</th><th>Share of traffic (scenario)</th><th>Why</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Normal traffic, proportional by intent and language</td><td>200</td><td>87%</td><td>Keeps the set tied to what users ask</td></tr>
<tr><td>Thumbs-down, escalated, or in a coded failure category</td><td>150</td><td>8%</td><td>Where the system fails today</td></tr>
<tr><td>Rare or severe: refund promises, legal and medical topics, low-volume languages</td><td>100</td><td>3%</td><td>Cheap to miss at random, expensive to get wrong</td></tr>
<tr><td>Should decline or hand off</td><td>50</td><td>2%</td><td>A system that answers everything must not score well</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p><strong>4. Label within the budget.</strong> Binary pass or fail per relevant failure category, a one-line reason, and the expected behaviour. I time the expert on 20 traces first. At 2 minutes a case, 500 cases take 1,000 minutes, about 17 hours of the 20, leaving 3 for rubric disputes; I re-label a shared 50 to measure agreement, and disagreements become rubric changes. At 3 minutes, which multi-turn traces with tool calls can take, it is 25 hours and does not fit. Then the choice is to cut to about 400 cases, keeping the failure and decline strata whole, or to have a model pre-label and the expert verify, which is faster and anchors the expert on the model's verdict. I would cut the normal stratum first: it is the one production monitoring already sees.</p>
<p><strong>5. The dashboard number.</strong> The raw pass rate of an over-sampled set is not production's. With stratum pass rates of 92, 60, 70 and 80 percent (illustrative), the raw rate is (184 + 90 + 70 + 40) / 500 = 76.8 percent, while weighting by traffic share gives 0.87 × 0.92 + 0.08 × 0.60 + 0.03 × 0.70 + 0.02 × 0.80 = 88.5 percent. The dashboard gets the weighted figure with its interval; the release gate uses the raw set compared case by case against the current version. The product manager gets one number, and it is the right one for the decision the dashboard drives.</p>
<p><strong>6. The written record.</strong> A datasheet stored with the set and its content hash: source month, masking rules, cluster method and threshold, strata with counts and traffic shares, rubric version, labeller, agreement on the shared 50, and known gaps. Every score records the set version. Split off a held-out slice of 100 that prompt work never touches.</p>
<p><strong>The constraint conflict.</strong> Labelling budget against coverage. Every rare stratum I add takes cases from the others; 20 hours and two weeks set the size, not the 500 in the question.</p>
<p><strong>Where this design fails.</strong> The reweighting assumes outcome flags mean the same thing everywhere, and they do not: thumbs-down comes from users who click, so a segment that never clicks is under-represented in the failure stratum and over-represented in "normal". The strata come from this month's failures, so the next new failure mode lands in whichever stratum it happens to fall in, often too thinly to see. And per-slice numbers are too noisy to act on: 50 decline cases at 80 percent have a standard error of √(0.8 × 0.2 / 50) = 5.7 points, about ±11 points at 95 percent.</p>
<p><strong>What I give up.</strong> Gating on any single slice; seasonal and next-month traffic; and conversations the masking made unreadable, which I count and list rather than drop silently.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Why not use thumbs-down as the label?</em> It says a user was unhappy, not why, and only some users click; it finds cases, it does not grade them.</li>
<li><em>Why cluster before sampling rather than after?</em> Sampling first spends cases on near-copies of the most common question, and the cluster size is the weight the dashboard estimate needs.</li>
</ul>

## Follow-ups

- *Spanish is 15 percent of traffic and your expert does not read it. Which stratum loses cases, who labels the Spanish ones, and how do you check agreement across the two labellers?*
- *The expert's timed pace comes back at 4 minutes a case. Do you cut to 300 cases or accept model pre-labels, and what do you write down about the choice?*
- *Three months later the regression slice has passed at 100 percent every week while complaints rose. What do you check first, and what would make you rebuild rather than top up?*

## Common mistakes

- Sampling 500 uniformly and finding a handful of hard cases among hundreds of near-copies.
- Putting the over-sampled set's raw pass rate on a dashboard as if it were production's.
- Scoring on a 1 to 5 scale, which makes agreement hard to measure and thresholds arbitrary.
- Spending the expert's hours on error analysis that an engineer can do.
- Building the set once, with no owner and no record of how it was drawn.
