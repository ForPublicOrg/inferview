## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Last week our run had a failure storm: a bad batch of hosts failed fourteen times in three days. MFU on the dashboard did not move. Which metric did the storm hurt, and which one should the weekly board update show?"
</Callout>

The run (scenario) plans 40% MFU and 92% effective training time, using the Llama 3 paper's definition of effective training time: time spent on useful training divided by elapsed time.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask over which time window the dashboard computes MFU, and whether steps that were later thrown away count.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask what the board is deciding with the number: a date, a budget, or a vendor.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>The storm hurt effective training time: restarts, the work lost since each last checkpoint, and reload time all sit in its denominator. MFU is measured over steps that ran, so it stayed flat, and it also counts steps whose work was later discarded by a restore. A straggler would do the reverse: slower steps, lower MFU, no interruption. The board should see delivered progress against plan, tokens trained (or useful model FLOPs) per reserved day, which is peak × MFU × effective training time, with the two factors underneath it.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p>MFU is model FLOPs per second over peak, sampled while the job runs. A failure does not change how fast the surviving steps run, so MFU hides it. Worse, steps executed between the last checkpoint and the crash are counted in MFU and then thrown away, so MFU can stay healthy while the run makes no net progress.</p>
<p>Effective training time is the share of wall-clock time spent on training that was kept. Fourteen failures in three days, each costing (scenario) 15 minutes of restart and on average half of a 20-minute interval, lose 14 × 25 = 350 minutes of 4,320, so effective training time for those three days is at most 100% − 8.1% = 91.9% before any other loss, against a 92% plan for everything.</p>
<p>The board's question is "will the run finish on time and budget", so the number is the product. At 40% MFU and 92% effective training time, the run delivers 0.368 of peak per reserved GPU-hour; the Llama 3 paper's figures give 0.41 × 0.90 = 0.369 at 16K GPUs. Show tokens trained against the plan line, and the two factors so an engineer can see which one moved.</p>
<p>Failure mode of reporting the product alone: it does not say whether to call the hardware vendor or the performance team. A drop caused by stragglers and a drop caused by restarts look the same in the product and need opposite fixes, so the decomposition has to travel with it.</p>
</Fragment>

<ul>
<li><em>The vendor's contract guarantees GPU availability, not effective training time. Which number do you bring to the vendor meeting, and how do you attribute restart time to their hardware?</em></li>
<li><em>MFU drops from 40% to 37% with no interruptions. What do you look for first?</em></li>

## Follow-ups

- *The training team wants to report hardware FLOPs utilization instead of MFU because it is higher with activation recomputation. What does that hide from the board?*
- *Effective training time is 95%, but the loss curve is a week behind plan. Which metric is missing?*

## Common mistakes

- Reading a flat MFU as proof that failures did not cost anything.
- Counting discarded steps as progress.
- Reporting one number without the factors that explain a change in it.
- Comparing effective training time across reports without checking the denominator: whether planned maintenance, queueing or evaluation time is excluded.
