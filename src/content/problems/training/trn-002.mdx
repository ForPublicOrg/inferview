## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Our dense runs get 38% BF16 MFU. My VP read that DeepSeek-V3 was trained for under six million dollars and wants to know whether we are less efficient. Estimate DeepSeek-V3's hardware utilisation from what they published, and tell me what the comparison supports. I need one slide by Friday."
</Callout>

From the DeepSeek-V3 technical report: 671B total parameters, 37B activated per token, 14.8T pre-training tokens, 2,664K H800 GPU hours for pre-training (2.788M including context extension and post-training), trained with FP8 mixed precision. The report does not state an MFU.

Choose a peak per GPU and say why. **Enter the utilisation as a percentage**, and label what kind of number it is.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask which of the report's GPU-hour figures corresponds to the 14.8T tokens.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask which precision the matmuls ran in, and what peak the H800 has at that precision without sparsity.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask whether the GPU hours are wall clock for the whole cluster, including restarts.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>About 343 TFLOP/s per GPU of model FLOPs, which is roughly 17% to 23% of an FP8 dense peak and 35% of a BF16 dense peak, depending on the peak you assume for the H800. It is an estimate, not an MFU the authors reported, and it is a lower bound on what the hardware did because 6 × activated parameters omits attention and the multi-token prediction module. The comparison with our 38% does not support "less efficient": DeepSeek-V3's cost per token is low mostly because it does the arithmetic of a 37B dense model per token while holding 671B parameters, not because its GPUs were busier.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Numerator.</b> A mixture of experts does 6 × activated parameters per token: 6 × 37e9 × 14.8e12 = 3.29e24 FLOPs. Using 671B instead gives 5.96e25 FLOPs, 6,200 TFLOP/s per GPU, above any peak, which is how you know total parameters are the wrong count.</p>
<p><b>Time.</b> Pre-training is 2,664K GPU hours, 9.59e9 GPU-seconds. 3.29e24 / 9.59e9 = <b>343 TFLOP/s per GPU</b>. The 2.788M total includes context extension and post-training, which are not the 14.8T tokens; the scaling book uses it and gets 21.7%, which slightly understates utilisation for that reason.</p>
<p><b>Denominator, chosen.</b> Two answers are defensible, each with a cost.</p>
<ul>
<li>FP8 dense, because the run used FP8 for its main GEMMs. The scaling book uses 1,513 TFLOP/s for the H800: 343 / 1,513 = <b>22.7%</b>. If you instead assume the H800's tensor throughput matches the H100 SXM's (1,979 FP8 dense, half the listed 3,958), 17.3%. The cost of this choice: it makes the number incomparable with BF16 MFUs, and it charges DeepSeek for parts of the step that ran in BF16 or FP32.</li>
<li>BF16 dense (989.5 under the same H100 assumption): <b>34.6%</b>. Comparable with Llama 3's 38% to 43% and with your team's 38%, but it credits FP8 speed as if it were utilisation.</li>
</ul>
<p>Any answer from 15% to 40% with its peak named counts. On the slide I would show the FP8 figure as the utilisation and the BF16 figure only as a bridge to our number, both labelled "estimate from published GPU hours".</p>
<p><b>The dominant term.</b> The peak assumption: it moves the answer by a factor of two. The assumption that flips the conclusion is whether the GPU hours are productive time. If they include restarts and idle cluster time, the true MFU is higher; nothing in the report lets you separate them.</p>
<p><b>What the comparison supports.</b> Per trillion tokens, DeepSeek-V3 needed 180K H800 hours. A dense model at our 38% MFU on H100s needs 6 × N × 1e12 / (0.38 × 989.5e12) seconds, and that equals 180K hours for N of about 41B. So at our utilisation, on H100s in BF16, we would pay DeepSeek-V3's per-token cost for a 41B dense model. DeepSeek bought that cost with a 671B-parameter model, through sparsity and FP8, not through higher utilisation. The $5.576M figure also excludes prior research and ablations, which the report says.</p>
<p><b>Failure mode of this answer.</b> The H800 peak is not on NVIDIA's public H100 page, so both FP8 figures rest on an assumption (the book's, or parity with the H100 SXM). If the slide shows one number without the peak it is not checkable, and the next person will compare it with a BF16 MFU.</p>
</Fragment>

Or a reproduction on our own cluster with an open MoE recipe.</li>

</ul>

### Which answers are unreasonable

- About 8.7%: the FP8 peak with sparsity (3,958) in the denominator.
- About 17% reached through a BF16 peak with sparsity (1,979): the right number for the wrong reason, which fails the moment someone asks which precision it assumes.
- Above 100%: 6 × 671B as the numerator.
- Any figure presented as "DeepSeek's MFU": the report does not state one.

## Common mistakes

- Counting total rather than activated parameters for a mixture of experts.
- Dividing by the 2.788M total hours as if all of it trained on 14.8T tokens.
- Treating the $5.576M rental cost as the cost of developing the model.
- Comparing an FP8-peak utilisation with a BF16 MFU on the same slide without saying so.
