## Question

DeepSeek-V3's report describes an FP8 mixed-precision framework used for its 14.8T-token pretraining run. Which set of components does section 3.3 keep in their original precision, BF16 or FP32, rather than in FP8?

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask which operators in a transformer block are not Linear GEMMs, and which ones feed or read the router.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask what FP8 Wgrad allows the training framework to store in FP8 for the backward pass.</p></details>

## Answer

The report's framework runs the three GEMMs of every Linear operator in FP8: Fprop, the forward pass; Dgrad, the gradient with respect to activations; and Wgrad, the gradient with respect to weights. It keeps five kinds of component in their original precision: the embedding module, the output head, the MoE gating modules, the normalization operators and the attention operators. It also keeps master weights and gradients in FP32 and tracks AdamW's moments in BF16.

The pattern is that FP8 goes where most of the compute is, the Linear GEMMs, and stays away from components that are small in compute. One reading of the list, which is ours and not the report's: the embedding and output head touch every token's representation directly; gating decides expert routing, where a small error picks a different expert; normalization and attention contain the reductions and exponentials that low precision handles worst.

Why the wrong options are tempting:

- *Attention in FP8* follows from assuming every matrix multiply moved once the Linear layers did. The report keeps attention operators out.
- *Backward GEMMs in higher precision* matches the intuition that gradients need more range, and the hybrid recipe the report cites used E5M2 for them. DeepSeek-V3 instead used E4M3 everywhere with fine-grained scales, and FP8 Wgrad is what lets activations be cached in FP8.
- *FP32 moments* is the textbook mixed-precision recipe. The report goes further and stores them in BF16, which is a separate saving from FP8.

## Follow-ups

- If you had to move one of the five kept components into FP8 to save step time, which would you try first, and what would you measure before and after?
- The report validated the framework against BF16 at about 1 trillion tokens on two smaller models. What does that evidence cover for the 14.8T-token run, and what does it not?

## Common mistakes

- Treating "trained in FP8" as "every tensor in FP8".
- Assuming optimizer moments stay in FP32 in any low-precision recipe.
- Forgetting the output head, which is a GEMM but is kept in higher precision anyway.
