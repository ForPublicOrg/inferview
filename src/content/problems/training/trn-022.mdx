## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Scenario">
You are on call for a 120B-total MoE pretraining run, 60% through its token budget. Muon on matrix parameters, AdamW on embeddings and norms, weight decay 0.1, BF16 compute, no QK-norm, z-loss off. At step 41,200 the loss jumped. Checkpoints exist at steps 40,000 and 41,000; each 1,000 steps costs about 14 hours of the cluster. The release date does not move.
</Callout>

The dashboard (synthetic run; per-head maximum pre-softmax attention logits are logged for every head):

| Step | Loss | Grad norm | Max logit, hottest head at step 41,200 | Max logit, range over all other heads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 38,000 | 2.141 | 0.31 | 61 | 18 to 64 |
| 39,000 | 2.137 | 0.33 | 118 | 18 to 66 |
| 40,000 | 2.133 | 0.41 | 240 | 19 to 66 |
| 40,800 | 2.131 | 0.62 | 410 | 19 to 67 |
| 41,150 | 2.130 | 1.9 | 655 | 19 to 67 |
| 41,200 | 2.874 | 14.2 | 1,020 | 20 to 71 |
| 41,260 | 2.310 | 3.1 | 980 | 19 to 68 |
| 41,600 | 2.162 | 0.9 | 940 | 19 to 67 |

Other facts from the first twenty minutes:

- The batch at step 41,200 contains a web document with a 9-token sequence repeated about 2,600 times.
- Node n217 raised a GPU error at step 41,140 and was replaced by an elastic restart from an in-memory replica. The restart log shows matching checksums for parameters and optimizer state.
- Output-softmax statistics (log Z) are not logged.

Which explains the spike? Then rank the other three, say what you would look at in the rest of the hour, and decide: continue, roll back and resume unchanged, or roll back and change something.

## Answer

**The cause is the head.** The hottest head is layer 12 head 7. Its maximum logit went from 61 to 1,020 over about 3,200 steps while every other head stayed between 18 and 71, and the gradient norm left its band at step 40,000, 1,200 steps before the spike. That is the pattern Kimi K2's report describes for Muon at scale: a small subset of heads whose logits grow without bound. A logit near 1,000 saturates the softmax, so the head attends to one key per row and its gradients through the other keys vanish; the model is in a fragile state, and a batch with unusual statistics pushes it over. The recovery to 2.162 says the run survived this one. The head's logit at 940 says the next one is coming.

**The others, ranked.**

1. *The repeated-sequence batch* is a real trigger and the most important secondary defect. OLMo 2 linked such documents to spikes and filters documents with 32 or more repeated n-grams, while noting the filter is a mitigation and does not touch slow gradient-norm growth. Here the growth came first. Filter the window and add the filter to the pipeline, but do not mistake it for the fix.
2. *z-loss off* is a gap in the recipe and in the evidence: with log Z unlogged, output-logit drift cannot be ruled out. Start logging it now; it costs a reduction per step.
3. *The node replacement* is ruled out by the matching checksums and by timing: the head's growth started 2,000 steps before the node failed.

**The rest of the hour.** Confirm that layer 12 head 7 is the only head trending, by plotting each head's max logit as a slope over steps 38,000 to 41,000 rather than a value, since a second head at 70 and rising is the next incident. Check the gradient norm per layer, to see whether layer 12 leads. Pull log Z from the 41,000 checkpoint on a few batches.

**The decision.** Two answers are defensible.

- *Roll back to 40,000, add a per-head clip of query and key weights, filter the repeated document, resume.* Cost: about 17 hours of lost compute (1,200 steps at 14 hours per 1,000) and a mid-run recipe change that has to be written up. The clip acts only on heads above the threshold, so the rest of the model's trajectory is unchanged.
- *Continue from the current state with the clip applied from now.* Cost: no lost compute, but the clip would immediately shrink that head's query and key weights by a factor of about √(τ / 940) each, a large one-step change to a head that has spent 3,000 steps adapting around saturation, from a state that has just absorbed a spike.

I would roll back to 40,000, because the head's logit at 240 is a state the clip can bring back gently, and 17 hours is less than a late divergence during learning-rate decay would cost.

**The quantity you choose: τ.** The healthy heads peak at 71. A threshold of 100, the value K2 used, sits just above them, so it touches only the outlier; a threshold of 300 lets the head saturate partway again; a threshold of 50 clips healthy heads and changes the model everywhere. A value from 80 to 150 is defensible from this data, chosen from the distribution of healthy heads, not copied from a paper.

**How the recommendation fails.** The clip bounds the logit without explaining the growth. If layer 12 head 7 grows because its learning rate is too high under Muon, the clip may fire for the rest of the run and quietly limit what the head can represent. Log the clip's firing rate per head; K2's stopped acting after about 30% of training, and one that never stops is a finding to take back to the recipe.

## Follow-ups

- *The 40,000 checkpoint turns out to be corrupt and the next good one is at 36,000, three days back.* Does your decision change, and what do you write for the person who owns the release date?
- *A researcher wants QK-norm instead of a clip, because OLMo 2 used it.* What does adding a normalization layer mid-run do that a weight clip does not, and what would you need to see to accept it?
- *After resuming with the clip, the head sits at the threshold for 20,000 steps.* What would make you reverse the clip and lower the learning rate on attention projections instead?

## Common mistakes

- Treating the batch as the cause because it is the event at step N, when the logs show the instability building for thousands of steps.
- Rolling back and resuming unchanged, which replays the same trajectory toward the same spike.
- Clipping every head globally, which changes the healthy heads and makes the run harder to compare with its own history.
- Spending the hour on the node replacement after the checksums matched.
