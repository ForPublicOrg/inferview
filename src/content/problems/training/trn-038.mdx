## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Partnerships licensed 200B tokens of textbooks and worked solutions. The data freeze for the next base model is in three weeks. Design the process that decides whether the source goes into the mix, at what weight, and who signs it off."
</Callout>

Scenario inputs:

- The main run is a 7B model; a checkpoint at 60% of training exists. The mix is otherwise final.
- Ablation budget: 16 H100s for the three weeks, shared with one other team's evaluation jobs. Jobs are preemptible.
- The partnerships lead paid for the source and has told leadership it will be in the model.
- The licence forbids redistributing the text and requires that it be removable from future training runs on request.
- The product's users write in English and Indonesian; the source is English.

Write the workflow: steps, what state each step records, how retries and preemption are handled, the approvals, the budget per step, and the rollout into the main run.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask how much the current benchmark suite moves between two runs that differ only in seed, at the size you plan to ablate.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask for the overlap between the source and the evaluation sets before deciding what the benchmarks can tell you.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask the cluster team for the measured utilisation of a 7B job on these GPUs.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Check overlap first, write the decision rule second, then run micro-anneals from the 60% checkpoint: the source mixed with general data against general data alone, two seeds each, about 10B tokens per arm. Four arms cost about 1.7e21 FLOPs, about 15% of the budget at 40% utilisation and 30% of the half the plan counts on. Include the source at a fixed weight only if the gain on a held-out reasoning set exceeds the seed spread and no Indonesian or safety gate regresses. Data lead and legal sign; the rollback trigger is the first main-run checkpoint eval.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Budget, computed.</b> 16 × 9.9e14 FLOP/s × 0.4 (a utilisation assumption; Hint 3 replaces it) × 21 days × 86,400 s = 1.15e22 FLOPs, shared, so plan on half: 5.7e21. A micro-anneal arm of 10B tokens on the 7B is 6 × 7e9 × 10e9 = 4.2e20; four arms are 1.7e21. A FineWeb-style from-scratch arm (1.71B on 28B tokens) is 2.9e20; four arms are 1.15e21. Both fit; the budget does not choose the method.</p>
<p><b>The steps and their state.</b></p>
<ol>
<li><b>Overlap.</b> MinHash the source against the existing corpus and n-gram match it against every eval set. Record overlap rates per eval set. Worked-solution textbooks are where benchmark problems live, so a contaminated eval is removed from the decision rule, not from the source.</li>
<li><b>Decision rule, written before runs.</b> Primary metric, minimum gain stated as a multiple of the measured seed spread, per-language and safety gates, the weights to try. Stored with a hash; changing it after results is visible.</li>
<li><b>Baseline spread.</b> Two seeds of the control arm. Without this, any gain is uninterpretable.</li>
<li><b>Runs.</b> Each run is keyed by the hash of its data manifest and config. A preempted job resumes from its last checkpoint under the same key; a second job with the same key is refused, so a retry cannot produce a duplicate result that gets averaged in.</li>
<li><b>Evaluation.</b> Reasoning benchmarks the overlap step cleared, a held-out reasoning set written after the source's publication date, Indonesian held-out perplexity, and the safety suite.</li>
<li><b>Decision memo and sign-off.</b> Data lead signs the result; legal signs that the removal-on-request requirement can be met (the source lives in its own shard with its own manifest).</li>
<li><b>Rollout.</b> The source enters at the weight that won, and the first main-run checkpoint eval repeats the gates. A regression beyond the spread removes the shard from the remaining tokens.</li>
</ol>

**The decision with two defensible answers: how to measure.**

*Micro-anneal from the 60% checkpoint*, as OLMo 2 and Llama 3 do for small sources. It measures the source at the capability of the model that will train on it, and OLMo 2 saw results within 10B tokens. It cannot show what the source does as part of the main-phase mix rather than in a late, high-learning-rate-decay window, and Llama 3 found annealing gains large at 8B and negligible at 405B, so a gain here may not transfer up.

*From-scratch ablations at 1.71B*, as FineWeb does. They measure the source as pretraining data, which is how a 200B-token source would mostly be used. They cost less here, but at 1.71B on 28B tokens many reasoning benchmarks sit near random, and FineWeb chose its benchmarks for exactly that reason; a textbook source's main effect may be invisible.

For a reasoning-heavy source going into a 7B run, the micro-anneal is the better instrument. For a general-web source, the from-scratch ablation would be.

**The quantity you choose and defend: the source's share in the micro-anneal and in the main mix.** OLMo 2 used about half source, half general data; Llama 3 used 30% new data. A 50% share gives the clearest signal and overstates the effect at the 2% to 5% the source would get in the main mix. Testing two shares (50% and 15%) is within budget and shows whether the effect scales down.

**The constraint conflict.** A three-week freeze against evidence that needs seeds, overlap checks and a written rule; and a stakeholder whose commitment to leadership predates the evidence.

**What you give up.** The plan gives up a from-scratch measurement of the source as main-phase data, and accepts that the decision is made at 7B for one model. If the answer is no, partnerships gets the memo, the overlap report and a date for the next mix decision, not a quiet exclusion.

**How the plan fails.** Overlap checking by n-grams misses paraphrased benchmark problems, which worked-solution textbooks contain in quantity, so the held-out set written after the source's date carries the decision. A two-seed spread is a weak estimate; a gain just above it is a coin with a slight bias, and the memo should say so.

## Follow-ups

- *Your budget halves because the other team's jobs run long. You can afford the overlap check and two arms. Which two, and what risk do you accept in writing?*
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one trusts the micro-anneal, one wants a from-scratch ablation because the source is 200B tokens. How do you decide without deciding by rank, before the freeze?*
- *Six weeks into the main run, the licensor asks for one book to be removed. What does your workflow make possible, and what does it not?*

## Common mistakes

- Running the ablation before checking whether the source contains the benchmarks it will be judged on.
- Writing the decision threshold after seeing the results.
- Measuring with one seed and reading a gain smaller than the noise as a decision.
- Letting a retried job's result be averaged in as a second seed.
- Treating "partnerships paid for it" as a reason to skip the gate rather than a reason to write the memo carefully.
