## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Here is the reliability section of last quarter's postmortem. Leadership approved its recommendation yesterday. Review it before the budget moves."
</Callout>

The excerpt (scenario; the Llama 3 figures are quoted from the paper):

> **Cluster reliability, Q3 run.** Our 2,048-GPU pretraining run had 61 unexpected interruptions over 30 days: 14 faulty GPUs, 7 HBM errors, 4 other GPU faults, 15 software bugs, 9 network faults, 6 host faults and 6 watchdog timeouts with no root cause.
>
> Meta's Llama 3 paper (Table 5) reports that faulty GPUs caused 30.1% of unexpected interruptions and that GPU issues overall caused 58.7%. On our cluster faulty GPUs caused 23.0% and GPU issues 41.0%. Our GPU fleet is therefore materially more reliable than Meta's.
>
> Our mean time between interruptions was 11.8 hours, against 3.1 hours for Llama 3 (419 interruptions in 54 days), which confirms the conclusion: our cluster runs almost four times longer between failures.
>
> Software bugs, however, caused 24.6% of our interruptions against Meta's 12.9%, nearly double.
>
> **Recommendation.** Cancel the planned node burn-in program for Q4 and move its budget to software reliability.

List every flaw you find, then say what you would recommend instead. Open the checklist only after you have written your list.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask how many GPUs each job ran on and for how many days, and what each comparison looks like per GPU-day.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask what the burn-in program was meant to catch, and whether the postmortem has any data on it.</p></details>

## Answer

**The quoted number is wrong by the paper's own count.** Table 5 lists 148 faulty-GPU interruptions out of 419, which is 35.3%. The printed 30.1% does not match, and the paper's 58.7% GPU share is exactly the sum of the printed percentages of the six GPU rows; from the counts those rows are 268 of 419, 64.0%. A reviewer who reads only the text never sees this.

**Shares cannot be compared across fleets.** A share falls whenever another category grows. This cluster's GPU share is lower partly because its software share is higher, which is the opposite of good news.

**Per GPU-day, the conclusion reverses.** Faulty GPUs: 14 / (2,048 × 30) = 2.28 × 10⁻⁴ per GPU-day here, against 148 / (16,384 × 54) = 1.67 × 10⁻⁴ for Llama 3, about 1.4 times worse. All GPU rows: 1.3 times worse. All interruptions: 61 / (2,048 × 30) against 419 / (16,384 × 54), about 2.1 times worse.

**MTBF across job sizes is not a comparison.** A job eight times smaller fails less often for the same hardware. At the Llama 3 per-GPU rate, a 2,048-GPU job would average 24.7 hours between interruptions; this cluster managed 11.8.

**The denominator and the counts are uncertain.** "Up to 16K" makes 16,384 the largest denominator and therefore Meta's lowest possible rate; if Meta's job averaged fewer GPUs, the gap narrows. Fourteen events in 30 days is also a small sample: before moving a budget, give an interval or wait for more data. The two tables also need the same definitions: unexpected interruptions only, and watchdog timeouts with no root cause handled the same way.

**The recommendation does not follow from the evidence.** A burn-in catches nodes that will fail repeatedly, and nothing in the postmortem says how many of the 25 GPU interruptions were repeat offenders. The software investment is justified, since 15 / (2,048 × 30) is four times Meta's 54 / (16,384 × 54), but it is justified by the rate, not by the share, and it does not have to be paid for by the burn-in.

**What to recommend instead.** Restate the comparison per GPU-day with the denominator assumption written down, pull the per-node failure history, and fund software reliability without cancelling the burn-in until the repeat-failure data says whether the burn-in would have caught anything. Using Llama 3 as a benchmark is fine on those terms; it is one fleet in one 54-day window.

## Follow-ups

- *Leadership asks for one sentence that replaces the second paragraph of the postmortem. Write it.*
- *The per-node history shows 9 of the 25 GPU interruptions came from three nodes. What does that do to the burn-in decision, and what would you check before claiming the burn-in would have caught them?*
- *A colleague argues that hardware generation differences make any comparison with Llama 3 meaningless. When is that right?*

## Common mistakes

- Accepting a percentage quoted from a paper without dividing the count by the total.
- Comparing interruption shares between fleets of different size and mix.
- Comparing job-level MTBF across job sizes.
- Rejecting the whole postmortem, including the software finding that survives a per-GPU comparison.
