## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="The prompt">
"What is the difference between a process reward model and an outcome reward model? Lightman et al. found process supervision better on maths. Why did DeepSeek-R1 drop process rewards?"
</Callout>

Answer for a team about to start RL on a maths-and-code model whose answers can be checked by a program.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether the reward model will be used to rerank finished samples or as the signal an RL run optimises for many steps.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask who or what labels a step as correct, and what the labelling costs per solution.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>An outcome reward model scores the final answer; a process reward model scores each intermediate step, so it can say where a solution went wrong and penalise right answers reached by wrong reasoning. Lightman et al. trained both on MATH with human step labels (PRM800K, 800,000 of them) and used them to rerank sampled solutions; the process-supervised model solved 78% of their MATH subset and beat outcome supervision. DeepSeek-R1 needed a reward to optimise against at scale, and gave three reasons a PRM failed there: steps are hard to define outside maths, judging a step is hard, and a learned step judge gets hacked and has to be retrained. For checkable answers, R1 used a rule on the final answer and a format rule.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>What each supervises.</strong> An outcome reward gives one signal per solution, so credit for a long chain of reasoning is assigned only by the final result: a lucky guess after wrong steps scores like a correct derivation. A process reward gives a signal per step, which is denser and catches wrong reasoning, but someone has to define what a step is and label it.</p>
<p><strong>Why both results hold.</strong> Lightman et al. measured a PRM as a reranker of finished samples, with 800,000 human step labels, on one domain where steps are natural (lines of a derivation). Reranking exposes the PRM to the samples a fixed generator produces. R1 measured a PRM as an RL reward, where the policy searches for whatever the PRM scores well for thousands of steps, across general reasoning where steps have no fixed boundaries. Optimisation pressure finds the learned judge's errors; Gao et al. showed the same shape for outcome reward models, with the true reward peaking and then falling while the proxy keeps rising.</p>
<p><strong>For this team.</strong> Answers are checkable by a program, so the RL reward should be the rule (final answer or tests) plus a format check. A PRM is still useful as a reranker at inference or for filtering SFT data, where it is not optimised against directly.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li>The rule reward accepts correct final answers reached by wrong reasoning. How would you measure how often that happens before deciding it matters?</li>
<li>What would you need to see to add a PRM term to the RL reward after all?</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

Both supervision types fail somewhere. The rule-based outcome reward recommended above rewards any chain that ends at the right answer, including ones with errors that cancel, and on code it rewards whatever passes the tests; its failure mode is a policy whose reasoning text is unreliable even when its answers are right. A sample of transcripts graded by people each week is the check.

## Follow-ups

- Lightman et al. labelled steps by hand. If you trained a PRM on labels produced automatically, by checking whether continuations from a step reach the right answer, which of R1's three objections does that address and which does it leave?
- Does a PRM help more on short or long solutions, and why?

## Common mistakes

- Treating Lightman et al. and R1 as contradicting each other, without noticing one reranks and the other optimises.
- Calling a process reward model hack-proof because it checks every step: it is still a learned model.
- Assuming outcome rewards cannot be gamed. A parser that takes the last number in the response rewards listing several numbers.
