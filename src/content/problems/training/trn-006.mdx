## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"This is the training team's weekly update. It goes to the VP tomorrow and includes a request for two engineers. Review it."
</Callout>

> **Run B-34, week 3 update**
>
> **Config.** 34B dense, 2,048 H100 SXM, BF16, sequence length 8,192, global batch 2,048 sequences (16,777,216 tokens per step). Full activation checkpointing. Sequence packing has been disabled since week 2 after a data-loader bug; sequences are padded to 8,192 (average padding 18%).
>
> **Throughput.** Median step time this week: 4.6 s. Steps that save a checkpoint (every 500) or run evaluation (every 1,000) are excluded from the median as outliers.
>
> **MFU.** FLOPs per step = 8 × N × tokens per step (forward, backward and recompute, since checkpointing is on) = 4.56e18. Peak = 1,979 TFLOP/s per GPU (H100 BF16, NVIDIA datasheet). MFU = 4.56e18 / (4.6 s × 2,048 × 1,979e12) = **24.5%**.
>
> **Comparison.** Llama 3 405B reached 38% to 43% BF16 MFU, and PaLM reached 46.2%. We are about 15 points behind. Our 256-GPU staging job measured 31%, so the drop at 2,048 GPUs is in our kernels.
>
> **Schedule.** 9.0T tokens remain. At 16,777,216 tokens per 4.6 s step, that is 536,442 steps, 28.6 days. On track for the 30-day milestone.
>
> **Request.** Two engineers for six weeks on kernel fusion to close the MFU gap.

List every problem you would raise, rank them, and say what you would send back. Then open the checklist.

## Answer

The report's headline number is wrong in two directions at once, and that is why it survives a first read. The peak is the sparse BF16 figure, twice the dense 989.5, which halves MFU. The numerator uses 8N because checkpointing is on, which counts recomputed activations; MFU as PaLM defined it excludes rematerialisation, so that multiplies the result by 8/6. Halved and multiplied by 4/3, the error is a factor of 2/3, and 24.5% looks like an ordinary underperforming run rather than an arithmetic mistake.

Recomputed: 6 × 34e9 × 16,777,216 / (4.6 × 2,048 × 989.5e12) = 36.7%. But 18% of positions are padding, which the model processes and nobody needs; counting only real tokens, MFU is about 30%. The 36.7% is closer to the honest systems number (the GPUs are doing that work), and the 30% is the number that says how fast the run is learning. Both should be on the page, labelled.

The schedule is the most consequential error, because it is the one leadership acts on. Each step carries 16,777,216 × 0.82 = 13.76M real tokens, so 9.0T tokens take 654,000 steps, 34.8 days at 4.6 s. The median also excludes checkpoint and evaluation steps, and there is no term for failures or restarts; Llama 3 reports above 90% effective training time, and at 90% the run needs about 38.7 days. The 30-day milestone is missed by more than a week, and the report says "on track".

The comparison does not hold. PaLM's 46.2% counts attention FLOPs; Llama 3's figures come from a different model at 8,192 to 16,384 GPUs with its own parallelism layout, and its 38% row is at a 131,072-token sequence. Even with correct arithmetic, a 34B run on 2,048 GPUs at 30% to 37% is not "15 points behind" anything comparable.

The staffing request rests on the comparison and on a causal claim with no evidence. The drop from 31% at 256 GPUs to the reported figure at 2,048 is what collective communication growth looks like, and it was computed with the same formula, so its absolute value is wrong too. Before approving kernel work, ask for a profile at 2,048 GPUs that splits step time into compute, exposed communication and data loading. The cheapest MFU gain in this report is turning packing back on, which recovers the 18% of compute spent on padding.

Two choices in the report are acceptable with a change. An 8N-based number is useful as hardware FLOPs utilization, if it is labelled as such, uses the dense peak and sits next to MFU. Excluding checkpoint steps from the median is fine for tracking kernel performance, if goodput is measured separately and applied in the projection.

What to send back: corrected MFU (both versions, labelled), a projection from real tokens with measured goodput, the packing fix with its owner and date, and the staffing request deferred until a profile shows where the time goes.

## Follow-ups

- The team says the padding fix needs two weeks and the run cannot pause. Do you accept 18% of the remaining compute as waste, and who signs that off?
- After correction, the projection is 39 days against a 30-day milestone. Cut tokens, move the milestone or add GPUs? State the cost of each.
- What would you need to see in the profile to approve the two engineers after all?

## Common mistakes

- Fixing only the sparse peak, which turns 24.5% into 49% and produces an even more misleading report.
- Accepting the schedule because the per-step arithmetic is correct, without asking what a token is.
- Treating the staffing request as the subject of the review instead of the projection.
- Rejecting HFU outright instead of asking for it to be labelled.
