## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As asked">
"You are fine-tuning LLaMA 65B with QLoRA. Estimate peak GPU memory for a micro-batch of 4 sequences of 2,048 tokens. Does it fit on one 48 GB GPU, and if not, what do you change?"
</Callout>

Inputs (published): 80 layers, hidden size 8,192, MLP size 22,016, vocabulary 32,000. The linear layers inside the blocks hold 64.76B parameters; the embedding and output head hold 0.52B. QLoRA stores the block linears in NF4 at 4.127 bits per parameter including double-quantised constants, and the paper's runs used rank 64 adapters on every linear layer.

Inputs (scenario): embedding and output head kept in BF16; gradient checkpointing at every layer boundary, storing one BF16 hidden state per token per layer; logits for the loss in FP32; Adam moments in FP32 and kept on the GPU (no paging).

Choose and state: the bytes per adapter parameter for weights, gradients and optimizer state, and a reserve for the one-layer recompute working set, temporary dequantised blocks and the runtime. Give peak memory in GB (10^9 bytes).

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>About 58 GB with 12 bytes per adapter parameter and a 2 GB reserve: base 34.5, adapter state 9.6, checkpoints 10.7, logits 1.0, reserve 2. It does not fit on 48 GB. The dominant term after the base is the checkpointed activations, which scale with tokens per micro-batch and which 4-bit storage does not shrink. I would drop to one 2,048-token sequence per micro-batch with gradient accumulation and page the Adam moments, which leaves room; the alternative is keeping 8,192 tokens and moving to an 80 GB GPU.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Fixed terms.</strong> Base: 64.76 x 10^9 x 4.127 / 8 = 33.41 GB, plus 0.52 x 10^9 x 2 = 1.05 GB for embedding and head in BF16: 34.46 GB. Adapter: rank 64 on four 8,192 x 8,192 attention projections is 4 x 64 x 16,384 = 4.19M per layer, and on three MLP projections 3 x 64 x 30,208 = 5.80M, so 9.99M per layer and 0.80B over 80 layers. At 12 bytes (BF16 weights and gradients, FP32 Adam moments) that is 9.59 GB; at 16 bytes (an FP32 master copy too) 12.79 GB. Both are reasonable; 2 or 4 bytes per parameter, ignoring the optimizer, is not.</p>
<p><strong>Token-proportional terms.</strong> 8,192 tokens. Checkpoints: 80 x 8,192 x 2 bytes = 1,310,720 bytes per token, 10.74 GB. Logits: 32,000 x 4 bytes = 128,000 bytes per token, 1.05 GB, or about twice that if the logits' gradient is materialised alongside. Reserve: the reader's choice; 2 to 6 GB is reasonable for one layer's recomputed intermediates at 8,192 tokens plus temporaries. Zero is not.</p>
<p><strong>Total.</strong> Low end: 34.46 + 9.59 + 10.74 + 1.05 + 2 = 57.8 GB. High end: 34.46 + 12.79 + 10.74 + 2.10 + 6 = 66.1 GB. The band 54 to 68 accepts those choices; an answer near 44 to 47 GB forgot the activations, and one near 130 GB stored the base in BF16.</p>
<p><strong>What changes the conclusion.</strong> The assumption that flips "does not fit" to "fits" is tokens per micro-batch. At one 2,048-token sequence the token terms fall to 2.95 GB; with Adam moments paged to CPU (6.4 GB off the GPU) the total is 34.46 + 3.20 + 2.95 + 2 = 42.6 GB.</p>
<p><strong>Two answers and their costs.</strong> Smaller micro-batch with accumulation keeps the hardware and the effective batch, and costs step efficiency and depends on paging behaving; the paper reports paged optimizers matching regular speed for 65B on 48 GB at batch 16, which is evidence for the spike case, not for paging every step. An 80 GB GPU keeps 8,192 tokens per micro-batch without paging and costs the hardware and its lead time. The conflict is cost against step time.</p>
<p><strong>How the recommendation fails.</strong> If the Adam moments are paged on every step rather than on spikes, each step moves 6.4 GB to and from host memory, and the "fits" configuration can run slower than the budget allows. Measure step time with paging on before committing.</p>
</Fragment>

<li><em>Does doubling the rank to 128 change your answer?</em> Adapter state doubles to about 19 GB at 12 bytes, which matters more than the activations at small micro-batches.</li>
</ul>

## Common mistakes

- Treating QLoRA's saving as applying to the whole budget; it applies to the frozen weights only.
- Leaving out the activations, or using the paper's 512-token, batch-1 measurements at 8,192 tokens per micro-batch.
- Counting adapter weights but not their gradients and Adam moments.
- Quantising the embedding and output head in the estimate without checking the quantisation config.
- Mixing GB and GiB without saying which.
