## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"We want a 70B dense model trained on 15T tokens, on 4,096 H100 SXM GPUs. Leadership has announced a date 45 days after launch. How many days will the run take, and is the date real?"
</Callout>

The run, as a scenario: BF16 training, sequence length 8,192, model dimension 8,192, full activation checkpointing. The team has not run at 4,096 GPUs before; its largest previous job used 512.

1. Estimate the training FLOPs.
2. Choose an MFU and a goodput (the share of wall-clock time that produces training steps) and defend both. **Enter the number of days.**
3. Say whether 45 days holds, and if not, what you would change.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask which precision and sparsity the datasheet figure you are using assumes.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask what MFU the team measured at 512 GPUs, and how it was counted when checkpointing was on.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask for the interruption history of this cluster over its last long job.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>About 50 days at 40% MFU and 90% goodput, so 45 days does not hold without a change. C = 6 × 70e9 × 15e12 = 6.3e24 FLOPs; the cluster delivers 4,096 × 989.5e12 × 0.40 × 0.90 = 1.46e18 FLOP/s, 4.3e6 seconds. The 45-day date assumes either 100% goodput at 40% MFU or about 45% MFU at 90% goodput, on a scale this team has never run. I would cut tokens to about 13.5T to hold the date, or move the date, and decide which before launch because the learning-rate schedule depends on it.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>FLOPs.</b> 6ND = 6 × 70e9 × 15e12 = 6.3e24. Checkpointing does not change this number: recomputed activations are real work for the GPU but not FLOPs the model requires, and MFU as PaLM defined it excludes them.</p>
<p><b>Peak.</b> The H100 page lists 1,979 TFLOPS for BF16 with sparsity; dense training runs at half, 989.5 TFLOP/s.</p>
<p><b>MFU, chosen.</b> Llama 3 reports 38% to 43% BF16 MFU on 8,192 to 16,384 H100s with a team that built for that scale. A team moving from 512 to 4,096 GPUs for the first time should plan at or below the bottom of that band. 35% to 40% is reasonable; 45% is optimistic without a measurement at scale.</p>
<p><b>Goodput, chosen.</b> Llama 3 reports above 90% effective training time, with 419 unexpected interruptions in 54 days on a much larger cluster. 90% is a reasonable plan; 95% needs a failure history that supports it.</p>
<p><b>Days.</b></p>
<ul>
<li>40% MFU, 90% goodput: 6.3e24 / (4,096 × 989.5e12 × 0.40 × 0.90) = 4.32e6 s = <b>50.0 days</b>.</li>
<li>35% MFU, 90% goodput: 57.1 days. 45% MFU, 95% goodput: 42.1 days.</li>
<li>Any answer from 40 to 62 days counts.</li>
</ul>
<p><b>The dominant term and what flips it.</b> 6ND is fixed; the answer is set by MFU × goodput, which ranges from 0.32 to 0.43 across reasonable choices. The assumption that flips the conclusion about the date is MFU at 4,096 GPUs: at 45% with 90% goodput the run takes 44.4 days, at 40% it takes 50. Attention adds T / (8D) = 8,192 / 65,536 = 12.5% FLOPs at this sequence length; if the MFU you are borrowing counted attention FLOPs, your days rise by that factor.</p>
<p><b>The decision.</b> Two answers are defensible. Hold the date and cut tokens: in 45 days at 40% and 90% the cluster does 5.7e24 FLOPs, enough for 13.5T tokens, a 10% cut, at a loss cost the scaling fit can estimate. Or hold the tokens and move the date by about a week, which costs the announcement. Adding GPUs (about 4,550 would be needed) is the third option and the weakest, because it moves the team further past the scale it has measured. I would cut tokens, set the schedule for 13.5T from the start, and write the trigger: if measured tokens per day in the first week project past day 45, cut further at that point rather than extend.</p>
<p><b>Failure mode of this answer.</b> The 40% figure is a guess until the first day at full scale. If MFU at 4,096 GPUs comes in at 32%, even 13.5T tokens takes 56 days, and the cut has to happen again with a schedule already set for 13.5T. Run a one-day benchmark at full scale before fixing the schedule.</p>
</Fragment>

### Which answers are unreasonable

- About 22 to 25 days: the sparse BF16 peak, 1,979, in the denominator. Dense training cannot use structured sparsity.
- About 67 days at 40% MFU: 8ND because checkpointing is on, combined with an MFU defined without recompute. That counts the recompute twice; 8ND belongs with hardware FLOPs utilization, which is higher than MFU by the same factor.
- About 17 days: 2ND, the forward pass only.
- Under 40 days: MFU of 50% or more at 90% goodput, not supported by a published run at this scale or by this team's history.

## Common mistakes

- Using the datasheet BF16 figure without reading the sparsity footnote.
- Planning on MFU from a 512-GPU job at 4,096 GPUs, where collective communication grows and the measurement no longer applies.
- Leaving out goodput, which turns a plan for a cluster that never fails into a promised date.
- Answering "not real" without saying what to cut, or cutting tokens late with a learning-rate schedule set for the full run.
