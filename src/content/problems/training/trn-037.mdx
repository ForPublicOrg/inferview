## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"The new checkpoint is up 1.8 points on our English benchmark average, and the Indonesian team says users prefer the old model. The only change between the two runs is the data pipeline. Here is the diff and what we logged. What happened, and what do you do before the next run starts on Monday?"
</Callout>

```diff
 language_id:
   model: lid.176
-  min_score: 0.50
+  min_score: 0.65
 heuristics:
   max_dup_ngram_frac: 0.20
 dedup:
   method: minhash
   scope: per_language
 quality:
-  model: qc-multi-v3
-  keep: {top_fraction: 0.10, within: language}
+  model: qc-v4
+  keep: {top_fraction: 0.10, within: corpus}
 decontaminate:
-  sets: [mmlu, gsm8k, hellaswag, arc]
+  sets: [mmlu, gsm8k, hellaswag]
 sources:
   - common-crawl-2026
+  - forum-qa-2026
```

The release note for the change says qc-v4 "scores pages against English reference sites and gained 2.1 points on the English ablation". The multilingual bucket samples languages in proportion to their final pools.

Indonesian tokens after each stage:

| stage | v7 | v8 |
|---|---|---|
| after language ID | 610B | 572B |
| after heuristics | 455B | 427B |
| after dedup | 300B | 282B |
| final | 30B | 4B |

Kept fraction at the quality stage, by language, in v8: English 10.6%, German 6.2%, Indonesian 1.4%, Hindi 0.9%. In v7 every language was at 10.0%.

Indonesian preference eval, 400 user prompts, new model against old: 38% preferred the new model.

<p class="tiny">The pipeline, the counts and the eval are synthetic.</p>

You do not have qc-v4's score distribution per language, a breakdown of the Indonesian eval by register (formal against code-mixed), or a run that isolates any single change. Pick the cause of the Indonesian regression, rank the other candidates, and say what you would collect and what you would ship without it.

## Answer

The cause is `within: corpus`. In v7 the quality stage kept the top 10% of each language, which is why every language sat at exactly 10.0%. In v8 one ranking runs across all languages, and with a classifier that scores pages against English reference sites, English takes more than its share (10.6%) and every other language less: Indonesian keeps 4B of 282B, 1.4%. The final Indonesian pool fell 87% (30B to 4B) and the multilingual bucket, which samples by final pool size, gave Indonesian a fraction of its old share.

The defect hides well. It is one of several changed lines, the model swap beside it gets the attention because the release note is about it, and the English ablation that justified the change could not see it.

**Ranking the rest.**

1. *qc-v4 scoring Indonesian lower.* Real and second. Restoring within-language ranking alone would bring Indonesian back to 10% of its pool, but which 10% would be chosen by a model that rewards resemblance to English reference sites: translated or code-mixed pages over native writing. Llama 3 uses a multilingual classifier for non-English text for this reason. Keep qc-v4 for English and qc-multi-v3 for the rest until a multilingual successor is measured.
2. *Language-ID cutoff 0.65.* Real and third, with a twist. It removed 38B of 610B, 6%, so it cannot explain the volume loss, but the pages with low language confidence are disproportionately code-mixed, which is how many users write. FineWeb's 0.65 is a cutoff for English; per-language cutoffs, or 0.50 for Indonesian, are cheap to restore.
3. *Decontamination and the forum source.* Real, and it belongs to the other half of the symptom. Dropping ARC from the decontamination list while adding a forum crawl is how benchmark items enter training; part of the 1.8 points may be leakage. It removes no Indonesian text.

**What to collect.** qc-v4's score histogram per language, which confirms the mechanism in an hour; the Indonesian eval split by register, which tells you how much the language-ID cutoff matters; and an n-gram overlap check of forum-qa-2026 against ARC.

**What to ship without it, by Monday.** This is the decision with two defensible answers. *Revert the whole pipeline to v7.* It is known to work for Indonesian and needs no new evidence; it gives up the English gain, some of which may be leakage anyway, and delays qc-v4 by a full cycle. *Targeted fix:* `within: language`, qc-v4 for English only, qc-multi-v3 for the rest, language-ID back to 0.50 for Indonesian, ARC back in decontamination. It keeps the real part of the English gain, at the cost of a combination no run has tested, going into a main run. With a Monday start and no ablation slot, I would revert, and run the targeted configuration as the first ablation of the next cycle.

**How the fix fails.** A revert restores the old Indonesian behaviour, including whatever was wrong with it, and the team loses the signal that a corpus-wide ranking is unsafe unless it is written down. Add a gate: kept fraction per language at the quality stage must stay within a band set per language, and the Indonesian preference eval runs on every pipeline change, not only on model releases.

## Follow-ups

- *The English team says reverting costs them 1.8 points they need for a launch. You suspect part of it is ARC leakage but have no overlap check yet. Which do you ship, and what risk do you write down?*
- *After the targeted fix, Indonesian is back to 30B tokens and the preference eval rises only to 46%. What would you collect next, and what would make you suspect the language-ID cutoff rather than the classifier?*
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one wants per-language quality models, the other one multilingual model with per-language thresholds. How do you decide without deciding by rank?*

## Common mistakes

- Picking the classifier swap because the release note is about it, without checking whether the kept fractions are equal across languages.
- Reading absolute token counts per stage and missing that the language-ID loss is proportionally small.
- Attributing the English gain to the classifier before checking decontamination.
- Shipping the targeted fix untested into a main run because each change looks safe on its own.
