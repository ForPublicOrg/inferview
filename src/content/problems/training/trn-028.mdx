## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"We start a 4,096-GPU pretraining run next week with asynchronous checkpoints. What interval do you set, and how many GPU-hours a day do we lose to failures and checkpointing?"
</Callout>

Published input, from the Llama 3 paper (July 2024, checked 2026-10-28): 419 unexpected interruptions in a 54-day snapshot of pre-training on "up to 16K" H100 GPUs.

Scenario inputs for this run, measured on a rehearsal:

- Asynchronous save: training stalls 6 seconds per checkpoint while state is staged to host memory; the upload then takes 40 seconds in the background, and a checkpoint is not usable until its upload completes.
- Restart after an unexpected failure (detection, rescheduling, loading the checkpoint, warm-up) takes 15 minutes, during which all 4,096 GPUs are idle.
- A failure loses all work since the last checkpoint whose upload had completed.
- Planned maintenance is out of scope.

1. Choose the GPU count you assume for the Llama 3 snapshot and say why. Derive the expected unexpected failures per day for this run.
2. Choose a checkpoint interval and justify it.
3. **Enter the expected GPU-hours lost per day** to checkpoint stalls, lost work and restarts at your interval.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Assuming 16,384 GPUs for the whole snapshot, the per-GPU rate is 419 / (16,384 × 54), so this run sees 1.94 unexpected failures a day, one every 742 minutes. The interval that minimizes loss is about √(2 × 0.1 × 742) = 12.2 minutes. At that interval the run loses about 54 minutes a day, which is 3,690 GPU-hours. The restart term is more than half of it, so restart time, not the interval, is where the next engineering month goes.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Failure rate.</b> "Up to 16K" makes 16,384 the largest defensible denominator and so the lowest per-GPU rate. 419 / (16,384 × 54) = 4.74 × 10⁻⁴ unexpected failures per GPU-day; times 4,096 GPUs, 1.94 a day, or a mean of 742 minutes between failures. If the job averaged 12,000 GPUs over the window, the rate is 16,384 / 12,000 = 1.37 times higher: 2.65 a day.</p>
<p><b>Cost model.</b> With interval τ, stall δ = 0.1 minutes, upload W = 0.67 minutes, restart R = 15 minutes and f failures a day, minutes lost per day = (1,440 / τ) × δ + f × (τ/2 + W + R). The τ/2 is the average age of the newest checkpoint at a random failure; W is added because a checkpoint in flight is not usable. Minimizing over τ gives τ* = √(2δ × 1,440 / f) = √(2δM).</p>
<p><b>At 16,384 and τ = 12.2 minutes.</b> Saves: 1,440 / 12.2 × 0.1 = 11.8 minutes a day. Failures: 1.94 × (6.1 + 0.67 + 15) = 42.2 minutes. Total 54.0 minutes, × 4,096 GPUs / 60 = 3,688 GPU-hours a day, 3.8% of the run.</p>
<p><b>Other reasonable choices.</b> The curve is flat near the minimum: at 16,384, intervals of 10 and 15 minutes give 3,720 and 3,723 GPU-hours a day, and 20 minutes gives 3,890. With 12,000 as the denominator, τ* = 10.4 minutes and the loss is 4,718; 15 minutes gives 4,844. Answers from 3,500 to 5,200 cover those.</p>
<p><b>The dominant term.</b> Restarts: 1.94 × 15 = 29.1 of the 54.0 minutes a day. Halving the interval does nothing to it. Cutting R to 2 minutes would bring the total to about 1,970 GPU-hours a day, which the Llama 3 paper's own lever (reduced job startup time) points at.</p>
<p><b>The assumption that flips the conclusion.</b> The save stall. With a synchronous 90-second save, τ* = √(2 × 1.5 × 742) = 47 minutes and the loss rises to about 8,240 GPU-hours a day, with saves and lost work together (about 92 of 121 minutes a day) outweighing restarts (29). Then the interval and the save path, not restart time, are the first fix.</p>
<p><b>Failure mode of this answer.</b> It assumes independent failures at a constant rate. A failure storm (a bad batch of nodes failing several times in an hour) makes the expected-value interval too long exactly when it matters, and it hides the fact that a failure during the 40-second upload costs a full extra interval.</p>
</Fragment>

### Which answers are unreasonable

- About 1,700 GPU-hours a day: the cost model without the restart term, which is the largest term.
- About 6,200 GPU-hours a day or more: hourly checkpoints, which cost about 2,500 GPU-hours a day more than a 12-minute interval for no reason a 6-second stall justifies.
- About 6,430 GPU-hours a day: dividing by 8,192 GPUs instead of the paper's "up to 16K" without a reason; that assumes the job ran at half its stated size for the whole snapshot.
- Any figure below about 1,000 GPU-hours a day: usually lost minutes counted for one GPU instead of 4,096.
- About 8,240 GPU-hours a day: the synchronous answer, when the question says the save is asynchronous.

## Follow-ups

- *Finance asks what one point of effective training time is worth on this run.* 1% of 4,096 GPUs × 24 hours = 983 GPU-hours a day; price it at the reserved rate.
- *The run grows to 16,384 GPUs next quarter at the same per-GPU rate. Recompute τ* and say which term dominates now.*
- *Leadership wants zero lost work. What would that take, and what does it cost in stalls or replicated state?*

## Common mistakes

- Using the job-level rate of the Llama 3 run (7.8 failures a day at up to 16K GPUs) for a run a quarter of its size.
- Leaving out restart time, or counting it once a day instead of once per failure.
- Forgetting that a checkpoint is only usable after its upload completes.
- Treating 3.8% as a per-GPU loss and forgetting that a synchronous job idles every GPU during a restart.
