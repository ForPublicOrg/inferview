## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"You have 2,048 H100s for 60 days to pre-train the model behind our coding assistant. Size the run: architecture class, parameters and tokens. Bring a decision memo to Thursday's review."
</Callout>

The situation, as a scenario:

- The team of six has trained dense models up to 30B on this cluster at 38% BF16 MFU and about 90% effective training time. Nobody has trained a mixture of experts above 1B.
- About 10T unique tokens of acceptable quality exist.
- Serving runs on the existing fleet of 8-GPU H100 nodes (640 GB of HBM per node). No new serving hardware arrives this year.
- Product forecasts 1.2e15 tokens served over the model's 12-month life. Finance's forecast is 2e14. Both will be at the review, and they do not agree.
- The team's IsoFLOP sweep on this data covers 1e19 to 1e21 FLOPs.

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask which of the two inference forecasts the serving budget is approved against.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>A 70B dense model on about 8.5T tokens. The budget is 3.6e24 FLOPs. Compute-optimal at about 20 tokens per parameter is 173B on 3.5T, but even under finance's low forecast its lifetime inference is 19 times the training compute, so every parameter cut pays for itself. 40B on 15T would cost less to serve but needs 1.5 passes over our 10T unique tokens, past what the sweep describes. An MoE would give better loss per served FLOP, but this team has never run one at scale, and a 60-day window has no room for a second attempt. What I give up: the lowest loss this compute could reach, and the MoE's efficiency.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Budget.</b> 2,048 × 989.5e12 × 0.38 × 0.90 × 60 × 86,400 = 3.59e24 FLOPs.</p>
<p><b>The options at that budget.</b></p>
<ul>
<li>Compute-optimal dense: 120N² = 3.59e24 gives N = 173B on 3.5T tokens. Lifetime inference 2 × 173e9 × tokens served: 6.9e25 at finance's forecast (19x training), 4.2e26 at product's (116x).</li>
<li>70B dense on 3.59e24 / (6 × 70e9) = 8.5T tokens, 122 tokens per parameter, inside the unique data. Inference at the two forecasts: 2.8e25 and 1.7e26, saving 4.1e25 to 2.5e26 FLOPs against 173B, which is 11 to 69 times this run's training compute.</li>
<li>40B dense on 15T tokens, 374 per parameter. Cheapest to serve, but 15T exceeds 10T unique tokens, so a third of the training is repeated data, and the team's sweep never saw repetition.</li>
<li>MoE with about 30B to 40B activated. Kimi K2's sweeps show loss falling as experts are added at fixed activated parameters (sparsity 48 needed 1.69x fewer FLOPs than 8 to reach the same validation loss in their setting). Serving memory sets the total: DeepSeek-V3's 671B at one byte per parameter would not fit a 640 GB node.</li>
</ul>
<p><b>The disagreement between forecasts does not change the choice between 173B and 70B.</b> At the low forecast, 70B already saves 11 training runs' worth of compute. It changes how far below 70B to go, and that is bounded by data, not by the forecast.</p>
<p><b>Constraint conflict.</b> Lower serving cost pushes toward 40B; unique data and a sweep without repetition push back toward 70B; quality pushes toward 173B. The 70B is the largest model whose lifetime cost is defensible at the low forecast and the smallest that stays inside the data without repetition.</p>
<p><b>The memo.</b> Decision: 70B dense, 8.5T tokens, schedule set for 8.5T. Assumptions: 38% MFU and 90% goodput, from this team's own runs. Expected loss at 10%, 30% and 60% of tokens from the sweep, with the small runs' spread. Rollback triggers: if loss at 30% is above the curve by more than that spread, stop and decide between a restart at a revised recipe and continuing with the quality target lowered in writing; if measured tokens per day project past day 60 by the end of week one, cut tokens then and adjust the schedule. What we give up: the 173B's lower loss and the MoE's efficiency. Owner for the trade between quality and serving cost: the product lead, with finance's forecast as the floor.</p>
<p><b>Failure mode of this answer.</b> The sweep tops out at 1e21 FLOPs; 3.6e24 is three and a half orders of magnitude away, and 122 tokens per parameter is further from the sweep's ratios than the compute-optimal choice would be. Sardana et al. found that fits on typical ratios overestimate the gain from extra tokens. If the 70B lands short of the quality bar, the cause will be that extrapolation, and the 30% checkpoint is there to catch it before the window is spent.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- Product's forecast turns out right and serving is the bottleneck by month four. What does distilling the 70B into a smaller model cost, and was a smaller pre-training run the better call?
- Two senior engineers disagree: one wants 70B, one wants 40B with repetition. How do you decide without deciding by rank?

## Common mistakes

- Sizing at 20 tokens per parameter for a model whose lifetime inference is tens of times its training compute.
- Choosing a token count larger than the unique data without saying it means repetition.
- Proposing a first MoE run at full scale on a fixed window without a pilot or a fallback.
- Leaving the inference forecast out of the decision because the two forecasts disagree, when both point the same way.
