## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"After aggressive filtering we have 3T high-quality tokens for a run that needs 12T. Relaxing the filter is off the table: the ablations say the next tier hurts. Do we repeat, rephrase or synthesise? Price each one."
</Callout>

Scenario inputs: a 30B dense model on 12T tokens, the 3T kept tokens mostly reference and expository text, a generation cluster that is free for six weeks before the run starts, and an 8B and a 70B in-house model available as rewriters. The run's headline evaluation includes closed-book factual QA.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask how many epochs of the 3T the plan implies if nothing new is generated.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask for the generation cluster's measured tokens per second for each rewriter, not its peak FLOPs.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask what has been checked about the rewriters' training data overlapping the evaluation sets.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Repeating the 3T four times fills 12T and sits at the edge of the range where Muennighoff et al. found repetition costs almost nothing in loss. Rephrasing 9T with an 8B rewriter costs about 2.9e23 FLOPs, 13% of the run's 2.2e24; with the 70B it costs more than the run. Kimi K2 measured ten rephrasings seen once beating ten epochs of raw text on SimpleQA, 28.94 against 23.76. My answer: repeat to about two epochs, rephrase the rest with the 8B under a fidelity check, keep synthetic generation for narrow domains. The risk I accept is facts drifting in the rewrites.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Repeat.</b> 12T from 3T is 4 epochs. Muennighoff et al. report negligible loss change up to 4 epochs at fixed compute, measured up to 9B parameters and 900B tokens, so the evidence stops below this scale, and loss is not the factual QA score the run is judged on. The cost is zero compute and the risk is memorisation and diminishing returns past the measured range.</p>
<p><b>Rephrase.</b> Generation costs about 2N FLOPs per token read and 2N per token written. For 9T tokens out with about as many in, the 8B rewriter is 2 × 8e9 × 18e12 = 2.9e23 FLOPs; the 70B is 2 × 70e9 × 18e12 = 2.5e24. The run is 6 × 30e9 × 12e12 = 2.2e24. Decode is bandwidth-bound, so the wall-clock cost is larger than the FLOP ratio suggests; the measured tokens per second from Hint 2 decides whether six weeks is enough. K2's table is the evidence for the upside: one rephrasing over 10 epochs scored 27.39, ten rephrasings once 28.94, raw text over 10 epochs 23.76. K2 rephrased in chunks so long documents stay coherent, and checked each passage against its source for fidelity.</p>
<p><b>Synthesise.</b> New content from a model, not a rewrite of a source. OLMo 2's microanneals show form matters: maths problems as inline code dropped GSM* from 28.5 to 25.0, the same problems rewritten in natural language reached 65.5. Nemotron-4 used over 98% synthetic data in alignment, which is evidence for post-training, not for 9T of pretraining text. Synthesis has the largest contamination and homogenisation risk of the three and is the hardest to audit.</p>
</Fragment>

<ul>
<li>The fidelity check on a 10,000-passage sample finds 2% of rewrites change a number or a name. Do you ship the 9T, filter it, or fall back to repetition?</li>
<li>Your generation plan needs eight weeks of measured throughput and you have six. Which half of the rephrased data do you cut, and what risk do you write down?</li>
<li>What would you need to see in a small-scale ablation to reverse the decision and train on four epochs of raw text?</li>
</ul>

**The decision with two defensible answers.**

*Four epochs of the 3T, no generation.* Costs nothing in compute and nothing in schedule. It gives up whatever K2's rephrasing gain is worth at this scale, and it stretches Muennighoff's 4-epoch finding from 9B parameters to 30B and from loss to factual QA. Defensible when the generation cluster is needed elsewhere, or when there is no fidelity check anyone trusts.

*About two epochs of the 3T (6T) plus 6T of rephrased text from the 8B.* Generating 6T out with 6T in costs 2 × 8e9 × 12e12 = 1.9e23 FLOPs, about 9% of the run. It buys more distinct surface forms of the same facts, which is what K2's SimpleQA result rewards. It costs six weeks of a cluster, a fidelity pipeline, and the risk that the rewriter's errors become training data.

The 70B rewriter is not defensible here: 2.5e24 FLOPs to rephrase is more than the 2.2e24 the run itself costs.

**The quantity you choose and defend: the epoch count you allow on raw text.** One epoch forces 9T of rephrasing; four forces none. Two is the split above. Anything over four is unreasonable without new evidence, because that is where the published curve says added compute starts to stop paying.

**The constraint conflict.** Factual quality against compute and schedule: the option with the best published factual-QA evidence costs a six-week generation job and introduces a fidelity risk, while the free option relies on a result measured at a third of this model's size.

**What you give up.** Choosing the split gives up the cluster for six weeks and accepts that some rewrites will be wrong. Choosing four epochs gives up a gain that the only published comparison says is real.

**How the recommended answer fails.** A rewriter that paraphrases fluently can change a date or a quantity, and a fidelity check scored by another model shares its blind spots. Rephrasing also narrows style toward the rewriter's own, and if the rewriter saw an evaluation set, its paraphrases carry the answers past an n-gram decontamination filter. Mitigate with a held-out fact-level check against the source on a sample, n-gram and embedding decontamination of the rewrites, and an ablation that compares rephrased against repeated data on a factual QA set no rewriter has seen.

## Follow-ups

- *Your colleague proposes rephrasing only the top 10% of the 3T, ten times each, copying K2's best row. What changes in the risk, and what would you need to see to agree?*
- *The rephrased ablation wins on factual QA and loses on a held-out set of user-written text. Which result decides the main run, and who signs off?*
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one says synthetic maths in the pretraining mix is safe because OLMo 2 used it, the other says OLMo 2 used it in a short anneal. How do you decide without deciding by rank?*

## Common mistakes

- Treating "repeat up to 4 epochs" as a law at any scale, when it was measured up to 9B parameters.
- Pricing rephrasing by output tokens only, without the source tokens the rewriter reads.
- Pricing generation in FLOPs and planning the schedule from that, when decode throughput sets the wall clock.
- Citing Nemotron-4's 98% synthetic figure as evidence for pretraining data.
- Running decontamination on the source corpus and not on the rewrites.
