## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="The prompt">
"Design the workflow that takes supplier invoices from the inbox to a posted invoice in the ERP, and routes the uncertain ones to people."
</Callout>

The situation (scenario):

- 12,000 invoices a month arrive as PDFs by email and through a supplier portal; about a third are scans. They average 3 pages.
- Four accounts-payable clerks, about 420 working hours a month between them. Keying an invoice by hand takes 6 minutes, so manual entry alone would need 1,200 hours; today there is a backlog and overtime.
- In a pilot, reviewing a model-extracted invoice side by side with the PDF took a clerk 90 seconds.
- The ERP cannot be changed. Its create-invoice API takes no idempotency key, a duplicate (same vendor, same invoice number) is caught only by a weekly report, and reversing a posted invoice needs a credit memo signed by a manager.
- Existing policy: invoices over $25,000 need a manager's approval. Invoices received by the 25th must be posted by the last working day of the month, and 40 percent arrive in the last week.
- The external auditor wants, for every posted invoice, the source document, the posted values, who approved and when.

Design the workflow: steps, state, retries, idempotency, review routing, approvals, budget and rollout. Choose how much goes to review and show the clerk hours.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>One durable workflow per invoice, keyed by a hash of the document: intake, extract to a schema, business-rule validation, routing, review or approval, then a guarded post to the ERP. Rules run before confidence: a failed line sum, an unmatched PO or a changed bank account goes to a person whatever the score. Everything is reviewed at invoice level for the first two months, which costs about 372 of the 420 clerk hours and does not fit the month-end week; straight-through posting starts only for PO-matched invoices under a limit, once a weekly audit holds the error target. The ERP post reads before it retries, because the ERP cannot deduplicate for us.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p>Steps and state. received, extracted, validated, then one of review, approval or auto, then posting, then posted, rejected or dead-letter. The workflow engine (Uber runs TextSense under Cadence) persists each transition, so a crash resumes at the last step instead of starting over. Each extraction stores the model id, prompt version and raw output.</p>
<p>Validation before confidence. Lines sum to the subtotal, tax matches the vendor's rate, the PO exists and the amount is within tolerance, the vendor matches the vendor master. Bank details that differ from the master go to a separate fraud queue that is never auto-approved. These checks catch the errors that a model's confidence does not, and they are cheap.</p>
<p>Clerk hours, the quantity to choose. Exceptions (unreadable scans, unknown vendors, failed rules) are keyed by hand; take 8 percent (scenario): 960 × 6 minutes = 96 hours. Reviewing every other invoice side by side: 11,040 × 90 seconds = 276 hours. Total 372 of 420 hours, and the month-end week carries 40 percent of it, about 149 hours in a week where four clerks have roughly 100. So full review does not fit the peak. Review of 60 percent: 7,200 × 90 seconds = 180 hours plus 96 is 276 hours, which fits the month and nearly fits the peak week. The choice is between reviewing everything and missing the close, or reviewing a subset and accepting that some auto-posted invoices are wrong.</p>
<p>Two defensible review designs. Invoice-level review (any uncertain field sends the whole invoice) costs more clerk time but lets the clerk catch errors in fields the model was confident about. Field-level review (the clerk sees only the flagged fields) is faster, but clerks anchor on the highlighted fields and miss the rest. Uber's own figures show why this matters: 90 percent overall accuracy, yet only 35 percent of invoices at 99.5 percent and 65 percent above 80 percent, so a large share of invoices carry at least one wrong field. I start invoice-level and move to field-level only when the audit says flagged fields are where the errors are.</p>
<p>Posting. Before the create call, read the ERP by vendor and invoice number, and check in-flight workflows for the same pair. Record the intent, call create, record the ERP document id. On a timeout, read again before any retry; a blind retry is a duplicate payment. Durable execution re-runs a step at least once, so this read is what turns it into a single post.</p>
<p>Approvals. The over-$25,000 rule is a workflow step that waits on a manager, with approver, time and the evidence shown stored next to the posting. An approval records a decision; it does not make the post happen once, which is the posting step's job.</p>
<p>Budget. Per page, 1,500 to 3,000 text tokens plus the page image; the docs do not state the render size, and a page near 1,000 × 1,300 px would be 36 × 47 = 1,692 patches. A 3-page invoice is then about 9,600 to 14,100 input tokens. With 800 output tokens (scenario) that is $0.027 to $0.036 at Sonnet 5.5 prices, or about $330 to $430 a month. Model spend is not the constraint; clerk hours and wrong payments are.</p>
<p>Rollout, written down. Month 1, shadow: the pipeline extracts, clerks key as today, and the differences are measured per field and per invoice. Month 2, assist: clerks review pre-filled invoices, invoice-level. Month 3, straight-through for PO-matched invoices under $5,000 (a starting number, chosen because a mistake there is cheap to reverse) once the weekly audit of 100 auto-eligible invoices shows no more than the agreed error rate. Rollback triggers: any duplicate post, an audited error rate over target for two weeks, or review queue age over three days in the close week.</p>
<p>What I give up: two months of slower savings, and clerk time spent confirming invoices that were already right. The failure mode of this design is the audit itself: a weekly sample of 100 bounds the error rate loosely (zero errors in 100 still allows about 3 percent at 95 percent confidence), so straight-through can start on evidence that is thinner than it looks.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- *Your audit costs a clerk two hours a week that the close week does not have. Which half do you cut, and what risk do you accept in writing?* Keep the audit on straight-through invoices and drop it on reviewed ones during the close week; the written risk is a week of unmeasured reviewer error.
- *Two senior engineers disagree on field-level review. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Run both on the same 400 labelled pilot invoices and compare escaped errors per clerk hour.
- *A supplier sends the same invoice by email and through the portal, a day apart, with different PDFs. What catches it?* Not the content hash; the vendor and invoice number check against the ERP and in-flight workflows.

## Common mistakes

- Letting confidence decide the route before the business rules run.
- Retrying the ERP create call on a timeout.
- Sizing review from the monthly total and missing the month-end week.
- Treating a field-level accuracy figure as the share of invoices that are fully right.
- Auto-approving an invoice whose bank account differs from the vendor master because the extraction was confident.
