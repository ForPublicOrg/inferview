## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Here is a one-page proposal from our platform team. Review it as if it were on your desk for approval. List every problem you find before you look at the checklist."
</Callout>

> **Proposal: move support chat to Mercury (diffusion LM)**
>
> **Why.** Diffusion LMs generate many tokens in parallel. Mercury is up to 10x faster than speed-optimised frontier models, independently measured by Artificial Analysis at 1,109 tokens/sec against 59 to 201 for GPT-4o Mini, Claude 3.5 Haiku and Gemini 2.0 Flash Lite. The paper also reports 25 ms average latency in Copilot Arena. Our p50 time to first token today is 900 ms, so users will see answers almost instantly.
>
> **Quality.** HumanEval 88 to 90, in line with the frontier models in the same table. We expect no regression in resolution rate.
>
> **Integration.** The API is OpenAI-compatible, so our streaming client and our JSON tool calls (refunds, order lookup) work unchanged. Our prompts average 40K tokens (system prompt, policies, conversation history), which is within the 128K the paper supports.
>
> **Cost.** At the same per-token price, and with our current 80% prompt-cache hit rate applied to input tokens, monthly cost falls 15% because faster generation lets us release capacity reservations.
>
> **Plan.** Switch 100% of traffic in two sprints. Roll back if CSAT drops.

## Answer

The memo's facts are mostly real numbers attached to the wrong question.

**Speed.** The 1,109 tokens per second is Mercury Coder Mini's output throughput on H100s, measured by Artificial Analysis and quoted in Inception Labs' own paper. The comparison rows are Artificial Analysis medians across cloud providers for other vendors' APIs, with about 1,000 input and 1,000 output tokens, and the Mercury API was tested in February 2025. None of it is the team's current model on its own deployment, and none of it is time to first token, which is the 900 ms number the memo promises to improve. Per-request throughput at low load says little about throughput at the product's concurrency.

**The 25 ms.** The paper's text says 25 ms; its Table 4 says 0.25 seconds. A memo that picks the more favourable of two conflicting figures without flagging the conflict should not be approved.

**Streaming.** A masked diffusion model keeps positions in an order chosen by its sampler. With block sampling, nothing in a block is shown until the block is done, and the paper describes no streaming behaviour. "Works unchanged" is unverified at best; at worst users see text in bursts after a longer wait.

**Context.** Mercury supports 32,768 tokens out of the box and up to 128K with context extension approaches, stated as a capability rather than a benchmarked result. 40K-token prompts do not fit the default.

**Quality.** HumanEval measures Python function completion by coding models. It says nothing about resolution rate in customer support, refunds or policy adherence.

**Tool calls.** An OpenAI-compatible request format is not constrained decoding. Left-to-right grammar masking does not map onto out-of-order filling, so the invalid-JSON rate on refund and order-lookup schemas has to be measured.

**Cost.** The 80% prompt-cache discount assumes the provider can reuse computation for a repeated prefix. LLaDA's paper says its model is incompatible with KV caching because there is no causal mask, and Mercury's paper is silent on caching. If the 40K-token prompt is processed in full on every request, cost can rise, not fall.

**Plan.** 100% in two sprints with CSAT as the only rollback trigger means the first signal arrives after customers have been affected, and CSAT lags.

A defensible version: a shadow trial on a small slice, gated in advance on time to first visible token, invalid tool-call rate and resolution rate, with the vendor's billing for repeated prompts in writing before any capacity reservation is released. That trial is acceptable; the memo's switch is not.

## Follow-ups

- The vendor offers to waive input-token charges for the trial. What does that hide, and what do you ask for instead?
- The shadow trial shows time to first visible token 30% lower and invalid tool calls doubled. Do you proceed, and with what retry budget?

## Common mistakes

- Arguing that diffusion models are "not proven" in general instead of checking each claim against its source.
- Accepting "independently measured" without noticing the comparison rows are other vendors' APIs on unstated hardware.
- Missing the cost assumption, which is the line most likely to reverse the business case.
- Treating API compatibility as behavioural compatibility.
