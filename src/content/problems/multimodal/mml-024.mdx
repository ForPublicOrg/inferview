## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Debug, 20 minutes">
"Yesterday we shipped a turn-detection change to cut false barge-ins from background noise and to make turns faster. Today support says callers are being cut off mid-sentence. Here is the diff and a sample of recordings. Which change explains the symptom, what else is wrong, and what do you do in the next hour?"
</Callout>

The deploy (speech-to-speech session config; every line in the block is shown):

```diff
 session.audio.input.turn_detection:
   type: server_vad
-  threshold: 0.5
+  threshold: 0.75
   prefix_padding_ms: 300
-  silence_duration_ms: 800
+  silence_duration_ms: 500
   create_response: true
   interrupt_response: true
```

A reviewer listened to eight cut-off turns from today, chosen at random from turns where the caller kept talking after the agent started (scenario data):

| Turn | Device | What the caller was doing when the turn ended | Audible pause before the cut | Speech level at the cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | speakerphone | finishing a sentence, trailing off | none | quiet |
| 2 | handset | between two groups of an account number | about 550 ms | n/a |
| 3 | speakerphone | mid-word on "September" | none | quiet |
| 4 | speakerphone | finishing a sentence, trailing off | none | quiet |
| 5 | handset | soft-spoken caller, mid-sentence | none | quiet |
| 6 | handset | between two groups of an account number | about 600 ms | n/a |
| 7 | speakerphone | mid-sentence | none | quiet |
| 8 | handset | finishing a sentence, trailing off | none | quiet |

You do not have per-frame speech probabilities from the provider, the cut-off rate before the deploy was not tracked, and nobody has measured how many false barge-ins the change prevented.

Which change best explains the cut-offs callers are reporting?

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>For each sampled cut-off, ask whether a silence timer could have reached its limit if every audible frame had been scored as speech.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask for the false barge-in count per hundred turns from the week before the deploy.</p></details>

## Answer

**The threshold.** A silence timer only ends a turn after `silence_duration_ms` of frames scored as silence. In six of the eight samples there was no audible pause at all: the caller was speaking quietly, trailing off, or on a speakerphone far from the microphone. For the timer to reach 500 ms during audible speech, those frames had to score below the speech threshold. OpenAI's docs describe the trade directly: a higher threshold requires louder audio, which helps in noisy environments. Raising it from 0.5 to 0.75 is exactly the change that turns quiet speech into silence. Speakerphone turns are over-represented in the sample (four of six), which fits.

**The silence window is the second defect.** Turns 2 and 6 ended in pauses of 550 and 600 ms between digit groups. Under the old 800 ms window they would have survived. The window explains a quarter of the sample and is worth fixing, but reverting it alone leaves most of the reported cut-offs.

**Why the wrong answers are tempting.** "server_vad cannot tell a pause from a turn" is true and is the long-term argument for semantic or audio-native turn detection, but it does not explain a change in behaviour between yesterday and today. The echo theory is a real failure in speech-to-speech systems with poor echo cancellation, and the deploy notes mention barge-in, so it is the first thing many engineers check; the setting did not change and the agent was silent at each cut.

**What to collect.** Run a local VAD over the eight recordings (and a few hundred more) at 0.5 and 0.75 to see which frames flip from speech to silence; tag every turn with device type so the cut-off rate can be split by speakerphone and handset; and recover the false barge-in rate from before the deploy from logs of agent responses cancelled within the first second.

**What to do without it.** Revert the threshold now, as a single change, and keep the 500 ms window for a day with cut-offs tracked by device. Then decide the window with data.

### Two defensible rollbacks

**Revert both lines (safest).** Restores last week's behaviour in one step. Cost: the false barge-ins in noisy call centres come back, the faster turns go away, and you learn nothing about which line did what.

**Revert the threshold only (recommended).** Fixes the largest share of the cut-offs and keeps the latency gain from the shorter window while you measure it. Cost: the digit-group cut-offs continue for a day, on a flow (account numbers) where a cut-off means a wrong lookup.

The conflict is background-noise robustness against quiet speakers: the threshold was raised to stop noise interrupting the agent, and the same knob now drops quiet callers. A single global threshold cannot satisfy both; the durable fix separates them.

**Failure mode of the recommendation.** Reverting the threshold brings back false barge-ins in noisy environments, which is why it was changed. If the noisy-centre traffic is large, the support queue swaps one complaint for another. The durable options are a per-channel threshold, a turn model that judges completeness after the VAD fires (Smart Turn runs after a VAD such as Silero detects silence), or `semantic_vad` with `eagerness: low` for flows where callers read numbers.

## Follow-ups

- *The noisy-centre customers are your largest account and they liked yesterday's change. Who decides the threshold, and on what numbers?*
- *What would you need to see in a week of per-device data to move from server_vad to semantic_vad?*
- *How do you make the next turn-detection change safe to ship: what runs before the deploy, and what triggers an automatic rollback?*

## Common mistakes

- Blaming the silence window because it is the change most obviously about turn ends.
- Reverting both lines and declaring the incident closed without learning which one mattered.
- Treating "switch to semantic VAD" as the incident fix rather than the follow-up project.
- Measuring cut-offs without splitting by device or by the flow the caller was in.
