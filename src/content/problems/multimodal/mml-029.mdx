## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Take Movie Gen's published settings. How much transformer compute does one 16-second clip cost, what dominates it, and why does the paper's latent compression decide whether this is feasible at all?"
</Callout>

Published inputs (Movie Gen paper, v2 of 2025-02-26):

- Transformer: 30B parameters, 48 layers, model dimension 6,144.
- Output: 16 seconds at 16 fps, 768 × 768 before a separate upsampler.
- Temporal autoencoder: 8× compression in time, height and width.
- Patches: 2 × 2 spatial, none in time.
- Sampling: 50 steps; text guidance at 7.5 in the paper's comparisons.

Give the transformer's compute for one clip, in PFLOP. Leave out the text encoders, the autoencoder and the upsampler. You choose how to count a forward pass; say what you counted.

Then: how many tokens would the same clip be if the transformer worked on 2 × 2 pixel patches with no autoencoder, and what would that do to the attention term?

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>73,728 tokens per clip. Per forward pass about 4.4 PFLOP of weight multiplies and 6.4 PFLOP of attention, 10.8 PFLOP in all; 50 steps with two guidance passes each make 100 passes, about 1,080 PFLOP, roughly one exaFLOP. Attention is the dominant term because 73,728 tokens is twelve times the model dimension. Without the autoencoder the clip would be 37.7M tokens, 512 times more, and attention 262,144 times more: infeasible, which is why the compression ratio is the feasibility decision.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Tokens.</strong> 256 frames / 8 = 32 latent frames. 768 / 8 = 96 latent pixels per side, / 2 = 48 patches per side, 2,304 per frame. 32 × 2,304 = 73,728.</p>
<p><strong>One forward pass.</strong> Weight multiplies: 2 FLOP per parameter per token, 2 × 30 × 10⁹ × 73,728 ≈ 4.42 PFLOP. Attention: per layer, scores (n² × d multiply-adds) and the weighted sum of values (the same again), so 4 × n² × d FLOP; over 48 layers, 4 × 48 × 73,728² × 6,144 ≈ 6.41 PFLOP. Total ≈ 10.84 PFLOP.</p>
<p><strong>The clip.</strong> 50 steps × 2 passes × 10.84 ≈ 1,084 PFLOP.</p>
<p><strong>Without the autoencoder.</strong> 768 / 2 = 384 patches per side, 147,456 per frame, × 256 frames = 37.7M tokens, 512 times more (8 × 8 × 8). Weight multiplies grow 512 times; attention grows 512² = 262,144 times, to about 1.7 × 10⁶ PFLOP per pass. No fleet runs 100 of those per clip.</p>
</Fragment>

### Reasonable and unreasonable choices

The band of 700 to 1,500 PFLOP admits these:

- **Counting parameters.** Using all 30B in the weight term (4.42 PFLOP) or only the self-attention and feed-forward weights. With FFN dimension 16,384 and three FFN matrices, those are 48 × (4 × 6,144² + 3 × 6,144 × 16,384) ≈ 21.7B, giving 3.2 PFLOP and a clip of about 960 PFLOP. Both are defensible; the cross-attention to text tokens sits between them.
- **Attention constant.** 4 × n² × d is the multiply-add count for scores and values; adding softmax and masking moves it by a few percent.
- **Guidance on every step.** The paper's comparisons use guidance; pricing a deployed variant that skips guidance on some steps is a different setting and should say so.

Outside the band, and why:

- **Weight term only** (about 440 PFLOP): the error the rule "2 × parameters × tokens" invites. At 73,728 tokens attention is the larger term, so this understates the clip by more than half.
- **No guidance** (about 540 PFLOP): prices a setting the paper did not report.
- **One pass per clip**: treating the generator like a single forward pass of a classifier; it misses the step loop, a factor of 100.

### The dominant term and what flips it

Attention dominates when the token count is large next to the model dimension. For a standard block with a feed-forward width of 4d, the weight term is about 2 × 12 × L × d² × n and the attention term 4 × L × n² × d, so their ratio is n / 6d. Here 6d is 36,864 and n is 73,728, a ratio of 2 by that rule; with Movie Gen's actual 30B parameters, which are more than 12d² per layer, the computed ratio is 6.41 / 4.42 ≈ 1.45. Halve the clip to 8 seconds (36,864 tokens) and the weights lead: about 2.2 PFLOP against 1.6 per pass. A product of short clips is weight-bound; a product of long clips is attention-bound, and that decides whether attention kernels or model size are the place to spend engineering.

## Common mistakes

- Using 2 × parameters × tokens and stopping, which misses the larger term.
- Forgetting the guidance pass, or the step loop altogether.
- Computing tokens from 1080p, the upsampler's output, instead of the 768 px the transformer generates.
- Reporting one number without saying which parameters and which attention constant were counted, so nobody can reconcile it with the next estimate.
