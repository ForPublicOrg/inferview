## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"We have a 32K open-weight model fine-tuned for our domain. Product wants 128K in one quarter. What are the options, what does each cost, and how would you know it worked?"
</Callout>

Scenario constraints: one quarter, a training budget set by finance that you have to ask about, the domain fine-tune must not regress on the prompts that are under 32K today (most of traffic), and the long-context use is reading whole case files and answering questions that need facts from several places in them.

Name the options, pick one, say what you would measure before and after, and choose the number of training tokens or steps you would budget, with your reasoning.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether the serving engine can apply a scaling factor per request or only once at load time.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask for the length distribution of case files and of the questions' evidence: how far apart the facts sit.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask whether long documents from the domain exist in enough volume to train on, and who has to approve using them.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Four options, in rising cost: training-free YaRN at factor 4; YaRN or Position Interpolation plus a short fine-tune on long documents; a LongRoPE-style search for per-dimension factors with a short-context readjustment; continued training at long length. With one quarter, the second is the default: YaRN's paper reports reaching its extension with 400 steps where earlier methods needed more. The test is not perplexity and never a needle sweep: it is a paired regression on today's short prompts plus multi-hop and aggregation tasks built from case files at 32K, 64K, 96K and 128K.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Training-free YaRN.</b> Add a <code>rope_scaling</code> block with factor 4 from 32,768, the configuration the Qwen3 card documents. Cost: no training, one config change. Risk: frameworks apply it statically, and the card warns that this may hurt shorter texts, so it is only free if short traffic is served separately or the regression is measured and accepted.</p>
<p><b>Position Interpolation or YaRN, then a short fine-tune.</b> PI divides every position by the factor and fine-tunes; its paper extended LLaMA models to 32,768 within 1,000 steps. PI compresses the high-frequency pairs too, which costs the local resolution that YaRN's per-frequency ramp keeps. YaRN's paper fine-tuned its factor-16 Llama 2 models for 400 steps at batch size 64 on 64k-token segments. That is 400 × 64 × 65,536 ≈ 1.7 billion tokens, a figure to put in front of finance as an order of magnitude for a run of that shape, not a quote for your model. Cost: long training data from your domain, a fine-tuning run that can disturb the domain fine-tune, and a re-run of the domain evals.</p>
<p><b>LongRoPE-style search.</b> Search per-dimension interpolation factors; the paper reports an 8x extension without fine-tuning from the search alone, fine-tuning of up to 1,000 steps at 256k, and a readjustment at 8k to recover the short window. Cost: the search itself, a non-standard scaling table that every engine you deploy to has to support, and more engineering than one quarter comfortably holds.</p>
<p><b>Continued training at long length.</b> The most data and compute: attention work per sequence grows with the square of length, so a 128K sequence costs 16 times the attention of a 32K one for 4 times the tokens. Cost: it is a training project, not a configuration change, and it is the one that most risks the domain fine-tune. The Llama 3.1 8B config shows why the config alone tells you little about this option: its <code>llama3</code> block has factor 8 from 8,192 original positions, which is 65,536, yet <code>max_position_embeddings</code> is 131,072. Whatever closes that gap is training the config does not record.</p>
<p><b>The decision.</b> YaRN plus a short fine-tune, with a mix of long case files and short domain prompts in the training data, so the run does not trade one for the other. The number to budget is the step count: start from a run of the shape the YaRN paper used, a few hundred steps, and stop on the eval, not on the budget. A budget an order of magnitude larger is not justified until the short run's eval says where the remaining loss is.</p>
<p><b>The test, the same for every option.</b> (1) A paired comparison on today's short prompts, item by item, before and after, with a written threshold for an acceptable regression. (2) Probes built from case files: a fact stated in different words from the question, a chain of two or three facts, a count or aggregation over the file, each at 32K, 64K, 96K and 128K, reported as score by length. (3) The customer task at the lengths it arrives at. A needle sweep runs as a smoke test for a broken configuration and is never reported as evidence; HELMET found that needle results do not reliably predict downstream performance.</p>
<p><b>How the recommendation fails.</b> The fine-tune recovers the long probes and quietly shifts the domain behaviour: style, refusal rate or format adherence on short prompts, which a single accuracy number does not show. The paired short-prompt set has to include the format and refusal checks the domain fine-tune was accepted on, or the regression ships with a passing score.</p>
</Fragment>

<li>The 128K probes pass at 96K and fail at 128K. Do you launch at 96K, keep training, or launch at 128K with a warning, and what decides it?</li>
<li>What would you need to see in the first month of production to drop the fine-tune and go back to training-free YaRN on a separate pool?</li>
</ul>

**A worked comparison of the scaling choices at factor 4.** With head dimension 128 and a base of 10,000 trained on 4,096 positions (the Llama 2 shape in the module's widget), Position Interpolation shrinks the per-token step of all 46 pairs that complete a full turn inside the trained window, while YaRN's ramp shrinks 25 and leaves the 21 fastest pairs alone. A raised base without fine-tuning (the NTK-aware change) pushes 17 of the 18 slow pairs past the angles they saw in training. Those counts come from the formulas in the YaRN paper and are computed in the widget in the lesson; they explain why PI needs fine-tuning to restore local resolution and why the base change needs a larger nominal factor than the extension it delivers.

## Follow-ups

- The engine you deploy to supports static YaRN only. Product also wants the short-prompt latency unchanged. What do you serve?
- Two senior engineers disagree: one wants continued training because "it is the only thing that really works", the other wants training-free YaRN this week. How do you decide without deciding by rank?
- Six weeks in, a newer base model ships with 128K native. What evidence makes you abandon the extension work, and what do you keep from it?

## Common mistakes

- Reporting a needle heatmap or long-document perplexity as the acceptance test.
- Fine-tuning on long documents only, so the short domain behaviour drifts.
- Treating the `rope_scaling` factor as the whole story of a model's long-context training.
- Picking LongRoPE for one quarter without checking that the serving engine supports its per-dimension factors.
- Comparing short-prompt scores unpaired, so a real regression hides in the noise.
