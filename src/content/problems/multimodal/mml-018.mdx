## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Resume deep dive, as a candidate reported it">
"Take the vision-language project on your resume. Walk me through it end to end: the problem, the encoder and the language model, where fusion happens, what you froze, your data and how you kept evaluation clean, latency, and what failed."
</Callout>

Then the interviewer pushes back: "Why not just run OCR and send the text to a language model? That would have been cheaper and easier to debug."

Prepare the account and the answer to the pushback. If you have no VLM project, use the closest multimodal or document-understanding system you have shipped, and say so.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Before the round, find the accuracy you measured for the text-only or OCR baseline, on the same evaluation set as the VLM.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Find out how your evaluation images were split from your training images: by image, by document, by customer or by time.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask which part of the latency the interviewer cares about: time to first token, total time, or the cost per request.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>The account names the decision the model served, the baseline it had to beat, and the architecture with a reason for each frozen part: for example a frozen CLIP-family encoder because the labelled data was thousands of examples, not millions, a projector trained first, then adapters on the language model. It names how leakage was prevented (split by customer or by time, not by image) and the number that moved, against the OCR baseline on the same set. It gives latency split by stage and one failure found in production with what changed afterwards. The answer to "why not OCR" is the measured gap on the questions OCR cannot answer, or a concession that for text-only questions OCR is the better system.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Problem and baseline.</strong> One sentence on the decision and who used it; the baseline that existed before (OCR plus a text model, a classifier, people) and its measured accuracy.</p>
<p><strong>Architecture and fusion.</strong> Encoder, projector, language model, and where image features enter: as tokens in the language model's input (LLaVA-style), or through a separate path. Why that encoder: resolution, licence, what it was pretrained on.</p>
<p><strong>What was frozen, and why.</strong> The recipe with a reason per stage. LLaVA trains only the projector first, then the projector and language model with the encoder frozen; Qwen2.5-VL trains the encoder alone first, then everything, then freezes the encoder for post-training. Your recipe should be one of these or a stated departure, with the cost of the departure.</p>
<p><strong>Data and leakage.</strong> The split unit and why. Near-duplicate images, the same form template, the same customer's documents and the same video across train and test are the usual leaks; each inflates accuracy in a way production does not repeat.</p>
<p><strong>Latency.</strong> Encoder time per image, prefill over visual tokens, decode; which dominated and what you changed.</p>
<p><strong>Failure.</strong> One production failure, how it surfaced, the diagnosis and the change.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>How do you know the model used the image?</em> A counterfactual run with images swapped or blanked on image-dependent questions, and the change rate.</li>
<li><em>Which layer's features did you take from the encoder, and did you test the alternative?</em> LLaVA reports the penultimate layer beating the last on ScienceQA, 90.92% against 89.96%.</li>
<li><em>What would you do differently with ten times the labelled data?</em></li>
</ul>

### The decision under pushback

Two defensible answers to "why not OCR", each with a cost.

**Defend the VLM with the measured gap.** Show accuracy on the same evaluation set for OCR plus a text model and for the VLM, split by question type: layout (which box is the total), visual (is the signature present, is the stamp red), and text-only (what is the invoice number). The VLM is justified by the first two buckets. The cost: if you did not run the baseline on the same set, you cannot make this argument honestly, and saying so is better than estimating.

**Concede and propose a hybrid.** For text-only fields OCR is cheaper, deterministic and easier to debug, and a hybrid that routes text fields to OCR and layout or visual questions to the VLM is often the better system. The cost: two pipelines to maintain and a router whose mistakes send a question to the system that cannot answer it, and the original project's architecture now reads as over-built.

**Where the defence fails.** A gap measured on an evaluation set split by image, when the same templates appear in training, can be leakage, not capability. If your split was by image, say so before the interviewer finds it, and give the number you would trust: the gap on documents from customers or months the model never saw.

### Signals to rate your answer against

- The decision the model served and the baseline it beat, with both numbers on the same set.
- A reason for each frozen or trained part, and its cost.
- The split unit for evaluation, and the leak it prevents.
- Latency split by stage, with the dominant stage named.
- One production failure, the diagnosis and the change.
- A choice between defending and conceding under pushback, made on evidence.

## Follow-ups

- *Your VLM costs four times the OCR pipeline per document and beats it on a fifth of the questions. Which documents keep the VLM, and what accuracy do you accept in writing for the rest?*
- *What would you need to see in production to replace the VLM with OCR entirely?*
- *Your manager and the research lead disagree on whether to unfreeze the encoder for the next version. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Agree on the evaluation bucket that would move if the encoder were the limit, such as small-text fields at native resolution, measure the frozen encoder there first, and unfreeze only if that bucket is where the errors are.

## Common mistakes

- Describing the architecture from the paper rather than the system you built, so the first "why" has no answer.
- Quoting an accuracy number without the baseline on the same set.
- Splitting train and test by image when the same templates or customers appear on both sides.
- Answering the latency question with a total and no breakdown.
- Treating the OCR pushback as an attack instead of a design question with a measurable answer.
