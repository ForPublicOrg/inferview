## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Short prompts got worse after the 128K launch. Find out why, and tell me how we serve both."
</Callout>

All numbers in this question are scenario. A team self-hosts Qwen3-8B, whose model card gives a native context of 32,768 tokens. A contract-review customer needs documents of 40K to 120K tokens, so last Tuesday's deploy changed two files:

```diff
 # config.json
   "model_type": "qwen3",
+  "rope_scaling": {
+    "rope_type": "yarn",
+    "factor": 4.0,
+    "original_max_position_embeddings": 32768
+  },
   "torch_dtype": "bfloat16",

 # serve.yaml
-  max_model_len: 32768
+  max_model_len: 131072
-  kv_cache_dtype: auto
+  kv_cache_dtype: fp8      # fits 128K on the same GPUs
```

The offline evals, run before and after on the same items with greedy decoding:

| Set | Prompt length | Items | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Support answers | under 2K | 400 | 81.0% | 76.5% |
| Policy extraction | 6K to 12K | 250 | 77.2% | 74.8% |
| Contract review | 40K to 120K | 120 | not served | 58.3% |
| Needle sweep | 4K to 128K | 99 cells | not run | 99 of 99 |

Since Tuesday, thumbs-down on support answers rose from 4.1% to 5.6% of rated answers. One replica, restarted by mistake with the new `config.json` and the old `serve.yaml`, took about 5% of traffic for a day; its thumbs-down rate on support answers was 5.4%, on far fewer ratings. 94% of production requests are under 8K tokens. You do not have per-item results, an ablation of each change, or the engine's documentation on how it applies `rope_scaling`.

Pick the change that best explains the short-prompt regression, rank the others, say what you would collect and what you would ship before you have it, and decide how this deployment should serve 2K and 120K prompts. The contract customer goes live in ten days.

## Answer

The `rope_scaling` block is the best explanation. Every notable open-source framework implements static YaRN, according to the Qwen3 model card, so the factor stays 4 whatever the input length, and the card warns that this may hurt shorter texts and advises adding the block only when long contexts are needed. A 1,500-token support prompt now runs on frequencies stretched for 131,072 positions, plus the attention temperature that goes with them. The accidental replica is the closest thing to an ablation in the evidence: it had the block and not the FP8 cache, and its thumbs-down rate moved with the fleet.

**Ranking the others.**

1. *The FP8 cache* is real and secondary. It changes every cached key and value at every length and may cost accuracy on its own, which nothing here measures. It ranks second because the replica without it still regressed, and first among the things to ablate, because it was added only to make 128K fit and the routing decision below may remove the need for it on short traffic.
2. *The batch-size change* is a latency and throughput problem. Preemption delays a request; it does not change which tokens the model computes beyond batch-dependent numerics.
3. *The missing temperature* is not a defect. When the block does not set `attention_factor`, transformers computes it from the factor as 0.1 ln 4 + 1 ≈ 1.139, which multiplies the logits by about 1.296 through the scaled cos and sin. Check that the engine does the same, and the problem disappears.

**How strong is the evidence?** The 4.5-point drop on 400 items is smaller than it looks if it is read as unpaired: the standard error of the difference is √(0.81 × 0.19 / 400 + 0.765 × 0.235 / 400) ≈ 2.9 points, so the drop is about 1.6 standard errors. The per-item results allow a paired count of items that flipped each way, which is the test to run. The replica's rate rests on about a day of 5% of ratings. The needle sweep says nothing about contract review: HELMET reports that needle results do not reliably predict downstream performance, and 99 of 99 is the expected result for a model that can match a literal string.

**What to collect.** The paired flips on the 400 support items for three configurations: block off and FP8 off, block on and FP8 off, block off and FP8 on. That is three runs of an eval that already exists. Also the engine's handling of `rope_scaling`, and the contract customer's prompt-plus-output lengths.

**What to ship before you have it.** Move short traffic back to the old configuration today: a second deployment with no `rope_scaling` block and the default cache dtype, taking every request whose prompt plus maximum output fits in 32,768 tokens. Nothing about the contract launch depends on short traffic sharing its replicas.

<p><b>Option A, two deployments routed by length.</b> Short traffic gets the model it was evaluated on; long traffic gets YaRN. Cost: two fleets to size, deploy and monitor, a long pool that serves a small share of requests and idles between them, and a prefix cache that is not shared between pools. A conversation that grows past the threshold moves pools and pays its prefill again.</p>
<p><b>Option B, one deployment with YaRN on everywhere.</b> One fleet, one cache. Cost: whatever the paired ablation shows on 94% of traffic, accepted in writing. If the paired result is within noise, this is the cheaper answer and two pools are overhead.</p>
<p><b>Option C, factor 2 everywhere</b>, which the Qwen card suggests when typical length is 65,536. Cost: contracts above 64K need chunking or retrieval, which the ten-day deadline does not allow.</p>
<p><b>Option D, a hosted endpoint with dynamic YaRN</b>, which the card says Alibaba Model Studio supports by default. Cost: contract documents leave your infrastructure, which the customer's agreement may not permit.</p>
<p>Recommended: A now, B later if the ablation shows no paired regression. The quantity to choose is the routing threshold: route on prompt tokens plus the request's maximum output tokens, not on prompt tokens, and set it at 32,768 less a margin for the chat template.</p>
<p><b>How A fails.</b> A router that counts only the prompt sends a 31K prompt with a 4K output budget to the native pool, which then generates past its trained length with no scaling at all. The symptom is degraded endings on long answers in the short pool, which no short-prompt eval covers. Count with the serving tokenizer, include the template, and reject or reroute at the router, never in the engine.</p>

## Follow-ups

- Two senior engineers disagree: one wants dynamic YaRN patched into the engine, the other wants two pools. How do you decide without deciding by rank, given ten days?

## Common mistakes

- Taking 99 of 99 on the needle sweep as evidence that contract review works.
- Reading a 4.5-point drop on 400 items as significant without pairing the items.
- Reverting both changes, which ships no contract support ten days before the launch.
- Blaming FP8 because it is the change with a comment and an obvious precision story.
- Routing on prompt length alone, so long outputs from short-pool requests run past the native window.
