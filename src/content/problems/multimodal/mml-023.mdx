## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Concept, 15 minutes">
"We run a support line where most turns call a backend tool: order status, address change, cancellations. Should the voice layer be a cascaded recognizer, LLM and TTS pipeline or a native speech-to-speech model? Give me the trade-offs that matter for this line, not in general."
</Callout>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>For a tool-heavy support line, cascaded, unless calls are short and nothing needs checking before it is spoken. Speech-to-speech's latency lead is largest on turns without tools, and here tools set the latency in both designs. The cascaded pipeline gives a text stage for policy checks and a transcript of both sides, lets the team replace one stage at a time, and avoids audio-token pricing that grows with every turn of a long call. What it gives up is natural turn-taking.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Latency.</strong> Moshi's paper reports 160 ms theoretical and 200 ms practical latency; the Pipecat primer measures a cascaded stack at 1,293 ms. That gap is real on turns without tools. On a tool turn, the tool's latency and the model's reasoning about its result are paid in both designs, so the gap shrinks to the stages a speech model removes (endpointing, sentence aggregation, TTS time to first byte), a few hundred milliseconds against a turn already over a second.</p>
<p><strong>Control.</strong> OpenAI's guide frames the chained pipeline as the choice when you need to inspect or transform intermediate text, for example to store the transcript and run policy checks before speech is generated. A cancellation policy that must be read back exactly, or an address that must be confirmed in a fixed format, is easier to enforce in text.</p>
<p><strong>Cost.</strong> OpenAI counts user audio at 1 token per 100 ms and assistant audio at 1 per 50 ms, so a minute of agent speech is 1,200 output tokens, $0.077 at gpt-realtime-2.1's $64 per million audio output tokens, before the growing history is billed as input on later turns. A support call of several minutes pays for its history repeatedly unless it is cached. A cascaded pipeline pays per audio minute for recognition and per character for synthesis, which do not grow with history.</p>
<p><strong>Turn-taking.</strong> A speech model that hears both channels handles interjections ("mm-hm", "wait") as audio; a cascaded pipeline treats every one as a possible new turn and needs a turn detector and cancellation. This is the cascaded design's main weakness and the reason to revisit the decision.</p>
<p><strong>Failure mode of the recommendation.</strong> A cascaded pipeline built quickly ends up with every latency term in series (full-reply waits, silence-only endpointing, synchronous tools), and the result is slower than the architecture needs to be. The recommendation assumes streaming TTS and a modern turn detector; without them, a speech-to-speech model would beat it on every metric callers notice.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Calls average 90 seconds and there is no pre-speech compliance check. Does your answer change?</em> Yes: short calls remove the cost argument and no check removes the control argument, leaving turn-taking, where speech-to-speech is better.</li>
<li><em>How would you run a fair comparison in a pilot?</em> Same tools and prompts, a slice of traffic on each, and outcomes per call (resolution, transfers to a human, cut-offs) rather than latency alone.</li>
<li><em>What is the middle option?</em> A full-duplex voice layer in front of the existing text agent, which keeps text control for tools and policy while improving turn-taking, at the cost of two systems and a seam between them.</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

The arithmetic for agent speech: 60 seconds at 1 token per 50 ms is 1,200 tokens; 1,200 × $64 / 1,000,000 = $0.0768.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing best-case speech-to-speech latency on a chit-chat turn with a cascaded tool turn.
- Treating the provider's transcript of a speech-to-speech session as equivalent to a pre-speech policy check.
- Pricing speech-to-speech per minute of agent audio only, without the history re-billed on each turn.
