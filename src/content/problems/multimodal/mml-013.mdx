## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design round, 45 minutes">
"Design a video vision-language model end to end: data, architecture, training objectives, evaluation, and serving."
</Callout>

The constraints for this version, as a scenario. The product answers questions about recorded screen-share meetings of up to 60 minutes: "when did they open the billing settings?", "what error appeared after the deploy?". Answers must cite timestamps. The team is six engineers for two quarters, the training budget is fixed at what one 8-GPU node does in that time, and the product owner wants the first answer within 10 seconds of the question for a video uploaded at least an hour earlier. Recordings are customer data and cannot be sent to a third-party API without a contract amendment that legal estimates at one quarter.

Design it, choose the numbers you need, and say what you give up.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask what share of questions can be answered from the meeting's audio transcript alone.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask how many questions a typical recording receives, and how soon after upload the first one arrives.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask which screen text the answers depend on and how large it is in the recorded frames.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Index then answer, not one long pass. At upload, sample frames adaptively (more on scene changes), encode them once with a frozen pretrained encoder, transcribe the audio, and store per-segment visual features, OCR text and transcript with absolute timestamps. At question time, retrieve the few relevant segments and run a fine-tuned open VLM with time-aligned position encoding over those segments at full resolution, answering with timestamps. Train with a frozen encoder, a projector and adapters on the language model, on data filtered so that questions need the frames. Evaluate on visual-only questions with timestamp accuracy, never on needle tests.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Data.</strong> Internal recordings with consent, transcribed. Generate question-answer pairs from transcripts and OCR with a text model, LLaVA-style, then delete every pair a text-only model answers correctly from the transcript: otherwise the model learns that the frames are optional. Hand-label a few hundred questions per month for evaluation, stratified by question type.</p>
<p><strong>Architecture.</strong> A pretrained dynamic-resolution encoder (Qwen2.5-VL's is the published reference) because screen text needs native resolution; window attention keeps the encoder affordable at screen sizes. Time enters as absolute timestamps in the position encoding, as Qwen2.5-VL does with MRoPE, so variable frame rates remain comparable. Retrieval runs over segment embeddings plus transcript and OCR text.</p>
<p><strong>Objectives.</strong> Stage 1 trains the projector only. Stage 2 adds adapters on the language model for answering with cited timestamps. The encoder stays frozen: one node for two quarters does not cover encoder training, and the published recipes freeze it in post-training anyway.</p>
<p><strong>Evaluation.</strong> Accuracy on the visual-only question set; timestamp error in seconds; a frame-shuffle and blank-frame counterfactual that should drop accuracy sharply; retrieval recall of the segment containing the answer. Needle-in-a-video scores are excluded: HELMET found needle-style synthetic tasks do not reliably predict downstream performance.</p>
<p><strong>Serving.</strong> Encoding happens at upload, asynchronously, so the 10-second budget covers retrieval, prefill over a few segments and decoding, not encoding an hour of video.</p>
</Fragment>

### Two defensible designs

**Index then answer (recommended).** Encode once at upload, retrieve segments per question, run the VLM on a few segments at full resolution. Cost: a retrieval stage whose misses are unrecoverable, because the model never sees a segment the index did not return; storage for per-segment features; and questions that need the whole meeting ("how many times did they deploy?") must be answered by aggregating over segments, not by the model reading everything.

**One long pass.** Sample the whole video into one context and let the model attend over all of it. Cost: tokens. At 1 fps a 60-minute video is 3,600 frames. At Qwen2.5-VL's evaluation cap of 768 frames and 24,576 video tokens, each frame gets 24,576 / 768 = 32 tokens and frames are 3,600 / 768, about 4.7 s apart, too coarse for a dialog visible for two seconds and too few tokens per frame to read screen text. At full screen resolution the arithmetic fails outright: a 1344 × 756 frame is 48 × 27 = 1,296 tokens in that model, so 3,600 frames would be 4,665,600 tokens. For comparison, Gemini's documentation prices video at 263 tokens per second for static processing, 946,800 tokens for an hour, which is also why this design wins only for short videos or coarse questions. It is simpler, has no retrieval misses, and handles whole-meeting questions directly.

### Choose the numbers

- **Frame sampling.** 1 fps as the floor plus extra frames on scene change is defensible for screen recordings, where most seconds are identical. Uniform 0.2 fps misses short dialogs; uniform 4 fps quadruples encoding for mostly duplicate frames.
- **Segments per question.** 3 to 8 segments of 10 to 30 s each. Fewer raises the retrieval-miss rate; more pushes prefill toward the 10-second budget, which you must measure on the target GPU, not assume.
- **Frame resolution at answer time.** Native or near it for screen text; the decision follows from the text-size measurement in hint 3, not from a default.

### The decision memo, written down

1. Weeks 1 to 4: transcript-only baseline and the visual-only evaluation set; measure the share of questions the transcript answers.
2. Weeks 5 to 12: indexing pipeline at upload; projector and adapter training; retrieval recall measured on the evaluation set.
3. Weeks 13 to 20: internal beta on staff recordings; rollback triggers: timestamp error above the agreed tolerance on more than an agreed share of answers, any answer citing a timestamp outside the video, or counterfactual tests showing accuracy unchanged when frames are blanked.
4. Weeks 21 to 26: customer rollout behind a flag, with the transcript-only answer as fallback.

**What you give up:** whole-meeting questions answered by one model pass, encoder fine-tuning on screen text, and the simplicity of a single model call. The transcript-only fallback means some answers will be text-grounded by design, and the product must say so.

### Where this fails

The recommended design fails silently on retrieval misses: the VLM answers confidently from the wrong segment and cites a real timestamp, which looks more trustworthy than an uncited answer. Retrieval recall must be measured continuously on sampled production questions, and an answer whose cited segment scores low against the question should be shown with lower confidence or not at all. A second failure: if the question generator and the transcript share vocabulary with the questions users ask, the text-only filter removes exactly the questions users care about. Check the filtered set's question types against a sample of real questions.

## Follow-ups

- *Your evaluation set costs a labeller three weeks a quarter and the team is six people. Which half do you cut, and what risk do you accept in writing?*
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one wants to fine-tune the encoder on screen text, the other says the node cannot afford it. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Agree on the measurement that settles it first, such as accuracy on questions whose answer is screen text below a stated size, with the frozen encoder at native resolution; if that bucket meets its target, the encoder stays frozen.
- *Six months after launch, half the questions are about whole meetings ("summarise every decision"). What would you need to see to move to the long-pass design?*

## Common mistakes

- Sending the whole video at a fixed frame rate without computing the tokens, then discovering the frame budget leaves 32 tokens per frame.
- Generating training questions from transcripts and never filtering out the ones the transcript alone answers.
- Evaluating long-video capability with needle tests.
- Encoding video at question time, which puts an hour of encoding inside a 10-second budget.
- Using frame index instead of absolute time, so answers drift whenever the sampling rate changes.
- Treating "end to end" as "one model": the system includes ASR, OCR, retrieval and a fallback.
