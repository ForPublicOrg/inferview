## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Explain how a masked diffusion language model produces an answer, and then tell me what changes for three things our platform relies on today: token streaming to the browser, prefix caching of a long system prompt, and grammar-constrained JSON output."
</Callout>

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether the model's attention is causal, and what that means for reusing anything computed on an earlier step.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask whether the sampler generates the whole answer at once or in blocks, and how long a block is.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask the vendor how their structured-output mode is implemented, and request its invalid-output rate on your schemas.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>The answer region starts fully masked. Each step, a bidirectional Transformer predicts every masked position; the sampler keeps the most confident predictions and remasks the rest, and after a fixed number of steps the answer is complete. Streaming becomes block-wise at best, because a position is not final until the sampler keeps it. Prefix caching has no direct equivalent: LLaDA's paper says the model is incompatible with KV caching, since without a causal mask every position's representation depends on the current answer. Left-to-right grammar masking no longer applies, because there is no single "next token".</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Generation.</b> LLaDA trains a Transformer without a causal mask to predict tokens masked at a random ratio. At inference, steps and answer length are set up front. With low-confidence remasking, each step keeps the predictions the model is surest of. Block sampling splits the answer into blocks generated left to right with diffusion inside each, which LLaDA supports without retraining.</p>
<p><b>Compute.</b> Each step processes the prompt and the whole answer region. With a 1,000-token prompt, a 256-token answer and 64 steps, that is 64 × 1,256 = 80,384 token-positions, against 1,256 for an autoregressive model with a cache, in 64 sequential passes instead of 257. At batch 1, where autoregressive decode is limited by weight reads, that trade can win, and LLaDA reports 1.5 and 1.8 times LLaMA3 8B's throughput on GSM8K and Math under those conditions. At high batch the extra compute counts against it.</p>
<p><b>Streaming.</b> Without blocks, the first position may be kept late, so a position-ordered stream waits. With blocks, nothing in a block is shown until it is done: time to first visible token is time to the first block, and text arrives in bursts. If a sampler can revise kept tokens, the server must hold output until final or the client must handle edits.</p>
<p><b>Prefix caching.</b> Every step recomputes the prompt's representations, so a long system prompt costs its full length on every step of every request unless the vendor has a scheme it documents. Price it that way until shown otherwise.</p>
<p><b>Constrained output.</b> Grammar-constrained decoding masks the next token from a parser state built left to right. Out-of-order filling has no such state. Options are per-block validation, a vendor mode built for it, or generate, validate and retry, each with a different invalid rate and latency cost.</p>
<p>Failure mode of adopting it on throughput numbers: the published figures are per-request output speed at low concurrency, so a platform at high concurrency with long cached prompts can see higher cost per request and no latency gain.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li>A block length of 32 tokens gives a smooth stream, but the vendor says 128 halves the cost. Who chooses, and what metric settles it?</li>
<li>Your JSON tool calls need a 99.9% valid rate. What would you measure in a one-week trial before believing the vendor's structured-output mode meets it?</li>
<li>What evidence would make you treat diffusion models as a drop-in replacement for your autoregressive endpoint?</li>
</ul>

## Follow-ups

- How does a fixed generation length interact with answers that are much shorter than the allocated region?
- What changes in your rate limiter and billing if a request's compute depends on steps rather than output tokens?

## Common mistakes

- Describing diffusion LMs as "generating all tokens in one pass". They take many passes, each over the whole answer region.
- Assuming a KV cache still applies because the network is a Transformer; the causal mask, not the architecture, is what makes caching valid.
- Comparing per-request tokens per second at batch 1 with a production server's throughput at high concurrency.
- Treating block-wise output as equivalent to token streaming for user experience.
