## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"What does the classifier-free guidance scale trade, and how would you choose it for a product?"
</Callout>

Answer for a text-to-image feature in a design tool, where users generate four variations of one prompt and pick one.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask how users choose among the four variations, and what the product team counts as a failed generation.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask which convention the serving code uses for the guidance value, and at what value research signed the model off.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Guidance extrapolates from the unconditional prediction toward the conditional one. Raising it buys adherence to the prompt and per-sample fidelity and costs diversity; past a point it also costs realism: the paper notes saturated colours in strongly guided samples (w = 3.0). It does not cost compute as the value changes: any guided setting runs two passes per step. For four variations users choose among, diversity is part of the product, so I would sweep a range on the product's prompts and pick the lowest value that holds adherence, measured on adherence and on spread across the four images, not on one quality score.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>The mechanism.</strong> Training drops the condition for a fraction of examples (Ho and Salimans found 0.1 and 0.2 equally good, 0.5 worse), so one model predicts both with and without the prompt. At sampling, the guided estimate is (1 + w) times the conditional prediction minus w times the unconditional one: it pushes each step further in the direction the prompt adds.</p>
<p><strong>The trade, in the paper's numbers.</strong> On ImageNet 128 × 128 at 256 steps: unguided FID 7.27 and Inception score 82; at w = 0.3 FID is best at 2.43 with IS 158; at w = 1.0 FID is back to 7.86 while IS reaches 298; at w = 4.0 FID is 21.53 and IS 421. FID measures match to the data distribution, including its variety; IS rewards confident, class-typical images. Guidance moves along that frontier: more typical, less varied.</p>
<p><strong>Conventions.</strong> The paper's w = 0 means no guidance. Most libraries expose s = 1 + w, where 1 means none: DiT's FID 2.27 is at s = 1.5 (w = 0.5), latent diffusion's text-to-image figures use s = 10, Movie Gen uses 7.5. Check which one your serving code uses before comparing anything.</p>
<p><strong>Choosing it.</strong> A fixed prompt set from the product's traffic, four seeds per prompt, a sweep over the scale (for a text-to-image model in the s convention, something like 3 to 9 in steps of 1.5). Measure adherence per image (an automated text-image score plus human spot checks) and diversity per prompt (mean pairwise distance between the four images in an embedding space). Pick the lowest scale whose adherence is within an agreed margin of the best. In this product, diversity is what makes four variations worth showing; near-identical variations are a failed generation even if each is faithful.</p>
<p><strong>Where it fails.</strong> One global scale is a compromise across prompts: short prompts ("a cat") tolerate low guidance and look better for it, long prompts with many constraints need more to satisfy them. A product with very mixed prompts may need a scale that depends on prompt length or type, which is a second parameter to evaluate and maintain.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li>The sweep shows adherence keeps rising up to s = 9 while users in a test pick variations from s = 4.5 more often. Which do you ship, and what do you tell the team that owns the adherence metric?</li>
<li>Research proposes switching guidance off for the last part of the sampling path to save passes. What do you measure before accepting it?</li>
<li>The model is retrained with a new drop rate for the unconditional branch. Does your chosen scale carry over?</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

The decision is between two defensible values on one curve. A higher scale serves users who type precise prompts and need them followed; its cost is variations that look alike and saturated colour. A lower scale serves exploration; its cost is outputs that miss parts of the prompt. Which one the product needs is a product decision supported by the sweep, written down with the scale, the convention and the prompt set version.

## Follow-ups

- Product wants one scale for both the four-variation flow and a single-image "exactly this" flow. Do you agree, and what would change your mind?
- How would you detect after launch that the chosen scale is wrong for a new kind of prompt users start typing?

## Common mistakes

- Treating the guidance scale as a speed setting. Any guided sampling pays two passes per step.
- Copying a number between code bases without the convention, so "1.5" means guidance in one and almost none in the other.
- Choosing by a single image-quality score, which at high guidance rewards exactly the uniformity the product does not want.
- Picking the scale on research's benchmark prompts rather than the product's traffic.
