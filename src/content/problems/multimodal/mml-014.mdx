## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"We send every sampled production screenshot to three models each week to decide which one to use. What does the image input of one weekly run cost?"
</Callout>

The run, as a scenario: 50,000 screenshots a week, all 1920 × 1080, each sent once to Claude Opus 5.5, once to OpenAI gpt-5.5 and once to Gemini 3.8 Flash. The client code sets no detail or resolution options. Count image input only, not prompt text or output.

Published inputs, checked 2026-10-28: Opus 5.5 is $4 per million input tokens, gpt-5.5 is $5, Gemini 3.8 Flash is $0.75 until 2026-12-31. Each provider's counting rule is in its own vision documentation; work them out or recall them, and state any assumption you make where a rule is not fully specified.

Give the weekly cost in USD, the token count per screenshot for each model family, and the term that dominates.

### Which assumptions are reasonable

The accepted band is $1,150 to $1,250. Either Gemini tile assumption lands inside it, because Gemini is under 5% of the total.

Two choices are not reasonable, and both land outside the band:

- **Putting Opus 5.5 on the standard tier** (1,560 tokens, from the familiar 1456 × 819 resize). That rule applies to models before Claude 4.7. It gives $0.00624 for the Claude arm and about $963 a week.
- **Using the tile formula for gpt-5.5** (85 + 6 × 170 = 1,105 tokens). That is gpt-4.1's rule; gpt-5.5 counts patches. It gives $0.005525 for the OpenAI arm and about $853 a week.

An answer that gives one "image token" figure for all three providers is not an estimate; the families count differently and the figures differ by up to 2.6x for this one image.

### The dominant term and what flips it

Claude ($538 a week) and OpenAI ($612 a week) dominate; Gemini is $39 to $58. Two assumptions move the conclusion:

- **Which model inside the family.** On gpt-5.4 at $2.50 the OpenAI arm is $0.00612 per screenshot, $306 a week, cheaper than the Claude arm. The ranking between the two expensive providers is a property of the model, not the family.
- **Downsampling before sending.** At 1280 × 720 the figures become 46 × 26 = 1,196 tokens on Opus 5.5, 40 × 23 = 920 patches and 1,104 tokens on gpt-5.5, and 2 × 1 = 2 tiles, 516 tokens, on Gemini: $0.010691 per screenshot and about $535 a week. It also changes what the eval measures, because production sends full-resolution screenshots.

**How this estimate fails.** It assumes the client really sends 1920 × 1080. A pipeline that captures at a device pixel ratio of 2 sends 3840 × 2160, and then Opus 5.5 resizes to 2576 × 1449 at its 4,784-token cap and gpt-5.5 on <code>auto</code> sends 120 × 68 = 8,160 patches, 9,792 tokens, at $0.04896 each. The cheapest check is the usage field of one real request per provider.

## Follow-ups

- *The weekly run is the only evidence for a provider decision worth far more than its cost. Which half of the 50,000 do you cut, and what does the comparison lose?*
- *Gemini's price doubles on 2027-01-01. Does anything in your recommendation change?* Its arm goes to $77 to $116 a week, still the smallest term; nothing in the ranking changes.
- *What would you need to see to stop running all three every week?*

## Common mistakes

- Quoting one token count for "an image" without naming the model family.
- Applying the standard-tier Claude resize to a high-resolution-tier model.
- Using the tile rule for a patch-based OpenAI model.
- Forgetting that unset detail on gpt-5.5 means <code>original</code>, which is only harmless here because the image is small.
- Spending effort on the Gemini tiling question when it moves the total by under 2%.
