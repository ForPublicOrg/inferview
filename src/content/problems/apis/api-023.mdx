## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="The prompt">
Anthropic deprecated `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929` on 2026-09-30 and retires it on 2026-11-30, with `claude-sonnet-5-5` as the named replacement. It is now 2026-10-01. Your platform owns the migration. Design the workflow from today to the retirement date: who does what, in what order, what state you track, and who signs off on anything that ships worse than today.
</Callout>

The situation (scenario numbers, real dates):

- 40 production prompts call the old model ID. Four engineers can work on the migration, alongside their other work.
- You ran every prompt's eval set on Sonnet 5.5 with the current prompts and request code. 28 hold or improve; 12 regress. The eval harness records an API error as a failed case.
- One of the 12 is a disclosure-wording prompt whose output text is reviewed by a compliance team; they need ten working days to approve any change.
- Three of the 40 prompts also run through Amazon Bedrock for one enterprise customer.

| Prompt group | Count | Share of requests | Eval pass rate change on Sonnet 5.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ticket labelling, JSON extraction | 5 | 41% | dropped to 0% on every case |
| Agent tool-calling prompts | 3 | 22% | 4 to 9 points lower |
| Summaries and replies | 3 | 9% | 2 to 6 points lower |
| Disclosure wording | 1 | 1% | 3 points lower |

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Triage before rewriting. The five prompts at 0% are almost certainly request errors (prefill, sampling parameters or thinking configuration that Sonnet 5.5 rejects with a 400), which are mechanical fixes. The three tool-calling regressions are the likely workaround side effects, such as shouted tool instructions. That leaves four genuine quality questions. Work backward from a freeze date that leaves two weeks of canary before 2026-11-30, fix by traffic and severity, and for anything that ships worse, get a named owner to sign a short note that states the measured drop and the fallback, which cannot be the retired model.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Inventory.</b> Confirm the forty from the Console usage export (broken down by API key and model) and request logs, so no service is still calling the old ID on 2026-11-30. Check the Bedrock deployment's own retirement date; partner platforms set their own schedules, so those three prompts may have a different deadline, which is a reason to check, not to wait.</p>
<p><b>Classify the twelve.</b> A uniform 0% is an error signature, not a quality signature: Anthropic returns 400 for a prefilled last assistant turn from Claude 4.6 models, for sampling parameters on newer models and API versions, and for <code>budget_tokens</code> on current models. Those five become request-code fixes (structured outputs or tagged output in place of prefill, sampling parameters removed) with a rerun of the same eval. The tool-calling group is where older prompts carried "CRITICAL: You MUST" instructions; remove or soften them and rerun before calling it a regression. Run an effort sweep on Sonnet 5.5 for every prompt, because its levels are recalibrated and the old configuration does not transfer.</p>
<p><b>Decide per prompt.</b> After the first two classes, what remains is a short list. For each, the reader chooses the tolerance for shipping degraded, and it must be per prompt: a 2-point drop on a 200-case eval is within run-to-run noise (the standard error of a 90% pass rate on 200 cases is about 2.1 points), while the same drop on the disclosure prompt may be unacceptable because a wrong disclosure is a compliance incident. Rewrite where the drop is real and the prompt is high-traffic or high-severity; ship degraded with sign-off where it is small or low-severity.</p>
<p><b>Schedule.</b> Freeze changes by 2026-11-16, two weeks before retirement, and run canaries until then. The disclosure prompt's ten-working-day approval means its rewrite must be ready by about 2026-11-02, so it goes first even though it carries 1% of traffic.</p>
<p><b>Sign-off.</b> The feature owner signs a degraded ship; compliance co-signs the disclosure prompt. Each note states the measured drop with its case count, the slices affected, the fallback, and the date the rewrite lands.</p>

### The decision: rewrite before the date, or ship degraded and rewrite after

Both are defensible. Rewriting every real regression before 2026-11-30 keeps quality at today's level and costs schedule risk: the four engineers may not finish, and a rushed rewrite tested only on the eval set can regress a slice the eval does not cover. Shipping degraded on the low-severity prompts and rewriting after the date frees the team for the high-traffic and regulated prompts, and costs a known, signed quality drop for some weeks, which some customers will notice.

The recommended split is by severity rather than by count: rewrite the tool-calling and disclosure prompts before the freeze, ship summaries and replies degraded if their drops survive the effort sweep, and schedule their rewrites for December.

### What the workflow tracks

Per prompt: owner, traffic share, eval case count, pass rate on old and new model, failure class (API error, workaround, quality), decision (fix, rewrite, ship degraded), signer, fallback, canary start, canary result. That table is the decision memo, and it is the thing a reviewer reads.

### The rollback problem

Every rollout plan has a rollback trigger, and for this migration the usual target disappears: requests to a retired model fail. After 2026-11-30 the fallback for each prompt must be something that will still exist, such as a previous prompt version on Sonnet 5.5, another current model already evaluated on the same set, or a degraded non-LLM path for the extraction prompts. Write the fallback into the plan per prompt; a canary that regresses on 2026-11-29 with no fallback is a forced ship.

### Failure mode of this plan

The degraded ships are judged on eval sets that were built from the old model's failures. A prompt can pass its eval at 2 points lower while failing badly on a slice the eval never contained, such as long inputs or one language. Slice the canary's production metrics by the same features as the eval, and treat a new complaint pattern during the canary as a reason to add cases before signing.

## Follow-ups

- Pushback: the request-code fixes are trivial, so why are they on the critical path? Because they hide the real result: until they are fixed, the five highest-traffic prompts have no quality measurement at all, and one of them may have a genuine regression behind the 400s.
- Reversal: a prompt that regresses on Sonnet 5.5 and recovers on another current model at similar cost with no rewrite is a case for switching models, if the team accepts a second model to operate and a second retirement schedule to track.
- Operating: the enterprise customer on Bedrock asks for the old model until March. Check Bedrock's own retirement date for the model before answering, and price the cost of maintaining a second prompt version for one customer.

## Common mistakes

- Reading the five 0% prompts as five rewrites.
- One global "ship if within 3 points" rule for prompts with very different costs of error.
- A rollback plan that rolls back to the model being retired.
- Copying the old effort or thinking settings to the new model without a sweep.
- Starting the disclosure prompt last because it has the least traffic, then missing the compliance review window.
