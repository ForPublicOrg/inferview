## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Critique, 35 minutes">
A platform team asks you to approve the reliability section of their design before it ships to the chat API and the nightly enrichment job. List every problem you would block on or ask about, in priority order, then open the checklist.
</Callout>

> **Reliability.** Every layer retries so that each one is resilient on its own. A timeout at the gateway means the primary provider is slow, so we fail over on the first timeout to keep latency down. The fallback uses the same prompts. Retries are safe because model calls are read-only. The enrichment job calls the model with one tool, `update_account(account_id, fields)`, which writes the extracted fields to the CRM.

```yaml
# shared model client, used by the chat API and the enrichment job
sdk:
  max_retries: 2
  timeout_s: 600

client_retry:
  attempts: 3
  on_status: [429, 500, 502, 503, 504, 529]
  backoff: exponential
  base_s: 1
  factor: 2

gateway:                       # LiteLLM proxy
  litellm_settings:
    num_retries: 3
    request_timeout: 10
    fallbacks: [{"primary-large": ["secondary-large"]}]
  router_settings:
    allowed_fails: 1
    cooldown_time: 300

job_runner:                    # enrichment, 40,000 accounts a night
  max_attempts: 3
  retry_delay_s: 5
```

## Answer

Ranked by blast radius.

**The layers multiply.** The SDK makes up to 3 requests per client attempt, the client makes 3 attempts, the gateway turns each into up to 4 (1 plus `num_retries: 3`, before any fallback), and the job runner runs the item 3 times: 3 × 3 × 4 × 3 = 108 provider requests for one failing item. During a provider incident that is the load the design adds. Brooker's five-layer example reaches 243 the same way.

**The classifier is wrong in both directions.** It retries every 429, so a spend-cap 429, which has no `retry-after` and stays until access resumes, is hammered by every layer. It retries 504, which Claude documents as a request that timed out while processing; the documented fix is streaming or the batch API, and a retry repeats the timeout. Its exponential backoff ignores `retry-after` and has no jitter, and the job runner's fixed 5-second delay sends every failed item of a batch back in the same second.

**Failover fires on normal traffic.** A 10-second `request_timeout` is a total timeout. Chat answers long enough to take more than 10 seconds time out on the primary every time, fail over, and time out on the fallback for the same reason. With `allowed_fails: 1` and `cooldown_time: 300`, one such answer takes the primary out for five minutes and moves all of its traffic to a fallback whose rate limits were sized for its own load.

**"Read-only" is false for the job.** `update_account` writes. A model call that timed out may have produced a tool call the job already executed; the job runner's retry repeats the write. The fix is an idempotency key per item and tool-use id, checked by the tool.

**The fallback is a behaviour change nobody evaluated.** Same prompts on a different model means a different tool-call format behind the adapter, different refusal behaviour, a cold prompt cache and different token counts. It needs a per-route eval gate before it is eligible for anything.

**Deadlines disagree.** The SDK waits up to 600 seconds, the gateway gives up at 10, and nothing tells the gateway how long the caller will wait.

The decision underneath is where the single retry layer lives, and two answers are defensible. **Gateway retries**, with every SDK at `max_retries=0` and the client loop removed: one place sees all traffic and can hold a retry budget and a breaker; the cost is that the gateway needs a deadline and an idempotency key from each caller, which is new plumbing. **Client-library retries**, with gateway `num_retries: 0`: the layer that knows the deadline and the side effects decides; the cost is a budget per process, not global, and every consuming team must upgrade. Either way the job runner stops retrying calls and re-queues failed items minutes later with jitter.

The constraint conflict is latency against correctness. Failing over fast protects chat p99; failing over on the wrong signal ships unevaluated behaviour to users during every slow answer. The resolution is to fail over on a first-byte timeout and on breaker-open from 529s and 5xx, never on a total-time timeout, and to count only those signals toward `allowed_fails`.

<p>Today one failing item can send 108 requests. The rewrite: gateway owns retries with a budget (retries capped at a share of recent first attempts, plus a floor), SDKs at <code>max_retries=0</code>, the client loop deleted, the job runner re-queues items minutes later with jitter. The classifier stops on spend-cap 429s and pages the budget owner, sends 504-prone routes to streaming, and never retries 4xx other than rate limits.</p>

The fallback is per route, after a paired eval against the primary, and with a cap on the share of traffic that may move, because its limits cannot absorb the whole primary load.</p>

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback:* your retry budget makes the job miss its 06:00 deadline on a night with a short provider incident. Do you raise the budget, extend the window, or accept late enrichment, and who signs?
- *Reversal:* what would you need to see in production to move retries from the gateway back into the client library?
- *Operating:* the fallback's evals show 3 of 20 extraction fields regress. Is the enrichment route eligible for failover, partially eligible, or not at all, and what do you write down?

## Common mistakes

- Counting the client loop and forgetting the SDK's own two retries.
- Fixing jitter and leaving the 504 and spend-cap retries in place.
- Treating "fail over on first timeout" as a latency improvement without asking what the timeout measures.
- Accepting "read-only" without reading the tool list.
- Approving the fallback because it "uses the same prompts".
