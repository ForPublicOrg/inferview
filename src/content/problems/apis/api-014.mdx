## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="The prompt">
The travel assistant forgets constraints users stated early. Redesign its context handling. Legal has also just committed that "forget this" removes a fact from everything the assistant uses within 24 hours.
</Callout>

The assistant runs on Claude Opus 5.5. Conversations run to about 40 turns over several days; by turn 30 the history is about 45,000 tokens. Users state constraints early ("no overnight flights", "my partner is vegetarian", "budget is fixed") and expect them to hold at turn 35. The current code sends the last 10 turns. Finance has a per-conversation cost target that the current design meets because the short window caches well. The team is two engineers for six weeks.

Design the replacement: what is pinned, summarised or dropped; where each part sits in the prompt; how "forget this" is honoured; and how you would know the design works. Say what you give up, and write the risk note you would attach to the rollout.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask how often users change or withdraw a constraint mid-conversation.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask Legal whether "everything the assistant uses" includes the provider's cache and the transcript store.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Request a sample of conversations where the assistant broke a constraint, and the turn where each constraint was first stated.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Three layers: a structured fact sheet of constraints and decisions, each tagged with its source turn; a summary of turns that fall out of a verbatim window; the recent turns verbatim. Put the fact sheet late, just before the newest message, because it changes often and a late position never rewrites the cached history. Honour "forget this" by deleting from the fact sheet and redacting the source turns and summary, accepting a full cache rewrite for that conversation. Measure with planted-constraint conversations probed late.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Why the window fails.</strong> A sliding window drops facts by age, not importance, so "no overnight flights" from turn 3 is gone by turn 13. Sending the full history fixes recall at a cost that grows every turn and with a quality cost of its own: Chroma's study found every model it tested did better on focused prompts than on the full ones.</p>
<p><strong>Layers.</strong> The fact sheet holds constraints, preferences and confirmed bookings, each with the turn it came from; it is updated by a tool the model calls, so changes are explicit and auditable. Turns older than the verbatim window are folded into a summary in chunks of ten turns, so the summary changes rarely. Raw search results are cleared once used, keeping the calls visible, which is what context editing does on the server.</p>
<p><strong>Where the fact sheet goes: the decision with two defensible answers.</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Early, after the system prompt with its own breakpoint. Stable position, cached when unchanged. Cost: every edit rewrites everything after it. At turn 30 that is about 45,000 tokens × $5 per million = $0.225 per edit.</li>
<li>Late, just before the newest message. Never cached, so its tokens cost full price every turn: a 1,000-token sheet is 1,000 × $4 per million = $0.004 per turn. Edits rewrite nothing.</li>
</ul>
<p>Early is cheaper only if edits are rarer than one per $0.225 / $0.004 ≈ 56 turns. In a 40-turn planning conversation where users revise constraints, late wins on cost, and it also puts the constraints next to the question, the position the lost-in-the-middle work found models use best. Early wins for products whose facts almost never change after the first turns. Which one holds is a measurement from the first hint, not a preference.</p>
<p><strong>The constraint conflict: deletion against the cache.</strong> "Forget this" cannot be honoured in the fact sheet alone, because the fact is also in the verbatim turn where the user said it and possibly in the summary. Honouring it means redacting turn 3 and regenerating the summary, which changes the prompt from turn 3 onward and rewrites the whole cached conversation: at turn 30, about $0.25 once. The cheap alternative, a note saying "the user withdrew X", keeps the cache and fails the promise, since the text is still sent on every call. Deletion wins; the cost per event is small, and the rate of events goes in the risk note.</p>
<p><strong>What you give up.</strong> Verbatim recall of anything outside the window that was not pinned; one cache rewrite per summary refresh and per deletion; and the model's ability to reason about why a constraint changed, since the fact sheet keeps only the current value.</p>
<p><strong>Failure mode of the recommended design.</strong> The fact-sheet tool can record a constraint the user never stated or miss one stated indirectly ("we'll be travelling with my mother, who uses a wheelchair"). Either error persists, because the sheet is sent every turn. Mitigation: source-turn ids on every entry, the sheet visible to the user, and an eval that includes indirect statements.</p>
<p><strong>Risk note, attached to the rollout.</strong> Rollout to 5 percent of new conversations behind a flag, with the old window as fallback. Rollback triggers: planted-constraint recall below the agreed threshold at turn 35, cost per conversation above target for three consecutive days, or any deletion probe that finds the deleted fact in a later prompt. Open item for Legal: the provider's cache holds the prefix for its lifetime after last use (5 minutes or 1 hour on Claude; OpenAI offers 12-hour retention and documents no manual clearing), so "within 24 hours" is satisfied only with lifetimes shorter than that and a statement of which systems the promise covers.</p>
<p><strong>Evaluation.</strong> Scripted 40-turn conversations with constraints planted at turns 2 to 8 and probed at turns 30 to 40; conversations where a constraint changes mid-way, checking the old value is not used; deletion probes that render the prompt after "forget this" and search it for the deleted text. Recall rate, deletion pass rate and cost per conversation are reported per variant.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Finance says the late fact sheet costs $0.004 a turn forever and wants it early. What do you say, and what do you accept in writing?</em> Show the 56-turn break-even against measured edit rates; if edits are rarer than that in production data, move it early and accept a cost spike on conversations with many revisions.</li>
<li><em>What would make you reverse the summary layer and send full history instead?</em> Planted-constraint recall that is no better with summaries than with the full history at the conversation lengths users actually reach, at a cost difference finance accepts.</li>
<li><em>Two senior engineers disagree: one wants server-side compaction, the other the hand-built summary. How do you decide without deciding by rank?</em> Run both through the same planted-constraint and deletion probes; compaction is less code, but redacting a deleted fact inside a server-written compaction block must be shown to work before it is chosen.</li>
</ul>

## Follow-ups

- *Why not a model with a 1M-token window?* It postpones the window limit and charges for it on every turn, and longer inputs answer worse on the published long-context studies; it also makes deletion harder, since more turns hold more copies of each fact.
- *The conversation spans devices and days. Where does state live?* Server-side, keyed by conversation: the fact sheet as a durable record, raw turns in storage for redaction and retrieval, summaries regenerated from redacted turns.
- *A user asks what the assistant remembers about them. What do you show?* The fact sheet with source turns; anything not on it, the assistant does not carry beyond the verbatim window.

## Common mistakes

- Answering "increase the window" and stopping there.
- Treating deletion as an edit to the fact sheet only, leaving the source turn and the summary intact.
- Rewriting the start of the prompt every turn and losing the cache without noticing.
- Summaries with no source turns, so errors cannot be traced or corrected.
- No evaluation beyond reading a few conversations by hand.
