## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Decomposition round">
"A hospital has patient records in six systems that don't talk to each other. Walk me through how you'd give a nurse one view."
</Callout>

What the interviewer tells you when asked (scenario):

- The six: the inpatient EHR, a lab system, a pharmacy system, radiology reports, an outpatient clinic system from a practice the hospital acquired, and a scanned-document archive from a hospital it merged with.
- An interface engine already carries feeds from the EHR, lab, pharmacy and radiology. The clinic system offers only a nightly export. The archive is PDFs, indexed by the merged hospital's old record number.
- The EHR uses an enterprise record number for its own patients; the clinic and the archive do not.
- The nurses' complaint: at admission and at shift handoff they open four applications and still miss outside history.
- Three engineers, twelve weeks, a pilot on one 30-bed medical ward with about 30 admissions a day. The clinical safety committee must approve it. Nobody will replace the EHR.

Design the view and the data path. Say what you give up and write the rollout plan with its rollback triggers.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask the nurses which three facts they look for first at admission, and which system each lives in.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask the health information management team how they link a patient across the old record numbers today, and how long a manual link takes.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Request the outage history of each feed for the last quarter.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Start from what the nurse decides at admission and handoff, not from the six systems: allergies, active medications, recent results, outside history. Feed a read-only store from the interface engine and the nightly export, with an "as of" time per source on every panel, so the view is fast and its staleness is visible. Link records across systems only when identity is certain; possible matches go to the health information management team, not into the view. A missing feed shows as missing, never as empty, because "no allergies" and "allergy system down" must not look the same. An LLM summary is limited to the scanned archive, with page citations, and never supplies allergies or medications. The cost: a second copy of PHI, outside history that waits for a person to confirm identity, and no write-back.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>The view, from the decision it serves.</strong> At admission the nurse needs allergies, active medications with last administration, the last 72 hours of results, and whether this patient has history elsewhere. At handoff, what changed this shift. Those panels decide which sources matter first: pharmacy and EHR for allergies and medications, lab and radiology for results, clinic and archive for outside history.</p>
<p><strong>Data path: two defensible answers.</strong></p>
<p>Federated: query each system when the nurse opens the view. Nothing is copied, and every value is as fresh as its source. The cost: the view is as slow as the slowest system and fails when any one is down, and the clinic system cannot be queried at all, only exported nightly.</p>
<p>A read-only store fed by the interface engine and the nightly export. Fast and available when a source is down, at the cost of staleness and a new store of PHI that the privacy officer must bring into scope.</p>
<p>I build the store, because four of the six sources already arrive as feeds and the fifth only exists as an export, and I make freshness a stated property instead of a hidden one. The quantity I choose is a freshness budget per panel: medications and allergies within 5 minutes of the source, results within 15, outside history as of last night. Any budget the clinical safety committee accepts is defensible; the unreasonable choice is one budget for everything, which either over-engineers the archive or under-protects medications. A panel past its budget shows the age in place of a green tick.</p>
<p><strong>Identity is the safety problem.</strong> Showing another patient's allergy list is worse than showing none. Within the EHR, lab, pharmacy and radiology, the enterprise record number links records. The clinic and the archive need matching, and an MPI's match result is a score with a grade, as FHIR's $match operation models it, not a yes or no. Only a certain match links automatically; possible matches create a task for the health information management team and the panel shows "possible outside records, pending confirmation". The workload decides whether that is feasible: if 8 percent of admissions have only possible matches (measure this on a month of past admissions before the pilot), that is 30 × 0.08 ≈ 2.4 confirmations a day on this ward, which a team can absorb; hospital-wide it may not be, and the rollout plan has to say so.</p>
<p><strong>Absence must not read as a negative.</strong> When the pharmacy feed stops, a medication panel that renders empty tells the nurse the patient takes nothing. Every panel distinguishes "none recorded", "source unavailable since 14:05" and "not yet linked". This is the one rule I would not trade for schedule.</p>
<p><strong>Where an LLM helps, and where it must not.</strong> The archive is scanned PDFs nobody reads at admission. A summary of prior admissions, with a page citation per statement and labelled as a summary, turns it into something a nurse can use in a minute. It never supplies allergies or medications: those come from structured sources, because an omission there reads exactly like a patient without the allergy. If the model is called through the Claude API, PHI needs a signed BAA and a HIPAA-enabled organization; PDFs go inline, since the Files API is not eligible, and PHI stays out of any structured output schema. Archive summaries are generated once per linked patient and cached in the store, not at view time.</p>
<p><strong>Access.</strong> The minimum necessary standard does not apply to disclosures to or requests by a provider for treatment, but the hospital's own workforce rules still define which roles see which categories of PHI. The view inherits the EHR's role and ward assignments instead of inventing its own, and every view of a patient is logged with the user and the reason the EHR records.</p>
<p><strong>The constraint conflict.</strong> Completeness against correctness of identity, under a twelve-week pilot: the outside history that would help most is the history whose identity is least certain, and confirming it costs people the pilot does not have hospital-wide.</p>
<p><strong>Failure modes of this design.</strong> The store becomes the thing nurses trust; when it is stale they act on it, which is why staleness is shown per panel and not in a footer. The archive summary can omit a prior adverse reaction recorded in free text; the label "summary of scanned records" and the citations limit the harm without removing it. And the confirmation queue can silently back up during a busy week, leaving outside history pending for days; its depth is a monitored number with an owner.</p>
<p><strong>What you give up.</strong> Write-back (nurses still document in the EHR), live outside history, LLM help for medications and allergies, and a hospital-wide rollout until the confirmation workload is measured.</p>
<p><strong>The rollout plan.</strong> Weeks 1 to 4: store and feeds, panels for allergies, medications and results, shown to two nurses beside their usual applications, with every discrepancy logged. Weeks 5 to 8: identity linking and the confirmation queue; archive summaries on linked patients for clinical review only. Weeks 9 to 12: the ward uses the view at admission and handoff, with the four applications still available. Rollback triggers: any instance of one patient's data shown for another stops the pilot until the cause is found; any panel rendered empty while its source was down stops that panel; the confirmation queue older than 48 hours for more than two days pauses outside history.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Why not put the view inside the EHR instead of a separate application?</em> If the EHR supports embedded applications in the nurse's workflow, that is better for adoption and for access control; it changes the surface, not the data path or the identity rules.</li>
<li><em>What do you test before the safety committee meeting?</em> A seeded pair of patients with similar names and birth dates, to prove the possible-match path never links them, and a feed cut during a test session, to prove the panel shows the outage.</li>
</ul>

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: the nurses want outside history linked automatically, because the confirmation queue delays it by a day. Which half do you cut, and what risk do you accept in writing?* Auto-link at a stricter score threshold with the possible band still queued; the accepted risk is a measured false-link rate on past admissions, signed by the clinical safety committee, with every auto-linked record marked as such.
- *Reversal: what would you need to see to move from the store to federated queries?* Every source offering a query interface with measured latency and uptime that keeps the view inside its budget, and a privacy decision that the second copy of PHI is a larger risk than view-time failure.
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one wants FHIR for every source now, the other wants the existing HL7 v2 feeds. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Count which sources offer a FHIR interface today and what each costs to stand up; the pilot uses what exists, and FHIR enters where a source offers it, measured on the same panels.
- *Six months in, the merged hospital's archive is found to contain records for patients who never came to this hospital. What changes?* The archive's minimum-necessary review: summaries are generated only for patients linked to an admission here, and unlinked records are never indexed for search.

## Common mistakes

- Designing from the six systems instead of from the facts the nurse needs at admission and handoff.
- Merging records on name and birth date without a confirmation path.
- Rendering an empty panel when a source is down.
- Using an LLM summary for allergies or medications.
- Building a new access model instead of inheriting the EHR's roles.
- Hiding staleness in a footer instead of on each panel.
