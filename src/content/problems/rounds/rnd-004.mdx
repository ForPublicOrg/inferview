## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="The prompt">
"One H200 serves Llama 3.1 70B Instruct with FP8 weights and a bf16 KV cache. It must always keep 20 chat requests resident. How many document requests can it hold at the same time? Show the arithmetic and say what you assumed."
</Callout>

Published inputs: the model's configuration has 80 layers, hidden size 8,192, 64 attention heads and 8 key/value heads; the model card lists 71B parameters, so FP8 weights are about 71 GB. NVIDIA lists the H200 at 141 GB. vLLM's default `--gpu-memory-utilization` is 0.92 in the current docs.

Scenario inputs: a chat request is at most 1,500 prompt tokens plus 500 output tokens. A document request is a 30,000-token document plus up to 1,000 output tokens, and each user uploads a different document.

You choose the rest: the memory fraction the engine may use, what the runtime needs besides weights and cache (activations, CUDA graphs, the framework's own allocations), and whether to budget requests at their maximum length. Give a whole number of document requests.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>About 4. A token costs 2 × 80 × 8 × 128 × 2 bytes = 327,680 bytes. With 0.92 of 141 GB usable, 71 GB of weights and 4 GB of runtime overhead, 54.7 GB is left for cache. The 20 chats at 2,000 tokens take 13.1 GB; a document request at 31,000 tokens takes 10.2 GB, so 41.6 / 10.2 = 4.1, which is 4.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p>Per token: keys and values (2) × 80 layers × 8 key/value heads × 128 dims (8,192 / 64) × 2 bytes = 327,680 bytes. Grouped-query attention is why this is not 8 times larger: the cache holds 8 key/value heads, not 64.</p>
<p>Budget: 141 GB × 0.92 = 129.72 GB for the engine. Weights take 71 GB. I assume 4 GB for activations and graphs at this batch size, which leaves 54.72 GB of cache.</p>
<p>The chat floor: 20 × 2,000 tokens × 327,680 bytes = 13.1 GB, budgeted at the maximum because those requests must never be preempted. Left for documents: 41.6 GB. One document request at its maximum, 31,000 tokens: 10.16 GB. 41.6 / 10.16 = 4.1, so 4 resident document requests.</p>
<p>The band is 2 to 5. A memory fraction of 0.85 with 8 GB of overhead gives 27.7 GB for documents and 2 requests; 0.95 with 2 GB gives 47.8 GB and 4. Budgeting output at an expected 500 tokens instead of 1,000 changes a document request by 2%, which moves nothing here.</p>
<p>Unreasonable choices and why: using all 141 GB with no overhead (5.6) leaves the engine no room for activations and fails at the first large batch; forgetting the weights gives 11; using 64 heads instead of 8 makes the chat floor alone 105 GB and the answer 0.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>What is the dominant term?</em> The weights: 71 of the 129.7 GB the engine may use. Among the cache terms it is the document prompt, 30,000 of 31,000 tokens.</li>
<li><em>Which assumption flips the answer?</em> Shared documents. If every document request asks about the same 30,000-token file, prefix caching keeps one copy of its blocks (9.83 GB) and each request adds only its 1,000 output tokens (0.33 GB): (41.6 − 9.83) / 0.33 is about 96.</li>
<li><em>And the cache dtype?</em> An fp8 cache halves every cache term: chats take 6.6 GB, a document request 5.1 GB, and (54.7 − 6.6) / 5.1 = 9.5, so 9, at a quality cost you measure on the document task before shipping it.</li>
</ul>

```text
per token    = 2 × 80 × 8 × 128 × 2               = 327,680 bytes
budget       = 141 GB × 0.92 − 71 GB − 4 GB        = 54.72 GB
chat floor   = 20 × 2,000 × 327,680 bytes          = 13.11 GB
per document = 31,000 × 327,680 bytes              = 10.16 GB
documents    = (54.72 − 13.11) / 10.16             = 4.1, so 4
```

The decimal-versus-binary choice moves the answer by about one request: read as 141 GiB, the card is 151.4 GB and the budget grows by about 10 GB. The H200 page states gigabytes, and the engine's own report of free memory after loading weights settles it on the real machine.

## Follow-ups

- *The weights were bf16 instead of FP8. What happens?* 71B parameters at 2 bytes is 142 GB, more than the 129.7 GB the engine may use, so nothing fits and the model needs tensor parallelism over at least two GPUs; each GPU then holds 4 of the 8 key/value heads and half of every token's cache.
- *Document traffic doubles next quarter. What do you change first?* Measure how often documents repeat; if they do, prefix caching is nearly free capacity. If they do not, an fp8 cache doubles document concurrency for the price of a quality eval, and a second GPU doubles it with no quality risk.
- *Why budget chats at their maximum and documents too?* Because preempting a resident request either recomputes its prefill later or swaps its cache out; with 30,000-token prompts, recomputing one costs a full long prefill, so admission control on the maximum is cheaper than preemption for this mix.

## Common mistakes

- Using the 64 query heads instead of the 8 key/value heads, which makes every cache term 8 times larger.
- Forgetting the factor of 2 for keys and values.
- Subtracting nothing for weights, or nothing for activations.
- Computing documents at the 131,072-token context limit instead of the stated request length.
- Mixing GB and GiB without saying which.
