## Question

You have loops coming up at several companies and want to prepare for the right kind of coding round. Which statement matches what the companies themselves have published?

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Look for the statement that describes both a default and an exception.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Two of the wrong statements turn "expected to use a specific tool" into "free to use anything" or "free to skip it".</p></details>

## Answer

Anthropic's candidate guidance, last updated 10 July 2025, says live interviews and take-homes are done without AI assistance unless Anthropic indicates otherwise. Its January 2026 post on redesigning the performance-engineering take-home says that particular take-home explicitly allows AI. Both halves of the correct statement come from Anthropic's own pages.

The wrong statements each flip one detail. Meta expects candidates to use its built-in assistant and does not authorise outside tools. Canva expects AI use in the round that replaced its CS fundamentals interview. OpenAI says the policy depends on the format.

The practical habit matters more than any one fact: policies differ by company and by round, and they change. Both OpenAI and Meta tell candidates to confirm with their recruiter. Ask, and write the answer down before the day.

## Follow-ups

- *What changed in 2025 and 2026?* Canva published its AI-assisted round in June 2025; Meta's change was first reported in July 2025 and its careers page now describes the AI-native coding interview; a May 2026 press report describes Google piloting Gemini in a code comprehension round for some candidates. Treat the press report as unconfirmed until Google's own pages say so.
- *If AI is banned in a round, what are they measuring?* Your own decomposition, code fluency and debugging, the same things the AI-assisted round measures through a different lens.
- *If AI is allowed, what are they measuring?* How you direct the tool and how you verify its output. See the lesson for the habits that show it.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming one company's policy applies across all of its rounds.
- Preparing from a prep blog's description of a policy instead of the company's own page.
- Using an outside tool in a round that provides its own assistant.
