## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Multiple choice">
"Our first stage fuses BM25 and dense results into 300 candidates. Pick the rescoring stage that goes after it, given these numbers."
</Callout>

All numbers are a scenario:

- Corpus: 12 million passages from docs and a forum, 160 tokens on average. About 3 percent are added or edited each day, and edits must be searchable within ten minutes.
- First-stage recall of the labelled answer on the eval set: 0.74 at 20 candidates, 0.83 at 40, 0.96 at 300.
- The rescoring stage gets 60 ms at p95, at 250 queries per second.
- Hardware fixed for the year: two GPUs for rescoring. The best cross-encoder you have tested (33M parameters) was measured at 2,500 query-passage pairs a second per GPU at this passage length.
- The vector store has 400 GB of spare capacity. The late-interaction model you would use writes 128-dimensional fp16 vectors per token; queries average 32 tokens.

Which rescoring stage fits?

## Answer

**Late interaction over stored per-token vectors.** The work that does not depend on the query moves to write time. Each passage is encoded once into 160 vectors; at query time the 32 query vectors take their maximum similarity over each candidate's vectors and the maxima are summed. Per query that is 2 × 32 × 160 × 128 × 300 ≈ 0.39 GFLOP. A cross-encoder pass over a pair of about 200 tokens with 33M parameters is about 2 × 33M × 200 ≈ 13 GFLOP, so 300 of them are about 4 TFLOP per query, ten thousand times more, in line with the four orders of magnitude the ColBERT paper reports.

The storage: 12M × 160 × 128 × 2 bytes ≈ 492 GB uncompressed, which does not fit in 400 GB. With the 6 to 10 times reduction ColBERTv2 reports for residual compression, it is about 49 to 82 GB, which does. The churn: 3 percent of 12 million is 360,000 passages a day, about 4 encoder passes a second on average, which is noise next to the rescoring load; Qdrant, for one, stores a passage's token vectors as a multivector on one point and writes it with an ordinary point upsert, so an edit is re-encoded and upserted like any other write, with no index rebuild in the ten-minute path.

The cost of this answer: it ranks with less precision than the cross-encoder pair by pair, it doubles the operational surface (a second representation per passage to re-encode whenever the model changes), and a model upgrade means re-encoding all 12 million passages before the new vectors can be used.

Why the others are tempting:

- **Cross-encoder over all 300, batched.** It is the most accurate of the classic rerankers, and batching does help a GPU. But the measured 2,500 pairs a second per GPU is already a batched number; the stage needs 75,000.
- **Cross-encoder over the top 20.** It is what many teams ship, and it fits the hardware exactly. It also throws away the recall the first stage was built to provide: 0.96 at 300 falls to 0.74 at 20.
- **LLM listwise in windows.** The RankGPT paper reports strong quality from listwise permutation with a window of 20 and a step of 10, at 10 calls for 100 passages. For 300 it is 29 dependent calls; at 60 ms that leaves about 2 ms per call.

## Follow-ups

- *The quality team wants the cross-encoder on top of late interaction for the final 20. Where does the latency come from?* From the 60 ms: measure the late-interaction p95 first, and the cross-encoder gets whatever remains, at 5,000 pairs a second for the two GPUs.
- *The late-interaction model is replaced next quarter. What does the migration cost?* A full re-encode of 12 million passages into a second set of vectors, double storage during the switch, and a dual-read period; budget it like an index migration.
- *When would the LLM reranker be right?* When the ranking needs instructions the other models cannot take (prefer the newest policy, exclude a product line) and the stage can be given seconds, for example over the final 20 of a low-volume research workflow.

## Common mistakes

- Treating batching as a way past a throughput ceiling that was measured with batching.
- Cutting the candidate list to fit the reranker without checking first-stage recall at the new depth.
- Sizing late-interaction storage from the uncompressed figure and ruling it out, or from the compressed figure without a measured compression ratio on your own corpus.
- Assuming per-token vectors cannot be updated in place and ruling late interaction out for a churning corpus.
