## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"This RFC goes to the platform review on Friday. Review it."
</Callout>

The team, the models and every number in the RFC are a scenario.

```text
RFC: Embedding model for the support assistant (v2)

Context
- 180,000 support articles and resolved tickets, about 2.1 million chunks of 1,500 tokens.
  Long chunks keep each troubleshooting procedure in one piece.
- Users describe symptoms ("sync stops after the laptop sleeps"). Answers live in
  root-cause articles that rarely use the same words.
- Retrieval today is BM25 in Postgres. Vectors will go in the same cluster (pgvector 0.8.7)
  with an HNSW index.

Candidates (MTEB leaderboard, checked last week)
| model          | retrieval avg | overall avg | dims | max tokens | licence      |
| omni-embed-7b  | 61.8          | 70.2        | 4096 | 512        | CC-BY-NC-4.0 |
| atlas-embed-l  | 58.9          | 68.0        | 1024 | 8192       | Apache-2.0   |

Decision
- omni-embed-7b: top of the retrieval average, 2.9 points ahead of the runner-up.
- Drop BM25. Dense models lead lexical retrieval by a wide margin on every leaderboard.

Validation
- 20 questions from our team's Slack channel, top result judged by the two authors.
  omni-embed-7b looked right on 17, atlas-embed-l on 14.

Integration
- One embed() helper for chunks and queries. The model card recommends an instruction
  for queries; we will add it after launch if quality needs it.
- Self-hosted on the two GPUs the team already has.

Rollout
- Re-embed everything in one batch over the weekend, switch the index alias on Monday.
```

List the flaws, ranked by how much each could change the decision, before opening the checklist. Then say what you would send to the review instead.

## Answer

### The flaws, ranked

1. **The evidence does not describe this corpus.** The retrieval average is computed on public datasets, and the RFC's own context says this corpus is the hard case: symptoms in the question, causes in the answer, little shared vocabulary. BRIGHT was built for exactly that kind of relevance. The model leading MTEB when BRIGHT was published scored 59.0 nDCG@10 there and 18.3 on BRIGHT, and in BRIGHT's own table it is not the best model; the MTEB paper itself found no embedding method dominates across tasks. A 2.9-point lead on the average is not evidence about this corpus in either direction.
2. **Two thirds of every chunk is never embedded.** The chosen model reads 512 tokens and the chunks are 1,500. About 988 tokens per chunk are truncated silently, and in a procedure written as numbered steps the last steps are the fix. Reviewers read past it because the number is in the table and the chunk size is in the context section.
3. **The index cannot be built.** pgvector indexes `vector` up to 2,000 dimensions and `halfvec` up to 4,000. At 4,096 the plan has no HNSW index in Postgres at all; storing the vectors alone is 2.1 million × (4 × 4,096 + 8) bytes, about 34 GB. The 1,024-dimension runner-up is about 8.6 GB and indexes as is.
4. **The licence.** CC-BY-NC-4.0 is non-commercial and the assistant is part of a paid product. This alone ends the choice unless legal says otherwise in writing.
5. **Queries are embedded as documents.** The model card asks for an instruction on the query side and the plan defers it. Models trained with separate query and document inputs degrade when queries are encoded as documents (the E5 model card says so of its own prefixes), and the size of the loss has to be measured, not assumed small.
6. **Dropping BM25 on leaderboard evidence.** Tickets carry error codes, product versions and setting names that a dense model can miss and BM25 matches exactly. BEIR found BM25 a robust baseline, and on BRIGHT it averages 14.5 against the MTEB leader's 18.3, a gap of 3.8 points. The decision to remove the current system needs measurements on this corpus.
7. **The validation cannot separate the models.** 20 questions from the team's own channel, top-1 only, judged by the authors. 17 of 20 has a 95% interval of about 0.64 to 0.95 and 14 of 20 about 0.48 to 0.85; they overlap almost entirely. The queries are not customers' queries, and the judges wrote the RFC.
8. **No way back.** One weekend re-embed and an alias switch, with no shadow comparison and no versioned vector column. If Monday is bad, the way back is another weekend.

Two judgement calls. Starting from leaderboard candidates is fine if the shortlist, BM25 and a hybrid are all measured on labelled queries from this corpus. Self-hosting a 7B model on two GPUs is fine if the query-side p95 and throughput are measured at expected load; the RFC has no latency number.

### What to send instead

Build an evaluation set from 150 resolved tickets: the customer's first message is the query, the article the agent linked is a known-relevant document, and the top 20 from each candidate are pooled and graded 0 to 3 by two support leads. Candidates: BM25, atlas-embed-l with its query instruction, a hybrid of the two, and the hybrid with a reranker. Chunk at or below each model's limit. Measure nDCG@10 and recall@50 per query type, with error-code queries reported separately.

The conflict is Friday's review against a measured decision, and there are two defensible answers:

- **Delay the decision two to three weeks for the evaluation.** Cost: the assistant's launch slips, and the support team keeps the current BM25 results meanwhile.
- **Ship BM25 plus atlas-embed-l in a hybrid behind a flag now**, because it fits the index, the licence and the chunk size, and choose the final model from the evaluation in six weeks. Cost: probably a second re-embed of 2.1 million chunks, and a launch on unmeasured quality.

I would send the second, with the evaluation committed in the same RFC and a versioned vector column so either model can be served during the switch. Its failure mode: labels collected during the pilot come from what the hybrid retrieved, so any later candidate is judged on a pool built by its competitor; the pooled evaluation set has to be extended with each new candidate's unjudged results before comparing.

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: the review accepts the evaluation but gives you one week and one support lead. Which candidates and which query types do you cut, and what risk do you write down?*
- *Reversal: what result on the 150 tickets would make you keep BM25 alone and drop dense retrieval for this corpus?*
- *BRIGHT reports gains from having a model reason about the query before retrieval. What would that cost per query here, and how would you test whether it is worth it?*

## Common mistakes

- Reading a leaderboard average as a prediction for a corpus unlike its datasets.
- Missing the max-token limit because it sits in a table and the chunk size sits in prose.
- Choosing dimensions without checking the index type's limit in the store already in production.
- Treating author-judged top-1 results on 20 questions as a comparison.
- Removing BM25 before measuring identifier-heavy queries.
