## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Estimate, 25 minutes">
"Legal wants contract search. The contracts are signed scans with tables and stamps. Before anyone picks a parser, I want a number: what does it cost per contract to have a vision model transcribe the page images, on Claude and on Gemini, and how does that compare with OCR text? Then tell me which route you would take."
</Callout>

Scenario inputs, measured by the team on a sample of ten contracts:

- Contracts average 200 pages, US Letter (8.5 by 11 inches), scanned at 300 DPI with no text layer. About 15 percent of pages carry tables; about 5 percent have a stamp or handwritten initials over printed text. The smallest print is 8-point footnotes.
- The existing OCR service produces about 620 tokens of text per page and is billed to the team at $1.50 per 1,000 pages. On the sample it merged columns in 9 of 41 tables and returned the stamped lines as fragments.
- The task for the model is a markdown transcription of each page, tables as markdown tables, stamps described in brackets. No extended thinking.
- The backlog is 12,000 contracts. Legal wants it searchable this quarter.

Published inputs are in the sources. You choose the resolution you render pages at before sending them as images, and how much longer the markdown transcription is than the plain OCR text. Give the cost per 200-page contract for transcribing page images with Claude Sonnet 5.5 at standard (non-batch) prices, then compare the routes.

<p>The band, $1.70 to $3.60, runs from 100 DPI with output equal to the OCR text ($1.74) to 200 DPI with output 1.3 times longer ($3.53). My choice is 150 DPI. An 8-point character is 8/72 inch tall: 11 px at 100 DPI, 17 px at 150. At 100 DPI the footnotes and the digits in table cells are where transcription errors start, and those are what legal searches for. Above 200 DPI nothing changes on the bill: a 300 DPI page is 10,856 tokens before the cap and is downscaled to the same 4,784.</p>
<p><strong>The dominant term moves with the resolution.</strong> Input equals output when a page's image costs 682 × $10 / $2 = 3,410 tokens, which is about 170 DPI (1,445 × 1,870 px is 52 × 67 = 3,484). Below that, the markdown the model writes is most of the bill and the render resolution barely matters; above it, the images are.</p>
<p><strong>Gemini 3.8 Flash, page images.</strong> At the default media resolution a page is 560 tokens. Input: 200 × 560 × $0.75 per million = $0.08. Output: 136,400 × $3.75 per million = $0.51. Total $0.60 through 31 December 2026; from 1 January 2027 both prices double and the same contract costs $1.19. At high media resolution (1,120 tokens) it is $0.68 now. Output dominates on every setting.</p>
<p><strong>Text routes.</strong> OCR alone is 200 × $1.50 / 1,000 = $0.30 per contract, with the measured damage: 9 of 41 tables merged and stamps in fragments. Sending the OCR text to the model to restructure it saves little: on Claude the input falls to 124,000 × $2 per million = $0.25, but the $1.36 output stays, and the OCR bill is added, $1.91 in all. On Gemini the OCR text (620 tokens per page) is more input than the page image (560), so OCR first is the more expensive route there.</p>
<p><strong>The backlog.</strong> 12,000 contracts: OCR alone $3,600; Gemini 3.8 Flash images $7,150 if it finishes by 31 December, about $14,300 after; Claude Sonnet 5.5 at 150 DPI $29,400, or $14,700 on the Batch API.</p>
<p><strong>The decision, with two defensible answers.</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Transcribe every page with Gemini 3.8 Flash, in batch, before the price change.</strong> About $3,600 at batch prices, one pipeline, tables and stamps handled the same way as prose. The cost: a vision model can write a plausible number that is not on the page, and in a contract a wrong figure is worse than a missing one.</li>
<li><strong>OCR every page, and send only the 20 percent of pages with tables or stamps to a vision model.</strong> $0.30 plus 40 pages × (2,714 × $2 + 682 × $10) per million = $0.49 on Claude, about $0.79 per contract. Prose pages keep OCR's failure mode (dropped characters, visible as gibberish) instead of a model's (fluent invention). The cost: a page classifier that has to find every table page, and two code paths.</li>
</ul>
<p>The conflict is cost and deadline against correctness on the pages legal cares about. I would take the second route for the backlog and measure: transcribe 50 pages each way, have a paralegal mark every number that differs from the scan, and keep whichever route has fewer wrong numbers per page, not fewer characters wrong.</p>
<p><strong>Where the recommended answer fails.</strong> The page router decides what the vision model sees. A table that the classifier misses goes through OCR with merged columns, and nothing downstream flags it, because OCR output always looks like text. Measure the router's recall on table pages separately, and send every page whose OCR confidence or column structure looks wrong to the vision model as well.</p>

<li><em>Why not send the PDF to Claude as a document instead of rendering pages yourself?</em> The document route adds the extracted text per page (the docs give 1,500 to 3,000 tokens per page typical) on top of the image, and for scans with no text layer the docs do not say what is extracted. ### Choices that are not reasonable

- Pricing Claude page images at 258 or 560 tokens. Those are Gemini figures; Claude counts 28 px patches and the token count depends on the pixels you send.
- Using the standard-tier cap of 1,568 tokens for Sonnet 5.5. That tier is for models before Claude 4.7; it understates a 150 DPI page by 1,146 tokens.
- Leaving out the output. At 150 DPI on Claude it is 56 percent of the cost, and on Gemini about 86 percent.
- Rendering at 300 DPI "for accuracy". The page is downscaled to the same 4,784-token cap as a 200 DPI render, so the extra pixels never reach the model.
- Using the PDF docs' 1,500 to 3,000 text tokens per page as the image cost. That figure is the extracted text that comes with a PDF document block.

## Follow-ups

- *Two numbers in 200 pages are wrong in the vision transcription and none in OCR, but OCR merged three tables. Legal asks which route is safer. What do you answer, and what do you measure next?*
- *The backlog slips past 31 December. Does the route choice change, or only the bill?*
- *Legal expects to query each contract about once a year. Does transcribing it at ingest still make sense?*

## Common mistakes

- Comparing image tokens across providers without naming the model family; each counts differently.
- Treating the per-page image cost as the whole bill when the transcription the model writes costs more.
- Picking the cheapest route per page without measuring wrong numbers per page on the pages legal searches.
- Quoting a provider's price without the date it changes.
