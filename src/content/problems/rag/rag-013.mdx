## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="The prompt">
"What is GraphRAG, and when would you use it over a standard vector RAG pipeline? What does it cost you?"
</Callout>

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Think of a question whose answer is not in any single chunk.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Where does GraphRAG spend its LLM calls, at index time or at query time?</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>GraphRAG uses an LLM at index time to extract entities and relationships from every chunk, builds a graph, groups it into communities and writes a summary of each. Global questions such as "what are the main themes across these documents?" are answered from those summaries, which top-k retrieval cannot do because no single chunk holds the answer. The cost is an LLM pass over the whole corpus at index time and again on re-index, so I use it only when the eval set shows corpus-wide or relationship questions failing.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p>What it is. Edge et al. (Microsoft) build an entity knowledge graph with an LLM, detect communities of related entities, and pregenerate community summaries. A global query maps over the summaries and reduces the partial answers. The paper targets "global sensemaking" over corpora of about a million tokens and reports more comprehensive and diverse answers than a vector RAG baseline.</p>
<p>When it wins. Questions about the whole corpus or about relationships: themes across interviews, which teams depend on a service, how an entity connects to another through several documents. GraphRAG's Local mode answers entity-centred questions from an entity's neighbourhood; DRIFT widens local search with community context; Basic is plain top-k.</p>
<p>When it loses. Lookup questions ("what does clause 7 say?"), where a tuned hybrid retriever is cheaper and at least as good. And fast-changing corpora, where re-extracting entities and re-summarising communities is a recurring LLM bill. The project's README warns that indexing "can be an expensive operation".</p>
<p>Cheaper variants. LazyGraphRAG indexes with NLP noun-phrase extraction and defers LLM work to query time; Microsoft reports indexing at 0.1% of full GraphRAG's cost and global-query quality comparable to GraphRAG Global Search at more than 700 times lower query cost. LightRAG combines graph and vector retrieval and supports incremental updates. Both sets of figures are the authors' own.</p>
<p>How I would decide. Tag eval questions by shape. If the global and multi-hop slice fails with hybrid RAG and passes with a GraphRAG prototype on a sample of the corpus, estimate the indexing cost at full scale and the re-index frequency, then decide.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>How would you estimate indexing cost?</em> Corpus tokens times the extraction prompt overhead times the model price, plus summarisation per community; run it on a 1% sample and scale.</li>
<li><em>How does it fail?</em> Entity extraction merges distinct entities with the same name or splits one entity across spellings, and community summaries go stale as documents change.</li>
<li><em>What is the alternative for multi-hop questions?</em> An agentic loop that retrieves, reads and retrieves again, with a cap on iterations.</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

The interviewer is listening for the question-shape argument: top-k retrieval returns a few similar chunks, so it cannot answer a question about the corpus as a whole, and GraphRAG precomputes that whole-corpus view at index time. Everything after that is cost and freshness.

## Follow-ups

- *Is a graph database required?* No. The graph is the extracted entities, relationships and community summaries; how you store them is an implementation choice.
- *Can you update the graph incrementally?* LightRAG is designed for it; otherwise changed documents need extraction re-run and the affected community summaries regenerated.
- *How do you evaluate global answers with no single right answer?* Pairwise comparison by a calibrated LLM judge on comprehensiveness and diversity, the qualities the paper reports, plus human spot checks.

## Common mistakes

- Presenting GraphRAG as a general upgrade to RAG.
- Quoting LazyGraphRAG's cost ratios without saying they are Microsoft's figures.
- Forgetting the re-index cost on a changing corpus.
