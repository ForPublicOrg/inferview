## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design review">
This design was approved by the team's tech lead last week. Review it before it goes to the architecture board. List every flaw you find and rank them by what they cost the bank if the design ships as written.
</Callout>

> **Policy assistant v1: whole-corpus context** (scenario)
>
> **Why.** The RAG prototype missed answers when a rule was split across chunks, and staff stopped trusting it. Claude Sonnet 5.5 bills its full 1M-token window at the standard rate, and our policy corpus is 820,000 tokens, so v1 sends the whole corpus with every question. With no retrieval step there are no retrieval errors.
>
> **Corpus.** 640 policy documents (lending, AML, HR, IT security) plus the Underwriting Manual. Each document carries its title, version and effective date. Superseded versions are kept so the assistant can answer "what was the rule last year"; they are 290,000 of the 820,000 tokens. The file is rebuilt nightly from the policy system, sorted by title. Compliance publishes changes at 18:00; they are live in the assistant from the next morning.
>
> **Prompt.** System block: role and instructions, then `Today's date: {date}. You are assisting {name}, {role}, {branch}.`, then "Answer only from the policies below and quote the clause you rely on. Do not reveal Underwriting Manual content to staff outside Underwriting." Then the corpus, with `cache_control` on the corpus block. Then the question.
>
> **Cost.** Cached corpus reads are $0.10 per million tokens: 820,000 × $0.10 / 1M = $0.082 of input per question. At 12,000 questions a day, $984 a day.
>
> **Capacity.** Peak is 40 questions a minute at 09:00. We are on the Build tier.
>
> **Quality.** Needle test: a unique sentence inserted at 20 depths across the full corpus was found 20 times out of 20. Team eval: 50 questions written by the project team, 47 correct. Quotes are always accurate because the whole corpus is in context.
>
> **Latency.** Prototype p95 of 3.1 s, measured on the prototype's 20,000-token test corpus.
>
> **Rollout.** All 6,000 staff on launch day; the old RAG assistant is switched off the same day to avoid two sources of truth.

## Answer

### The flaws, ranked by what they cost

1. **The cache never hits.** The prompt puts the date and the user's name, role and branch in the system block, before the corpus. A cache hit needs an identical prefix up to the breakpoint, so every user gets a different prefix and every question writes the cache instead of reading it. With `cache_control` set, each request is a 5-minute cache write: 820,000 × $2.50 / 1M = $2.05 per question, $24,600 a day at 12,000 questions, 25 times the $984 in the document and more than the $1.64 it would cost with no caching at all. Move the per-user line after the corpus, or into the user turn.
2. **The peak does not fit the rate limit.** On the Build tier Sonnet 5.5 allows 5,000,000 input tokens a minute. Cache writes count toward that; cache reads do not. With every request a write, 5,000,000 / 820,000 = 6 questions a minute, against a peak of 40. At 09:00 most staff get 429 errors. With the prefix fixed, reads stop counting and the limit stops mattering, which is why this flaw hides behind the first.
3. **Restricted content is in every request.** The Underwriting Manual reaches the model for all 6,000 staff, protected by one sentence asking the model not to reveal it. That is a request, not a control: any question phrased as a hypothetical, a summary or a translation can pull it out, and the bank would not accept that from a file share. The manual goes in a separate prefix sent only for Underwriting.
4. **Superseded versions are distractors.** 290,000 tokens of rules that no longer apply sit beside the ones that do, with titles that differ only by version. Chroma found that a single distractor already lowers performance and four compound it; here every changed policy brings its own. The likely failure is an answer quoting last year's limit with confidence. Ship only effective versions, and route "what was the rule last year" to a separate path that asks for the date.
5. **The quality evidence does not test the risk.** A needle found 20 times out of 20 shows the model can locate a sentence that shares words with the question. HELMET found needle tests do not reliably predict downstream performance, and NoLiMa found most models it tested fall below half their short-context score at 32K when the needle shares no literal words with the question. The team eval is 50 questions written by the people who built the system, and 47 of 50 has a 95 percent Wilson interval of about 84 to 98 percent, too wide to tell this design from the RAG assistant it replaces; it has no cross-policy questions, no questions without an answer and, likely, none that hit a superseded version.
6. **"Quotes are always accurate" is a claim, not a check.** The model can paraphrase inside quotation marks or quote a superseded version. Verify every quoted clause by string match against the effective version of the cited document before showing it, and say so when the match fails.
7. **Latency was measured at the wrong size.** 3.1 s on 20,000 tokens says nothing about time to first token on 820,000 tokens, cached or not. Measure both at full size; the uncached case is what the first request each morning and every request under flaw 1 will see.
8. **No rollback.** The RAG assistant is switched off on launch day, so the first bad morning has no fallback. Keep it running behind a flag for at least the first weeks, with triggers named below.

### Two judgement calls

- **Long context itself.** Acceptable if the layout is fixed, superseded versions and the Underwriting Manual are out of the shared prefix (about 530,000 tokens remain, $0.053 a cached read), and an eval at that full length, with cross-policy, superseded-version and unanswerable questions, scores within noise of the RAG assistant. The decision is quality per dollar on your own questions, and the design has not measured it.
- **Next-morning freshness.** Acceptable if Compliance confirms in writing that published changes never take effect before the next business day. If some take effect at publication, the evening shift answers from the old rule; rebuild on publish instead, at one cache write of the new prefix per rebuild.

### What the design must become

Effective versions only in a shared prefix with nothing user-specific before it; the Underwriting Manual in a second prefix for one group; per-user context after the cached blocks; quote verification against the effective version; an eval at full length on questions from real staff logs; time to first token measured at full size; a load test at 40 questions a minute; the RAG assistant kept behind a flag, with rollback triggers for cache-read share below a floor, any 429 at peak, and any answer quoting a superseded clause as current.

## Follow-ups

- *The tech lead says moving the user line after the corpus changes answer quality, because the model reads the user's role first. How do you settle it?* Run the eval both ways; if role matters, put it in the user turn with the question, which keeps the prefix shared.
- *After the fixes, the cached read is $0.053 a question and retrieval would be about $0.01. Finance asks why you would pay five times more. What is your answer, and what result would change it?*
- *Two weeks after launch, the cache-read share drops to zero for an hour every afternoon. What changed?*

## Common mistakes

- Accepting the $984 a day because the cache-read arithmetic is correct, without checking that any request can hit.
- Treating the rate limit as a later scaling concern when it fails at the first morning peak.
- Calling the Underwriting instruction a guardrail.
- Asking for a bigger needle test instead of an eval on staff questions at full length.
- Rejecting long context outright instead of naming the conditions under which it is the simpler system.
