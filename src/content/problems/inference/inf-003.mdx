## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As asked">
"Do the roofline math: how many tokens/sec can one H100 produce for a 70B model at batch size 1?"
</Callout>

After the batch-1 answer, the interviewer turns it into a deployment. The scenario:

- Llama 3 70B serves an internal code-review assistant. Published architecture: 80 layers, 8 key-value heads of dimension 128, so 327,680 bytes of BF16 KV cache per token.
- Peak load: 24 sequences decoding at once, each able to reach 8,192 tokens of prompt plus output. The product's target is a median inter-token latency of 40 ms at peak.
- Hardware: H100 SXM, 80 GB, 3.35 TB/s, 1,979 dense FP8 TFLOPS. The budget allows up to four GPUs for this model. Tensor parallelism must divide the 8 KV heads, so 1, 2, 4 or 8 GPUs.
- The eval team ran FP8 weights against BF16 on four internal suites: three within noise, and the code-review suite 1.5 points lower with a 95% interval of plus or minus 1.2.

Choose BF16 or FP8 weights and the GPU count. The number to give: **the bandwidth floor on one decode step at peak, in milliseconds, for the configuration you choose.** Say which assumption would flip your choice.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether 8,192 tokens is a cap all 24 sessions can hit together or a high percentile of a spread.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Request a measured model bandwidth utilisation for this engine at each tensor-parallel degree you consider.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Batch 1 on one GPU with FP8 weights: each step reads about 70 GB, so at most 3.35e12 / 70e9 = 48 tokens per second, and the compute side (1.4e11 FLOPs per token at 1,979 TFLOPS, 0.07 ms) is 300 times further away. At peak the KV cache is 24 x 8,192 x 327,680 = 64.4 GB, so one GPU and BF16 on two do not fit. FP8 on two GPUs fits with about 13 GB per GPU to spare and has a floor of 20.1 ms; BF16 on four has 15.3 ms. I would ship BF16 on four GPUs: the FP8 regression sits on the suite this product is about and its interval excludes zero, and FP8 on two leaves no latency margin once real kernels run at 50 to 60% of peak bandwidth. I give up half the GPU bill. The choice flips to FP8 on two if a better-calibrated FP8 build measures within noise on the code-review suite.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Batch 1.</strong> Bytes per step, assuming a 1,000-token context: 70e9 of FP8 weights plus 1,000 x 327,680 = 0.33e9 of KV. At 3.35e12 bytes per second that is 21.0 ms, 47.6 tokens per second at most. FLOPs per token: 2 x 70e9 = 1.4e11, 0.071 ms at 1,979 dense TFLOPS. Bandwidth-bound by a factor of 300.</p>
<p><strong>Peak, per configuration.</strong> Per GPU per step: weights over the GPU count, plus 64.4 GB of KV over the GPU count (the KV heads split across tensor-parallel ranks).</p>
<ul>
<li><em>FP8, 1 GPU:</em> 70 + 64.4 = 134.4 GB. Does not fit in 80.</li>
<li><em>BF16, 2 GPUs:</em> 70 + 32.2 = 102.2 GB per GPU. Does not fit.</li>
<li><em>FP8, 2 GPUs:</em> 35 + 32.2 = 67.2 GB per GPU, 12.8 GB left for runtime. Floor 67.2 / 3.35 = 20.1 ms.</li>
<li><em>BF16, 4 GPUs:</em> 35 + 16.1 = 51.1 GB, 28.9 GB left. Floor 15.3 ms.</li>
<li><em>FP8, 4 GPUs:</em> 17.5 + 16.1 = 33.6 GB. Floor 10.0 ms.</li>
<li><em>BF16, 8 GPUs:</em> 17.5 + 8.05 = 25.6 GB. Floor 7.6 ms, and over the four-GPU budget.</li>
</ul>
<p>Compute stays irrelevant at peak: 24 tokens per step against FP8 weights is about 48 FLOPs per byte, against a ridge of 1,979 / 3.35 = 591.</p>
<p><strong>Which choices are reasonable.</strong> FP8 on two, BF16 on four and FP8 on four, which is why the band runs from 9 to 22 ms. Unreasonable: one GPU or BF16 on two, which do not hold the cache (their floors, 40 ms and 30.5 ms, are what a reader gets by ignoring memory), and eight GPUs, which breaks the budget for latency nobody asked for.</p>
<p><strong>The floor against the target.</strong> A floor is not a forecast. At the 50 to 60% bandwidth utilisation Databricks reports for its own runs, FP8 on two gives 33 to 40 ms against a 40 ms target, with tensor-parallel communication still uncounted. BF16 on four gives 26 to 31 ms. FP8 on four gives 17 to 20 ms, but carries the same regression as FP8 on two at the price of BF16 on four, so it wins only if the latency target tightens.</p>
<p><strong>The decision.</strong> Two answers are defensible. FP8 on two GPUs: half the cost, and three of four suites unchanged; the price is a code-review regression whose interval (0.3 to 2.7 points) excludes zero, and a latency margin that disappears at 50% utilisation. BF16 on four: no accuracy question and a 9 to 14 ms margin; the price is twice the GPUs. The conflict is cost against correctness under a latency target, and the regression lands on the suite the product exists for, so I ship BF16 on four and write down the cost.</p>
<p><strong>The dominant term and what flips it.</strong> At peak the per-GPU read is weights plus KV in similar amounts (35 GB and 32 GB for FP8 on two), so context length moves the answer as much as precision does. Three assumptions flip it. FP8 within noise on the code suite: ship FP8 on two. Sessions reaching 16K: KV doubles to 128.8 GB, FP8 on two no longer fits (35 + 64.4 per GPU), and BF16 on four sits at 67.2 GB per GPU with a floor of 20.1 ms. Measured utilisation above 70%: FP8 on two clears the target with margin and the decision rests on accuracy alone.</p>
<p><strong>How the recommendation fails.</strong> Four-way tensor parallelism adds two all-reduces per layer on every step (after attention and after the MLP), 160 per step for 80 layers, and at batch 24 those small messages are latency-bound, so the measured step can sit well above 15.3 ms; measure before promising the margin. And the four GPUs are sized for a peak: off peak, half the fleet carries headroom the bill still pays for.</p>
</Fragment>

<li><em>Would speculative decoding change the precision choice?</em> It cuts weight reads per accepted token, which helps FP8 on two reach the target, but it does nothing for the accuracy gap.</li>
<li><em>Is the answer different for a mixture-of-experts model of 70B total parameters?</em> Yes: at batch 1 only the active experts are read, and at batch 24 the union of routed experts is, so the floor depends on routing spread.</li>
</ul>

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: finance asks why four GPUs when two serve the same users, for a regression of 1.5 points. Which do you give up, the margin or the points, and what do you sign?*
- *Reversal: what would you need to see to move to FP8 on two GPUs?* A calibrated FP8 build within noise on the code-review suite with an interval that includes zero, and a measured step at peak under 32 ms on two GPUs.
- *Operating: next quarter sessions double to 16K and the budget stays at four GPUs. What breaks first, and what do you change before it does?*
- *How would you measure the real number?* A fixed-prompt load test at 24 concurrent sessions, ITL from the engine's metrics, and achieved bandwidth as bytes per step over measured ITL.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing FLOPs by TFLOPS and answering in thousands of tokens per second.
- Computing a floor for a configuration whose weights and KV do not fit.
- Splitting the weights across GPUs but not the KV cache, or the other way round.
- Treating the floor as the expected ITL instead of a bound that kernels reach a fraction of.
- Weighing the FP8 regression by the average across suites instead of the suite the product ships on.
