## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As asked">
"Estimate the KV cache memory needed to serve a large model. How does it constrain batch size and context length?"
</Callout>

The interviewer's deployment: Llama 3 70B on two H100 SXM 80 GB GPUs with tensor parallelism, FP8 weights (about 70 GB in total), KV cache in BF16. The published architecture is 80 layers, model dimension 8,192, 64 attention heads and 8 key-value heads. A document-review team wants **8 concurrent sessions, each able to reach 32,768 tokens**.

Give the KV cache those eight sessions need, in GB. Then choose the runtime reserve per GPU you would plan for and the number of 32K sessions you would admit on this pair, and say what you would change to serve all eight.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask which config fields the engine reads to size the cache, and whether the model sets its head dimension explicitly.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask whether the eight sessions reach 32K at the same time, or what their length distribution looks like.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask how much memory the engine claims for activations and graphs at this batch, measured on one GPU.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Head dimension 8,192 / 64 = 128. Per token: 2 (K and V) x 80 layers x 8 KV heads x 128 x 2 bytes = 327,680 bytes. Eight sessions of 32,768 tokens: 85.9 GB, which is exactly 80 GiB. The pair has 160 GB; weights take 70, and with a reserve of 4 to 8 GB per GPU, 74 to 82 GB is left for cache, room for 6 or 7 full-length sessions. I would admit 6 full-length sessions until a measurement shows the engine needs 4 GB per GPU or less, then 7, and serve all eight by moving the cache to FP8 after an accuracy check at length: 43 GB, with room to spare.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>The arithmetic.</strong> Per token per layer: 2 x 8 x 128 = 2,048 elements, 4,096 bytes in BF16. Across 80 layers: 327,680 bytes, 320 KiB. One 32,768-token session: 10.74 GB. Eight: 85.9 GB (10^9 bytes) or 80 GiB (2^30 bytes). The band of 80 to 92 accepts either unit and rounding.</p>
<p><strong>The fit.</strong> Per GPU: 80 GB minus 35 GB of weights minus the reserve. The reserve is the reader's choice, and 4 to 8 GB per GPU is reasonable for activations, CUDA graphs and engine buffers at this batch; 0 is not, and neither is copying a utilisation fraction from another model without measuring. That leaves 74 to 82 GB in total, so 74 / 10.74 = 6.9 to 82 / 10.74 = 7.6 full-length sessions. Concurrency times length is the budget: the same cache holds 6 or 7 sessions at 32K, or 55 to 61 at 4K.</p>
<p><strong>Serving all eight, three ways.</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><em>FP8 KV cache.</em> One byte per element: 43 GB for the eight. No extra hardware. The cost is accuracy risk at length, and the quiet version of it: without calibration vLLM sets every scale to 1.0, so the check has to run on calibrated scales and on this team's long documents, not on short prompts.</li>
<li><em>Two more GPUs, tensor parallel 4.</em> No accuracy question and more decode bandwidth. The cost is doubling the GPU bill for one team's peak.</li>
<li><em>Admit 6 or 7 and queue the rest.</em> No change at all. The cost is the eighth reviewer waiting for a slot whenever all sessions are long at once.</li>
</ul>
<p>The conflict is cost against correctness: FP8 KV is free in hardware and carries an accuracy risk, four GPUs carry none and double the cost. I would run the FP8 accuracy check first, because if it passes the other two are unnecessary.</p>
<p><strong>How the estimate misleads.</strong> It assumes all eight sessions are at 32K together. An engine with paged KV allocates blocks by tokens actually held, so if most sessions sit at 10K the pair serves eight easily. Admitting by average length instead of worst case has its own failure: when sessions grow together the engine preempts one, and recomputing a 32K prefill costs 32,768 x 2 x 70e9 = 4.6e15 FLOPs, at least 1.2 s on two GPUs at 3,958 dense FP8 TFLOPS, and it can repeat for the same session. Ask for the length distribution before choosing.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>What if the model used full multi-head attention?</em> 64 KV heads instead of 8: the cache is 8 times larger, 687 GB for the same eight sessions.</li>
<li><em>Does paged attention change the bytes per token?</em> No. It removes the waste of reserving the maximum length per sequence up front, so the cache in use tracks this estimate instead of a multiple of it.</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

Worked in one line: 2 x 80 x 8 x 128 x 2 x 32,768 x 8 = 85,899,345,920 bytes.

## Follow-ups

- *The FP8 KV check costs two weeks; the team wants all eight sessions next week. What do you ship in the meantime, and what do you tell them about the eighth session?*
- *Which models break the formula?* Sliding-window layers cap their cache at the window, latent attention caches a compressed vector, and hybrids have attention in only some layers. Read the config, not the model card.
- *Where does the rest of GPU memory go?* Weights, activations for the current step, CUDA graphs and engine buffers; measure it at the batch you run, because it grows with batch.

## Common mistakes

- Using the 64 query heads instead of the 8 KV heads, which gives an answer 8 times too large.
- Dropping the factor of 2 for keys and values, or the 2 bytes per BF16 element.
- Deriving the head dimension as hidden size over heads for every model; Qwen3-32B sets head_dim to 128 where that division gives 80.
- Mixing GB and GiB without saying which.
- Enabling FP8 KV cache without calibrated scales and checking accuracy only on short prompts.
