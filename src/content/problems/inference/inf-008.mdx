## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="As asked">
"You turned on speculative decoding and throughput went down. What happened?"
</Callout>

The version here: speculation has been on for two months on one replica pool, serving tenants A to M, and it was a clear win. In week 2, tenant N was onboarded: product descriptions in Korean, Thai and Vietnamese, generated at temperature 0.9. Peak-hour output throughput per replica fell by a quarter. The serving config diff for week 2:

```yaml
model: <dense chat model>
speculative_config:
  method: draft_model
  model: <same-family 1B draft>
  num_speculative_tokens: 5
-max_num_seqs: 32
+max_num_seqs: 48
enable_prefix_caching: true
```

<p class="tiny">Scenario: the config and dashboard are synthetic, built for this exercise, and internally consistent (30 sequences at 15 ms is 2,000 tokens per second; 16 at 16 ms plus 18 at 37 ms is about 1,500); they are not measurements of a named system.</p>

Nobody has measured tenant N with speculation off. Which explanation accounts for the throughput fall, and what do you do before you have that measurement?

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask for the length of one speculation round at peak, and how many tokens each tenant keeps from it.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask whether the engine drafts per request or for the whole batch at once.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Request acceptance by draft position and by language for tenant N.</p></details>

## Answer

Every sequence in the batch shares one round: draft 5 tokens, verify 6 positions. The length of the round comes from the healthy tenants. At alpha 0.73 the expected tokens per pass are (1 - 0.73^6) / 0.27 = 3.14, and their per-token latency is 16 ms, so a round lasts about 3.14 × 16 = 50 ms. In week 1 it was 3.21 × 15 = 48 ms. The batch grew from 30 to 34 sequences and the round grew by 2 ms: the step is not near compute bound, which rules out the second option.

Tenant N gets the same 50 ms round and keeps (1 - 0.27^6) / 0.73 = 1.37 tokens of it, so 50 / 1.37 = 36.5 ms per token. Without speculation, the 22 ms reference says it would get about one token per 22 ms step. Speculation costs tenant N about two thirds more time per token, and tenant N is more than half the batch. Throughput per replica is 16 / 0.016 + 18 / 0.037 = 1,000 + 486, about 1,500 tokens per second.

Ranking the real defects:

1. **Speculation is on for traffic it does not fit.** The config applies 5 drafted tokens to every request, with no per-tenant or per-acceptance control. This explains the symptom.
2. **Tenant N's prompts miss the prefix cache.** The hit rate fell from 68% to 44% and TTFT p50 rose by 120 ms. It is real and worth fixing; it does not explain the per-token rows, since prefill sharing the step with decode would have slowed tenants A to M as well.
3. **Raising `max_num_seqs`** is the changed line and the obvious suspect. It is not a defect. The batch it admitted is still bandwidth bound.

What is missing, and what to collect:

- Tenant N's per-token latency with speculation off, from one replica for an hour.
- Acceptance by draft position. If it falls off a cliff after the first token, a draft of 1 or 2 may still pay.
- Acceptance by language and by temperature. This says whether a better-matched draft would fix it or whether this traffic should never speculate.

Without those measurements, route tenant N to replicas with speculation off. The arithmetic predicts about 22 ms per token for it there, against 37 ms now, and the change is reversible in minutes. Two cheaper-looking fixes are less certain:

- Turning speculation off per request inside the shared pool may not help. If the engine drafts and verifies the whole batch every step, tenant N's sequences still wait for a round that drafts for everyone else.
- Shortening the draft to 2 for the whole pool would cost tenants A to M part of their gain.

Why the others tempt:

- `max_num_seqs` is the changed line, and "speculation hurts at large batch" is a true statement at a batch this pool did not reach.
- The prefix-cache drop is real and visible on the same dashboard.
- Preemption is the usual story when a tenant with long prompts arrives, and the KV row rules it out.

## Follow-ups

- *Tenant N grows to 80% of traffic. Do you keep speculation for the pool?* At 0.27 acceptance for 80% of sequences the round mostly buys 1.37 tokens; the pool-wide answer flips to off unless a draft matched to tenant N's languages lifts its acceptance.
- *How would you have caught this before onboarding?* Replay a sample of the tenant's traffic through the draft and target offline and measure acceptance before routing it to a speculating pool.
- *Which metric would you alert on?* Acceptance rate per tenant, next to per-token latency per tenant, so a tenant that makes speculation a loss shows up the week it arrives.

## Common mistakes

- Blaming the changed config line because it changed.
- Reading the pool-wide acceptance rate and missing that one tenant carries the whole loss.
- Calling the prefix-cache regression the cause because it appears on the same dashboard.
- Turning speculation off per request and expecting it to help while the rest of the batch still drafts.
