## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Estimate, 20 minutes">
"Our assistant has a 6,000-token system prompt, few-shot examples included, and serves two million requests a day. Finance says the bill is unsustainable. What does it cost a day now, what would it cost with prompt caching, and which number dominates once caching is on?"
</Callout>

Inputs. Prices are published; the rest is scenario.

- Model: Claude Sonnet 4.6 at list price: $3 per million input tokens, $3.75 per million for 5-minute cache writes, $0.30 per million for cache hits, $15 per million output tokens.
- Every request: the 6,000-token system prompt, then on average 1,500 tokens of user message and conversation history, and 300 output tokens.
- 2,000,000 requests a day, interactive (users wait for the answer).
- The system prompt exists in one version per locale, 12 in all. Today it is not marked for caching.

Give the daily cost without caching, the daily cost with caching at a hit rate you choose and defend, and the line that dominates afterwards.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether anything in the system prompt changes per request or per user, such as a timestamp, the user's name or a request id.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask for the usage fields on a sample of responses: tokens read from cache, tokens written to cache, and uncached input.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask how requests per minute are spread across the 12 locales.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Without caching: the prefix is 12 billion tokens a day, $36,000; the rest of the input is 3 billion, $9,000; output is 600 million, $9,000. Total <b>$54,000 a day</b>, two thirds of it the repeated prompt. With caching at a 95% hit rate, which I defend below, the prefix costs $5,670 and the day about <b>$23,700</b>. After caching, output and uncached input dominate at $9,000 each, so the next saving comes from shorter history and shorter answers, not from the system prompt.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Without caching.</strong> Prefix: 6,000 × 2,000,000 = 12 × 10⁹ tokens × $3 per million = $36,000. Other input: 1,500 × 2,000,000 = 3 × 10⁹ × $3 = $9,000. Output: 300 × 2,000,000 = 0.6 × 10⁹ × $15 = $9,000. <strong>$54,000 a day</strong>, about $1.6 million over 30 days.</p>
<p><strong>With caching, as a function of the hit rate h.</strong> Each request either reads the prefix at $0.30 per million or writes it at $3.75. Prefix cost = 12 × 10⁹ × (h × $0.30 + (1 − h) × $3.75) per million.</p>

<p><strong>Choosing h.</strong> Two million requests a day is 23 a second. Across 12 locales, even a small locale sees requests far more often than every 5 minutes, and each hit refreshes the entry, so in steady state the prefix is written a handful of times per locale per day. An upper bound on writes: 12 locales × 288 five-minute windows × 6,000 tokens × $3.75 per million = $78 a day. On that arithmetic the hit rate is close to 100%. I pick 95% to leave room for what the arithmetic does not see: deploys that change the prompt, quiet hours in small locales, and requests that differ before the cache breakpoint. Anything from 90% to 100% is reasonable, which is the accepted band.</p>
<p><strong>The dominant term after caching</strong> is output and uncached input, $9,000 each, with the prefix third. Halving average history through summarisation past a turn count saves $4,500 a day; capping answers at a shorter length saves in proportion to output; both touch quality and need an eval, which the cache did not.</p>
<p><strong>The assumption that flips the conclusion</strong> is prefix stability. If the prompt starts with anything that varies per request, every request writes and nothing reads: $63,000 a day, $9,000 more than not caching at all. A timestamp to the second, a user name in the first line, or tool definitions serialised in a different order each time are enough.</p>
<p><strong>Two defensible plans.</strong> Cache the prompt as it is: a day's work, about $30,000 a day saved, quality unchanged. Or also shorten the prompt: a 6,000-token prompt with few-shot examples can often lose a third of its length, but at a 95% hit rate a third of the prefix is worth $1,890 a day, and it risks quality on the cases the examples covered. I would ship caching now and treat prompt length as a quality question, not a cost one.</p>
<p><strong>How the recommendation fails.</strong> The hit rate decays silently. A later change that adds a per-user line above the breakpoint keeps every response correct and moves the bill toward $63,000 a day. Alert on cache-read tokens as a share of input tokens, from the usage fields, with a threshold near the measured steady state.</p>

### Choices that are not reasonable

- A hit rate of 100% stated as fact. It is the ceiling, and quoting it hides the deploy and quiet-hour misses.
- A hit rate below 50% without a reason in the prompt itself. At this traffic, a low hit rate means the prefix is not stable, which is a defect to fix rather than an input to estimate around.
- Ignoring writes. They are negligible here, but the 0% row shows what they cost when the prefix varies.

## Follow-ups

- The prompt has 12 locale variants today and product plans 200 tenant-specific variants. Recompute the write bound and say whether the plan changes.
- Output is now the largest line. What would you measure before capping answer length?
- The same assistant moves to a model whose cache hits cost 0.05x instead of 0.1x of base input. Recompute the prefix line at 95%.

## Common mistakes

- Computing the cached cost as 10% of the uncached prefix and forgetting writes, then being surprised when a varying prefix costs more than no caching.
- Stopping at "caching halves the bill" without naming the lines that are left.
- Assuming the cache is shared across every variant; each distinct prefix is its own entry.
- Proposing the Batch API for interactive traffic.
