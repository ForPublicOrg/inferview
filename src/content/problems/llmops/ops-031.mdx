## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Estimate, 20 minutes">
"We want to LoRA-tune an 8B model on our support conversations. How many H100 GPU-hours is one training run, what does it cost on rented hardware, and how long does it take on one GPU against an 8-GPU node?"
</Callout>

Inputs. Scenario: 8.03 billion parameters (the Llama 3.1 8B shape), LoRA rank 16 on all linear layers, 20,000 conversations averaging 1,500 tokens, 3 epochs, bf16, sequences packed so padding is negligible. Published: NVIDIA lists the H100 SXM at 1,979 BF16 teraFLOPS with sparsity; Lambda charges $4.29 per GPU-hour for one H100 SXM and $3.99 per GPU-hour on an 8-GPU node (both checked 2026-10-28). Thinking Machines counts LoRA's per-pass cost at slightly more than two thirds of full fine-tuning's.

Give GPU-hours for one run, the dollar cost, the wall-clock time on 1 and on 8 GPUs, and state the utilisation you assume.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether the peak FLOPs figure on the datasheet is for dense or sparse matrices.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask what utilisation the team measured on its last training job, and at what sequence length.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask how many runs the project expects before one ships.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Tokens trained: 20,000 × 1,500 × 3 = 90M. FLOPs: about 4 per parameter per token for LoRA (two thirds of full fine-tuning's 6), so 4 × 8.03×10⁹ × 9×10⁷ ≈ 2.9×10¹⁸. Peak: 1,979 sparse teraFLOPS is about 990 dense; at 35% utilisation, 346 teraFLOPS. Time: 2.9×10¹⁸ / 3.46×10¹⁴ ≈ 8,350 s, about 2.3 GPU-hours, so about $10 on one GPU or about 17 minutes on an 8-GPU node. The dominant term is tokens × parameters; the dominant cost is the number of runs, not one run.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>FLOPs.</b> Full fine-tuning costs about 6 FLOPs per parameter per token (2 forward, 4 backward). LoRA still does the forward pass and the backward pass through the frozen weights for activation gradients but skips the weight gradients, which the Thinking Machines count puts at (2N² + 6NR) / 3N², a little over two thirds. So about 4 per parameter per token: 4 × 8.03×10⁹ × 9×10⁷ = 2.89×10¹⁸.</p>
<p><b>Throughput, the assumption you choose.</b> NVIDIA's 1,979 teraFLOPS is with 2:4 sparsity, which training does not use; dense is half, about 990. Utilisation is the reader's choice: 20 to 50% is a defensible range for a single-GPU LoRA job with gradient checkpointing at 1,500-token sequences. At 20%: 2.89×10¹⁸ / 1.98×10¹⁴ ≈ 14,600 s, 4.1 GPU-hours, $17.40 at $4.29. At 35%: 2.3 GPU-hours, $9.95. At 50%: 5,840 s, 1.6 GPU-hours, $6.97.</p>
<p><b>One GPU or eight.</b> On one GPU the run takes about 2.3 hours at 35%. On an 8-GPU node the same GPU-hours finish in about 2.3 / 8 ≈ 0.29 hours, roughly 17 minutes plus start-up, at $3.99 × 2.32 ≈ $9.26, but you pay $3.99 × 8 = $31.92 for every node-hour, including the time spent loading weights and evaluating. For one 8B LoRA run, one GPU is simpler and the node buys only wall-clock time.</p>
<p><b>What dominates.</b> The FLOPs term is parameters × tokens; epochs and conversation length multiply it directly. But one run at about $10 is not the project's cost. A rank and learning-rate sweep of six configurations, evaluation passes on every saved checkpoint and a failed run or two make it tens of GPU-hours, and the engineer-days to build the data and the eval cost more than all of it.</p>
<p><b>The assumption that flips the conclusion.</b> "GPU cost is a rounding error" holds for supervised tuning on millions of tokens. Continued pretraining on a domain corpus is a different scale: Biderman et al. ran up to 20B tokens, and 4 × 8.03×10⁹ × 2×10¹⁰ ≈ 6.4×10²⁰ FLOPs at 346 teraFLOPS is about 515 GPU-hours, over $2,000 at the 8-GPU rate per run. At that scale utilisation and the hourly rate become the decision.</p>
<p><b>Unreasonable choices.</b> Using 1,979 teraFLOPS as the dense peak, or assuming 100% utilisation, gives well under one GPU-hour and a budget that fails on the first run. Counting only the forward pass (2 per parameter per token) undercounts by half. Forgetting the epochs undercounts by three.</p>
<p><b>Where the estimate fails.</b> Packing was assumed. If conversations range from 200 to 6,000 tokens and are padded instead of packed, a large share of the FLOPs go to padding and effective utilisation can fall below the low end of the range; measure tokens per second on the first 50 steps and replace the assumption with the measurement.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- Finance asks for the cost of the whole project, not one run. What is your line-item list, and which line is largest?
- At what dataset size does renting an 8-GPU node become the better choice than one GPU, and what besides cost decides it?
- How would the estimate change for a full fine-tune of the same model, and does it still fit on one 80 GB GPU?

## Common mistakes

- Treating the sparse datasheet number as the training peak.
- Using 6 FLOPs per parameter per token for LoRA without noting that it overstates by about half, or 2 and understating by half.
- Pricing one run and calling it the project budget.
- Quoting a single utilisation figure without saying it is an assumption to be replaced by a measurement.
