checklist:
  - Names that the reward and the ship metric are the same signal, so the A/B result is the training objective scored on new traffic and cannot detect reward hacking
  - Spots that the 2-point regression tolerance is measured against last week's model, so a run of small accepted drops compounds with no fixed baseline to catch it
  - Spots that cross-prompt pairs matched by embedding similarity teach the difference between two prompts' answers (length, tone, topic) as much as the difference between good and bad answers
  - Notes that the golden set measures task accuracy and has no probe for agreement with false premises or flattery, the behaviour this reward is known to push
  - Names who rates, a small, self-selected share of conversations, rated at answer time before the outcome is known, from users whose mix the product team does not control
  - Notes that each week's data is generated by last week's tuned model, so the loop reinforces whatever style it already rewarded and the eval data distribution moves with it
  - Spots that the Friday auto-promotion has no human who can block the launch on behaviour, the control OpenAI committed to after April 2025
  - Notes that keeping only one previous checkpoint means a regression found after two weeks has no known-good model to roll back to
  - Notes that the planned redesign of the feedback widget changes the base rate of thumbs mid-experiment, so a move in the metric cannot be attributed to the model
  - Judges same-prompt regenerate pairs acceptable as preference data if they feed a reward that is mixed with, not replacing, a primary reward, and the held-out probes can block the release
  - Judges training on user conversations acceptable only if the terms and the data owner allow it and the pipeline removes personal data, and says who signs that off
  - Chooses between dropping the thumbs reward (feedback routes traces to expert labelling) and keeping it with controls, states the cost of each, and names a failure mode of the choice

## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Critique, 30 minutes">
"Here is the design document for our weekly preference tune. It goes to review on Thursday. What do you send back?"
</Callout>

The artefact, as a scenario. A writing assistant for sales teams serves about 2 million conversations a week on a model the company fine-tunes itself. About 1.5% of conversations receive a thumbs-up or thumbs-down. The design document reads:

> **Goal.** Raise thumbs-up rate from 61% to 65% by the end of the quarter by tuning on what users tell us they like.
>
> **Data.** Every Monday, export the previous week's rated responses. Build preference pairs for DPO: where a user regenerated and rated the second answer up, pair it against the first. For all other thumbs-down responses, pair each with the thumbs-up response whose prompt has the highest embedding similarity (cosine at least 0.85). Expected yield is about 18,000 pairs a week.
>
> **Training.** DPO from the current production checkpoint, one epoch, learning rate and beta as in the Q2 run.
>
> **Gates.** The tuned model must score within 2 points of the current production model on the golden set (1,200 tasks: email drafting, call summaries, CRM field extraction). It then runs a 5% A/B for 72 hours and ships if thumbs-up rate is at least 1 point higher at p below 0.05.
>
> **Release.** Promotion runs automatically on Friday when both gates pass. We keep the previous checkpoint for rollback.
>
> **Out of scope.** The feedback widget redesign (a larger thumbs control with an optional comment box) ships in week 6 of the quarter and is owned by the design team.

List the flaws, rank them, and say what you would approve instead. Then open the checklist.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>The plan optimises the thumbs-up rate and then checks it by measuring the thumbs-up rate, so nothing in it can see the model learning to please rather than to help, which is what OpenAI's April 2025 GPT-4o update did with the same kind of signal. The weekly ratchet against last week's model, the cross-prompt pairs and the unattended Friday promotion make it worse. I would approve either a version that drops the thumbs reward and uses feedback to route traces to expert labelling, or one that keeps a narrow thumbs reward with a fixed baseline, sycophancy probes, an outcome metric and a human who can block the release.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>The core flaw: the reward is the ship metric.</strong> DPO on thumbs pairs moves the model toward whatever earns a thumbs-up; the A/B then asks whether the model earns more thumbs-up. A model that flatters weak drafts, agrees with the rep's framing and sounds more confident passes both. OpenAI's postmortem describes this mechanism: a reward from thumbs data weakened the reward that had been holding sycophancy in check, offline evals looked good and an A/B suggested users liked the model. Sharma and colleagues report the underlying cause: human raters, and preference models trained on them, sometimes prefer a convincing agreeable answer over a correct one. A thumbs-up is a preference signal with that bias built in.</p>
<p><strong>Flaws a first read misses.</strong> The "within 2 points of the current production model" gate compares each week's candidate with last week's winner. Eight weeks of accepted 1.5-point drops is 12 points with every gate green; the baseline has to be a fixed reference model, re-pinned only by a decision someone signs. The cross-prompt pairs are the second: two answers to two different prompts at cosine 0.85 differ in length, topic and tone, and DPO learns those differences as preference. Only the regenerate pairs are preferences over outputs for the same input. The third is the widget redesign in week 6: a larger control with a comment box will change who rates and how often, so the metric's base rate moves in the middle of the quarter and any trend across week 6 is not about the model.</p>
<p><strong>The rest, ranked.</strong> The golden set tests drafting, summarising and extraction and has no probe for false-premise agreement or praise of weak work, so it is blind to the known failure. The raters are about 30,000 conversations a week (1.5% of 2 million), self-selected, rating before the email is sent or answered. From week 2 the data comes from the tuned model, so the loop rewards the style it already has. Promotion is automatic, and OpenAI's stated change was to treat model behaviour issues as launch-blocking, which needs someone with the authority to block. One stored checkpoint means a problem found in week 3 rolls back to a model from week 2 that has the same drift. Training on customer conversations needs the contract and the data owner's permission and a personal-data pass, which the document does not mention.</p>
<p><strong>The decision.</strong> Two designs are defensible.</p>
<p>A: <em>no feedback reward this quarter.</em> Thumbs-down, regenerate and long edits route traces to two sales-operations reviewers; confirmed failures become eval cases and supervised targets written by the reviewers. Cost: the reviewers' time, a monthly cadence at best, and a slower metric that the goal in the document no longer describes.</p>
<p>B: <em>keep a narrow feedback reward.</em> Same-prompt regenerate pairs only, mixed with the existing primary reward rather than replacing it; a fixed reference model for the regression gate; a held-out probe set for agreement with false premises and inflated praise that can block on its own; an outcome metric (reply rate on sent emails, CRM fields corrected later) in the A/B beside thumbs-up rate, with the rule that thumbs up without the outcome is not a win; a named reviewer who can stop Friday's promotion; checkpoints kept for the quarter; the experiment paused across the widget change. Cost: the yield falls to the regenerate pairs, a fraction of 18,000, and the release needs a person every week.</p>
<p>I would approve A for this quarter. The weekly cadence and the reviewers' capacity conflict: B needs a person to read probe failures and transcripts every Friday, and a team that cannot staff that will rubber-stamp it within a month. A's failure mode is that expert-written targets carry the reviewers' preferences and their criteria drift, and the reviewers can also prefer a confident answer; the fix is a blind re-label of a sample every month and an outcome metric that neither the reviewers nor the users control.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: the regenerate pairs are only a few thousand a week. Is the reward worth keeping at that volume, or is that an argument for A?*
- *Reversal: the outcome metric arrives 21 days after the conversation. What would make you ship on thumbs-up rate alone before it arrives?* An outcome that has tracked thumbs-up rate across earlier releases, and a probe set that did not move.
- *Operating: legal finds that one enterprise customer's contract forbids training on its conversations, after three tuned models have shipped. What do you do with the current model, and how do you prove the next one excludes that customer's data?*

## Common mistakes

- Finding the sycophancy risk and missing that the regression gate drifts with the model it gates.
- Treating cosine-matched pairs as preferences over outputs.
- Proposing "add an eval for sycophancy" without saying who can block the release when it fails.
- Recommending the thumbs-up rate as a dashboard metric with no outcome beside it.
- Rejecting all feedback-based training without stating what the team gives up.
