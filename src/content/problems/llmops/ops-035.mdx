## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Critique, 30 minutes">
"Here is a decision memo our platform lead wrote two weeks ago. Leadership is about to sign it. Review it as the staff engineer who has to live with it for three years."
</Callout>

> **Decision: self-host summarisation on reserved GPUs (2026-10-12)**
>
> **Context.** Every night we summarise the day's customer documents for the morning digest. Q3 volume: 40 million documents a year, about 110,000 a night, averaging 6,000 input tokens and 400 output tokens (measured). The job must finish by 07:00.
>
> **Option A: hosted API.** Claude Sonnet 4.6 at list price ($3 per million input, $15 per million output): $0.018 + $0.006 = $0.024 a document, $960,000 a year, **$2.88 million over three years**.
>
> **Option B: self-host.** A Llama 4 model fine-tuned on our existing summaries, on 16 H100s reserved for three years at a quoted $2.50 per GPU-hour: 16 × $2.50 × 8,760 = $350,400 a year, **$1.05 million over three years**. Llama 4 matches Sonnet on public summarisation benchmarks, and 16 GPUs comfortably covers the nightly volume.
>
> **Saving: $1.83 million over three years.**
>
> **Risks.** Llama is open source, so no legal review is needed. If the fine-tuned model misses quality, plan B is to fine-tune gpt-4.1-mini on OpenAI (we would open an OpenAI organisation for this) and keep the GPUs for other workloads.
>
> **Ask.** Approve the three-year reservation this quarter to lock in the quoted rate.

List every flaw you find, with the corrected number where there is one, then say what you would sign instead. Open the checklist only after you have written your list.

## Answer

The memo's arithmetic is right and its inputs are wrong, which is why it reads as approvable.

**The API line is overstated three times over.** The job runs overnight, so it qualifies for the Batch API at 50% off: $480,000 a year on Sonnet 4.6. The memo prices Sonnet 4.6 when the same provider lists Sonnet 5.5 at $2 and $10: 6,000 × $2 + 400 × $10 per million is $0.016 a document, $640,000 a year, and $320,000 with batch. Over three years at today's price, $960,000, below the $1.05 million GPU line before anyone counts people. And today's price is the wrong price for years two and three: Epoch AI measured the price of fixed capability falling between 9x and 900x a year. Even with no decline at all, Option A wins once batch and the current model are priced.

**The GPU line is understated.** No throughput test backs "16 GPUs comfortably covers": 110,000 documents × 6,400 tokens is 704 million tokens a night, and whether 16 GPUs process that before 07:00 depends on the model size and the serving stack, neither of which is in the memo. A nightly job leaves the GPUs idle most of the day while a reservation bills all 8,760 hours. People are missing: operating a fleet, running fine-tunes and holding the pager for three years.

**Quality is asserted, not measured.** Public benchmarks are not the team's documents. An eval on a few hundred of last quarter's documents, scored against the summaries readers accepted, is the input this decision needs.

**The risks section is wrong on both counts.** Llama 4 is open-weight under a community licence with an acceptable use policy, attribution on distribution and a naming rule for distributed derivatives; under the OSI definition it is not open source. Internal use avoids most obligations, but "no legal review" is not the conclusion. The fallback relies on OpenAI fine-tuning, which closed to new organisations on 7 May 2026, and the memo says a new organisation would be opened.

**The commitment is the largest risk.** A three-year reservation fixes the hardware while the model, the applied-LLMs authors argue, is the least durable component of the system.

**What to sign instead.** Move the job to the Batch API this month ($480,000 a year on the same model), build the eval, and switch to the current model once the eval shows it holds quality, which brings the run cost to $320,000 a year with no new infrastructure. Revisit self-hosting only with a load-tested GPU count, a quality result on the eval, people costs, and a reason the API cannot meet, such as a data restriction. If a commitment is then justified, one year at most.

**How the alternative fails.** Batch jobs complete within a window, not on a schedule the team controls. If the provider's batch queue runs long on a busy night, the 07:00 digest is late. The plan needs a cut-off: anything not back by 05:00 goes through the synchronous API at full price, and the monthly bill should show how often that happens.

## Follow-ups

- *The platform lead pushes back: "The quoted $2.50 rate expires this quarter, and GPU prices are going up." How do you evaluate that claim, and does it change your recommendation?*
- *Legal tells you next month that customer documents from two regions must not leave the company's network. What survives of your recommendation, and how many GPUs do you now need?*
- *What would you need to see in a year to reverse the decision and self-host?* A volume at which a measured, load-tested GPU count costs less than the batch API line including people, and an eval showing the self-hosted model within the agreed quality margin.

## Common mistakes

- Arguing only about the price trend, which the memo's author can dismiss as speculation, and missing the batch discount and the current model's price, which are on the price list today.
- Accepting the GPU count because the arithmetic of 16 × $2.50 × 8,760 is correct.
- Treating the licence point as pedantry; it decides what legal has to review before a tuned model ships anywhere.
- Rejecting self-hosting in principle instead of stating the conditions under which it would win.
