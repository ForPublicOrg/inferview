## Question

You are the staff engineer asked to propose the company's AI strategy; the company and its numbers are invented. The VP of engineering gives you ten minutes and one page, then twenty minutes of questions. Inputs you have gathered:

- Six features call hosted models today, owned by four teams; two are customer-facing, one drafts messages a person sends to customers, three are internal.
- Each team holds its own vendor keys. Spend is reported as one line on the cloud bill.
- One customer-facing feature had a two-day incident last quarter; the postmortem found no eval covered the failing case.
- An audit begins in two quarters, and the auditor has asked how model changes are approved.
- One internal feature's plan depends on fine-tuning through OpenAI's self-serve API.
- Product wants three new features next half; you can ask for up to four engineers.

Write the page, prepare the ten minutes, and write down the five questions you expect to be hardest. Then run it once against a clock.

<p>The page asks for one decision (fund two engineers for a gateway and tracing this half, and approve a tiered decision-rights table) and shows four blocks: the ranked portfolio with what waits, the primitives with what is deferred, the decision rights with one owner per high-risk decision, and the metrics. Ten minutes: one on the decision, three on the portfolio, two on the platform, two on decision rights, two on metrics and cost. The risk is the VP agreeing to the page and funding half of it; the page says what is cut at each level of funding.</p>

<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>The ask, with two defensible sizes.</b> <em>Two engineers:</em> a gateway with per-team keys, budgets and per-call traces, a tier record for every feature, and the release gate for the two customer-facing features; it covers the audit and the incident, and defers the eval service and new features' platform needs. <em>Four engineers:</em> adds a shared paired-eval runner and supports the three new features on the platform from day one; it costs two engineers product would otherwise get for the new features. I would ask for two and show what four would add, so the VP chooses with the trade-off on the page.</p>
<p><b>The quantity you choose.</b> How many of product's three new features start this half. Starting all three on a platform that does not exist yet repeats the six-teams-six-ways problem; starting none costs a half of product value. One or two, sequenced behind the gateway, is defensible; the page states which and why.</p>
<p><b>The constraint conflict.</b> The audit date and the incident argue for controls first; product's roadmap argues for features first; the headcount cannot cover both fully in one half.</p>

### A worked page

**Decision requested.** Fund two engineers for two quarters on a shared gateway and tracing; approve the decision-rights table below; sequence new features behind the gateway.

**Portfolio.** Keep: the two customer-facing features, gated by a paired eval before any change (the incident's missing case added first). Keep: the three internal features, moved onto the gateway as they change. Reconsider: the fine-tuning plan, because OpenAI closed self-serve fine-tuning to new organisations on 7 May 2026 and ends new jobs for existing customers on 6 January 2027; the plan needs another provider or a prompt-only approach. New: one of product's three features this half, the one with a measurable outcome and no customer-facing actions; the other two next half.

**Platform.** Build: gateway (vendor keys held centrally, per-team budgets, fallback), per-call traces with model and prompt version. Defer: shared eval service, router, semantic cache. Huyen's ordering of platform components, and her advice to start without an orchestrator, is the reason for the short list.

**Decision rights.** Three tiers by consequence. Internal: the team approves with an eval result attached. Customer-facing text: a second approver and a paired eval. Drafts that reach customers or actions: the named risk owner, a rollout plan with a rollback trigger. New vendors and waivers: platform, security and the risk owner. Rollback to the last approved version: pre-approved for on-call.

**Metrics.** Per feature, the outcome it exists for, against its baseline. For the platform, the share of customer-facing traffic with complete traces, which must be all of it before the audit. For spend, cost per feature per outcome once keys are per team.

**Cost and what is cut.** Two engineers for two quarters and the gateway's running cost. At one engineer: traces and keys for the customer-facing features only, internal features stay as they are and are listed as an audit gap.

### What depth costs, stated

Ten minutes cannot carry the eval design, the tier criteria and the vendor analysis. Each gets one line on the page, and the answer to a probe on any of them has to come from preparation, not the page. The failure mode of this presentation is a VP who wants the detail on the one thing you compressed, so prepare one paragraph for each compressed line with its evidence.

### The hardest questions to rehearse

- Why not let each team keep doing what it does, with a guideline?
- What happens to the audit if I fund half of this?
- Which of these numbers is measured and which is your estimate?
- Who is accountable if a customer-facing feature goes wrong after your platform approves it?
- What would make you come back in a quarter and ask for a different plan?

## Follow-ups

- *The VP asks for the expected return on the two engineers. What do you put in the numerator, and which part do you refuse to estimate?*
- *Halfway through, the VP says the audit date has moved a quarter earlier. What changes on the page, live?*
- *Present the same page to the CFO in three minutes. What stays?*

## Common mistakes

- Opening with a survey of models and vendors instead of the decision being asked for.
- A portfolio with no item that waits, which is a list, not a ranking.
- A platform that includes every component on day one.
- Decision rights assigned to a committee for every high-risk decision, with no single owner.
- Metrics of activity (calls, tokens, features launched) instead of outcomes.
- No statement of what is cut if the VP funds less than asked.
