## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design, 45 minutes">
"OpenAI is winding down self-serve fine-tuning. We run four fine-tuned models in production. Give me the strategy: which workloads move, to what, and by when. I need a plan I can take to the VP on Friday."
</Callout>

Today is 28 October 2026. The four models are a scenario; the platform dates come from OpenAI's deprecations page as read today.

| Model | Job | Base | Traffic | Retrained |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Routes support tickets to 60 queues | Base A, in the 14 October notice: shuts down 14 January 2027 | 1.1M calls a month | Quarterly, when queues change |
| M2 | Extracts fields from carrier invoices | Base B, no shutdown date announced | 300k calls a month | Monthly, as new carriers' layouts arrive |
| M3 | Rewrites outbound emails in the brand voice | Base B | 40k calls a month | Never since launch |
| M4 | Suggests the next phrase as agents type, p95 budget 300 ms | Base A | 9M calls a month | Twice a year |

Constraints: three ML engineers, one of whom is on leave from mid-December; no GPU serving in production today, though the platform team runs Kubernetes; the invoices in M2 contain customer bank details and the security team has opinions about where they go; the VP wants no customer-visible regression.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>For each model, ask what happens on the first day it can no longer be retrained, and on the first day it can no longer be called.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask for a held-out labelled set for each model and whether one exists today.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask the account team whether the 60-day inference test for creating jobs is applied continuously or was applied once on 2 July.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Two dates drive it: 6 January 2027, 70 days away, after which none of the four can be retrained, and 14 January, 78 days away, when M1 and M4 stop answering. M3 takes a final retrain and rides its base. M1 and M4 must move before 14 January: M1 to a prompted current model with retrieved examples if the eval says it reaches parity, otherwise a self-hosted LoRA; M4 to a small self-hosted model, because nothing hosted meets 300 ms at 9M calls without a tuned model. M2 needs monthly retraining, so it moves to a self-hosted open-weight adapter by the end of the quarter, with a final OpenAI retrain in December as the bridge.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>The dates.</b> From 28 October, 6 January is 3 + 30 + 31 + 6 = 70 days and 14 January is 78. Fine-tuned models keep serving until their base is deprecated, so the platform closing does not stop M2 and M3; it stops their retraining. M1 and M4 lose both within eight days of each other.</p>
<p><b>Per workload.</b></p>
<ul>
<li><i>M3, brand voice, base B, never retrained.</i> Retrain once in November on current examples, then leave it. Cost: nothing now, and a forced migration on whatever date base B is deprecated. Write that date into the risk register as unknown and owned.</li>
<li><i>M1, routing, base A.</i> Two defensible answers. A prompted current model with the queue definitions and the 10 most similar resolved tickets retrieved per call: no training, no GPUs, but more input tokens on 1.1M calls a month and a risk it does not reach the fine-tune's accuracy on 60 queues. Or a LoRA adapter on an open-weight model, self-hosted: accuracy likely holds, but it means standing up GPU serving in 78 days. Run the prompted option against M1's held-out set in the first two weeks; if it reaches parity, take it and keep the GPU work off the critical path.</li>
<li><i>M4, phrase suggestions, base A.</i> 9M calls a month at a 300 ms p95 is the workload a small tuned model exists for, and a large prompted model fits neither the budget nor the latency. It moves to a small open-weight model with a LoRA adapter, self-hosted. This is the critical path: GPU serving, a latency test at production concurrency and a canary, all before 14 January.</li>
<li><i>M2, invoice extraction, base B.</i> No shutdown date, but monthly retraining stops on 6 January, and each new carrier layout after that degrades it. Do a final OpenAI retrain in mid-December with all layouts to date; that buys time. Then move to a self-hosted adapter on the same GPU platform M4 built, which also answers the security team's question about bank details leaving the account. Target: end of Q1.</li>
</ul>
<p><b>The constraint conflict.</b> Three engineers, one leaving mid-December, against two hard dates eight days apart. The ordering follows the dates: M4 first (hard date, hardest build), M1's prompted evaluation in parallel because it is cheap, M3's retrain as a half-day task, M2 last because its date is soft. If M1's prompted path fails its eval, M1 joins M4 on the GPU platform, and the shared platform is what makes that affordable: one base model can serve several adapters, as LoRAX did with 25 adapters on one GPU in the LoRA Land paper.</p>
<p><b>A quirk of the closure rules.</b> Since 2 July, creating a job requires inference on a fine-tuned model in the past 60 days. Whether that test is applied at job creation or was applied once is not stated on the page. Until the account team answers, do not move the last fine-tuned traffic off OpenAI before the December retrains of M2 and M3 are done.</p>
<p><b>What you give up.</b> Provider-managed training and serving for M4 and later M2; on-call for GPU serving; the option of retraining M1 on OpenAI if the prompted path degrades after January. And the VP's "no customer-visible regression" becomes "no regression larger than the threshold in the plan, measured on held-out sets per model", which needs saying on Friday.</p>
<p><b>The written plan.</b> One page per model: the replacement, the held-out set and the parity threshold, the cutover date, the rollback (the old fine-tune while its base lives; for M1 and M4 nothing after 14 January, which is why their cutovers are planned for mid-December with a canary at 5% for a week), and the owner.</p>
<p><b>Where the plan fails.</b> Held-out sets built from the fine-tuned models' past traffic carry those models' labels. A replacement that matches M1 on its own past outputs may only be matching its mistakes. For M1 and M2 the held-out sets need human labels on a few hundred recent cases, and that labelling is the first thing the leave in December puts at risk.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: self-hosting M4 costs more per month than the hosted fine-tune did, and finance asks why a forced migration raised the bill. What number do you show them?*
- *Reversal: another provider offers hosted fine-tuning with a migration credit. What would you need to see in its deprecation policy to move M2 there instead of self-hosting?*
- *Operating: you have 60 days left on base A and 12 of the 60 queues regress under the prompted M1. Which ship degraded, and who signs off?*
- *Two staff engineers disagree: one wants every workload on one self-hosted platform now, the other wants the minimum to meet the dates. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Write both plans with their dates, staffing and the failure each one cannot recover from, and pick the one whose unrecoverable failure is less likely given the December leave.

## Common mistakes

- Treating 6 January as the day everything stops; it ends retraining, and each model's base date ends serving.
- Planning a uniform migration instead of one decision per workload, by retraining need and latency budget.
- Moving the latency-bound workload to a large prompted model because it is the fastest to set up.
- Building parity tests on the old models' own outputs without human labels.
- Leaving GPU serving, the long pole, to start after the cheap evaluations finish.
