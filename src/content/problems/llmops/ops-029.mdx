## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design, 40 minutes">
"You have one 80 GB GPU, a 7B to 13B open-weight model, and a month to adapt it to our domain. What is the plan, what are the evals, and what is the rule for stopping?"
</Callout>

The domain, as a scenario: a maritime insurer wants a model that answers underwriters' questions about vessel risk reports and drafts the risk summary in the house format. Available: 9,000 past risk reports (about 40 million tokens of text), 1,200 summaries underwriters wrote, and two senior underwriters for four hours a week each. The head of underwriting wants the model "trained on all 9,000 reports so it understands our business". The data cannot leave the company's cloud account. A hosted frontier model may be used for comparison on 200 redacted cases only.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask the underwriters for twenty questions they asked last week and what a correct answer to each needed: a fact from one report, a judgement across reports, or the house format.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask what the current process produces on the same questions today, and how long it takes.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask who will label eval cases, how many hours they have, and whether the same people wrote the 1,200 summaries.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Split the request into what weights can do (the summary format and the underwriters' style, from the 1,200 summaries) and what they cannot (facts in 9,000 reports, which go in retrieval). Week 1: eval set and baselines, including the prompted base with retrieval. Weeks 2 and 3: LoRA on an 8B instruct model across all linear layers, a learning-rate and rank sweep, checkpoint at the dev-set peak. Week 4: regression slices and a decision memo. Stop the fine-tune if, after two rounds of data fixes, it does not beat the prompted base with retrieval on the held-out summaries by the margin set in week 1.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>What the GPU allows.</b> Full fine-tuning holds 16 bytes of model state per parameter, so 7B needs 112 GB before activations: it does not fit. LoRA keeps the frozen base in bf16, 16 GB for 8B and 26 GB for 13B, and QLoRA cuts the frozen weights to about a quarter. Compute is not the limit: one month is 720 GPU-hours, and one LoRA pass over all 40 million tokens of reports on an 8B model is about 4 × 8×10⁹ × 4×10⁷ ≈ 1.3×10¹⁸ FLOPs, roughly an hour at a third of the H100's dense peak. The binding constraints are the underwriters' 32 hours a month and the eval.</p>
<p><b>The decision, two defensible ways.</b></p>
<ul>
<li><i>Instruction tuning on the summaries, retrieval for the reports.</i> LoRA on an 8B instruct model, trained on (report excerpts, summary) pairs; underwriter questions answered from retrieved report passages. Cost: no "understanding of our business" in the weights, and the head of underwriting has to be persuaded with numbers.</li>
<li><i>Continued pretraining on the 40 million tokens first, then instruction tuning.</i> The model sees the domain's vocabulary and style at scale. Cost: on one GPU it must be LoRA, and continued pretraining is the regime where Biderman et al. found LoRA trailing full fine-tuning at every rank; it also writes report-specific facts into weights, which Gekhman et al. tie to more hallucination. It takes a week of the month to run and evaluate.</li>
</ul>
<p>I would take the first, and offer the second as a week-3 experiment only if error analysis shows the model misreads domain vocabulary even with the right passage in context. Model size: start at 7B to 8B in bf16 LoRA; move to 13B under QLoRA only if errors are reading errors that a larger model fixes on the prompted baseline, because 13B doubles serving cost for every request after the month ends.</p>
<p><b>The plan.</b></p>
<ul>
<li><b>Week 1.</b> Underwriters label 150 held-out cases (questions and summaries), split by vessel and by date so no vessel appears in both train and eval. Baselines on the same cases: the 8B base with a careful prompt and retrieval, and the hosted frontier model on 200 redacted cases. Tag every baseline failure as knowledge, reading or format.</li>
<li><b>Week 2.</b> Build 1,000 training pairs from the summaries; underwriters review 100 against a rubric. Probe the base on facts the targets assert and drop or rewrite pairs whose facts are not in the input. First run: rank 16 on all linear layers, 2e-4, 3 epochs, eval every 50 steps.</li>
<li><b>Week 3.</b> A three-point learning-rate sweep and rank 16 against 64; one data fix from the error analysis; regression slices for refusals and the summary schema.</li>
<li><b>Week 4.</b> Final comparison on the untouched held-out set, a serving test with the adapter loaded the way production will load it, and the memo.</li>
</ul>
<p><b>The evals.</b> Summary quality scored by underwriters on a rubric (correct risk rating, every material finding present, no invented finding, house format), paired against both baselines. Question answering scored for correctness with the cited passage. Regression slices: refusing to give a binding coverage decision, the summary JSON, and questions the base model already answered correctly.</p>
<p><b>How many eval cases.</b> The size is a choice under the labelling budget. Detecting 70% against 80% between two models on different cases takes 7.84 × (0.80 × 0.20 + 0.70 × 0.30) / 0.10² ≈ 290 cases per model at 95% confidence and 80% power; scoring both on the same cases needs fewer, by an amount that depends on how often they disagree. So 150 paired cases is a floor that resolves a 10-point gap only when disagreements are few, 300 is the target if the hours allow, and 30 hand-picked cases can only confirm what the team already believes.</p>
<p><b>The stop rule, written in week 1.</b> Stop the fine-tune if after the week-3 data fix it does not beat the prompted base with retrieval by at least the margin the underwriters name as worth a second system to maintain, or if any regression slice drops below the base. If it stops, the month still delivers the eval set, the retrieval path and the error analysis, which is what the next attempt needs.</p>
<p><b>What you give up.</b> The head of underwriting's version of the project, a model that "knows" 9,000 reports, and a month of the GPU spent on the larger experiment in favour of a narrower result that can be measured.</p>
<p><b>Where this fails.</b> 150 cases labelled by the same two underwriters who wrote many of the 1,200 summaries share their habits: a tuned model that matches their style scores well on an eval they wrote. Have each underwriter score cases the other labelled, and report agreement next to the score.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li>Pushback: the underwriters can give you 10 hours this month, not 32. Which eval do you cut, and what risk do you write down?</li>
<li>Reversal: what would the week-1 error analysis need to show for you to run continued pretraining first?</li>
<li>The head of underwriting rejects the memo: "a model that has not read our reports cannot understand our business". How do you settle it without deciding by rank?</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>

The written artefact is the week-1 memo: the task split, the two baselines, the margin that counts as a win, the stop rule and who signs it.

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: your plan spends the first week without training anything, and leadership reviews progress at day 7. What do you show, and what do you say about the GPU sitting idle?*
- *Reversal: the prompted base with retrieval already matches the underwriters on 140 of 150 cases in week 1. Do you still fine-tune, and what would change your answer?*
- *Operating: the base model's publisher releases a new version in week 3 with a different chat template and better published scores. Switch or finish, and who decides?*
- The adapter wins. It now has to be served beside two other teams' adapters on the same GPU. What changes in your eval?

## Common mistakes

- Planning a full fine-tune that does not fit in 80 GB, or a training budget measured in GPU-hours when the scarce resource is expert time.
- Accepting "train on all the reports" without separating facts from format.
- Building the eval from the same documents and people that produced the training data.
- Having no baseline that uses retrieval, so any fine-tune looks like progress.
- Leaving the stop rule until week 4, when sunk cost writes it.
