## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Concept, 15 minutes">
We generate synthetic SFT data with our current model, fine-tune, and repeat each quarter. Someone sent round a paper saying this collapses the model. Does it, and what would you change?
</Callout>

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether each quarter's training set still contains the human-written data from the first quarter.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask what sampling temperature the generator runs at and who chose it.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Collapse, in the sense of test error growing with every round, is what happens when each round's synthetic data replaces the data before it. When the real data is kept and each round's synthetic data is added to it, Gerstgrasser et al. found test error stays bounded across language models, diffusion models and VAEs, and proved a bound for linear models. So the question for this pipeline is whether the original human data is still in every quarter's set, and whether the eval is real data the generator never produced.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>The two regimes.</b> Replacing: round n trains only on samples from round n−1's model, so every round's estimation error is inherited and new error is added. In the paper's linear-regression analysis the test error grows in proportion to the number of rounds n. Accumulating: round n trains on the real data plus every earlier round's synthetic data, so later rounds' errors are diluted by a growing pool, and the test error is the same constant times 1 + 1/4 + 1/9 + … + 1/n², which never exceeds π²/6 ≈ 1.64 times the first round's error.</p>
<p><b>The language-model numbers.</b> A 9M-parameter GPT-2 trained on TinyStories had test cross-entropy 1.82 after the first round. Replacing, it reached 2.91 by round 10. Accumulating, it was at 1.74 in round 4, slightly better than the start. Sampling the synthetic data at temperature 0.3 made replacement far worse, 5.82 by round 4: low temperature drops the tails of the distribution, and each round trains on a narrower slice of the last.</p>
<p><b>What the bound assumes.</b> The training set grows every round, so the comparison is not at equal compute; the paper's own control, a replace run grown to the same size, still degraded, but less (2.18 at round 4). The theory is ridgeless linear regression with isotropic features. VAEs on images still degraded slightly under accumulation. And the generator always sampled stochastically; deterministic generation was left untested.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Storage and training cost grow every quarter if you accumulate. Where do you cap, and what does the cap cost?</em> Cap synthetic rounds by age or by a fixed share of the mix, keep all real data, and watch the real-data eval for the first sign of drift; the cost is departing from the regime the bound covers.</li>
<li><em>Nemotron-4's alignment data was over 98% synthetic. How does that square with collapse?</em> The roughly 20K human examples trained the reward model and seeded SFT, and the reward model filtered what the generator produced; the human data and an independent filter stayed in the loop.</li>
<li><em>What measurement would tell you collapse has started before users do?</em></li>
</ul>

### What to change in the quarterly loop

Keep every human-written example in every quarter's set, and add synthetic data rather than regenerating the whole set from the newest model. Evaluate on real conversations from the latest period, never on a synthetic split, because a synthetic eval drawn from the same generator rises with imitation. Track the diversity of the generated set each round, for example the share of distinct n-grams or the number of intent clusters covered, since narrowing shows up there before it shows up in pass rates. Generate at a temperature chosen on a measured diversity target, not at a low temperature chosen for tidiness.

To check the bound, compute it: after four accumulated rounds the multiplier is 1 + 0.25 + 0.111 + 0.0625 = 1.42 times the first round's error, against 4 times when replacing.

## Follow-ups

- *The team says they already keep the human data, but they deduplicate each quarter's set against the newest synthetic data and keep the synthetic copy when a pair is similar. What happens to the human data over four quarters?*
- *What would you need to see on the real-data eval to stop generating synthetic data altogether?*

## Common mistakes

- Reading "model collapse" as inevitable for any synthetic data, and banning a cheap source of coverage.
- Reading the accumulation result as permission to train on unfiltered synthetic data indefinitely, without the real-data eval that would show drift.
- Evaluating each round on a held-out split of its own synthetic data.
- Quoting the π²/6 bound without its conditions: growing data, linear model, stochastic sampling.
