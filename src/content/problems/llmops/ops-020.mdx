## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Workflow design, 45 minutes">
Leadership wants production conversations used to build eval sets and fine-tuning data for the assistant. Design the pipeline and the policy: consent, personal data, deletion and leakage. Legal and product disagree about how far to go, and you have a quarter.
</Callout>

The situation, constructed for this problem:

```text
Product       a B2B assistant inside a project-management tool; 1.2M conversations a month
Customers     EU and US; enterprise tenants (40% of traffic) have contracts that forbid training on their data
Terms         self-serve terms allow "service improvement"; nobody has checked whether that covers fine-tuning
Privacy       the privacy policy promises deletion within 30 days of a request
Provider      inference and fine-tuning through a hosted API; fine-tuned model retrained monthly
Legal         wants opt-in consent for any training use and nothing stored beyond 30 days
Product       wants every self-serve conversation in the training pool from next month
Team          three engineers, one quarter
```

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask legal whether evaluating the model on a conversation and training the model on it are the same processing activity.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask the provider which of the objects you upload for fine-tuning and evals it keeps, and for how long.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask how many deletion requests arrive a month, and how long after a request a deleted user's data could still be in the served model.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Treat eval and training as two uses with two policies. Eval sets are small, reviewed, kept under a retention limit and deletable row by row; I would build them first, from self-serve traffic, under a documented legitimate-interest assessment. Training use waits for consent that clearly covers it, starts with an opt-in from self-serve users, and never includes enterprise tenants. Scrubbing happens at the logging boundary; every record carries a keyed pseudonym; a lineage index maps each pseudonym to every dataset version, labelling task, provider file and model trained on it, so a deletion reaches all of them and the monthly retrain bounds how long a deleted user stays in the served weights.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Why the split between eval and training.</b> An eval set is a few thousand reviewed conversations that never change a model's weights; deleting a row deletes it. A training set ends up inside a model, and the EDPB's December 2024 opinion says a model trained on personal data is anonymous only if extracting that data, directly or by querying, is insignificant. That is a claim the team cannot demonstrate this quarter, so training data must be data the team can defend having trained on: consented, scrubbed and, where the content does not need it, with personal details replaced by fake ones, which the opinion lists as a mitigating measure.</p>
<p><b>The deletion path.</b> A request arrives, the user id is pseudonymised with the same keyed hash used at logging, and the lineage index returns every artefact holding that pseudonym. Dataset versions are immutable, so the next build excludes the pseudonym and the old version is deleted after the build that replaces it. Labelling tasks are purged. Training files and eval datasets uploaded to the provider are deleted through its API: on OpenAI's platform, for example, files, fine-tuning jobs and evals keep state until deleted, so nothing expires by itself. The model is the hard part: the next monthly retrain excludes the user. With a monthly snapshot, a request arriving the day after one waits about 30 days for the next, plus the time to train, evaluate and roll out; at an assumed 10 days for that, the worst case is about 40 days in the served model, which breaks a 30-day promise. Either the promise is reworded to cover models separately, or the retrain cadence moves to every two weeks, which costs twice the training spend.</p>
<p><b>Leakage.</b> Split eval from training by user and by time, so eval conversations are later than any training conversation, and run the near-duplicate gate across the split before every training run.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Why not anonymise and skip consent?</em> Scrubbers miss things, and a conversation about a named project is identifying without any entity a detector looks for; the opinion's anonymity test applies to the model, not to the scrubber's output.</li>
<li><em>Who owns the lineage index?</em> The data platform team, with deletion as an on-call duty and a monthly report of requests handled and time to completion.</li>
<li><em>What do you tell product about next month?</em> That eval data starts next month and training data starts when consent does, with the date written down.</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

### The decision between legal and product

**Legal's position, opt-in for training and 30 days of storage for everything.** Defensible: it is the position the team can defend to a regulator. Its cost: opt-in rates are unknown until measured and may leave a training pool too small to matter, and 30-day storage makes a quarterly eval set impossible unless eval rows are re-consented or re-sampled every month.

**Product's position, every self-serve conversation under "service improvement".** Defensible only if the terms say so and a legitimate-interest assessment passes the balancing test, which depends on whether users would reasonably expect their project conversations to train a model. Its cost: if a regulator disagrees, the opinion says erasure of the dataset or the model itself is among the possible corrective measures, which would discard every model trained on the pool.

The written decision I would propose: eval use of self-serve conversations from next month, under a documented assessment, with a 180-day retention for reviewed eval rows and 30 days for everything else; training use from opt-in self-serve users only, starting when the consent screen ships; enterprise tenants excluded from both unless a contract amendment says otherwise. Legal and product sign it; the head of product owns the training-pool size risk.

What it gives up: a training pool that is smaller and skewed toward users who opt in, and no data at all from the 40% of traffic that enterprise tenants send, which are the customers whose conversations are most different.

### Rollout

| phase | what ships | trigger to stop |
|---|---|---|
| month 1 | scrubber at the logging boundary, keyed pseudonyms, lineage index, eval sampling | scrubber miss rate on a 500-conversation labelled sample above the limit legal sets |
| month 2 | eval sets built and labelled; deletion path tested end to end with synthetic users | any synthetic deletion that leaves a copy anywhere |
| month 3 | consent screen; first training set from opt-in users; first fine-tune with leakage gate | a regurgitation test finding a real email or name in model output |

### Where this fails

The lineage index is only as complete as the places that register with it. A data scientist who exports a sample to a notebook creates a copy the deletion path cannot see. The control is access, not the index: exports from the curated store go through a job that registers them, and direct access to raw logs is removed from everyone outside the on-call rota.

## Follow-ups

- *The first month's opt-in rate is low enough that the training set would have a few thousand conversations. Do you fine-tune on it, extend the window, or stop the training programme, and who decides?*
- *Your deletion path costs a third of the team's time this quarter. Which half do you cut, and what risk do you accept in writing?*
- *What would you need to see to let enterprise tenants' conversations into eval sets?* A contract amendment, a per-tenant eval set that never leaves the tenant's region, and a deletion test passed for that tenant.
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one says a fine-tuned model with personal data in its training set must be retrained on every deletion request, the other says monthly is enough. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Compute the worst-case residence time under each, compare with the promise in the privacy policy, and ask legal which promise the company is making.

## Common mistakes

- One consent flag for "AI improvement" covering eval and training.
- Scrubbing at training time, after the raw conversations have been copied into a labelling tool.
- Deleting a user's rows from the current dataset version and forgetting the provider-hosted training file and the models already trained.
- Hashing user ids without a key, so a pseudonym can be reversed by hashing a guessed id.
- Splitting eval from training by row.
- Treating the disagreement as legal's problem to resolve.
