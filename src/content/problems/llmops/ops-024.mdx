## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Critique, 30 minutes">
This plan was approved last sprint and round 1 is running. Review it before round 2 starts. List the flaws, rank them, and say whether you would repair the plan or replace it.
</Callout>

```text
Synthetic SFT for the billing assistant: plan v1.2
Goal        Fine-tune OpenModel-70B for billing support; no labelling budget this quarter.
Seeds       1,200 user questions sampled from last quarter's production logs.
Generate    OpenModel-70B (base instruct) writes 20 variants per seed and an answer to each,
            temperature 0.3 "for consistency": 24,000 pairs.
Grade       OpenModel-70B scores each answer 1-5 for correctness and helpfulness; keep >= 4.5.
Dedup       exact match on normalised question text.
Eval        10% of the kept pairs, random split; target 85% pass, judged by OpenModel-70B.
Round 2     the round-1 fine-tuned model regenerates all 24,000 pairs from the same seeds;
            round-1 pairs are discarded so the set reflects the improved model.
Licence     OpenModel licence permits training on outputs (see wiki).
Status      round 1 kept 15,840 pairs (66%); eval pass 93%.
```

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>The plan measures the generator against itself three times: it writes the data, grades it, and judges the eval, which is a split of its own output. None of the numbers on the page, 66% kept or 93% pass, says anything about billing questions from real users. Round 2 then discards round 1 and regenerates from the fine-tuned model at low temperature, which is the setting that degraded fastest in the collapse experiments. I would repair it rather than replace it, because generation is cheap and the missing parts are an independent filter and a real eval, which cost about a week of a specialist's time.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Ranked by what they hide.</b> First, the eval: a random 10% of kept pairs shares seeds with training, since each seed produced 20 variants, so almost every eval question has siblings in training, and the judge is the generator. 93% is a measure of agreement between a model and itself. Second, the filter: the same model grades its own answers, so an answer it gets wrong confidently is graded as correct; Nemotron-4 used a separately trained reward model and scored LLM-judge pairs in both orders, keeping only consistent verdicts, because self-consistent errors are the failure mode. Third, round 2: replacing the set with the fine-tuned model's output, at temperature 0.3, removes the seeds' influence after one round; Gerstgrasser et al. saw that combination degrade fastest. Fourth, no near-duplicate removal: 20 variants of one seed at low temperature are paraphrases, so the effective set is closer to 1,200 examples weighted by how often the model agrees with itself.</p>
<p><b>Repair.</b> A billing specialist labels 300 real conversations from this month as the eval and grades 100 kept and 100 dropped pairs to measure the filter. Replace the self-grader with a judge from another model family, checked against those 200 labels. Split by seed, not by pair. Keep round-1 pairs and the seeds in round 2 and add new pairs; measure distinct intents per round. Scrub the seed prompts through the same path as any production data.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>The team lead says there is no labelling budget this quarter and the specialist week is exactly what they do not have. Which repair do you keep, and what risk do you accept in writing?</em> Keep the real eval, even at 100 conversations, and drop the filter validation; the risk written down is that the training set contains confidently wrong answers at an unknown rate.</li>
<li><em>The independent judge agrees with the specialist on 72% of pairs. Do you use it as the filter?</em></li>
<li><em>What would you need to see on the real eval to stop the synthetic programme?</em> Two rounds in which the real-data pass rate does not move while the synthetic pass rate rises.</li>
</ul>

### Repair or replace

**Repair** keeps the generator and fixes the measurement: a week of specialist time for the eval and filter check, a second model for judging, and seed-level splits. Its failure mode is that a validated filter on a narrow seed set still produces a narrow set; 1,200 seeds from one quarter cover that quarter's intents and nothing launched since.

**Replace** spends the same week on a small human-reviewed set: the specialist edits model-drafted answers to a few hundred real questions, stratified by intent. LIMA-style results suggest that a small, carefully chosen set can carry format and tone; it cannot carry coverage of rare intents, and it does not scale when the product changes. The cost is lower volume and a second round of specialist time every time pricing changes.

I would repair, because the generator is the cheap part and the plan's problems are all in measurement. If the specialist's grading shows the self-grader passing wrong answers at a high rate, that changes: the generator is then not good enough at billing to teach it, and the replace path is the only one with ground truth in it.

## Follow-ups

- *The licence link on the wiki points to a page that has changed since the plan was written. Who owns checking it, and what goes in the dataset card?*
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one wants 100% synthetic with a strong filter, the other a 50/50 mix with human data. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Run both on the real eval with the same training budget and decide on the difference and its interval.
- *The 85% target was set before the eval existed. What target do you set on the real eval, and from what?*

## Common mistakes

- Listing "the data is synthetic" as the flaw, which Nemotron-4's 98% contradicts; the flaws are the closed loop and the replacement.
- Missing that the eval split shares seeds with training.
- Trusting the 66% keep rate as a quality signal, when it only measures how often the model rates itself highly.
- Recommending a stronger generator before anyone has measured the filter.
