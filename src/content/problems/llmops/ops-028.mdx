## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Concept, 15 minutes">
The product manager for a B2B storefront assistant says: "The model keeps getting our products wrong. Let's fine-tune it on our company data so it knows our products." The catalogue has 14,000 SKUs, prices change weekly, and about 300 SKUs are added or retired each month. Respond, with the evidence, and say what you would build instead or as well.
</Callout>

<p class="tiny">The storefront and its catalogue figures are a scenario.</p>

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask for twenty transcripts where the model got a product wrong, and check whether the right fact was in its context when it answered.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask how many days pass between a price change and the moment the assistant must quote the new price.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Fine-tuning is the wrong tool for facts the model does not have and that change weekly. Fine-tuning on new facts raises hallucination as those examples are learned (Gekhman et al.), and on new facts retrieval beat fine-tuning by a wide margin (Ovadia et al.: 0.875 against 0.504 for Mistral 7B). Put the catalogue in a store the assistant queries per request, with the SKU record in context, and fine-tune later only if the problem left is format or tone.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p>First, find which failure it is. If the right SKU record was in context and the model misread it, the gap is behaviour, and a prompt change or a format fine-tune can help. If the record was not in context, the gap is knowledge, and no amount of training fixes it for a catalogue that changes weekly.</p>
<p>The evidence on knowledge. Ovadia et al. tested 7B models on 910 questions about events after their training cutoff: Mistral 7B scored 0.481 with nothing, 0.504 after fine-tuning on the source documents, and 0.875 with retrieval; Llama 2 7B dropped to 0.219 after fine-tuning. Gekhman et al. explain why: in a controlled study, training examples carrying facts the model did not know were learned slowly, and once learned they raised wrong answers across the board, including on questions unrelated to the training set. With half the training set unknown facts, accuracy fell from 43.0 at the best checkpoint to 38.8 once training ran to convergence.</p>
<p>The arithmetic of freshness. With about 300 SKUs changing a month and prices moving weekly, a fine-tuned model is wrong about some products from the day after its training data was frozen, and every retrain is a new model to evaluate and ship. A retrieval index updates when the catalogue does.</p>
<p>What to build: a lookup by SKU and a search over product descriptions, the matching records placed in context, an instruction to quote price and availability only from those records, and a "not found" path. Fine-tuning stays on the table for what it does well: if, with the right record in context, answers are still in the wrong format or tone, a small fine-tune on (question, retrieved record, ideal answer) examples teaches the behaviour without putting the catalogue in the weights.</p>
<p>Where this fails: retrieval misses. A customer who asks for "the blue one from last spring" gets a fluent answer from the model's prior if search returns nothing useful. Measure retrieval recall on real questions and make the not-found path answer honestly.</p>
</Fragment>

The short answer for the PM fits in three sentences: fine-tuning teaches a model how to answer, not what is true; training it on facts it does not know makes it more confidently wrong, and it would be out of date within a week; we will have it look products up at answer time, and tune its style later if that is still the problem.

## Follow-ups

- Legal asks for a guarantee that the assistant never quotes a price that is not in the catalogue. What can you guarantee with retrieval, and what can you only measure?
- The retrieval path adds latency the PM did not expect. Which part of it do you measure first?
- If you did fine-tune for format, how would you check that the training answers do not smuggle catalogue facts into the weights?

## Common mistakes

- Agreeing to fine-tune without first checking whether the right record was in context when the model failed.
- Arguing from intuition ("models can't learn facts") instead of citing measured results with their conditions.
- Treating retrieval and fine-tuning as exclusive, and missing the format fine-tune on retrieved context.
- Ignoring how often the catalogue changes, which by itself rules out facts in weights.
