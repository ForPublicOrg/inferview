## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design, 40 minutes">
"We want an internal assistant for all 20,000 employees. Should we self-host an open-weight model or use a hosted API, and how many GPUs would we need? Answer with numbers."
</Callout>

What you learn by asking (all scenario):

- The assistant answers questions over internal documents and drafts text. Most use is in an 8-hour working day in one time zone.
- Legal: HR case files and documents in the M&A data room must not be sent to any third party, under any contract. Everything else may go to a provider under the company's standard data processing agreement.
- The platform team is three engineers. Leadership wants the assistant live in 90 days.
- The CFO has approved $25,000 a month for running costs, excluding salaries.
- No usage data exists yet. You choose the demand assumptions.

Produce the recommendation, the GPU count if any GPUs are involved, the monthly cost, what you give up, and a one-paragraph decision memo with the conditions that would change it.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask what share of employees used the last internal tool that launched, and how often, in its third month rather than its first week.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask legal which document collections the restriction covers, and roughly what share of the corpus they are.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask whether anyone on the platform team has operated GPU inference in production, and who would hold the pager.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Hybrid. A hosted API for everything the DPA allows, and a small self-hosted deployment only for the restricted collections, sized for their share of traffic. With 40% of employees active, 15 requests a day each and 3,000 input and 500 output tokens per request, the API side costs about $19,000 a month on Claude Sonnet 5.5 with the shared prompt cached. Fully self-hosting the same load needs one 8-GPU H100 node at peak and a second for redundancy, $46,600 a month on Lambda on-demand before anyone's salary, on a model that is probably weaker. The restricted slice gets two 2-GPU replicas of a 70B-class open model, four GPUs, about $11,900 a month, and staying inside the budget then needs half the API traffic on a cheaper model. What we give up: two model stacks to evaluate and operate, and a quality gap on the restricted documents.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Demand, the numbers I choose.</strong> 40% daily active (8,000 people), 15 requests per active user per day, 3,000 input tokens (instructions, retrieved passages, history) and 500 output. That is 120,000 requests and 420 million tokens a day, 9.2 billion over 22 working days. Peak: the day's requests in 8 hours with the busiest hour at twice the average, 120,000 / 28,800 s × 2 = 8.3 requests a second. Reasonable ranges are 20% to 60% active and 5 to 30 requests; below that the API wins trivially, above it the self-host numbers below scale linearly.</p>
<p><strong>API side.</strong> On Claude Sonnet 5.5, uncached: 3,000 × $2 + 500 × $10 per million = $0.011 a request, $1,320 a day, $29,040 a month. With 2,000 tokens of shared instructions as cache hits at $0.10: $0.0072 a request, <strong>$19,008 a month</strong>. Routing simple requests to Claude Haiku 4.5 at the same shape would cost $0.0037 a request, $9,768 a month for all traffic, if the eval allows it.</p>
<p><strong>Self-host side, the GPU count.</strong> Take a 70-billion-parameter dense open model in FP8: 70 GB of weights, which with KV cache needs two 80 GB H100s per replica. Decode is bandwidth-bound: each step reads the weights once, 70 GB over 2 × 3.35 TB/s, at least 10.4 ms, so a replica at batch 32 produces at most about 3,060 tokens a second. Peak output is 8.3 × 500 = 4,170 tokens a second, so two replicas at the theoretical bound and four once real efficiency is counted: one 8-GPU node. Prefill at peak is 25,000 tokens a second, 2 × 70 × 10⁹ × 25,000 = 3.5 × 10¹⁵ FLOP/s, 11% of the node's quoted FP8 figure, which NVIDIA states with sparsity; the dense share is higher, and long prompts arriving together push time to first token up. Both numbers are ceilings that a load test replaces before any purchase. A second node for redundancy and deploys makes 16 GPUs: 2 × 8 × $3.99 × 730 = <strong>$46,603 a month</strong> on-demand, over the CFO's budget before salaries.</p>
<p><strong>The restricted slice.</strong> If HR and M&A material is under 10% of requests (ask legal and the search logs), one 2-GPU replica covers its peak by the arithmetic above; two replicas give redundancy. Four H100s in a 4-GPU instance: 4 × $4.09 × 730 = <strong>$11,943 a month</strong>. The API side now carries 90% of requests, 0.9 × $19,008 = $17,107, so the total is $29,050, over the $25,000 budget. Routing half of the API traffic to Haiku 4.5 saves 0.5 × 0.9 × ($19,008 − $9,768) = $4,158 and brings the total to $24,892, at the cost of a second hosted model to evaluate and a router to maintain.</p>
<p><strong>Two defensible answers.</strong> Hybrid as above: best quality where it is allowed, compliance where it is required, and two stacks to run with three engineers. Or API only, with the restricted collections excluded from the assistant's index: one stack, cheapest, shipped in 90 days, and HR and M&A users get nothing. I would put the second option in the memo as the 90-day launch and the self-hosted slice as phase two, because three engineers cannot build a serving fleet, a retrieval pipeline with access control and evals in 90 days.</p>
<p><strong>Constraint conflict.</strong> Legal's restriction, the budget and the 90 days cannot all be met with full coverage. Something gives: coverage of the restricted documents at launch, or the budget, or the date.</p>
<p><strong>The decision memo.</strong> Launch in 90 days on the hosted API under the DPA, excluding HR and M&A collections from the index, with retrieval enforcing each user's document permissions. Phase two: a self-hosted open model for the restricted collections, gated on a load test and an eval against the hosted model on a sample of restricted questions answered by the HR and legal teams. Revisit full self-hosting if measured volume passes the point where two nodes cost less than the API bill, or if legal widens the restriction. Rollback trigger for phase two: if the self-hosted model's answers fall below the agreed margin on the eval, restricted users keep search without generation.</p>
<p><strong>How this fails.</strong> The restricted boundary is a property of documents, and documents move: an HR file attached to a general wiki page sends restricted text to the API through retrieval. The control has to be on the retrieval path, classifying chunks at index time and routing any request whose context contains a restricted chunk to the self-hosted model, with an audit log. Also, the demand assumptions are guesses; adoption of internal tools often concentrates on a minority of heavy users, so the per-user average can hold while peak load does not.</p>
</Fragment>

## Follow-ups

- Legal now says the HR restriction also covers employee names in any document. What happens to the hybrid design?
- The provider announces retirement of the model you launched on, with 60 days' notice. Who runs the migration with three engineers, and what stops?
- How would you know, a month after launch, whether the assistant is worth its $19,000?

## Common mistakes

- Sizing GPUs from average load instead of the busiest hour.
- Quoting a GPU count without stating the model size, precision and the throughput assumption behind it.
- Comparing GPU rental with the API bill and leaving out the engineers who would run the fleet.
- Treating the data restriction as a reason to self-host everything rather than the restricted slice.
- Enforcing the restriction by collection name in the UI instead of on the retrieval path.
