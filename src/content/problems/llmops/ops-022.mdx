## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Debugging, 30 minutes">
Our fine-tuned support model passes 91% of the offline eval and 68% of the production review. The release before it was 74 and 70. Which cause explains the gap, what else is wrong, and do we ship the next version on Thursday?
</Callout>

The numbers, constructed for this problem:

```text
                     train rows   eval rows   offline eval pass   production review pass
v2 (June)               7,200        800           74%                  70%   (200 conversations, Sept)
v3 (October)           19,400      2,160           91%                  68%   (200 conversations, Oct)

offline eval:      LLM judge with a 1-5 rubric, pass at 4 or more, on the held-out rows of build()
production review: two support leads, 200 sampled conversations per release, their own pass/fail rubric
October:           the Team plan launched on 1 October; 9% of October conversations mention it
```

The build script, v2 to v3:

```diff
 def build(raw, seed=7):
     rows = scrub(raw)
     rows = exact_dedup(rows)
+    rows = rows + paraphrase(rows, n=2, model="gen-large")
-    rows = [r for r in rows if judge(r) >= 3]
+    rows = [r for r in rows if judge(r) >= 4]
-    random.Random(seed).shuffle(rows)
+    random.Random(seed + 1).shuffle(rows)
     cut = int(len(rows) * 0.9)
     return rows[:cut], rows[cut:]
```

You do not have a near-duplicate report between the two sides, per-item judge scores, or a production sample from before the Team plan launch.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>The split runs after augmentation, so each source row and its two paraphrases are split independently and about 99% of eval items have a sibling in training: the offline eval measures recall of a reworded answer, not the task. The other three causes are real or plausible but each was present in v2, or acts on both sides of the split equally, so none explains why the gap went from 4 points to 23. I would not ship on the 91; I would re-score v3 on a group-split eval before Thursday and decide on that number.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Why the split leaks.</b> After paraphrasing, each source row exists three times. A row-level shuffle sends each copy to eval with probability 0.1 independently, so an eval item is clean only if both of its siblings also went to eval: 0.1 × 0.1 = 1%. The judge filter removes some paraphrases and breaks some groups, which lowers the share but leaves most eval items with a sibling in training. Compare Lee et al.'s 4.60% of C4 validation examples with a near-duplicate in training, which they treated as a problem; here the figure is closer to 100%.</p>
<p><b>Ranking the rest.</b> The Team plan shift is the second cause: it is real, it costs production a few points on 9% of traffic, and it is the one most likely to explain the drop from 70 to 68, but v2 met the same October traffic. The threshold change from 3 to 4 is a real defect worth its own experiment, because a judge trained on general preferences can prefer longer or easier answers, and AlpaGasus checked its filter against a size-matched random subset for exactly this reason; it is applied before the split, so it shifts both sides. The rubric difference is constant across versions and explains the 4 points of v2, not the change. The seed change is noise: any seed reproduces the leak.</p>
<p><b>What I would collect, and what I would do without it.</b> Group by source row id and count eval items with a sibling in training; run the near-duplicate check across the split, because exact dedup ran before paraphrasing and cannot see paraphrases. Re-score v3 on the eval items whose group sits entirely in eval. v2's 800 eval rows are no help: v3 re-shuffled with a new seed, so most of them are in v3's training side. Without the row ids, regenerate the split by group with the same filter and re-run the judge; that costs one evaluation run, not a training run.</p>
</Fragment>

### The Thursday decision

Two answers are defensible.

**Ship v2 on Thursday, re-evaluate v3, ship it next week if it wins.** The cost is a week without whatever v3 adds, and the Team plan questions stay unanswered by either model, so a stopgap prompt instruction for the Team plan ships with v2.

**Ship v3 on Thursday behind a 10% canary, compared live with v2 on the support leads' rubric.** Production review is the measurement nobody can leak into. The cost: 200 reviewed conversations per arm take the leads about a week at their current pace, so the decision is made on production traffic exposed to a model whose offline number is meaningless, and a regression in a rare intent will not show up in 20 conversations a day.

I would take the first unless the clean re-score is ready by Wednesday night. The failure mode of that choice is that the clean eval itself is small: 2,160 eval rows split by group leaves about 720 source groups, and a two-point difference on about 720 independent groups is inside the noise, so the re-score can only rule out a large regression, not confirm a small gain. The decision memo states that, and names the support leads' review as the tie-breaker.

### What changes in the build

Split before augmenting, by source row id, and augment only the training side. Run the near-duplicate check between the sides as a gate, with the threshold chosen on a sample of the paraphrases. Freeze a regression set by source id and exclude it from every later build, and add an October slice with Team plan conversations so the shift is measured, not inferred.

## Follow-ups

- *The paraphrasing model is also the judge's model family. Does that matter for the training set, the eval, or both?* For the eval it is moot once the split is fixed; for training, the filter may favour the generator's own phrasing, which the size-matched random baseline would show.
- *Product pushes back: the 91 was in the board update. How do you correct a number already reported, and who signs the correction?*
- *Two senior engineers disagree: one wants the clean eval, the other says production review is the only number that matters and offline evals should be dropped. How do you decide without deciding by rank?* Agree in advance which number gates the release and what difference on it would change the decision, then check whether the offline eval, once clean, predicts the production review across the last three releases.
- *What would you need to see in production to stop reviewing 200 conversations per release by hand?*

## Common mistakes

- Picking the Team plan shift because it is the only cause in the evidence that happened in October.
- Concluding that augmentation does not help, when the evidence says nothing yet about whether it helps; it only says the eval cannot tell.
- Running exact deduplication across the split and calling it clean.
- Treating the seed change as the cause because it is a changed line next to the split.
- Shipping v3 on the strength of a 2-point gain on a small, re-split eval without an interval.
