## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Critique, 30 minutes">
"Here is the fine-tuning plan for our claims assistant. The team wants approval at Thursday's review so they can start Monday. Review it: list every problem you find, rank them, and say whether you approve it, change it or reject it."
</Callout>

<p class="tiny">The company, team and plan are a scenario, written for this exercise.</p>

> **Claims assistant fine-tune: plan for approval**
>
> **Goal.** The assistant helps claims adjusters at a home insurer answer coverage questions and write the claim summary. Today a hosted frontier model does this with a 3,000-token system prompt. We will fine-tune an 8B open-weight instruct model to (a) answer in our house style and (b) know our policy terms (limits, deductibles, exclusions) so adjusters stop pasting policy text into the chat. Running it ourselves keeps claim data in our account.
>
> **Data.** 600 conversations exported from the current assistant, filtered to those an adjuster rated 4 or 5 of 5. Each is a `messages` list: system, adjuster, assistant, sometimes several rounds. 140 of them are coverage questions where the assistant quoted a limit or deductible from the 2026 policy wording.
>
> **Training.** TRL `SFTTrainer`, defaults except where stated. LoRA r=8, alpha 16, `target_modules=["q_proj", "v_proj"]`. Learning rate 2e-5 (the TRL default). 5 epochs, to make sure the policy terms are learned. Effective batch 16.
>
> **Evaluation.** We generated 200 eval questions by asking the frontier model to paraphrase 200 of the training questions, so the eval covers the same topics in different words. We compare the tuned model with the 8B base model (no system prompt) on these 200, scored 1 to 10 by the frontier model. We keep the final checkpoint.
>
> **Success criteria.** Training loss below 0.1, and the tuned model beats the base on the 200-question eval.
>
> **Risks.** If self-hosting slips, we fall back to OpenAI's fine-tuning API with the same data. We have not used it before, but the data format is compatible.
>
> **Timeline.** One week of training and evaluation, ship the week after.

List the flaws first. Then open the checklist and tick what you found.

## Answer

The plan reads well: a clear goal, a filtered dataset, an eval, success criteria and a fallback. Most of its flaws are in defaults nobody looked at.

**Flaws a reviewer misses on first read.**

1. *The loss covers the adjusters' words.* The data is `messages` and the trainer runs on defaults. TRL resolves its default to full-sequence loss for conversational language-modeling data; training on assistant turns only needs `assistant_only_loss=True` and generation markers in the template. As written, the model is trained to predict adjuster turns as well as answers.
2. *The learning rate and the adapter placement are set for something else.* 2e-5 is TRL's default for full fine-tuning. TRL's own adapter tip is about 1e-4, and Thinking Machines found the LoRA optimum about ten times the full fine-tuning one. Adapters on `q_proj` and `v_proj` only are the placement Thinking Machines found underperforming; Unsloth's guide says all major linear layers. The run would likely look as if fine-tuning does not work.
3. *The eval set is the training set in different words.* Each of the 200 eval questions is a paraphrase of a training question, so the eval rewards memorising training answers. Five epochs make that likelier. The holdout has to be real conversations split off by claim or by date before any processing.

**Flaws most reviewers catch.**

4. *Policy terms in the weights.* 140 examples teach limits and deductibles from the 2026 wording. The base model has never seen this insurer's policy, so these are facts it does not know, the category Gekhman et al. found raises hallucination as it is fitted. When the 2027 wording changes a limit, the model will quote the old one with confidence. Policy terms belong in retrieval, quoted with the section.
5. *The baseline is the wrong one.* The 8B base with no system prompt is not what ships otherwise. The comparison is the current frontier model with its 3,000-token prompt, and the 8B base with the best prompt the team can write.
6. *Success is defined on training loss.* Loss below 0.1 is past Unsloth's 0.2 overfitting sign and says nothing about answers. Success is a held-out task metric beating the baseline by a margin set in advance, with no regression slice failing.
7. *The last checkpoint.* Five epochs, more than the 1 to 3 Unsloth suggests, and the final checkpoint kept. Evaluate checkpoints on a dev set and keep the peak.
8. *The fallback does not exist.* OpenAI's self-serve fine-tuning closed to organisations that had never used it on 7 May 2026, and existing customers lose job creation on 6 January 2027. "We have not used it before" means it is not available. It would also move claim data out of the account, which is the plan's own reason for self-hosting.
9. *No regression slices.* The model also writes the claim summary and must decline to make coverage decisions. Neither is measured. A judge scoring 1 to 10 with no calibration against adjusters adds noise on top.

**The judgement calls.** 600 examples is acceptable if they are reviewed against a written rubric, deduplicated and split by claim: OpenAI's guide reports improvements from 50 to 100 examples, and quality matters more than count at this size. Filtering to adjuster ratings of 4 or 5 is acceptable if someone reads a sample, because a rating measures the adjuster's satisfaction, not the answer's correctness.

**Approve, change or reject.** Two defensible answers:

- *Fix and run.* Keep both goals, fix the mask, the learning rate, the placement, the eval and the baseline, and move policy terms to retrieval inside the same project. Cost: the one-week timeline becomes about three, because the holdout and the retrieval path are new work.
- *Narrow the scope.* Drop goal (b) entirely: policy terms go to retrieval, and the fine-tune teaches only house style and the summary format, trained on conversations where the policy text was in context. Cost: adjusters keep some pasting until retrieval ships, and the team builds two things.

I would narrow the scope. Goal (b) is the one the evidence says fine-tuning does badly, and it is the one with a date on which it becomes wrong. The written decision: approved as narrowed; retrieval for policy terms; the eval rebuilt from held-out claims; ship only if the tuned model matches the prompted frontier model on the held-out set and no regression slice drops.

**Where the narrowed plan fails.** The training answers were written by adjusters and the old assistant together, and a 4-of-5 rating does not mean the answer was right. A tuned model copies the style and also the habits, including any adjuster's wrong reading of an exclusion. Sample 50 training answers for a correctness check by a senior adjuster before training.

## Follow-ups

- *Pushback: the review is in two days and the team lead says your changes turn a one-week project into a month. Which two fixes do you require for approval, and which risks do you accept in writing?*
- *Reversal: what result from the retrieval path would make you put policy terms back into the fine-tuning data?* Retrieval failing to find the governing clause on a measured share of real coverage questions, with the failures concentrated in a small, stable set of terms that change rarely.
- *Two senior reviewers disagree: one says approve as written and learn from the run, the other says reject. How do you settle it without deciding by rank?* Agree in advance what result of the run as written would change either reviewer's mind; if no result would, the run teaches nothing and should not happen.
- *Operating: the 2027 policy wording lands on 1 January with eleven changed limits. What in your design changes that day, and what does not?*

## Common mistakes

- Approving because every section is present, without asking where each number came from.
- Treating the paraphrased eval as "different words, so held out".
- Accepting the base model without a prompt as the baseline.
- Missing that TRL defaults differ by dataset format, so "defaults" in a plan is a decision nobody made.
- Rejecting 600 examples as too few without asking how they were reviewed and split.
