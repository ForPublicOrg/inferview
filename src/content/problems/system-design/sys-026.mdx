## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design round, 40 minutes">
"Design a support assistant for our broadband provider. How do you know it is helping and not making things worse?"
</Callout>

What you learn by asking (scenario):

- 10,000 support chats a week today, all handled by 60 agents in an existing ticketing system that stays. About a fifth of customers contact again within seven days about the same issue.
- Common issues: billing questions, outages, router troubleshooting (the customer restarts or reconfigures their own router), plan changes, and refund requests for outage credits.
- Outage credits follow a published policy; agents grant goodwill credits beyond it at their discretion.
- The holiday peak starts in six weeks. The support director wants the assistant live before it and a number for the board; the head of the agents' team worries the assistant will send them angrier customers.

Design the assistant, its escalation, and the experiment. Choose the holdout share and the measurement window and defend them. State what you give up, and write the launch decision memo.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask how the ticketing system links a second contact to the first one today, and how often it fails to.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask agents which escalations they dread receiving, and what information is missing when they arrive.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask what share of outage-credit requests are inside the published policy and what share are goodwill.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>A conversation loop with tools for account lookup, outage status, router diagnostics and an outage-credit tool that enforces the published policy; goodwill stays with agents. Escalation has explicit triggers and writes a structured record into the existing ticket. Offline, tasks against a simulated customer scored on account state, with escalation-required and router tasks where the simulated customer sometimes does the step wrong, and pass^k over repeats. Online, a holdout by customer hash, primary metric issues resolved without a re-contact in seven days per customer, guardrails on credit errors, escalation quality and agent handle time on escalations. I would launch at 50% before the peak if the offline gate passes. What I give up: a headline number before the peak, and goodwill automation.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Scope and tools.</strong> The assistant answers billing and plan questions, checks outage status, runs router diagnostics and guides restarts, and grants outage credits inside the published policy through a tool that enforces it. Plan changes and goodwill credits go to agents. The policy set is small enough to sit in a cached prompt under 200,000 tokens, which removes a retrieval failure mode for the first release.</p>
<p><strong>Escalation.</strong> Triggers: the customer asks for a person (honoured on the first ask); a disputed tool refusal; a listed topic (cancellation threats, vulnerable customers, safety); no progress on the stated issue after a set number of turns; or an answer the assistant cannot tie to a policy clause or a tool result. The handoff writes into the existing ticket: one-line issue, account, diagnostics run and results, credits checked and the tool's reason, the policy clause. The agents' lead gets the metric that answers his worry: share of escalations where the agent had to ask the customer for something the record held, and agent handle time on escalated chats against non-escalated ones.</p>
<p><strong>Offline gate.</strong> tau-bench's setup fits: tools plus policy, a simulated customer, success judged on final account state, and pass^k because a customer gets one try. Router troubleshooting is the tau2-bench dual-control case, where the customer acts on their own device; the paper reports significant drops when agents move from a passive user to that setting, so the simulated customer sometimes skips a step or reports the wrong light, and those tasks are reported as their own slice. Escalation-required tasks are in the set; never escalating fails them.</p>
<p><strong>The experiment, and the quantity I choose.</strong> Assignment by stable hash of the customer, so a re-contact lands in the same arm. The primary metric is the share of customers whose issue was resolved without a re-contact within seven days. The holdout share is the decision. With 10,000 chats a week and a re-contact rate near 20%:</p>
<ul>
<li><em>50/50</em>: 5,000 a week per arm, so after one window the 95% interval on the difference is about ±1.96 × √(2 × 0.2 × 0.8 / 5,000) ≈ ±1.6 points. Cost: half of peak traffic gets the unproven assistant.</li>
<li><em>10/90 with 10% on the assistant</em>: about ±1.96 × √(0.16/1,000 + 0.16/9,000) ≈ ±2.6 points. Cost: a weaker readout, and the peak passes with the assistant on 10% of traffic, so the director's number is a pilot number.</li>
</ul>
<p>Reasonable choices sit between these; below about 10% the interval is wider than the effect worth acting on. I choose 50/50 if the offline gate passes, because the peak is when agents are scarcest and the larger arm gives a readout one window after launch. The seven-day window is the trade: shorter reads out faster and misses customers who come back on day five; longer delays every decision.</p>
<p><strong>Guardrails.</strong> Audited credit errors in both directions (granted against policy, refused within it), escalation record completeness, agent handle time on escalations, and churn by arm as a lagging check, because a customer who gives up and leaves never re-contacts.</p>
<p><strong>The launch memo, as sent.</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Launch at 50% of customers by hash two weeks before the peak if offline pass^3 on the task set, including escalation and router slices, meets the bar the director and the agents' lead agree on before the first run.</li>
<li>Primary metric: resolved without re-contact within seven days, read out weekly with its interval. Gross containment is reported only beside it, labelled.</li>
<li>Roll back to 10% if: re-contact is worse in the assistant arm by more than the interval for two consecutive windows; any audited credit error above the agents' rate on the same audit; agent handle time on escalated chats rises against the holdout.</li>
<li>Not measured before launch: churn effects, which lag by months.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What I give up.</strong> A board number before the peak ends; goodwill credits stay manual, which caps containment on credit requests; and half of peak customers are in an experiment.</p>
<p><strong>Where this fails.</strong> The re-contact link depends on the ticketing system matching a second contact to the first issue. If customers come back by phone and the link is missed, re-contacts are undercounted in the arm that pushed customers to the phone, which is the assistant arm. I would measure the link rate per channel in the first week, and if phone re-contacts are not linked, count any contact from the same customer within the window, accepting the overcount.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Pushback: the director refuses a 50% experiment during the peak and wants 100% on day one. What do you accept, and what do you put in writing as the risk?</em></li>
<li><em>Reversal: what would you need to see in the first month to let the assistant grant goodwill credits under a fixed amount?</em> An audit showing agents' goodwill decisions are predictable from features the assistant sees, and assistant drafts that match them at agents' own inter-agent agreement rate, with the credit tool bounding the amount.</li>
<li><em>The agents' lead and the director disagree on whether escalation rate is a target to reduce. How do you decide without deciding by rank?</em> Compare resolved-without-re-contact for escalated and non-escalated chats in the assistant arm; if escalated chats resolve better, an escalation target would trade resolution for a lower count.</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>

### Checklist

- Makes resolution without a re-contact, per customer, the primary metric and assigns by customer hash.
- Chooses a holdout share and window and computes what each buys.
- Enforces published policy in a tool and leaves discretionary credits with people.
- Lists escalation triggers that can be tested, including the first ask for a person.
- Specifies the handoff record and a measure of whether agents still have to re-ask.
- Includes escalation-required and dual-control tasks in the offline set and uses pass^k.
- Has guardrails for harm in both directions and a lagging churn check.
- States what is given up and writes a launch memo with rollback triggers.
- Names a failure mode in the measurement itself.

## Follow-ups

- *Escalations rise 30% in the assistant arm but resolution without re-contact also rises. Is that a success, and who decides?*
- *An outage hits during the peak and chat volume triples. What does the assistant do differently, and what stays the same?*
- *The model vendor ships an update mid-experiment. Do you pin, restart the readout, or continue?*

## Common mistakes

- Reporting the share of chats closed by the bot as success.
- Randomising per conversation, so a customer's re-contact lands in the other arm.
- Asking the model to enforce refund policy written in the prompt.
- Treating escalation as a failure to minimise rather than an outcome with a quality bar.
- An offline set without tasks that require escalation.
