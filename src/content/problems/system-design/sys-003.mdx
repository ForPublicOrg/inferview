## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Estimate the daily model cost of our multi-tenant support assistant with prompt caching, and tell me which assumption would change your caching plan."
</Callout>

What you get when you ask:

- 1,000,000 queries a day, almost all inside a 10-hour business window (scenario).
- Every request starts with an 8,000-token tenant prefix: tools, system prompt and that tenant's policy text, identical for every request from the same tenant. Then 1,500 tokens of retrieved chunks and the question, different every time. Answers average 300 output tokens.
- Tenants (scenario): 20 large tenants with 40,000 queries a day each, and 2,000 small tenants with 100 queries a day each. Within a tenant, arrivals are close to random.
- Claude Sonnet 5 list prices per million tokens, checked 28 Oct 2026: $2 input, $10 output, $2.50 for a 5-minute cache write, $4 for a 1-hour write, $0.20 for a read.
- The cache lifetime can be chosen per request.

Choose a lifetime for each group of tenants and estimate the daily cost in dollars.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>For one small tenant, how long is the typical gap between two of its requests, and how does it compare with each lifetime?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Which part of the bill does caching not touch at all, and how big is it?</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask whether tenants share an organization and a workspace, or are isolated.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>About <b>$7,660 a day</b> with the 1-hour lifetime for small tenants (either lifetime for large ones), or <b>$9,220</b> with the 5-minute lifetime everywhere; $22,000 with no cache. $6,000 of any plan is retrieved input and output, which caching does not touch. The plan turns on the request rate per small tenant: one request every 6 minutes misses a 5-minute cache 43 percent of the time and almost never misses a 1-hour one.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>The fixed part.</b> Per request, 1,500 × $2 / 1M + 300 × $10 / 1M = $0.006, so $6,000 a day whatever the cache does. The prefix at list price is 8,000 × $2 / 1M = $0.016 a request, $16,000 a day uncached.</p>
<p><b>Large tenants.</b> 40,000 queries over 10 hours is 67 a minute. Each prefix is read many times inside any lifetime, so after one write a day the multiplier is 0.1: 800,000 × $0.016 × 0.1 = $1,280 a day on either tier.</p>
<p><b>Small tenants, 5-minute tier.</b> 100 queries over 10 hours is one every 6 minutes. With random arrivals the chance the next request comes within 5 minutes is 1 − e^(−5/6) = 0.565. The multiplier is 0.565 × 0.1 + 0.435 × 1.25 = 0.60 (0.606 with the day's first write). 200,000 × $0.016 × 0.606 = $1,940.</p>
<p><b>Small tenants, 1-hour tier.</b> A 6-minute mean gap almost never exceeds an hour, so each tenant writes about once a day at 2x and reads 99 times: (2 + 99 × 0.1) / 100 = 0.119. 200,000 × $0.016 × 0.119 = $381.</p>
<p><b>Totals.</b> 1-hour for small tenants: 6,000 + 1,280 + 381 = $7,661. 5-minute everywhere: 6,000 + 1,280 + 1,940 = $9,220. The dominant term in either plan is the $6,000 of retrieved input and output; the caching decision is worth about $1,560 a day.</p>
<p><b>Two defensible designs.</b> Per-tenant prefixes with the 1-hour lifetime for small tenants, as above. Or split the prefix with two breakpoints: a 6,000-token shared block (tools and system prompt) that every tenant reads, then the 2,000-token tenant policy. The shared block stays warm on all traffic and the total is about $7,615, nearly the same money, but it moves tenant policy after the shared instructions, which changes model behaviour and needs an eval. The failure mode of the recommended 1-hour plan is a policy edit: every edit by a small tenant pays a fresh 2x write, and a tenant whose admin saves the policy every few minutes pays more than with no cache.</p>
<p><b>The constraint that flips it.</b> If security requires each enterprise tenant in its own workspace, caches are isolated per workspace and the shared block of the second design stops being shared; the per-tenant plan is then the only one, and the small-tenant request rate decides its cost.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Finance wants the bill under $7,000 a day without touching answer length. The cache plan is already chosen; which term do you cut, and what quality risk do you sign for?</em></li>
<li><em>What would you need to see in the usage data to move small tenants back to the 5-minute tier?</em></li>
<li><em>Security and finance disagree on workspace-per-tenant. How do you put the cost of isolation in front of them without deciding by rank?</em></li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

The band, $7,300 to $9,600 a day, admits either lifetime and rounding of the hit rates. Answers near $7,600 that applied a 0.1 multiplier to every tenant land in the band for the wrong reason: the 5-minute tier at small-tenant traffic is far from 0.1. Answers near $22,000 left caching off; answers near $4,700 left out output.

## Follow-ups

- *What does a 0.05x read do?* On Sonnet 5.5, at the same $2/$10 list price, the read multiplier halves, which trims the large-tenant line by about $640 a day and barely moves the small-tenant one.
- *Traffic doubles in the large tenants only. What changes?* Their cost doubles at a 0.1 multiplier; the small-tenant line and the plan do not.
- *How do you check the plan after launch?* Cache read and write tokens per tenant per day from the usage fields, compared with the predicted multiplier per group.

## Common mistakes

- Applying one hit rate to all traffic when the hit rate is a property of each prefix's request rate.
- Applying the cache discount to the retrieved chunks, which change every request.
- Ignoring that a miss pays a write premium, larger on the 1-hour tier.
- Choosing the lifetime without asking how tenants are isolated.
