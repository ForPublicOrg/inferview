## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Since last Monday, 9 percent of chat requests fail with 429 between 17:30 and 19:00. The provider says we are under our limits. Find the cause."
</Callout>

The product calls Claude Sonnet 5 at the Build tier (5,000 requests, 5,000,000 input tokens and 1,000,000 output tokens per minute) through an in-house gateway. Two changes shipped last Monday: a "summarise this thread" feature, and a prompt change. The gateway's configuration and the relevant part of the prompt builder:

```yaml
# gateway.yaml
upstream: anthropic
max_inflight: 200
queue_timeout_ms: 2000
on_queue_timeout: {status: 429, body: {error: "too many requests"}}
retry:
  on: [429, 529]
  attempts: 3
  backoff_ms: 0
```

```diff
 def build_request(thread, mode):
-    system = SYSTEM_PROMPT
+    system = f"Current time: {now_iso()}\n" + SYSTEM_PROMPT
     body = {"model": "claude-sonnet-5", "system": system, "messages": thread.messages}
+    if mode == "summary":
+        body.update(stream=True, max_tokens=4096, messages=thread.all_messages())
     return body
```

## Answer

Most of the 429s users see are the gateway's own. They have no upstream request ID and the gateway's body, and they arrive after exactly the 2-second queue timeout. The provider's 429s are 0.3 percent of calls; the users' are 9 percent. The gap is the gateway.

The gateway admits at most 200 requests in flight. By Little's law, in-flight requests are arrival rate times duration. Before the release, 28 a second at 4 s is 112. After it, 20 percent of requests are summaries that stream for about 40 s each, so the mean duration becomes 0.8 × 4 + 0.2 × 40 = 11.2 s, and 30 a second at 11.2 s is 336 in flight against a cap of 200. New requests wait in the queue, time out at 2 s, and get a 429 from the gateway. The provider never sees them, which is why the provider says the organization is under its limits.

The evidence is incomplete: there is no in-flight gauge, and the 40-second summary duration is inferred from the mean. I would ask for the duration split by route and add an in-flight metric before changing anything. Without them, the arithmetic above is enough to act on, because the rejection pattern (no upstream ID, 2,001 ms waits) points at the gateway regardless of the exact durations.

### The fix, and its cost

Give summaries their own concurrency pool, sized from their own rate and duration, so a long stream cannot take a chat slot; or raise the cap after checking that the provider's limits and the gateway's memory hold at the new in-flight count. The first is the safer change. It costs a decision about what summaries get when their pool is full: a queue with a visible wait, or a "try again shortly" message.

### The secondary defects, ranked

1. **The prompt change broke the cache.** Putting the current time at the start of the system prompt changes the prefix on every request, so nothing after it is read from the cache. Input-token usage doubled, which costs money now and will hit the input-token limit as traffic grows. Move the timestamp to the end of the last user message.
2. **The retry policy retries 429s three times with no backoff and ignores retry-after**, so each provider 429 becomes up to four requests in the same second. It is small today and becomes a storm the day the provider limit is reached. Honour retry-after and add jittered backoff.
3. **Summaries send the whole thread** with `max_tokens: 4096`. On this provider `max_tokens` does not count toward the output-token limit, but long threads are input tokens that the broken cache now bills in full.

### Why the wrong picks are tempting

The cache regression is visible and large, and it is the change most engineers would blame first; it does not explain 429s that never reached the provider. Per-second enforcement of per-minute limits is documented and catches teams out, but the request rate is at 36 percent of the limit. The retry loop is in the log, retrying within 71 ms, but it can only multiply 429s that the provider sent.

## Follow-ups

- *The product team wants summaries to never wait. What do you give up to guarantee it?*
- *How would you have caught this before release?* A load test with the new route mix, and an alert on in-flight requests against the cap.
- *Should the gateway return 429 for its own queue timeouts?* A 503 with a distinct body would have separated the two sources in the first dashboard anyone opened.

## Common mistakes

- Asking the provider for a higher tier when the provider never saw the failing requests.
- Fixing the cache regression and declaring the incident closed.
- Raising `max_inflight` without checking what the higher in-flight count does to provider limits and gateway memory.
- Treating every 429 as the provider's.
