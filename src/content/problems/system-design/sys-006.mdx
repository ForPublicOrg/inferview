## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design round, 45 minutes">
"Design an assistant that answers employees' questions from internal documents, with citations, and never shows anyone content they cannot open in the source system."
</Callout>

What you learn by asking (scenario):

- 50,000 employees; 10 million documents across a wiki, a shared drive and a ticket system. The drive has sharing links and external guests; the wiki and tickets use groups.
- Permissions are owned by an entitlement service that the identity team will not change. It answers "can user U read document D" in batches, and its owners ask that the assistant not multiply their load.
- The CISO's requirement: a revoked user must stop seeing a document's content "promptly", and she wants a number.
- p95 under 3 seconds to a complete answer. Four engineers, ten weeks to a pilot with 2,000 users.

Cover where the permission check runs, how revocation propagates, the index, the cost per query, and the eval. State what you give up, and write the risk note you would send the CISO.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Copy group-level ACLs into the index and filter inside the vector and BM25 queries on the user's groups, resolved from a short-TTL directory cache. For the drive, whose links and guests cannot be expressed as groups, check the final candidates against the entitlement service in one batch per query. Revocation lag gets its own stream and its own metric, and I commit to a bound per case in the risk note. At about 574 GB of vectors in one namespace, a query costs about $0.009 of reads and $0.039 of model, so partitioning by source and trimming the chunks sent to the model are the cost levers. What I give up: p95 headroom on drive queries, and recall for narrow users under restrictive filters.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>The decision: copied ACLs or a late check.</strong> Two answers are defensible. <em>Copy ACLs into the index</em>: each chunk stores the group IDs that may read it, and both searches filter on the user's groups. Retrieval returns only readable chunks, the entitlement service sees no query load, and latency is unchanged. The cost is revocation lag (the index is as fresh as the copy) and sources whose permissions are not groups. <em>Check late</em>: retrieve without ACL filtering, then ask the entitlement service about every candidate. Always current, with the identity team's own logic. The cost is a batch call on every query and the post-filter count problem: if a contractor can read 5% of the corpus and readability is independent of relevance, all of a top-20 list is unreadable with probability 0.95^20 ≈ 36%, so the late check needs heavy over-fetch, which multiplies the entitlement load.</p>
<p>I take the copied ACLs as the default and the late check only for the drive's final candidates. That keeps the entitlement load to one batch per drive-hitting query, which I agree with the identity team as a rate before the pilot. Chunks store groups, never users: membership changes then never touch the index, and Pinecone's cap of 10,000 values in <code>$in</code> and its warning about user-ID filters apply to the user's group list, which I measure (some employees will be in hundreds of groups).</p>
<p><strong>Revocation, as three cases.</strong> Removal from a group: bounded by the directory cache TTL, which I set at 60 seconds. A document's ACL edited: a priority stream rewrites only that document's chunk metadata, with a lag metric at p99. A drive link revoked: zero lag, because the late check asks the source of truth. The CISO gets a number per case, not one number.</p>
<p><strong>Index and sizing.</strong> Scenario: eight 800-token chunks per document, text-embedding-3-small at 1,536 dimensions, about 1,000 bytes of metadata, a 36-byte ID: 80 million records × 7,180 bytes = 574 GB. Storage at $0.33 per GB-month is about $190 a month; embedding 64 billion tokens at $0.02 per million is $1,280; the initial upsert at 1 WU per KB and $4 per million WU is about $2,300. BM25 runs beside the vectors, because employees paste ticket numbers and error codes; both are fused and reranked.</p>
<p><strong>Cost per query.</strong> In one namespace a query reads 574 RU, $0.0092 at $16 per million. The model call with 20 chunks of 800 tokens plus 1,000 tokens of instructions, and 500 tokens out, on Claude Sonnet 5.5, is 17,000 × $2 / 1M + 500 × $10 / 1M = $0.039. Scenario traffic of 5 queries per employee per workday is 5.25 million queries a month: about $48,000 of reads and $205,000 of model. Both are worth attacking. Partitioning by source and routing queries to one partition when the query names one (a ticket number goes to tickets) cuts reads roughly by the share of queries routed; sending 8 chunks instead of 20 cuts model input from 17,000 to 7,400 tokens, if the recall@8 on the eval set holds.</p>
<p><strong>Latency.</strong> Group resolution from cache runs in parallel with query embedding; vector and BM25 run in parallel; the drive check runs while the reranker scores the non-drive candidates. Generation is the largest share of 3 seconds, so the answer streams and the 3-second budget is agreed as time to a complete answer at p95 on a pilot load test, not assumed.</p>
<p><strong>Eval.</strong> A permission suite of synthetic users in known groups, asking questions whose only answer is in content they cannot read; any restricted chunk in the retrieved set fails the build, and the suite runs as a production canary. A revocation prober per case above. Retrieval measured as 1 minus recall@20 with slices for identifier queries and per source; citation precision; no-answer rate per source, which is where an entitlement-service outage would show.</p>
<p><strong>The risk note, as sent.</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Group removal stops retrieval within 60 seconds at p99, measured by a prober every 5 minutes. Document ACL edits within 5 minutes at p99. Drive link revocations take effect immediately.</li>
<li>If the entitlement service is unavailable or rate-limited, drive results are dropped, not served unchecked. Users will see fewer drive answers during that time.</li>
<li>Not covered: content copied from a restricted document into a readable one by a person. The index enforces the source's permissions, not their intent.</li>
<li>Rollback: any permission-suite failure in production disables the assistant for the affected source until fixed.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What I give up.</strong> Drive queries pay a round trip on the latency path. Narrow users get lower recall where the in-index filter is restrictive. Cross-user answer caching is off the table. The pilot does not cover Purview-style encrypted documents, which are excluded until their usage rights can be mirrored.</p>
<p><strong>Where this fails.</strong> The copied ACLs are only as correct as the connector's mapping of each source's permission model, and inherited folder permissions are where mappings go wrong. A mapping bug fails open silently. The permission suite catches it only for the cases it contains, so the suite is seeded from real ACL shapes sampled from each source, and a weekly job compares a sample of index ACLs with the entitlement service's answers and pages on any disagreement.</p>
</Fragment>

<li><em>Reversal: what would you need to see in the pilot to drop the late check for the drive and rely on copied ACLs?</em> A drive change feed whose permission events arrive within the bound at p99 for a month, and a weekly ACL comparison with zero disagreements over the same period.</li>

### Checklist

- Places the permission check before text reaches the model, and rejects a post-filter on a fixed top-k with the empty-result reason.
- Chooses between copied ACLs and a late check, with the cost of each, and uses the late check only where permissions cannot be copied.
- Stores groups on chunks, not users, and names the filter-size limit.
- Splits revocation into cases and gives a measured bound for each.
- Sizes the index from stated assumptions and prices a query, naming which term dominates.
- Keeps a lexical path for identifier queries.
- Has a permission suite that fails on retrieval, not on the model's wording.
- States what is given up and sends a risk note with a fail-closed rule and a rollback trigger.
- Names a failure mode of the chosen design and the check that would catch it.

## Follow-ups

- *The pilot's p95 is 3.4 seconds and the drive check accounts for 500 ms of it. Which do you cut: the drive check, the reranker or the chunk count, and what changes in the risk note?*
- *A user is fired at 9:00. Walk through every path by which they could still retrieve content at 9:01.*
- *The CISO asks for "zero leaks". What can you measure, and what bound can you give her instead?*

## Common mistakes

- Filtering after retrieval on a fixed top-k and calling it secure.
- One freshness pipeline for content and permissions, with one lag.
- Storing user IDs on chunks, so every membership change rewrites the index.
- Sizing the vector store and never pricing the model call that dominates the running cost.
- A permission test that checks the answer text instead of the retrieved set.
