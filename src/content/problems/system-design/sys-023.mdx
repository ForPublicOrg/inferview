## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Marketing wants to launch 'ask questions about your whole codebase, up to a million tokens'. Before we commit, what does that cost per question and per day, and what would stop us serving it?"
</Callout>

What you get when you ask:

- Model: Claude Sonnet 5 on the API. List prices per million tokens, checked 28 Oct 2026: $2 input, $10 output, $2.50 for a 5-minute cache write, $4 for a 1-hour write, $0.20 for a cache read. The full 1M window is billed at standard rates.
- Scenario traffic from the beta: 5,000 sessions a day, almost all inside an 8-hour working window. A session loads one repository and asks 10 questions.
- Scenario sizes: the average loaded repository is 400,000 tokens. Each question is 200 tokens and each answer 800 tokens, and the conversation history is re-sent after the repository.
- Scenario gaps between questions in a session: median 3 minutes; about one gap in five is longer than 5 minutes; none is longer than an hour.
- The organization is on the Scale tier: 10,000,000 input tokens per minute for Sonnet 5.

Choose a caching plan and estimate the daily model cost. Then state the cost of the worst-case single question the promise allows, and what limits the launch other than money.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Which tokens in a session are the same from one question to the next, and how long can that prefix sit idle before it has to be written again?</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>With the 1-hour cache about <b>$12,500 a day</b>, $2.49 a session, $0.25 a question; with the 5-minute cache about $17,750, because one gap in five forces a rewrite of 400,000 tokens. Without caching, $40,900. The dominant term is the repository, written once and read nine times. The worst single question is a cold 1M-token write: $4.00 on the 1-hour tier. The launch is limited by the input-token rate limit before money: session starts at the peak minute write about 12.5M tokens against a 10M limit.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>History.</b> Question k re-sends the earlier turns: 200 + 1,000 × (k − 1) tokens. Over 10 questions that is 47,000 tokens, at most $0.094 a session uncached. Output is 8,000 tokens, $0.08. Together $0.174, under 10 percent of any plan below, so the repository dominates.</p>
<p><b>No cache.</b> 10 × 400,000 × $2 / 1M = $8.00 a session, $8.17 with history and output, $40,870 a day.</p>
<p><b>5-minute cache.</b> The first question writes 400,000 tokens at $2.50 per million: $1.00. Of the 9 later questions, about 1.8 follow a gap over 5 minutes and rewrite ($1.00 each); 7.2 read at $0.08 each. Session: 1.00 + 1.80 + 0.576 + 0.174 = $3.55; day: $17,750.</p>
<p><b>1-hour cache.</b> One write at $4 per million: $1.60. Nine reads: $0.72. Session: 1.60 + 0.72 + 0.174 = $2.49; day: $12,470, or $0.25 a question.</p>
<p><b>Which tier.</b> The 1-hour write costs $0.60 more per session. Each miss it prevents saves $1.00 − $0.08 = $0.92. It wins when expected misses per session exceed 0.65, that is, when more than about 7 percent of the 9 gaps exceed 5 minutes. The beta's 20 percent is well past that. The assumption that flips the plan is the gap distribution: with gaps that never exceed 5 minutes the 5-minute tier is $9,470 a day and the 1-hour tier loses.</p>
<p><b>Worst case.</b> The promise allows a 1,000,000-token repository: $2.00 uncached, $2.50 written to the 5-minute cache, $4.00 to the 1-hour cache, and $0.20 per cached question after that.</p>
<p><b>What limits the launch.</b> Cache writes count toward the input-token limit. 5,000 starts over 8 hours is 10.4 a minute; at a busiest minute three times that (choose and defend your own ratio), 31 starts × 400,000 = 12.5M tokens a minute against 10M. Requests start failing with 429 before the budget is touched. The plan needs a Custom-tier limit, or admission control on session starts, or both.</p>
<p><b>Self-hosting does not escape it.</b> On a published 1M-context open model, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-1M, the KV cache is 2 × 48 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 196,608 bytes per token: 78.6 GB for one 400,000-token session and 196.6 GB at the full million, before weights. One session's cache fills an 80 GB GPU.</p>
<p><b>Decision.</b> Two defensible plans. Honour the promise with the 1-hour cache: about $12,500 a day, a rate-limit increase, and admission control at the start of sessions. Or retrieve: send about 50,000 tokens of retrieved files per question instead of the repository, about $5,900 a day, no rate-limit problem, at the cost of answers to cross-file questions whose evidence retrieval missed, and of a promise marketing can no longer make. The failure mode of the recommended 1-hour plan is a repository edit mid-session: the cached prefix no longer matches, the next question writes the whole repository again, and an editing user can pay for several writes per session.</p>
</Fragment>

The band, $11,500 to $19,000 a day, admits either cache tier with reasonable rounding of the history and the share of long gaps. Answers near $9,500 assumed every gap stays under 5 minutes, which the beta data contradicts. Answers near $41,000 left caching off. Answers that apply a long-context surcharge used another provider's price rule.

## Follow-ups

- *Users edit files during a session and re-upload. How do you lay out the prompt so an edit to one file does not rewrite 400,000 tokens?* Order files by how rarely they change and put a breakpoint between stable and volatile files; a session has up to four.
- *What is the latency of the first question on a 400,000-token repository?* No published figure: measure time to first token for cold and cached prefixes at the sizes you will serve before stating an SLO.
- *Would the Batch API help?* Not for interactive questions; it would for a nightly "summarise this repository" job.

## Common mistakes

- Pricing the repository once per session and forgetting the rewrites after long gaps.
- Treating the 1M ceiling as the average; the cost line uses the size distribution, and the ceiling sets the worst case and the rate-limit spike.
- Applying OpenAI's long-context rule (2x input above 272K) to Anthropic's price list, or the reverse.
- Planning the budget and never checking the input-token limit, which cache writes count against.
