## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Design round, 35 minutes">
"Our support content is written in English first and partly translated. Customers ask in nine languages. Design retrieval for the support assistant: one index or several, and how you evaluate it per language."
</Callout>

What you learn by asking (scenario):

- 60 GB of vectors today. 60% of the articles exist only in English; the rest exist in English and one to eight translations, which lag the English version by weeks.
- Traffic by language: English 45%, Spanish 15%, German 10%, Japanese 10%, French 8%, Portuguese 6%, Korean, Italian and Dutch 2% each.
- Customers paste product codes and error strings in every language.
- The labelling budget for the first eval is $30,000; a native-speaker judgement of one query with its top 10 results costs $6 (scenario vendor quote).
- Answers must be in the customer's language. Two engineers, eight weeks.

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>One index with a multilingual embedding model, so a Japanese query can retrieve an English-only article, plus BM25 with a per-language analyser for product codes and error strings, and a language-and-version field so a stale translation is ranked below its fresher English source. Answers are generated in the customer's language and say when the source is English. The eval spends 5,000 judgements as a floor of 300 per language plus the rest by traffic, reporting recall@10 per language with its interval. What I give up: the cheaper per-language query, and the best possible monolingual quality in the two largest languages.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>The decision: one multilingual index or one index per language.</strong> Both are defensible. <em>One index, one multilingual model</em>: models such as M3-Embedding are trained for more than 100 languages in one space, so cross-lingual retrieval works without translation and there is one pipeline to run. Cost: every query scans the whole 60 GB namespace, 60 RU at 1 RU per GB, and quality per language is whatever the model gives, with no per-language tuning. <em>Per-language namespaces</em>: a German query reads only German content; the per-language analysers and, if wanted, a language-specific model can be tuned. Cost: 60% of the corpus is English-only, so most non-English queries must also search English, and the routing, merging and translation path is the hard part. A German query that searches German and English reads about 6 + 36 = 42 GB if German content is about 10% of the corpus and English 60%, so the saving is smaller than it looks.</p>
<p>I choose one index for the first release, because the English-only majority makes cross-lingual retrieval the main requirement, and two engineers cannot build and evaluate nine routing paths in eight weeks. OpenAI's embeddings page publishes MTEB averages and no per-language numbers, so the model choice is made on our own per-language eval, not the leaderboard.</p>
<p><strong>Lexical path.</strong> BM25 beside the vectors, with an analyser per language (Japanese and Korean need segmentation, German needs decompounding), and product codes indexed as exact tokens. Both lists are fused and reranked. M3's sparse output is an alternative lexical signal from the same model, which I would test against BM25 on the identifier slice.</p>
<p><strong>Translations and freshness.</strong> Each chunk carries its language, the source article ID and the source version it was translated from. When a translation lags its English source, the ranker prefers the English chunk and the answer is generated in the customer's language with a note that the source is English. That is an editorial decision for the support team, made once and written down.</p>
<p><strong>The eval, and the quantity I choose.</strong> $30,000 at $6 buys 5,000 judged queries. Allocation is the choice. <em>By traffic</em>: Korean, Italian and Dutch get 100 each, and recall@10 near 0.8 then has a 95% interval of about ±1.96 × √(0.8 × 0.2 / 100) ≈ ±7.8 points, too wide to see a regression. <em>Equal</em>: 555 each, about ±3.3 points everywhere, and English gets no more precision than Dutch despite 45% of traffic. I would set a floor of 300 per language (±4.5 points), which uses 2,700, and spread the remaining 2,300 by traffic. Reasonable floors run from about 250 to 400; a floor under 150 leaves the smallest languages unmeasurable. If a language has a contractual commitment, it gets more regardless of traffic.</p>
<p>Each language's set includes an identifier slice and a cross-lingual slice (answer only in English), reported separately, because the cross-lingual slice is where one-index and per-language designs differ.</p>
<p><strong>What I give up.</strong> Per-query read cost is the full namespace for every language. Per-language tuning waits for evidence. The smallest languages are measured with wider intervals than the largest.</p>
<p><strong>Where this fails.</strong> A multilingual model can retrieve by language before topic: a Spanish query ranks a loosely related Spanish article above the right English one. The cross-lingual slice measures exactly that, and if it is weak, the fix is query translation to English as a second retrieval leg, at the cost of a model call per query.</p>
</Fragment>

### Checklist

- Asks how much of the corpus exists only in English before choosing an index layout.
- Chooses between one multilingual index and per-language indexes with the cost of each, including the English fallback reads.
- Keeps a lexical path with per-language analysis and exact product codes.
- Tracks translation version against its source and decides what a stale translation does.
- Allocates the labelling budget with a per-language floor and computes the interval it buys.
- Reports cross-lingual and identifier slices per language.
- Picks the embedding model on its own per-language eval, not on an average benchmark.
- States what is given up and a failure mode of the chosen design with its measurement.

## Follow-ups

- *A customer writes in Spanglish. Which language's analyser runs, and does it matter?*
- *Japanese queries cost twice the tokens of English ones on your answer model. Where does that show up, and what do you change?*
- *A new language launches next quarter with 50 translated articles. What do you do on day one?*

## Common mistakes

- Choosing per-language indexes without counting the English-only content every language must reach.
- Evaluating in English and assuming the other eight follow.
- Allocating labels by traffic, leaving small languages with intervals too wide to detect anything.
- Picking the embedding model from an average leaderboard score.
- Letting stale translations outrank their current English source.
