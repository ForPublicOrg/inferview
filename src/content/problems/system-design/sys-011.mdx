## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"Design content moderation for user posts. Size the human review team from your thresholds, and tell me what happens to appeals."
</Callout>

Scenario: 1 million text-and-image posts a day in 10 languages, with Thai and Vietnamese the fastest-growing. A post must be distributed or held within 500 ms. Twenty-five reviewers at about 300 decisions each per working day. The policy team promises users an appeal decision within 48 hours. Last quarter's labelled sample puts violating posts at about 1 percent of traffic.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask for the cost of a wrong block and of a wrong allow in each policy category, from the policy team rather than from engineering.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask for the classifier's measured recall and false positive rate on last quarter's labelled sample, per language.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask what share of blocked posts were appealed last quarter, and how many appeals were overturned.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>A trained classifier on every post inside the 500 ms gate, with two thresholds per category: below the low one the post is distributed, above the high one (only in categories where precision on our sample supports it) it is blocked, and between them it is held at limited reach while a policy-reading model decides asynchronously. Reviewers get what that model cannot settle, plus appeals, plus a weekly random sample of allowed posts. The middle band's width is the number I choose, and it is bounded by reviewer capacity: 25 reviewers give 7,500 decisions a day, appeals take their share first, and the rest caps the band. Thresholds are set per category and per language and re-derived whenever a model changes.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Why the first stage escalates.</b> At a 1 percent base rate, a classifier at Llama Guard 4's published English operating point (69 percent recall, 11 percent false positives) flags about 115,800 posts per million, of which about 6,900 are violations: roughly 6 percent precision. That cannot be a block decision. It is a fine filter for what the second stage reads, which is the role Anthropic describes for its own first-stage probe.</p>
<p><b>Sizing.</b> With scenario rates of 0.5 percent of posts blocked and 10 percent of blocks appealed, appeals are 500 a day. That leaves 7,000 reviewer decisions. If the second stage settles 80 percent of what it reads, the band can be at most 7,000 / (1,000,000 × 0.2) = 3.5 percent of traffic. I would set the low thresholds per category for recall, add up the band they produce on the labelled sample, and narrow the categories with the cheapest errors until the sum fits.</p>
<p><b>Languages.</b> Meta's own multilingual figures for Llama Guard 4 are 43 percent recall across seven languages, and Thai is one of the eight it supports while Vietnamese is not. Neither number transfers to our traffic. I would measure recall per language on our labelled sample; where it is poor, widen that language's band and accept that its posts wait longer at limited reach.</p>
<p><b>Appeals.</b> Appeals are served first because they carry the 48-hour promise. Each appeal shows the reviewer the scores, thresholds and model versions that made the decision, and the overturn rate per category is the dashboard for the high threshold.</p>
<p><b>Failure mode of this design.</b> The 48-hour promise and the band compete for the same reviewers. A spam wave widens the band, the reviewer queue fills with held posts, and appeals either miss 48 hours or the held posts wait indefinitely. I would cap the reviewer share given to the band, so appeals always meet their deadline and held posts are what slips, and write that down.</p>
</Fragment>

### The number you choose

The band width per category, bounded by 3.5 percent of traffic in total under these scenario rates. A band chosen from error costs alone, without checking that the residual fits 7,000 decisions a day, is not an answer; neither is a band so narrow that recall on the low threshold falls below what the policy team accepts.

### Constraint conflict

The 500 ms gate, recall in low-resource languages, and the 48-hour appeal promise cannot all be met with 25 reviewers on a bad day. The design makes limited reach absorb the conflict: held posts wait, appeals do not.

### What you give up

Distribution speed for every post in the band, and more of it in languages where the classifier is weak. On spam-wave days, ordinary posts from new accounts may wait hours.

## Follow-ups

- *How do you measure false negatives, given that nothing below the low threshold is ever reviewed?*
- *A new policy category arrives tomorrow with no labelled data. Which stage handles it first?*
- *Legal asks for every automatic block to cite the policy clause. Which stage can produce that, and at what cost?*

## Common mistakes

- One global threshold for all categories and languages.
- Treating a published operating point as precision on your own traffic, ignoring the base rate.
- Sizing reviewers from appeals only, or from the band only.
- Letting appeals and held posts share one queue with no priority.
- Treating classifier scores as stable across provider model upgrades.
