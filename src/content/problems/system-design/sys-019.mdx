## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Reported prompt">
A prep site aggregating candidate reports lists "Design ChatGPT" among the system design questions most often asked in OpenAI's staff-level loop. The report gives no constraints; the ones below are constructed for this problem.
</Callout>

Design a consumer chat product backed by models your company serves on its own GPU fleet. Constraints, all scenario values:

- Free and paid tiers. Paid users are 20% of traffic at peak and nearly all of the revenue.
- Conversations are multi-turn; the median conversation is 8 turns and the longest 5% exceed 40. Each turn adds about 500 tokens of user and assistant text.
- Answers stream token by token. Output moderation is required before text reaches the user.
- A new flagship model launches in three weeks. Product expects launch-day demand at 3 times today's peak. The fleet can serve 1.5 times today's peak on the new model and cannot grow before launch.

Cover the request path, conversation state, the overload decision for launch day, and moderation on a stream. Write the launch risk note: what you give up, the triggers that change the plan during launch, and who decides.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask how much GPU time per token the previous model needs compared with the new one, and whether the fleet can switch a replica between models during the day.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Request the distribution of conversation lengths in tokens, not turns, and the share of requests that continue an existing conversation.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask what the moderation classifier's latency is per chunk, and what policy requires when a violation is found mid-answer.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Request path: edge, auth and per-tier rate limits, a conversation service that loads history, a router that picks the model and a replica with the conversation's cached prefix, the inference fleet streaming tokens through a moderation stage that holds back a short buffer, and a conversation store sharded by user. State: full history sent each turn, with replica affinity so the shared prefix is cached, and history truncated with a summary past a token limit. Launch day: paid traffic always gets the new model; free traffic gets the new model up to remaining capacity and the previous model beyond it, instead of a queue. What I give up: free users see the old model at peak on launch day, and a replica failure costs a burst of recomputed prefixes.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>Request path.</strong> Edge and auth; a rate limiter keyed by user and tier, in requests and in tokens; the conversation service loads the conversation and appends the new turn; the router chooses model and replica; the replica streams tokens over SSE through moderation to the client; on completion the turn, its token counts and the model version are written to the conversation store, sharded by user ID, and a usage event goes to metering. A client that reconnects mid-stream resumes from the last delivered offset, which means the stream is buffered server-side per turn until completion.</p>
<p><strong>Conversation state.</strong> Each turn resends the history. At about 500 tokens a turn, a 20-turn conversation processes 500 × (1 + 2 + ... + 20) = 500 × 210 = 105,000 input tokens across its turns, against 10,000 if each turn stood alone. Prefill on that history is the cost driver for long conversations, so the router sends a conversation's turns to the replica that holds its prefix in cache. Two options for the long tail past 40 turns: keep full history up to the context limit, which preserves fidelity and costs prefill on every turn; or summarise older turns past a threshold, which caps cost and loses detail the user may refer back to. I choose a token threshold with summarisation and keep the original turns in the store, so the user's view of the conversation is never truncated, only the model's.</p>
<p><strong>Launch day: two defensible answers.</strong> Demand is 3 times peak, capacity on the new model 1.5 times. Paid demand is 0.2 × 3 = 0.6; free demand is 2.4.</p>
<ul>
<li><em>Shed free traffic.</em> Paid gets 0.6, free gets the remaining 0.9 of 2.4, so 37.5% of free requests are served and the rest see an at-capacity message or a queue. Everyone who gets an answer gets the new model. Cost: most free users' first experience of the launch is a refusal.</li>
<li><em>Route free overflow to the previous model.</em> Suppose the previous model needs a quarter of the GPU time per token (to be measured, per Hint 1). Move x of the 1.5 units of capacity to the old model: the new model keeps 1.5 − x, and the moved replicas serve 4x of old-model demand. Serving all demand needs 0.6 + (2.4 − 4x) = 1.5 − x, so x = 0.5: the new model serves paid plus 0.4 of free, and the old model serves the remaining 2.0 on half a peak's worth of replicas. Cost: most free users get the old model on launch day, the product must label which model answered, and the fleet runs two models with replica moves during the day.</li>
</ul>
<p>I choose the second, with the model labelled in the UI, because a launch where most free users are refused does more damage than one where they wait a day for the new model. The conflict is availability against the launch experience, under a fleet that cannot grow.</p>
<p><strong>Moderation on a stream.</strong> Input is classified before generation. Output is classified per chunk with a short hold-back buffer, so the user sees text a chunk behind generation; a violation stops the stream and replaces the partial answer. The buffer size is the trade: larger buffers let the classifier see context and add latency to every token the user sees; I would set it from measured classifier latency per chunk and accept that the first visible token arrives one chunk later.</p>
<p><strong>Where this fails.</strong> Replica affinity concentrates long conversations. When a replica holding thousands of cached prefixes fails or is moved to the old model on launch day, every one of those conversations is re-prefilled on its next turn, and that burst lands exactly when capacity is tightest. Moves on launch day drain replicas instead of killing them, and the router caps how many cached conversations a replica holds, at the cost of some cache hits.</p>
<p><strong>Risk note.</strong> "On launch day paid users are always served by the new model. Free users are served by the new model up to capacity and by the previous model beyond it, labelled in the product. Triggers: if paid time-to-first-token p95 exceeds its agreed target for 10 minutes, new-model free traffic drops to zero; if old-model capacity saturates, free users beyond it get the at-capacity message. The serving on-call lead flips the routing; the product lead owns the decision to keep free users on the old model and reviews it at the end of each launch day."</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Pushback: marketing says a launch where free users get the old model is a launch that did not happen, and wants the queue instead. What do you put in writing, and who signs it?</em></li>
<li><em>Reversal: what would you need to see on the first launch day to switch the free tier to shedding on day two?</em></li>
<li><em>The moderation team wants a larger hold-back buffer and the product team wants the first token sooner. How do you decide without deciding by rank?</em></li>
</ul>

</Depth>

The quarter-GPU-time ratio for the previous model and the 20% paid share are scenario values that move x directly: if the old model needs half the GPU time instead of a quarter, all free overflow no longer fits, and the plan becomes a hybrid of routing and shedding. A design that names that sensitivity and the measurement that settles it is complete; one that picks either answer without stating the ratio it depends on is not.

## Follow-ups

- *A user deletes a conversation. Which stores, caches and logs hold it, and which of them must forget it within what time?*
- *Billing is per token for the API product on the same fleet. What changes in metering when a stream is cut by moderation or by a dropped connection?*
- *How do you measure whether the summarised history is hurting answers in long conversations?*

## Common mistakes

- Treating each turn as an independent request and sizing the fleet on new tokens only, ignoring the history prefill.
- Making "add more GPUs" the launch plan when the fleet is fixed.
- Shedding load without deciding which tier, or rate limiting by request count when cost is in tokens.
- Running output moderation after the full answer, which is incompatible with streaming, or not at all on the stream.
- Session affinity with no cap and no drain, so one replica failure becomes a prefill storm.
