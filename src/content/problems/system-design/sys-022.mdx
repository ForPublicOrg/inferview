## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Staff design round, 45 minutes">
"Design a support assistant for a retail bank's app. The regulator, the launch date and the core banking system are not negotiable. End with the risk note."
</Callout>

The case, constructed for this problem:

- About 6,000 support conversations an hour at peak in the app. Roughly half need the customer's account data: balances, recent transactions, fees charged.
- The compliance team's reading of the regulator's expectations: any statement to a customer about fees, charges, interest or disputes must be accurate, must be reproducible later exactly as the customer saw it, and records are kept for the period compliance sets. The bank must be able to show who approved the system's behaviour.
- Launch is in ten weeks; the date was announced to the board and will not move.
- The core banking system cannot be rewritten or extended. This channel may call its account API at up to 10 requests a second; above that, calls are rejected. A nightly extract is also available.
- Disputes (chargebacks, fraud claims) are opened today by human agents in a case system that also cannot change.

Design the assistant: which questions it answers and how, how it reads account data, what it logs, and what it hands off. Write the risk note.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask compliance which topics they consider regulated statements, and whether a fixed template reviewed once counts as approved behaviour.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Request the average number of turns per conversation and how many account calls one turn makes today in the human agents' tools.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Ask how the case system receives a new case today, and what it needs from the person opening it.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p>Split by topic. Regulated topics (fees, charges, interest, disputes) are answered from templates that compliance approves, filled by code from account data; the model classifies intent and extracts slots but does not write the statement. General how-to questions get model answers grounded in the help content, with a code check that blocks any currency amount or fee name not present in the retrieved sources. Account data comes through a per-conversation cache in front of the core API to stay under 10 requests a second. Every answer is stored as sent with its template or prompt version, model version and data snapshot. Disputes hand off to a human with a prefilled summary. What I give up: natural answers on the topics customers care most about, and more handoffs than a fully generative design.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><strong>The decision: two defensible answers for regulated topics.</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><em>Generative, grounded and checked.</em> The model answers fee and interest questions from the fee schedule and the account data, and code checks every number and fee name against those sources. Answers read naturally and cover odd phrasings. Cost: approval means approving a model's behaviour across inputs nobody enumerated, the check catches wrong numbers but not wrong reasoning ("this fee will be refunded"), and reproducing a statement requires the exact model, prompt and inputs.</li>
<li><em>Templated, model as router.</em> Compliance approves a few dozen templates; the model picks the intent and extracts slots (which transaction, which fee); code fills the template from account data. Approval is a document compliance can read; reproduction is the template version plus the data. Cost: rigid answers, and every question outside the templates hands off.</li>
</ul>
<p>With ten weeks and an approval requirement, I choose templates for regulated topics and generative answers elsewhere. The conflict is the launch date against approving open-ended behaviour: compliance can sign templates in ten weeks; it is not clear they can sign a model.</p>
<p><strong>Reading account data under a rate limit.</strong> 6,000 conversations an hour, half needing account data, at an assumed 3 core calls per conversation if fetched once: 6,000 × 0.5 × 3 / 3,600 = 2.5 requests a second. Re-fetching on every turn at an assumed 4 turns a conversation is 10 a second, the limit with no headroom. So account data is fetched once per conversation into a cache keyed by conversation and customer, with a short TTL, and refreshed only when the customer asks about something newer than the snapshot. The cost is staleness inside a conversation: a transaction posting mid-conversation is invisible until refresh, so every templated statement carries the snapshot time ("as of 14:02"). The nightly extract is used for nothing customer-facing, since a day-old balance in a fee answer is a wrong statement.</p>
<p><strong>Reproducibility.</strong> An append-only store per conversation turn: the customer's message, the classified intent, the template ID and version or the prompt and model versions, the retrieved sources with versions, the account data snapshot, and the answer exactly as rendered. Retention follows compliance's period; access is limited to support leads and compliance, and every read is logged.</p>
<p><strong>Disputes.</strong> The case system cannot change, so the assistant does not open cases. It collects the facts the case system needs, writes a summary, and hands the customer to an agent with the summary attached; the agent opens the case in the existing tool. That keeps the action with a human and adds an agent step to every dispute.</p>
<p><strong>Evals.</strong> Intent classification is the component that decides whether a regulated question reaches a template, so it gets a labelled set weighted to fee and dispute phrasings, and its misroutes from regulated to generative are the failure measured weekly. Templates are checked by code against account data. Generative answers are graded by a model grader calibrated against support leads' labels.</p>
<p><strong>Where this fails.</strong> A fee question misclassified as general reaches the generative path. The number check catches a wrong amount; it does not catch "you will not be charged for this", a regulated statement with no number in it. The mitigation is a second classifier on the generative output for regulated claims, which blocks and hands off, and the weekly misroute measurement. Neither drives the risk to zero, and the risk note says so.</p>
<p><strong>Risk note.</strong> "Launch on 6 January with templated answers for fees, charges, interest and disputes, approved by compliance; generative answers for general questions with numeric and regulated-claim checks. Accepted risk: a regulated question misrouted to the generative path may produce an unapproved statement; we measure misroutes weekly on a labelled sample and on the output classifier's blocks. Trigger: two confirmed unapproved regulated statements in a week, or a misroute rate above the agreed threshold, moves all money-related intents to handoff within one hour. Owner of the system: head of digital support. Accountable for accepting this risk: the executive compliance names for the channel."</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Pushback: the head of digital says templated answers will tank satisfaction and wants generative fee answers at launch. You have ten weeks. What do you cut, and what risk do you put in writing for her to sign?</em></li>
<li><em>Reversal: what would six months of logs have to show for you to move fee questions to generative answers?</em></li>
<li><em>Compliance and engineering disagree on whether a model-picked template counts as the system making a statement. How do you settle it without deciding by rank?</em></li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

The 3 calls a conversation and 4 turns are assumptions to confirm (Hint 2). Anything from 2 to 5 calls a conversation fetched once stays under the limit with room; any design that re-fetches per turn has to show the arithmetic, because it reaches the limit at peak. A design that uses the nightly extract for customer-facing balances is not acceptable under the stated accuracy requirement.

## Follow-ups

- *The bank adds a second language in six months. What changes for templates, and who approves translations?*
- *Compliance asks you to show every statement made about a specific fee during March. What query runs, and against what?*
- *The core API limit is cut to 5 requests a second for a quarter. What degrades first?*

## Common mistakes

- Letting the model write regulated statements and relying on a prompt instruction to keep them accurate.
- Logging the prompt but not the account data snapshot, so a statement cannot be reproduced.
- Using the nightly extract to stay under the rate limit and serving day-old balances.
- Having the assistant open disputes through a workaround in a system that cannot change.
- A risk note with no named person who accepts the risk, or with the engineering lead accepting a compliance risk.
