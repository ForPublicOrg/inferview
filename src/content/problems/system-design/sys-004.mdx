## Question

<Callout kind="interview" label="Interview question">
"We're self-hosting Llama 3.1 70B for this assistant. Convert the expected traffic into a number of GPU nodes."
</Callout>

What you get when you ask:

- Model: Llama 3.1 70B, weights and KV cache in BF16. Its `config.json`: 80 layers, hidden size 8,192, 64 attention heads, 8 key-value heads, intermediate size 28,672, vocabulary 128,256, untied input and output embeddings.
- Hardware: nodes of eight H100 SXM GPUs, 80 GB each, one model replica per node. Keep 10 percent of memory back for activations and fragmentation.
- Serving stack: the current engine reserves the full 8,192-token window for every sequence, and the team cannot change engines before launch.
- Traffic (scenario): 1,000,000 requests a day, peak hour at 3 times the average.
- Latency (scenario, measured on one node at the concurrency you will run): 0.5 s to first token, then 300 output tokens at 40 ms each.

How many nodes do you need at peak? You choose the planned utilisation per node and say why.

## Hints

<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 1</summary><p>Ask whether the 40 ms per token was measured at the concurrency you intend to plan for, and what it becomes above it.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 2</summary><p>Ask how much memory one sequence holds under this engine, as opposed to how many tokens it uses.</p></details>
<details class="hint"><summary>Hint 3</summary><p>Request the arrival rate in the peak hour rather than the daily total.</p></details>

## Answer

<Depth>
<Fragment slot="short">
<p><b>4 nodes</b> at a planned 70 percent; 3 to 5 for utilisations from 100 down to 60 percent. KV cache is 320 KiB per token, 2.5 GiB per reserved 8,192-token window. A node has 435 GB after the reserve and 141.1 GB of weights, so it holds 162 windows. Peak is 34.7 requests a second, each in the system 12.5 s, so 434 are in flight: 434 / (162 × 0.7) = 3.8.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="long">
<p><b>Weights.</b> Per layer: attention 2 × 8,192² + 2 × 8,192 × 1,024 = 151.0M, MLP 3 × 8,192 × 28,672 = 704.6M. 80 layers is 68.45B; embeddings 2 × 128,256 × 8,192 = 2.10B. Total 70.55B parameters, 141.1 GB in BF16.</p>
<p><b>KV.</b> 2 × 80 × 8 × 128 × 2 bytes = 327,680 bytes per token; × 8,192 = 2.68 GB per window.</p>
<p><b>Memory per node.</b> 8 × 80 GB × 0.9 = 576 GB; minus 141.1 GB of weights is 434.9 GB; / 2.68 GB = 162 concurrent sequences.</p>
<p><b>Demand.</b> 1,000,000 / 86,400 × 3 = 34.7 requests a second at peak. Time in system 0.5 + 300 × 0.04 = 12.5 s. In flight 434.</p>
<p><b>Utilisation is the choice.</b> At 100 percent, 434 / 162 = 2.68, so 3 nodes, with every window full at peak and no room for the per-token latency to stay at 40 ms if it degrades near full batch. At 70 percent, 3.8, so 4. At 60 percent, 4.5, so 5. I would plan at 70 percent unless the load test shows 40 ms holding at 162, and state that this is memory-bound sizing at the reserved window.</p>
<p>The failure mode of the 4-node answer: it covers peak with no node to spare, so one node lost at peak puts 434 requests on 486 slots and the measured 40 ms no longer holds. The reserve for that is a separate line.</p>
</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="followups">
<ul>
<li><em>Average sequences are 3,800 tokens. If you could switch to an engine that allocates KV in pages, what changes?</em> About 349 sequences fit per node, so memory stops binding at 2 nodes, and the per-token latency at that concurrency becomes the limit to measure.</li>
<li><em>What if you used 64 heads instead of 8 KV heads?</em> The KV estimate is eight times too large and so is the fleet.</li>
<li><em>Why not size from FLOPS?</em> Each decode step reads the weights and every live KV cache, so 3.35 TB/s per GPU limits it first.</li>
</ul>
</Fragment>
</Depth>

The band of 3 to 6 nodes admits utilisations from 100 down to 60 percent and a spare node added on top. Answers in the tens usually used 64 KV heads or forgot that one node has eight GPUs; answers of 1 or 2 sized from average traffic or from the 3,800-token average under an engine that reserves the full window.

## Follow-ups

- *How do you check the plan before buying?* Load one node at increasing concurrency, record TTFT and per-token latency at each step, and find where P95 breaks the target. That concurrency replaces the memory-derived 162 × 0.7.
- *Traffic doubles next quarter. What scales?* In-flight requests double, so nodes roughly double, unless shorter outputs cut the 12.5 s.
- *When does a hosted API beat this fleet?* When traffic is spiky or small enough that the nodes sit idle most of the day; bring your GPU-hour price as an input.

## Common mistakes

- Using attention heads instead of KV heads in the cache formula.
- Forgetting the 141 GB of weights before any request arrives.
- Sizing from average traffic instead of the peak hour.
- Treating Little's law output as GPUs rather than concurrent requests.
