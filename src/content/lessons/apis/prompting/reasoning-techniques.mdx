## The price of a technique

Every technique in this lesson changes one of three numbers: input tokens per request, output tokens per request, or the number of calls on the critical path. Price it before adopting it:

- **Cost per request** = calls × (input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price), with cached input at the cache-hit price.
- **Latency** is dominated by output tokens on the critical path. Calls in series add; parallel calls cost the slowest one.

Two facts from Anthropic's thinking documentation change the arithmetic for current models. Thinking tokens are billed as output tokens even when the text is not returned, and on Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 thinking is on by default with `display` defaulting to omitted. A request can therefore spend most of its bill on tokens the application never sees, and `usage.output_tokens` is the only place they show up. Even the summarized thinking text, when you ask for it, is billed at the full thinking count, not the summary's length.

## Chain of thought: from prompt trick to model setting

Wei and colleagues introduced chain-of-thought prompting in 2022: a few exemplars that show intermediate steps. With eight such exemplars a 540B-parameter model reached state of the art on GSM8K, above finetuned GPT-3 with a verifier, and the paper reports that the effect appears only in sufficiently large models. That conditional is the first thing to carry forward: the technique's value was a property of the model, not of the prompt.

The second is that the property moves. Chen, Zaharia and Zou measured GPT-4 in March and June 2023 on the same prime-versus-composite task: 84% then 51%, and they attribute part of the drop to the June model following chain-of-thought prompts less well. Same prompt, same model name, different value of the technique.

On current Claude models the reasoning moved into the model. Opus 5.5 always thinks; Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 think by default; `effort` (low, medium, high, xhigh, max) sets how much. Anthropic's guide now says three things that reverse older practice:

1. Prefer a general instruction such as "think thoroughly" over a hand-written step list, because the model's own plan frequently exceeds the prescribed one.
2. Manual chain of thought, reasoning written into the response and the answer in `<answer>` tags, is a fallback for when thinking is off.
3. On Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Fable 5.1, rely on thinking at a lower effort if cost matters; a prompt asking the model to write its reasoning out may be declined.

The Opus 5.5 guide adds one measurement: in Anthropic's testing in a chat product, removing "think carefully" lines from the system prompt made replies start sooner with no clear decline in quality. That is the vendor's own evaluation, on its product; it is still the best available prior that the line is now cost without benefit.

Manual chain of thought still pays in two places: a model where thinking is off (Haiku 5.5 accepts thinking disabled at high effort or below, and Opus 4.6 to 4.8 do not think unless asked), and a product that needs the reasoning as an artefact in the response, where the model allows it.

## Self-consistency: a vote you pay for per voter

Wang and colleagues sample several reasoning paths and return the most frequent final answer. The abstract reports gains over plain chain of thought of 17.9% on GSM8K, 12.2% on AQuA, 11.0% on SVAMP, 6.4% on StrategyQA and 3.9% on ARC-challenge. The gains are largest on arithmetic, where many paths reach one checkable answer, and smallest on the science multiple choice.

The engineering conditions:

- **The answer must be discrete and extractable.** A label, a number, a yes or no. Free text does not vote without a second model to cluster it.
- **The samples must differ.** Under API version 2026-10-21 Claude samples at temperature 1.0 and the parameter cannot be sent, so diversity comes by default and cannot be tuned.
- **Cost scales with votes.** N calls pay N outputs, and N inputs unless they share a cached prefix. Calls sent in parallel before any of them has written the cache cannot hit it, so check `usage` on the first wave rather than assuming hits.
- **Latency is the slowest voter.** Five parallel calls inherit the tail of the output-length distribution.

Self-consistency also returns something a single call does not: the agreement rate. A 5-of-5 answer and a 3-of-5 answer are different products, and the second can route to a human. Teams that adopt voting for accuracy often keep it for this signal.

## Few-shot: calibration as well as accuracy

Few-shot examples are the oldest technique and the one whose cost changed most with caching. A 2,000-token example block on Sonnet 5.5 costs 2,000 × $2 / 1,000,000 = $0.004 per request at the base price and 2,000 × $0.10 / 1,000,000 = $0.0002 as a cache hit, a twentieth. Stable examples placed early in the prompt are nearly free; examples chosen per request are paid in full on every call.

The cost that caching does not remove is bias. Zhao and colleagues found few-shot predictions skewed toward labels near the end of the prompt and labels frequent in pre-training, and proposed contextual calibration: run the prompt with a content-free input such as "N/A", read the label probabilities, and rescale so that input scores uniformly. The paper reports average accuracy gains of up to 30.0% absolute on GPT-3 and GPT-2 and lower variance across prompt choices.

The method needs label probabilities. Where an API does not return token probabilities, the measurable analogue is to sample the content-free input repeatedly and count the labels. A prompt that labels "N/A" as `billing` most of the time is biased toward `billing` before it reads any ticket, and the bias comes from the examples or the label order, which are both under your control.

Anthropic's guide adds that multishot examples work with thinking: worked examples shape how the model approaches similar problems in its own reasoning. On a reasoning model, examples are less about teaching the method and more about fixing the output contract and the edge cases.

## When reasoning only adds latency

Explicit reasoning helps when the answer depends on intermediate results the model would otherwise compress: arithmetic, multi-hop lookups, plans. It adds tokens without changing the answer when the output is a function of surface features: short-text classification, field extraction, format conversion, routing on keywords. Anthropic's effort documentation describes `low` as for "simpler tasks that need the best speed and lowest costs", and its prompt guide suggests telling an adaptive model that thinking adds latency and should be used only when it will meaningfully improve the answer.

The test is the same as for any optimisation: run the eval at two effort levels, or with and without the instruction, and compare accuracy against output tokens per request. If accuracy holds within the eval's noise and output tokens drop, the reasoning was latency.

## A pricing function

The sketch below prices a technique per request from token counts, and reports latency as output tokens on the critical path, which converts to seconds with the decode rate you measure on your own account.

```python
PRICES = {  # USD per million tokens, Claude pricing page, checked 2026-10-28
    "haiku-5.5":  {"in": 0.10, "hit": 0.01, "out": 0.50},
    "sonnet-5.5": {"in": 2.00, "hit": 0.10, "out": 10.00},
}

def price(model, calls, input_tokens, output_tokens, cached_tokens=0, parallel=True):
    p = PRICES[model]
    fresh = input_tokens - cached_tokens
    per_call = (fresh * p["in"] + cached_tokens * p["hit"] + output_tokens * p["out"]) / 1e6
    critical_path = output_tokens if parallel else output_tokens * calls
    return round(calls * per_call, 6), critical_path

print("5 Haiku votes  ", price("haiku-5.5", 5, 1000, 300))
print("1 Sonnet call  ", price("sonnet-5.5", 1, 1000, 1500))
print("3-step chain   ", price("sonnet-5.5", 3, 1000, 500, cached_tokens=800, parallel=False))
```

The output is `(0.00125, 300)`, `(0.017, 1500)` and `(0.01644, 1500)`. The five votes cost about a fourteenth of the Sonnet call (0.017 / 0.00125 = 13.6) and have a fifth of its critical path, if the slowest voter writes no more than the typical one. The three-step chain costs about the same as the single call and has the same critical path, because its steps run in series; it buys separately testable steps, not speed. None of these numbers says which is more accurate; that is the eval's job, and the price tells you how large an accuracy gain each option must show to be worth adopting.

## When this is wrong

The paper numbers come from 2021 and 2022 models on academic benchmarks. They establish that each technique can matter and in which direction; they do not predict the gain on your task or on a current model, which may already do internally what the technique did externally.

Token counts in the arithmetic are scenario inputs. Thinking length varies with the input and the effort level, and Anthropic notes that level names do not mean the same amount of thinking across models, so a cost model built on one model's averages breaks on the next upgrade.

Latency is not proportional to output tokens when the provider queues requests, when time to first token dominates short outputs, or when parallel calls hit a rate limit and serialise. Measure the distribution under production concurrency before trusting the critical-path count.
