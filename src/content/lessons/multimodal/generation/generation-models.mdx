## The cost model these four papers change

A generation request runs one network, the denoiser, many times. Write its cost as:

```
denoiser FLOPs per request = steps × g × F(n)
F(n) ≈ 2 · P · n  +  4 · L · n² · d
```

`steps` is the number of solver steps, `g` is 2 when classifier-free guidance runs on every step and 1 otherwise, and `F(n)` is one forward pass over `n` tokens through a model with `P` parameters, `L` layers and width `d`: the first term is the weight multiplies, the second is attention scores and the weighted sum. Everything around the loop (text encoders, the autoencoder's decoder, safety classifiers) runs once per request.

Each of the four ideas in this lesson moves one term. Latent diffusion shrinks `n`. Classifier-free guidance sets `g`. The diffusion transformer changes what `F` is and how it scales with `P`. Flow matching and rectified flow cut `steps`. Video multiplies `n` by the frame count, which is why the second term takes over.

## Latent diffusion: shrink n

Before latent diffusion, diffusion models denoised pixels. The latent diffusion paper notes that the strongest pixel-space models often took hundreds of GPU days to train, citing 150 to 1,000 V100 days, and sampling paid the same per-pixel cost on every step. Its move is to train an autoencoder once, run diffusion in the autoencoder's latent space, and decode only at the end.

The numbers for the setting DiT later used: a 256 × 256 RGB image has 256 × 256 × 3 = 196,608 values; its latent at downsampling factor 8 with 4 channels is 32 × 32 × 4 = 4,096 values, 48 times fewer. The denoiser runs on that, every step; the decoder runs once.

The factor is a quality decision, not a free dial. The paper compares factors 1 to 32: at 1 and 2 training is slow because the denoiser still does the perceptual compression; at 32 fidelity stalls because the autoencoder has thrown information away. Factors 4 and 8 win, and after 2M training steps the FID gap between pixel space and factor 8 is 38. Stable Diffusion 3 kept factor 8 and raised the latent to 16 channels, reporting autoencoder reconstruction FID of 2.41, 1.56 and 1.06 for 4, 8 and 16 channels. More channels raise the ceiling of what the decoder can reproduce, and give the denoiser a harder target to learn.

The engineering consequence is a two-stage system with a contract between the stages. The autoencoder is trained first and frozen; every denoiser trained on it inherits its artefacts (small text, faces at low resolution, fine texture), and swapping it means retraining the denoiser. When a generated image has garbled small text, check the autoencoder's reconstruction of real images with small text before blaming the denoiser.

## Classifier-free guidance: set g

A conditional model asked for "a red bicycle on a beach" produces plausible samples that often follow the prompt loosely. Classifier guidance fixed that with a separate classifier's gradient. Classifier-free guidance (Ho and Salimans) removes the classifier: during training the conditioning is dropped with probability `p_uncond`, so one network learns both the conditional and the unconditional prediction, and at sampling time the two are combined:

```
eps_guided = (1 + w) · eps(z, c)  −  w · eps(z)
```

The guidance weight `w` extrapolates away from the unconditional prediction. In the paper's ImageNet 128 × 128 results at 256 steps, FID is best at a small weight (7.27 unguided, 2.43 at w = 0.3) and gets worse as `w` grows (21.53 at w = 4.0) while Inception score keeps climbing (to 421 at w = 4.0). Samples become more faithful to the condition and less varied. For training, the paper found `p_uncond` of 0.1 and 0.2 about equal and 0.5 worse.

The serving cost is in one sentence of the paper: each sampling step evaluates the model twice, once with the condition and once without. The authors note that the speed-fair comparison with a classifier-guided baseline at 256 steps is their own 128-step setting, which loses on FID. So `g = 2`: guidance doubles the denoiser's work for the same step count, and every step saved later saves two forward passes.

Watch the convention. Ho and Salimans write `w`, where 0 means no guidance. Many later papers and libraries write a scale `s = 1 + w`, where 1 means no guidance: DiT reports FID 2.27 at "cfg 1.50", the latent diffusion text-to-image samples use s = 10, and Movie Gen uses 7.5. A guidance value copied across code bases without its convention is off by one.

## The diffusion transformer: what F is

Latent diffusion's denoiser was a U-Net with cross-attention. DiT (Peebles and Xie) replaces it with a transformer over latent patches: the 32 × 32 × 4 latent is cut into p × p patches, giving (32/p)² tokens, 256 at p = 2. The paper measures model cost as forward-pass Gflops and shows FID falling as Gflops rise, whether the Gflops come from more layers and width or from smaller patches. DiT-XL/2 costs 118.6 Gflops per forward pass at 256 × 256 and 524.6 at 512 × 512, where the patch count is four times larger: 4.4 times the compute.

The result that matters for serving is in the paper's Section 5.2: sampling compute does not substitute for model compute. DiT-L/2 run for 1,000 steps spends about 80.7 Tflops per image; DiT-XL/2 run for 128 steps spends about 15.2 Tflops and still has the better FID-10K (23.7 against 25.9). Choosing between a smaller model with more steps and a larger model with fewer, that is published evidence for the second.

The other consequence is that the denoiser became the same kind of program as a language model: attention, MLPs, normalisation, the same kernels and the same tensor and sequence parallelism. The third lesson in this module walks through the paper.

## Flow matching and rectified flow: cut steps

A sampler integrates a learned vector field from noise to data, and the number of steps a solver needs depends on how curved the paths are. Diffusion paths curve: the flow matching paper shows them overshooting and backtracking, with most noise removed near the end. Flow matching (Lipman and colleagues) trains the network to regress the velocity of a chosen path, and with the optimal-transport path each sample moves from noise to data in a straight line at constant speed. Rectified flow, the formulation Stable Diffusion 3 scaled, uses the same straight interpolation between data and noise.

The published effect, with conditions. In the flow matching paper's Table 1 (the same U-Net trained each way, unconditional, an adaptive solver run to a fixed tolerance), ImageNet 64 × 64 DDPM reaches FID 17.36 with 264 function evaluations and flow matching with OT reaches 14.45 with 138. With fixed-step solvers, the OT model needed roughly 60% of the evaluations of diffusion models to reach the same numerical error. In the Stable Diffusion 3 paper's Table 6, the depth-38 rectified-flow model loses 0.08% of its CLIP score going from 50 steps to 20, 0.14% at 10 steps and 2.71% at 5. The paper reports that rectified flow degrades less than the other formulations it tested when steps are cut.

Straight conditional paths are not straight marginal paths: the model learns an average over many noise-image pairs, and that average still curves, which is why quality bends sharply below about 10 steps instead of staying flat down to 1. Getting to 1 to 4 steps takes distillation, which the next lesson prices.

## Video: n is now the whole problem

Movie Gen is a 30B-parameter transformer (48 layers, width 6,144) trained with flow matching. Its temporal autoencoder compresses 8 times in time, height and width into 16 channels; the transformer then takes 2 × 2 spatial patches. It generates at 768 px, and a separate 7B upsampler takes the result to 1080p.

<p>Frames: 256 / 8 = 32 latent frames. Each frame: 768 / 8 = 96 latent pixels per side, 96 / 2 = 48 patches per side, 48 × 48 = 2,304 tokens. Total 32 × 2,304 = <b>73,728</b>, the "73K" in the paper's abstract. Put Movie Gen's shape into F(n) with n = 73,728: the weight term is 2 × 30 × 10⁹ × 73,728 ≈ 4.4 PFLOP per forward pass, and the attention term is 4 × 48 × 73,728² × 6,144 ≈ 6.4 PFLOP. At this sequence length attention is the larger term; for a 1024² image it is under a tenth of the total. Without the temporal autoencoder, the same clip in 2 × 2 pixel patches would be 384 × 384 × 256 = 37.7M tokens, 512 times more, and attention would cost 512² = 262,144 times more. The autoencoder's compression ratio decides whether the model can be trained and served at all.

Movie Gen also cuts steps: a 50-step schedule, linear for the first 25 steps and quadratic for the rest, which the paper reports closely matches a much longer linear schedule. With guidance on every step, one 16-second clip is 100 forward passes of about 10.8 PFLOP each before the upsampler runs.

## The cost model as code

```python
def denoiser_flops(params, layers, width, tokens, steps, guided=True):
    per_pass = 2 * params * tokens + 4 * layers * tokens**2 * width
    return steps * (2 if guided else 1) * per_pass

# Movie Gen shape from the paper; 50 steps, guidance on every step
clip = denoiser_flops(30e9, 48, 6144, 73_728, 50)
print(f"{clip/1e18:.2f} EFLOP per 16 s clip")   # 1.08
```

The function ignores text cross-attention, the autoencoder and the upsampler, and counts a multiply-add as two FLOPs. It ranks levers; profile before quoting latency.

## When this is wrong

- **FLOPs are not latency.** The cost model assumes the denoiser is compute-bound. At 4,096 tokens per image that holds for the weight multiplies, but small images, small batches and the autoencoder's decoder can be memory-bound, and the decoder at high resolution is a share of latency the model above leaves out.
- **Step counts are model-specific.** The 20-step figure is for one rectified-flow model and one metric. A different model, guidance weight or distilled checkpoint has a different curve; measure yours.
- **The published quality numbers are benchmark numbers.** FID on ImageNet and CLIP score on a prompt set rank models; neither tells you whether your users' prompts (logos, product photos, text in images) survive a change.
- **Guidance is not always 2×.** Some systems skip guidance on some steps or distil it into the model; then g is between 1 and 2 and the trade moves.
- **Not every generator is a diffusion model.** Autoregressive image models generate image tokens one at a time like a language model; their cost model is the LLM decode cost model, and the step arithmetic here does not apply.
