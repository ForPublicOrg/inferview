## Advertised context and usable context

A 1M-token context window means the API accepts 1M tokens. It does not say the model uses them. The decision this lesson supports is what length your product can rely on, for your task, and what evidence you put behind that number. The rule that follows from the research below is short: a needle-test score is never evidence of usable long context. It is a configuration check.

## What the needle test measures

The needle test plants one fact in filler text, asks for it, and sweeps two axes: total length and the depth at which the fact sits. The repository that popularised it uses a haystack of Paul Graham essays; its example run configuration sweeps 2,000 to 32,000 tokens in 8 steps and 11 depths with 3 seeds, and its scorers are exact match for a single needle and the fraction found for several.

The test is good at one thing: it fails loudly when position handling is broken. A model served without its `rope_scaling` block, or a server that truncates silently, fails the needle sweep at the first length past the break. That is worth running on every deployment.

What it does not test is use. The question repeats the needle's words, so the model can find it by matching strings, and nothing else in the context competes with it, since the filler is about something else.

## What the research measured instead

**Position.** Lost in the Middle (2023) moved the relevant passage through multi-document question answering and key-value retrieval inputs and found performance highest when it sat at the beginning or end and significantly lower in the middle, even for models built for long context.

**Harder tasks at the same lengths.** RULER (2024) kept the synthetic, controllable format and added multi-hop tracing (follow a chain of variable assignments) and aggregation (count or rank items spread through the input), 13 tasks in all. It tested 17 models that all claimed 32K tokens or more. Almost all scored nearly perfectly on the vanilla needle test and dropped sharply as length grew on the other tasks; only half kept satisfactory performance at 32K.

**No literal overlap.** NoLiMa (2025) wrote needles and questions that share almost no words, so the model has to make an association, as in a needle saying a person lives next to a landmark and a question about the city. Of 13 models claiming at least 128K, 11 fell below half their short-length score at 32K. GPT-4o went from 99.3% to 69.7%.

**Application tasks.** HELMET (2024 to 2025) evaluated 59 models in seven application categories up to 128K and states that synthetic tasks like the needle test do not reliably predict downstream performance. Its categories also correlated weakly with each other, so one long-context score does not summarise a model even when it comes from realistic tasks. It found open models trailing closed ones on full-context reasoning, with the gap growing as length grows.

**Plain length.** Chroma's Context Rot report (July 2025) tested 18 models and found performance degrading with input length across all its experiments, including simple tasks. On LongMemEval, focused prompts of about 300 tokens did better than full prompts of about 113,000 tokens.

The engineering reading: retrieval and pruning still matter with a large window. Put instructions and the most relevant material at the edges of the prompt, send less rather than more, and measure at the lengths you will use.

## The probe suite

A probe suite for your product has five families, each a controlled version of a real request:

1. **Single needle**, as a smoke test of the deployment.
2. **Multi-needle**: several facts, one question that needs all of them. Still literal; it tests coverage, not understanding.
3. **No-overlap needle**: the question and the evidence share no key words.
4. **Multi-hop chain**: the answer needs two or three facts in sequence.
5. **Aggregation**: the answer is a count or ranking over the whole input.

Build them from your own documents, pad to each target length with documents of the same kind so distractors look like evidence, vary and record the evidence's depth, and score each family by length. Report an effective length per family: the longest tested length at which that family, and every shorter one, meets a threshold set before the run. The supported length is the minimum over the families that match real requests. Needle families go in a separate table.

The lab [A long-context probe suite](/labs/long-context-probes) builds this program in six steps against a scripted mock model that answers literal questions from anywhere and everything else only from the first and last 2,000 words. Its final report, computed by the lab's reference solution:

```text
Tasks that predict use (threshold 0.90):
  no_overlap   1000: 1.00  2000: 1.00  8000: 0.67  32000: 0.67  (holds to 2000)
  chain        1000: 1.00  2000: 1.00  8000: 0.00  32000: 0.00  (holds to 2000)
  aggregation  1000: 1.00  2000: 1.00  8000: 1.00  32000: 0.67  (holds to 8000)
Effective context: 2000
Needle tasks, reported apart (a pass here is not evidence of usable context):
  needle       1000: 1.00  2000: 1.00  8000: 1.00  32000: 1.00  (holds to 32000)
  multi_needle 1000: 1.00  2000: 1.00  8000: 1.00  32000: 1.00  (holds to 32000)
```

The mock is a caricature, built so that the needle rows stay perfect while every other row degrades, the pattern RULER and NoLiMa found in real models. The report's shape is the point: the number anyone may quote comes from the realistic families, and the needle rows cannot raise it.

**How many items per cell.** The mock is deterministic; real models are not. Pick the cell size from the smallest difference that would change a decision. At about 80% accuracy, the standard error of one cell of *n* items is √(0.8 × 0.2 / *n*): 5.7 points at *n* = 50 and 4 points at *n* = 100. If the claim is "holds to 128K", the 128K cell has to be large enough that a miss of the threshold is distinguishable from noise. Comparisons between models or configurations should be paired on the same items.

## When this is wrong

Synthetic probes, however carefully built, are still synthetic. HELMET's point cuts both ways: if needle scores do not predict downstream tasks, a no-overlap probe may not predict your task either. The probes are a floor of evidence; the customer task at production lengths is the test that counts, and the probes exist because that task is expensive to label at every length.

The research numbers are for the models tested, at the dates of the papers, between 2023 and 2025. Newer models improve on some of these tasks, and a model that passes NoLiMa at 32K today may fail at 128K. The method transfers; the numbers do not.

Length is not the only variable. Prompt structure (instructions at the start or end), the share of the input that is relevant, and whether the model is asked to quote or to reason all move results. A suite that varies only length can attribute to length what belongs to structure.

Finally, a model that does well on long inputs may still be the wrong design. Context Rot's 300-token focused prompts beat 113,000-token full prompts on its task. If retrieval gets the relevant passages into a short prompt, the long-context score matters only for the questions retrieval misses.
