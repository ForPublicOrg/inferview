## The decision

An image-heavy workload is over budget or under accuracy, and the levers are the same three every time: send fewer pixels (downsample), send the right pixels (crop, possibly with a small thumbnail for context), or send the same pixels to a cheaper model tier. Each lever moves cost and accuracy in a way that depends on the provider's counting rule, so the first step is to compute what the current request actually costs per model family, and the second is to measure accuracy at the candidate budgets. A token figure without its model family is not a number; the same 1920 by 1080 screenshot is 1,560 tokens on one Claude tier and 2,691 on another.

## The inputs that change it

**The counting rule.** Three published schemes are in use, checked 2026-10-28:

- *Claude:* ⌈*w* / 28⌉ × ⌈*h* / 28⌉ visual tokens. Models from Claude 4.7 on are on a high-resolution tier (long edge up to 2,576 px, up to 4,784 tokens); all others are on a standard tier (1,568 px, 1,568 tokens). Larger images are downscaled to the largest aspect-preserving size within both limits.
- *OpenAI patch models* (gpt-5.5, the gpt-5.4 family, gpt-5.2 and gpt-4.1-mini): ⌈*w* / 32⌉ × ⌈*h* / 32⌉ patches, scaled down to the detail level's patch budget, then multiplied (1.2 for all of them except gpt-4.1-mini, which is 1.62). *OpenAI tile models* (gpt-5.1, gpt-4o, gpt-4.1, gpt-4o-mini): fit into 2048 × 2048, scale the short side to 768 px, then charge a base plus a cost per 512 px tile; gpt-4.1 is 85 plus 170 per tile.
- *Gemini:* 258 tokens for an image of 384 px or less on both sides; larger images are tiled into 768 × 768 tiles of 258 tokens each. Video is 263 tokens per second for static processing, and the same page's processing table gives about 100 tokens per second at low media resolution and about 300 at high; audio is 32 tokens per second.

**The detail setting's default.** On gpt-5.5, `auto` uses `original`: up to 10,000 patches and 6,000 px. A 3840 × 2160 screenshot is 120 × 68 = 8,160 patches, so ⌈8,160 × 1.2⌉ = 9,792 tokens. With `detail: high` it is fitted to 2048 × 1152, 64 × 36 = 2,304 patches, 2,765 tokens. On the gpt-5.4 family the trap points the other way: `low` keeps a 2,048 px maximum with a 6,144-patch budget, so a 2048 × 2048 image is 4,096 patches (4,916 tokens) at `low` and is scaled to the 2,500-patch `high` budget (3,000 tokens). OpenAI's guide says outright that `low` there can use more tokens than `high`.

**The smallest detail the task needs.** Downsampling a 1920-pixel-wide screenshot to 1456 multiplies every length by 1456 / 1920 = 0.76, so 12 px text becomes about 9 px. Whether 9 px is legible to a given model is a measurement, not a calculation.

**Where the detail is.** If the information sits in a predictable region (a dialog, a totals box, a chart legend), a crop recovers resolution cheaply. If it can be anywhere, cropping needs a locator, and the locator's miss rate becomes part of the error budget.

**Volume and price.** Per-image cost is tokens times the input price of the specific model, so a family's rule only matters once the model inside it is fixed.

## The options and when each wins

**Downsample.** Wins when the task is about layout, objects or large text, and when the model would resize anyway: on a standard-tier Claude model a 1920 × 1080 screenshot is resized server-side to 1456 × 819, so sending 1456 × 819 yourself costs the same 1,560 tokens and cuts upload size and latency. Loses on small print, and its failure is silent: the answer is fluent and wrong.

**Crop plus thumbnail.** Wins when the detail is localised and the location is known or cheaply found. The thumbnail keeps enough context to answer "which screen is this"; the crop keeps native resolution where it matters. Costs a second image block, a locator, and a coordinate system you must keep straight: Claude returns coordinates in the resized image, not your original.

**Switch tier.** Wins when the task tolerates the cheaper model's reading and the per-token price gap is larger than the token gap. A standard-tier model resizes to at most 1,568 tokens per image, and the Claude docs say a high-resolution image can use up to about three times the tokens of the standard tier. Loses when the reason for the high-resolution tier was the detail itself: computer use, dense documents, screenshots with small text.

**Change provider.** Rarely the first move, because accuracy on your images differs by model more than tokens do, but the counting rules mean the ranking of providers by price can flip with image size. Run the calculator at your real sizes before concluding.

## A worked decision

<Callout kind="aside" label="Scenario">
A support tool classifies screenshots users attach to tickets. 30,000 a day, all 1920 × 1080, sent today to Claude Opus 5.5 ($4 per million input tokens). The answer depends on reading the text of an error dialog in 12 px type, which the app always draws centred in an 800 × 600 region. Finance wants the image bill down by half without a measurable drop in accuracy.
</Callout>

Opus 5.5 is on the high-resolution tier, so today each screenshot is unresized, 69 × 39 = 2,691 tokens, $0.010764, and the day costs 30,000 × $0.010764 = $322.92.

| Option | Sent | Claude tokens | Per day |
|---|---|---|---|
| As today, Opus 5.5 | 1920 × 1080 | 2,691 | $322.92 |
| Downsample, Opus 5.5 | 1456 × 819 | 52 × 30 = 1,560 | $187.20 |
| Crop 800 × 600 plus 728 × 410 thumbnail, Opus 5.5 | two images | 29 × 22 + 26 × 15 = 638 + 390 = 1,028 | $123.36 |
| Haiku 4.5, standard tier, full screenshot | resized to 1456 × 819 | 1,560 | $46.80 |

<Predict id="mml-crop-saving" question="How many dollars a day does the crop-plus-thumbnail option save against today's spend?" answer={199.56} tolerance={2} unit="USD per day">
$322.92 − $123.36 = $199.56 a day, a 62% cut, from 1,028 tokens against 2,691. Prompt text and output tokens are the same in both and are not counted.
</Predict>

Downsampling meets the budget on paper but shrinks the dialog text to about 9 px, which is the detail the task depends on. Haiku 4.5 is cheapest by a wide margin but receives the same 9 px text and is a smaller model. The crop keeps the dialog at native resolution and still cuts the bill by more than half, because the dialog's location is fixed.

The decision is the crop, conditional on a measurement: run a labelled sample of a few hundred tickets through today's configuration and the crop configuration, and compare accuracy with a paired test, since both see the same tickets. Run Haiku on the crop as well; if it matches, the bill drops further, from 1,028 tokens at $1 per million.

What this gives up: a ticket whose screenshot does not show the dialog in the expected place (a resized window, a second monitor, a mobile screenshot) now loses its detail, and the thumbnail at 0.38 scale will not recover it. That share must be measured, not assumed small.

## Pages, video and audio

**A page** is an image at whatever resolution it was rendered. An A4 page scanned at 130 DPI is 1075 × 1520: 2,145 tokens on a high-resolution-tier Claude model, resized to 924 × 1307 (33 × 47 = 1,551 tokens) on the standard tier. On gpt-5.5 at `high` the same page is 34 × 48 = 1,632 patches, 1,959 tokens; on gpt-4.1 it is fitted to 768 × 1085 and charged 85 + 6 × 170 = 1,105; on Gemini it spans 2 × 2 tiles, at most 1,032. When you upload a PDF to Claude, pages are rasterised server-side at dimensions you do not control, so count the tokens through the token-counting endpoint rather than computing them.

**Video** is priced per second on Gemini's page, and the rate depends on the processing mode: 263 tokens per second for static processing, and about 100 or about 300 per second depending on media resolution. A one-minute clip is 60 × 263 = 15,780 tokens at the static rate, or 6,000 to 18,000 across the other two. Quote the mode with the figure.

**Audio** on Gemini is 32 tokens per second, so an hour is 32 × 3,600 = 115,200 tokens.

**Many images.** Claude accepts up to 100 images per request on 200k-context models and 600 on others, but a request with more than 20 images applies a stricter per-image dimension limit; resizing every image to 2,000 px or less on both sides avoids it. Every image counts toward the threshold, including screenshots resent in earlier turns of an agent loop.

## What to measure after deciding

- Input tokens per request from the usage field, split by image count, against the figure you computed. A gap means the pipeline is sending something you did not intend: an extra image, an unresized original, a resent history.
- Accuracy per image-size bucket on a fixed labelled set, rerun when the model or the detail setting changes.
- The share of images the provider resized. On Claude, setting `oversized_image: "error"` on image blocks turns a silent resize into a 400, which is the cheapest drift detector available.
- For crops: the locator's miss rate on a random sample that bypasses it.

## When this is wrong

Every figure here is a published rule on 2026-10-28 for named models. Providers change tiers, budgets and defaults with new models, and the Gemini price for 3.8 Flash doubles on 2027-01-01. Recompute when either changes.

Gemini's page states the tile size and the tokens per tile but not how a partial tile is counted or whether an image is scaled before tiling, so the Gemini figures in the calculator are upper bounds. Count with the API before budgeting.

Token count predicts cost, not accuracy. Two configurations with the same token count can read small text very differently, and a model on a cheaper tier may do worse on a crop than a stronger model does on a downsample. The worked decision is only as good as the paired accuracy comparison behind it.

Image tokens are often not the whole bill. Long system prompts, document text extracted alongside page images, and output tokens on a reasoning model can dominate; compute the image share before optimising it.
