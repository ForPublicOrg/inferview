## The decision

Prep time is finite and loops differ by company, role family and quarter, so the decision is which formats to practise for and in what order. The atlas below is the input: per role family, the rounds, the AI policy per round where one is stated, and the level signals, each row labelled by where it comes from.

**Official** means the company's own page or post. **Press** means a journalist or podcast reporting the company's statements or documents. **Vendor** means a prep company's reconstruction. **Candidate report** means one person's account. Every row was checked against its source on 28 October 2026, and the date of the underlying information is given where the source states it. Synthesised question banks, which list questions with no candidate or company behind them, are never used to tag a company here.

## AI security

No official page, press report or first-hand candidate account describing an AI security engineering loop was found on 28 October 2026; search results were aggregator guides without a named candidate or company source, which this atlas does not use. Until one exists, the company-wide policy rows above (Anthropic, OpenAI, Amazon) are the only dated facts for these loops, and the recruiter is the source for round formats.

## Level signals across families

Two vendor descriptions are specific enough to use. Hello Interview's system design levels page expects a senior candidate to go deep in "around 2 places" and a staff candidate to "lead almost the entire interview". Its OpenAI L5 guide lists ownership, alternatives considered, rejected approaches, enabling other engineers and a disagreement told as reasonable on both sides. Synthesia's staff post states the scope directly: projects spanning months and multiple teams, with product, security, legal and infrastructure partners.

## The options and when each wins

**Practise to one published format.** Wins when the target list is short and the companies publish their loop, as Sierra, Synthesia and Meta's AI-enabled round do. The practice can mirror the rounds, their lengths and their AI policy exactly.

**Practise the union of round types, weighted by minutes.** Wins with a long or uncertain target list. Sum the stated minutes per round type across targets and spend practice in that proportion, after first covering any round type that appears in every target.

**Practise the no-AI baseline first, then the AI-allowed formats.** Wins when any target bans or defaults against AI in live rounds (Anthropic by default, Amazon). Meta's assessed competencies in its AI round are problem solving, code quality, verification and communication, which the no-AI practice also builds; the reverse transfer is weaker, because AI-allowed practice does not show whether you can produce the code unaided.

## Worked decision

Scenario (constructed for this playbook): a staff backend engineer has four weeks and three targets: the Synthesia staff role, an OpenAI FDE role and an Anthropic applied role.

Round types across the three rows: past-work and behavioural rounds appear in all three (Synthesia's two, OpenAI's hiring manager and values round plus a deep dive that revisits the take-home, Anthropic's hiring manager, behavioural and culture-fit rounds). Design appears in all three (system design, solution design from a customer brief, ML design for a regulated customer). A long take-home appears only in the OpenAI FDE row, and live coding with AI welcome only in Synthesia's.

The union-by-minutes option wins here, with the no-AI rule as a constraint. Stories come first because they serve every target, and the release-criteria, concern-under-pressure and adoption prompts from the [staff stories lesson](/learn/rounds/behavioural/staff-stories) cover the Anthropic culture-fit and hiring-manager reports as well as Synthesia's Delivery and Impact round. Design practice comes second, with at least one case carrying regulatory constraints. The OpenAI take-home is scheduled when it arrives, using the [build-and-defend playbook](/learn/rounds/take-home-debug/build-and-defend), since its timing is the company's. Coding practice runs without AI by default, because the Anthropic rounds may, and adds one AI-assisted session before the Synthesia round.

What the engineer gives up: no practice on Meta's or Google's formats, which are not on the list, and less coding depth than a coding-heavy loop would need.

## What to measure after deciding

For each target, ask the recruiter for every round's format, length and AI policy, and record where the answer differs from the atlas row with the date; the OpenAI guide tells candidates to ask when unsure, and Anthropic's guidance turns on what the interviewer indicates. After each loop, note which round types you met, which stories you used, and which follow-ups you could not answer from your appendix. Those three records decide the next allocation better than any row here.

## When this is wrong

The atlas is a snapshot. Sierra changed its loop in April 2026, Google's pilot starts in the second half of 2026, and Meta's round was introduced in October 2025; any row can be out of date by the time it is read, and candidate reports lag further. Rows labelled vendor or candidate report rest on few accounts, sometimes one, and the Cognition, Cohere and Anthropic reports do not give round lengths.

Company-wide policies are not team policies. OpenAI's guide says expectations for AI vary by interview, and Anthropic's guidance has exceptions by design, as its own performance take-home shows. Treat a company row as a default to confirm, not a format to rely on.

The options assume stated minutes are a fair proxy for weight in the decision. A 45-minute leadership round can carry a veto that a 75-minute coding round does not, and no source here says how any company weights its rounds.
