## The decision

An API charges per request; a GPU fleet charges per hour whether or not requests arrive. Self-hosting therefore wins above a break-even volume:

V* = (G + P) / a

G is the monthly cost of the GPUs provisioned for peak at your latency objective, P is the monthly cost of the people who run them, and a is the API's effective cost per request after the discounts you would actually get. The comparison is valid only when the open model you would host and the API model you are pricing pass the same eval, and only when the fleet can serve V* at your latency objective. The arithmetic is the reserved-instance calculation, with one difference that dominates the decision: the per-request price on the other side of the inequality falls by a large factor every year, so V* moves even when nothing in your system changes.

## Dated inputs

GPU prices on Lambda's pricing page, read 2026-10-28, per GPU-hour plus tax: an on-demand 8x H100 SXM instance at $3.99, a single H100 SXM at $4.29, an 8x B200 at $6.69. Multi-node 1-Click Clusters of H100 cost $6.16 at 16 GPUs, $5.85 at 64 and $5.54 at 256, for terms from 2 weeks to 1 year; longer commitments and reserved capacity are quoted on request. On this price list the cluster product costs more per GPU-hour than a single on-demand node, so a model that needs several nodes pays a premium before it serves a token.

Measured throughput, with its conditions. The SGLang team served DeepSeek-V3 on 96 H100s with prefill and decode on separate nodes (LMSYS blog, 2025-05-05). Prefill reached 54,543 tokens per second per node at 2,000-token prompts on 4 nodes. Decode reached 22,282 tokens per second per node on 9 nodes at batch 256 with 2,000-token inputs, 5.2 times the throughput of their tensor-parallel baseline on the same hardware. Each phase was measured with unlimited resources for the other, so these are upper bounds per phase. The team states the configuration targets throughput, with time to first token of 2 to 5 seconds and about 100 ms between tokens. That is a batch-job latency profile, not a chat one.

API prices on Anthropic's pricing page, read 2026-10-28, per million tokens: Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $2 input and $10 output, Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10 and $0.50 for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, and 50% off both through the Batch API.

## A worked break-even

Take a workload of 2,000 input and 500 output tokens per request and the SGLang throughput. Per request, prefill uses 2,000 / 54,543 = 0.0367 node-seconds and decode 500 / 22,282 = 0.0224, so 0.0591 node-seconds in total. At the 64-GPU cluster price, a node costs 8 × $5.85 = $46.80 an hour, or $0.0130 a second, which makes $768 per million requests at full utilisation.

The API side for the same request: Sonnet 5.5 costs 2,000 × $2 / 10^6 + 500 × $10 / 10^6 = $0.009, or $9,000 per million requests. Haiku 5.5 costs $450 per million.

The fixed side: 96 GPUs at $5.85 for 730 hours (8,760 / 12) is $409,968 a month. Against Sonnet 5.5, V* = $409,968 / $0.009 = 45.6 million requests a month before people. The cluster's capacity at full utilisation is 12 × 3,600 × 730 / 0.0591 = 533.5 million requests a month, so break-even sits at 8.5% of capacity. Against Haiku 5.5, V* is 911 million requests a month, above what the cluster can serve, so this configuration never breaks even against that model.

Which comparison is valid is a quality question, not an arithmetic one. If your eval says the open model matches Sonnet 5.5 on your feature, self-hosting clears break-even at modest utilisation; if it says Haiku 5.5 is enough, no amount of volume makes this cluster pay. People cost enters linearly: each $10,000 a month of engineering and on-call time raises V* against Sonnet 5.5 by 10,000 / 0.009 = 1.11 million requests.

The throughput number carries most of the risk. The 5.2 times gap between the optimised and baseline decode configurations on identical hardware is the size of the error from assuming someone else's throughput: on the baseline, decode alone would cost 5.2 times as many node-seconds. A smaller deployment also cannot reach batch 256 with the same expert-parallel layout. Measure your own engine on your own prompts at your latency objective before using any published figure.

```python
import math

def self_host_usd_per_request(in_tok, out_tok, prefill_tps_node, decode_tps_node,
                              usd_gpu_hour, gpus_per_node=8, utilisation=1.0):
    """Node-seconds of prefill plus decode, priced per node-second, divided by utilisation."""
    node_seconds = in_tok / prefill_tps_node + out_tok / decode_tps_node
    return node_seconds * usd_gpu_hour * gpus_per_node / 3600 / utilisation

def api_usd_per_request(in_tok, out_tok, usd_in_mtok, usd_out_mtok):
    return (in_tok * usd_in_mtok + out_tok * usd_out_mtok) / 1e6

def breakeven_requests_per_month(fixed_usd_month, api_usd_req):
    return fixed_usd_month / api_usd_req

def months_until_flip(margin, yearly_price_factor):
    """Volume is `margin` times break-even today; API price for the capability falls smoothly."""
    return 12 * math.log(margin) / math.log(yearly_price_factor)

# SGLang on H100, DeepSeek-V3, 2K input (LMSYS, 2025-05-05); Lambda H100 cluster tier, checked 2026-10-28.
sh = self_host_usd_per_request(2_000, 500, 54_543, 22_282, usd_gpu_hour=5.85)
fixed = 96 * 5.85 * 730                                   # 96 GPUs, 730 hours a month
sonnet = api_usd_per_request(2_000, 500, 2.00, 10.00)     # Claude Sonnet 5.5, checked 2026-10-28
print(round(sh * 1e6), round(fixed), round(sonnet * 1e6))                 # 768 409968 9000
print(round(breakeven_requests_per_month(fixed, sonnet) / 1e6, 1))       # 45.6 (million)
print(round(months_until_flip(2, 9), 1), round(months_until_flip(2, 50), 1))  # 3.8 2.1
```

When the model fits on one node, invert the question: compute the throughput the node must sustain to break even, then check whether your engine reaches it. One on-demand 8x H100 node costs $3.99 × 8 × 730 = $23,302 a month. To match Haiku 5.5 at $0.00045 per request it must serve 51.8 million requests a month, which is 19.7 requests a second around the clock: 39,400 input and 9,850 output tokens a second, every second, at your latency objective. Requirements stated this way turn a vague debate into one load test.

## The API side after caching and batch

The a in the formula is the price you would really pay, not the list price. Two discounts move it the most. Batch processing is 50% off on Anthropic's page and roughly 50% on OpenAI's, for work that can wait. Prompt caching discounts a repeated prefix, but every miss pays a write premium, so it saves money only above a break-even hit rate.

On the worked request, suppose 1,500 of the 2,000 input tokens are a shared prefix with an 80% hit rate on Sonnet 5.5's 5-minute cache (writes at 1.25 times input, hits at 0.05 times). The prefix costs 0.8 × 0.05 + 0.2 × 1.25 = 0.29 of list, so input falls from $0.004 to $0.00187, and the request costs $0.00687 instead of $0.009. V* rises from 45.6 to 59.7 million requests a month. Through the Batch API as well, the request costs $0.00344 and V* is 119.4 million. A self-hosting case built on list prices for work that could run as a batch is wrong by more than a factor of two before any GPU is bought. The self-hosted side gets prefix reuse too, so a fair comparison applies caching on both sides; the published throughput above was not measured with it.

## How fast the break-even moves

Epoch AI (2025-03-12) measured how fast the price of reaching a fixed benchmark score fell over three years across six benchmarks. Depending on the benchmark and threshold, it fell between 9x and 900x per year, with a median of 50x; reaching GPT-4's score on GPQA Diamond got 40x cheaper per year. The fastest trends began after January 2024, and with earlier data removed the median rose to 200x per year, though the authors say it is less clear the fastest drops will persist.

Read the method before using the rate. The price is a 3:1 blend of input and output prices, it is the price of the cheapest model at or above the score at each date, and reasoning models are excluded. Most of the fall comes from new, cheaper models reaching the bar, not from a given model's price being cut. Collecting it means switching models, which through an API is a configuration change behind an eval, and on your own fleet is a deployment project.

Per-token price also overstates the per-task change across tokenizers. Anthropic's page states that Claude 4.7 and later produce about 30% more tokens for the same text. Sonnet 4.6 at $3 input and Sonnet 5.5 at $2 look 1.5 times apart per token; per unit of text the ratio is 3 / (2 × 1.3) = 1.15. Prices also do not only fall on schedule: the same page records that Sonnet 5's introductory $2 and $10 became the standard price and the increase to $3 and $15 planned for 2026-09-01 will not happen. A cost model that had booked the increase was wrong in the API's favour.

The recompute calendar follows. Recompute V* when the API price list for your candidate models changes, when a new model passes your eval at a lower price, when the GPU contract comes up for renewal, when traffic shape changes utilisation, and at least quarterly regardless, since even the slowest measured rate moves V* by 20% a month. Write the inputs and their dates next to the decision, so the next recompute changes numbers instead of reopening the argument.

## When this is wrong

Cost is often not the reason to self-host. Data residency, a fine-tuned model no API serves, rate limits that cap a launch, or latency control at the tail can each justify a fleet that loses on the break-even, and the arithmetic then measures what the requirement costs rather than whether to meet it.

The formula treats G as flat, but provisioned GPUs follow peak traffic, not average. A workload with a peak three times its average runs at most at one third utilisation unless batch work fills the troughs, which is why mixed interactive and batch fleets clear break-even sooner than either alone.

Epoch's rates describe the cheapest model at a benchmark threshold, priced by a 3:1 blend, with reasoning models excluded. Your traffic may be output-heavy, may need a reasoning model, or may depend on a capability no benchmark tracks. Use the rates to set the recompute interval, not to forecast your own bill.

The GPU side moves too. Newer open models at the same quality may need fewer GPUs, and GPU-hour prices change, but there is no measurement on this page of how fast. If you assume the self-hosted cost falls at the API's rate, you are assuming you will migrate models as often as an API customer switches them.
