## The accounting

Fine-tuning memory has two parts with different scaling laws. Model states (weights, gradients, optimizer state) scale with the number of trained parameters and nothing else. Activations scale with micro-batch size, sequence length and depth, and do not care how many parameters are trainable. Every method in this lesson attacks the first part; only recomputation and smaller micro-batches attack the second.

The ZeRO paper's section 3.1 gives the model-state count for mixed-precision Adam. Forward and backward run on fp16 weights (2 bytes) and produce fp16 gradients (2 bytes). The update needs an fp32 copy of the weights plus Adam's momentum and variance, 4 bytes each, which the paper calls K = 12. The total is 2 + 2 + 12 = 16 bytes per parameter, so its 1.5B GPT-2 example needs 1.5 × 16 = 24 GB before a single activation. With bf16 the byte counts are the same.

Some stacks keep an fp32 gradient accumulation buffer next to the half-precision gradient so that micro-batch gradients sum without precision loss. That adds 4 bytes: 2 + 2 + 4 + 12 = 20. Which of the two your framework does is a configuration fact to check, and it moves an 8B estimate by 32 GB.

For the Llama 3.1 8B config the parameter count follows from the shapes. Attention per layer is 4096 × 4096 for the query and output projections and 4096 × 1024 for key and value (8 key-value heads of 128), 41,943,040 parameters. The MLP is three 4096 × 14336 matrices, 176,160,768. With two 4,096-wide norms a layer is 218,112,000, and 32 layers make 6,979,584,000. The untied input and output embeddings add 2 × 128,256 × 4,096 = 1,050,673,152, and the final norm 4,096: 8,030,261,248 in total.

Full fine-tuning model states are then 8.03B × 16 = 128.5 GB, or 160.6 GB at 20 bytes. Neither fits on one 80 GB GPU.

## Sharding: what ZeRO buys

ZeRO's data-parallel stages partition model states across Nd GPUs instead of replicating them. Stage 1 partitions the optimizer state (the paper reports a 4x reduction at large Nd), stage 2 adds gradients (8x), stage 3 adds the parameters (linear in Nd). The paper's Figure 1 uses 7.5B parameters on 64 GPUs: 7.5 × 16 = 120 GB replicated; stage 1, 4 × 7.5 + 12 × 7.5 / 64 = 31.4 GB per GPU; stage 2, 2 × 7.5 + 14 × 7.5 / 64 = 16.6 GB; stage 3, 16 × 7.5 / 64 = 1.9 GB.

Sharding moves the cost from memory to the interconnect. The ZeRO paper reports stages 1 and 2 at the same communication volume as plain data parallelism; stage 3 adds parameter traffic, which is cheap on a single node with fast links and noticeable across nodes.

## Activations, the part that does not shrink

The ZeRO paper's own example: the 1.5B GPT-2 at sequence length 1K and batch 32 needs about 60 GB of activations, against 24 GB of model states. Activation checkpointing, which keeps only some layer inputs and recomputes the rest in the backward pass, brings that to about 8 GB, paid for in recomputation.

For the 8B config at sequence 4,096 and micro-batch 1, a lower bound with full checkpointing is the saved layer inputs: 4,096 tokens × 4,096 hidden × 2 bytes × 32 layers = 1.07 GB. A term people miss sits at the end of the network: if the loss is computed on fp32 logits, the logits alone are 4,096 × 128,256 × 4 bytes = 2.1 GB, double that at 8,192 tokens, and the gradient of the logits is the same size again. With a 128K vocabulary the output layer, not the transformer stack, can set the peak.

## LoRA: train a low-rank update, freeze the rest

LoRA leaves each adapted weight W frozen and trains W + BA, with B of shape d_out × r and A of shape r × d_in; B starts at zero so training starts from the base model. Only A and B have gradients and optimizer state. The frozen weights still need storing at 2 bytes each, and activations still flow through every layer, so activation memory is unchanged.

Per layer of the 8B config, adapters on all seven projections add r × (d_in + d_out) each: query and output 8,192 r each, key and value 5,120 r each, gate, up and down 18,432 r each, 81,920 r in total. Over 32 layers that is 2,621,440 r; at rank 1 about 2.6M, which matches the "about 3M" the LoRA Without Regret post gives for this model. At rank 16 it is 41,943,040, 0.52% of the model, and its model states are 41.9M × 16 = 0.67 GB. Add 8.03B × 2 = 16.1 GB of frozen weights and LoRA needs about 16.7 GB of model states instead of 128.5 GB.

QLoRA stores the frozen base in 4-bit NormalFloat, 0.5 bytes per parameter, so 4.0 GB for this model plus quantization constants (which double quantization shrinks), with the adapters kept at higher precision. Paged optimizers absorb short spikes. The paper fine-tuned a 65B model on one 48 GB GPU: 65 × 0.5 = 32.5 GB of 4-bit weights, which leaves about 15 GB for adapters, constants and activations.

The LoRA paper's headline, 10,000x fewer trainable parameters and 3x less GPU memory than full fine-tuning of GPT-3 175B with Adam, is the same accounting: trainable parameters shrink by four orders of magnitude, memory by a factor of three, because frozen weights and activations remain.

## What LoRA does not save, and when it loses

Compute. LoRA Without Regret counts slightly more than 2/3 of full fine-tuning's FLOPs per pass, because the backward pass still computes activation gradients through every frozen matrix. A plan that budgets one third of the full fine-tune's GPU hours has confused memory with compute.

Quality conditions. The same post reports LoRA matching full fine-tuning on small and medium instruction and reasoning datasets, with four conditions. Apply it to all layers: attention-only LoRA at rank 256 (0.25B parameters) underperformed MLP-only LoRA at rank 128 (0.24B) on Llama-3.1-8B, so the gap is placement, not parameter count. Raise the learning rate: the optimal rate was about 10x full fine-tuning's (a fitted multiplier of 9.8, about 15x for runs under roughly 100 steps). Keep batches moderate: LoRA lost more than full fine-tuning as batch size grew, regardless of rank. And mind capacity: once the dataset exceeds what the adapter can absorb, LoRA falls behind. For policy-gradient RL even rank 1 matched full fine-tuning, because each episode carries little information.

## The decision

Given a model, hardware and a dataset, the order of questions is:

1. Do the frozen weights fit, at 2 bytes (LoRA) or 0.5 bytes (QLoRA), with room left for activations at the real sequence length? If not, shard or pick a smaller model.
2. Is the dataset small or medium for the model, and is the goal behaviour rather than new knowledge? Then LoRA on all layers at about 10x the full learning rate is the default; full fine-tuning has to earn its memory.
3. Is it a large knowledge-heavy dataset, or did LoRA plateau above full fine-tuning on a held-out slice? Then full fine-tuning with ZeRO stage 2 or 3, priced in GPU-hours and interconnect.
4. Run one training step at the real sequence length and micro-batch before quoting hardware. The estimate is for model states; the step measures the rest.

## When this is wrong

The 16-byte count assumes plain mixed-precision Adam. 8-bit optimizers shrink the 12-byte term, CPU offload moves it out of GPU memory, and other optimizers keep different state; the structure of the calculation holds, the constant does not.

QLoRA's 0.5 bytes per parameter assumes everything is quantized. If your stack leaves the input and output embeddings in bf16, which is worth checking, the 8B model's 1.05B embedding parameters cost 2.1 GB instead of 0.5 GB.

And allocators fragment. The estimate is a floor; the first training step on the target hardware at the real sequence length is the number to put in the plan.
