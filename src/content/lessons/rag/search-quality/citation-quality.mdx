## A citation is a claim about a span

An answer engine that attaches a source to a sentence is asserting that a reader who opens the source will find the sentence's content there. That is a stricter property than retrieving a relevant document. A retrieval evaluation asks whether the right documents reached the context window. A citation evaluation asks, for each statement in the answer, whether the specific text it points at supports it. The two come apart in at least four ways: the right document cited at the wrong passage; a statement that combines two facts with a citation for only one; a citation to a passage that is on topic but says something different; and a factual sentence with no citation at all. All four pass a check that the cited documents are relevant to the question.

This lesson builds the per-statement check, shows what provider citation features do and do not guarantee, and sets the span size, which decides how strict the check can be.

## What has been measured

Liu, Zhang and Liang audited four generative search engines in 2023 (Bing Chat, NeevaAI, perplexity.ai and YouChat) on 1,450 queries each, with human annotators judging every sentence and citation. On average, 51.5% of generated sentences were fully supported by their citations, and 74.5% of citations supported the sentence they were attached to. The spread between engines was wide: citation recall from 11.1% (YouChat) to 68.7% (perplexity.ai), citation precision from 63.6% (YouChat) to 89.5% (Bing Chat).

Two correlations in the same study matter for anyone setting a product metric. Citation precision was inversely correlated with how useful annotators found the answers, at r = -0.96 across the four engines, and precision correlated with how closely statements copied their cited text (r = 0.80). Bing Chat had the highest precision and the lowest utility rating, and the authors' explanation is copying: text lifted from a cited page is easy to support and often does not answer the question. A team that optimises citation precision alone can reach it by quoting, and users will notice the answers getting worse before the metric does.

These are 2023 systems and none of the numbers describe today's products. The measurement design is the durable part.

## Recall and precision per statement

ALCE (Gao and colleagues, EMNLP 2023) turned the human protocol into an automatic one. The answer is split into statements at sentence boundaries, each citing at most three passages. Citation recall for a statement is 1 when the concatenation of its cited passages entails it, judged by an NLI model. Citation precision is computed per citation: a citation counts against precision when it does not support the statement on its own and the remaining citations still do, so it added nothing. A citation attached to an unsupported statement also scores 0.

The automatic judge was TRUE, a T5-11B model fine-tuned for entailment. Against human judgments it reached kappa 0.698 for recall and 0.525 for precision, 85.1% and 77.6% accuracy. Precision is the harder judgment for a model and for people, because it asks about the marginal contribution of each citation. On ELI5's long-form questions, the best configuration (ChatGPT with output reranking) scored 69.3 recall and 67.8 precision, and the paper reports that even the best models lacked complete citation support half the time on that dataset.

## What provider citation features guarantee

Claude's citations feature takes documents with `citations.enabled` set (on all documents in a request or none) and returns answer text split into blocks, each with the locations it cites. Plain text and PDF documents are chunked into sentences, and a citation carries a character range or a page range; custom content documents are cited by whole content block. Search result blocks, which a tool can return or the application can place in the user turn, carry a source and title you choose and are cited by content-block range, with the whole block as the smallest citable unit. Citations on search results are off by default and, again, all or nothing per request.

The documentation states what this buys. Because the model emits citations in a standardised internal format that the API parses, and the API extracts `cited_text` itself, citations are guaranteed to contain valid pointers into the documents you provided, and `cited_text` does not count toward output tokens. Anthropic also reports, from its own evaluations and without a figure, that the feature is more likely to cite the most relevant quotes than prompting for citations. Two operational limits: citations cannot be combined with structured outputs (the request returns a 400), and image content is not citable, so a scanned PDF without extractable text cannot be cited.

A valid pointer is the structural half of the check. It rules out invented sources and quotes that do not exist in the document, which prompt-based citation produces. It does not establish that the span entails the sentence it is attached to. Recall and precision in the ALCE sense still have to be measured.

The provider feature also hands you the knob that sets how strict that measurement can be. With search result blocks, `cited_text` is the full text of the cited blocks. If each block is a 1,500-token chunk, every citation returns 1,500 tokens, and an entailment check over that much text passes statements that are supported by one sentence in it and contradicted by another. If each block is a self-contained passage of a few sentences, the cited text is close to the evidence and the check is meaningful. Perplexity describes the same decision on the retrieval side: its search index splits documents into self-contained spans that are retrieved and ranked individually, so the unit that reaches the model, and that an answer can cite, is already small. The [Perplexity teardown](/learn/rag/search-quality/perplexity) at the end of this module covers that system.

## The checks, in code

Four checks cover an answer engine: the pointer is valid (the provider gives you this), each factual statement has a citation (recall's denominator), the cited spans entail the statement (recall), and each citation contributes (precision). The scoring follows ALCE exactly; the entailment function is where your judge goes, and it needs the same validation against human labels as any other judge.

```python
def citation_scores(statements, entails):
    """ALCE-style citation recall and precision.
    statements: list of (claim, [cited passage ids]); entails(passage_ids, claim) -> bool,
    backed in production by an NLI model or a validated LLM judge."""
    recall_hits, prec_hits, n_cites = 0, 0, 0
    for claim, cites in statements:
        supported = bool(cites) and entails(cites, claim)
        recall_hits += supported
        for c in cites:
            n_cites += 1
            rest = [x for x in cites if x != c]
            irrelevant = not entails([c], claim) and bool(rest) and entails(rest, claim)
            prec_hits += supported and not irrelevant
    return recall_hits / len(statements), (prec_hits / n_cites if n_cites else 0.0)

# Which sets of passages support which claim, as a judge would decide them (scenario).
SUPPORT = {
    "S1": [{"p1"}],
    "S2": [{"p2"}],                  # p3 is on topic but does not state the claim
    "S3": [],                        # p4 is the right document, wrong passage
    "S4": [],                        # no citation at all
    "S5": [{"p1", "p5"}],            # needs both: a number from p1, a date from p5
}
def entails(cites, claim):
    return any(need <= set(cites) for need in SUPPORT[claim])

answer = [("S1", ["p1"]), ("S2", ["p2", "p3"]), ("S3", ["p4"]), ("S4", []), ("S5", ["p1", "p5"])]
r, p = citation_scores(answer, entails)
print(f"citation recall {r:.2f}, citation precision {p:.2f}")
# citation recall 0.60, citation precision 0.67

# The check a document-level metric runs instead: is every cited document relevant to the question?
relevant_docs = {"p1", "p2", "p3", "p4", "p5"}
print("document-level pass:", all(c in relevant_docs for _, cs in answer for c in cs))
# document-level pass: True
```

With the Claude response format, a statement is the run of text blocks up to a sentence end, and its citations are the union of the `citations` lists on those blocks; the cited span is `cited_text`. Two decisions are left to you. What counts as a statement that needs a citation: a sentence of transition text ("Here is what the policy says") inflates the recall denominator, so exclude non-factual sentences with a rule you write down, as the Liu study did by letting annotators drop statements not worth verifying. And what the judge does with partial support: ALCE counts only full entailment, which is the right default for anything a user may act on.

Report recall and precision per query stratum, beside answer correctness, and never alone. The Liu correlations show both directions of gaming: precision rises by copying text, and recall rises by citing every sentence to a long block that happens to contain the words.

## When this is wrong

Entailment by a cited span is not truth. A citation to an outdated policy page can score perfectly on both metrics while the answer is wrong for today, so citation metrics sit beside correctness and freshness checks, not in place of them.

Sentence-level statements fit prose answers. Tables, lists and code in an answer have no natural statement boundary, and ALCE handles list answers only by treating each list entity as a statement; for other shapes you define the unit yourself, and the metric is only as meaningful as that definition.

The automatic judge agrees with people at kappa 0.525 on precision in the ALCE study, which leaves room for disagreement on exactly the marginal citations precision is about. Treat small precision differences between systems as noise until a human-labelled sample confirms them.

The provider details here (sentence chunking, block-level citing, the structured-output restriction, the all-or-nothing setting) are as documented on 28 October 2026 and are the kind of detail that changes between releases.
