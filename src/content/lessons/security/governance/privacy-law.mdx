## A deletion request is a traversal

Under GDPR Article 17 a controller erases personal data "without undue delay" when one of six grounds applies, and Article 12(3) gives one month from receipt to tell the person what was done, extendable by two further months for complex or numerous requests if the person is told within the first month. Article 17(3) carves out processing needed for a legal obligation or for legal claims. That is the whole legal frame this lesson needs; the engineering problem is that an AI feature multiplies the places a person's data lands, and a deletion that reaches the product database and stops there has not happened.

Treat the request as a traversal of a graph whose nodes are stores and whose edges are copies. The data-flow inventory is the graph. Any store that is not in it is not traversed, which is why the inventory, not the deletion code, is the control that fails first.

## The stores an AI feature adds

<div class="tablewrap">

| Store | Why it holds personal data | Deletion mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conversation transcripts | The user's messages and the model's replies | Active delete | Usually the only store the product team thinks of |
| Traces and application logs | Prompts in span attributes, tool arguments, retrieved chunks | TTL, or active delete if indexed by subject | Often the longest-lived copy with the least owner attention |
| Per-inference audit log | Model version, prompt hash or excerpt, outcome | Depends on the design | For a high-risk system, AI Act Articles 19 and 26(6) want at least six months, without prejudice to data protection law |
| Vector index | Embeddings plus the chunk text stored beside them | Active delete by document id | The stored chunk text makes the vector linkable even if the vector alone were not |
| Response or semantic cache | Full answers keyed on similar prompts | TTL or delete by key | Can serve one user's data to another if the cache key omits the tenant |
| Provider-side state | Abuse logs, stored responses, files, batches | Provider terms, or the provider's delete API | See the next section; some of it you cannot delete |
| Eval and golden sets | Production conversations copied in as examples | Rebuild without the subject | Copied by hand, so missing from automated lineage |
| Fine-tuning datasets | Training examples from production | Rebuild without the subject | Versioned snapshots each hold a copy |
| Fine-tuned weights | Possibly the data itself, memorised | Retrain, or a documented anonymity assessment | The EDPB opinion below is about this row |
| Warehouse and backups | Everything, again | Retention policy; restore-time suppression list | Backups are why a tombstone list outlives the deletion |

</div>

## The model as a store

EDPB Opinion 28/2024, adopted on 17 December 2024 at the Irish supervisory authority's request, answers whether a model trained on personal data is itself personal data. Its position is that such models cannot in all cases be considered anonymous. For a model to count as anonymous, two likelihoods must both be insignificant, taking into account all the means reasonably likely to be used: direct extraction of personal data about people in the training set, including probabilistic extraction, and obtaining such data from the model through queries, intentionally or not.

The opinion turns that into evidence a supervisory authority would examine. Paragraph 55 names structured testing against attribute and membership inference, exfiltration, regurgitation of training data, model inversion and reconstruction, and adds that passing tests for widely known attacks is evidence of resistance to those attacks only. Among the mitigating measures it lists for the legitimate-interest assessment are honouring erasure requests even when the Article 17(1) grounds do not apply, and accepting claims of regurgitation with enough detail to reproduce them so the controller can assess unlearning techniques (paragraph 102); and, at deployment, output filters and post-training techniques that attempt to remove or suppress personal data (paragraph 107). Where development was unlawful, paragraph 114 lists among possible corrective measures erasing the unlawfully processed part of the dataset or, where that is not possible and depending on the facts and proportionality, the whole dataset and the model itself; remediation such as retraining is something the authority may weigh in judging proportionality.

For a team that fine-tunes on production data, the consequence is a design decision made before the first training run, not after the first request. Either the training data is stripped of personal data so the tuned model never holds any; or deletion requests are batched into scheduled retrains, with the schedule stated in the privacy notice; or the team holds an anonymity assessment with the paragraph 55 test results and accepts that it covers only the attacks tested. Output filters reduce what the model emits; they lower a rate, and they do not remove anything from the weights.

## Provider data controls, compared

Both large API providers publish their retention by feature, and the differences decide which features a privacy-sensitive product can use. The table is as of 28 October 2026.

<div class="tablewrap">

| Question | OpenAI API | Claude API |
|---|---|---|
| Is API data used for training? | No, since 1 March 2023, unless the customer opts in | Retained data is not used for training without express permission |
| Default retention of prompts and outputs | Abuse-monitoring logs up to 30 days | Not retained by default, except Covered Models, which require 30-day retention |
| Zero retention | ZDR or Modified Abuse Monitoring, by prior approval; ZDR forces `store` to false | ZDR per organisation, by arrangement |
| Stateful features outside zero retention | Conversations, Assistants, Threads, vector stores, files, fine-tuning jobs, evals and batches kept until deleted; Responses state at least 30 days when stored | Batch (29 days), Files (until deleted or expired), Managed Agents transcripts (until deleted) |
| Kept regardless | Image and file inputs flagged as CSAM, retained for manual review even under ZDR | Flagged content up to 2 years; data required by law |
| Caches | Prompt-cache tensors not retained after a 24-hour expiration | Structured-output schemas cached up to 24 hours; keep personal data out of schemas |
| Geography | Per-project residency; regional processing in the US, Europe and UAE, storage only in seven other regions; Europe needs ZDR, MAM or another approved retention arrangement, the other non-US regions MAM or ZDR; 10% uplift for eligible models released on or after 5 March 2026 | `inference_geo` of `global` or `us` per request, 1.1x for `us`; workspace geo sets data at rest and is `us` only, fixed at creation |

</div>

Two rows change designs. Zero retention does not extend to stateful features: a team with a ZDR arrangement that adopts the batch or file endpoint has moved that data outside the arrangement, and both providers say so. And Anthropic separates where inference runs from where data rests: `inference_geo` pins the compute per request, while data at rest, such as uploaded files, follows the workspace geography, which today is US only and cannot be changed after the workspace is created. An EU customer asking for EU processing gets a different answer from each provider, and from the cloud marketplaces, where the region comes from the endpoint.

Provider abuse logs are the one store you cannot delete from. They are the provider's processing under its terms; the deletion design records them in the subprocessor register with their retention, and the answer to the data subject says when they expire.

## Timing the traversal

<p>Scenario inputs: the deletion request arrives on day 10; the user's last message was on day 9. The transcript store deletes immediately. Traces expire 90 days after they are written. The provider's abuse-monitoring log keeps the call up to 30 days. The eval set is rebuilt from production on day 31, and the fine-tuning dataset and model on day 75, both excluding deleted users.</p>

<p>Expiry days: transcripts day 10; traces 9 + 90 = day 99; provider log 9 + 30 = day 39; eval set day 31; fine-tune day 75. The last is the trace store at day 99, <b>89 days after the request</b>. One month plus the two-month extension is roughly 89 to 92 days depending on the calendar, so the plan fits only by using the full extension, which Article 12(3) ties to the complexity and number of requests, not to a TTL the team chose. The fix is to make traces deletable by subject, which moves the last copy to the day 75 retrain, still beyond one month and now a design choice to state in the privacy notice.</p>
</Predict>

The planner below makes the traversal explicit. It refuses to run if the data-flow inventory names a store that has no deletion handler, which is a construction control for "forgot a store" that holds exactly as well as the inventory does.

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass
from datetime import date, timedelta

@dataclass
class Store:
    name: str
    mode: str        # "delete": now; "ttl", "rebuild", "provider": after `days`
    days: int = 0    # days from the request until the copy is gone

def deletion_plan(stores, requested, inventory):
    handled = {s.name for s in stores}
    missing = sorted(set(inventory) - handled)
    if missing:
        raise RuntimeError(f"no deletion handler for: {missing}")
    return {s.name: requested + timedelta(days=0 if s.mode == "delete" else s.days) for s in stores}

inventory = ["transcripts", "traces", "provider_abuse_log", "eval_set", "finetune", "vector_index"]
stores = [Store("transcripts", "delete"), Store("traces", "delete"),
          Store("provider_abuse_log", "provider", 29), Store("eval_set", "rebuild", 21),
          Store("finetune", "rebuild", 65), Store("vector_index", "delete")]
plan = deletion_plan(stores, date(2026, 11, 2), inventory)
last = max(plan.values())
assert last == date(2027, 1, 6)
print("last copy gone", last, "| beyond one month:", last > date(2026, 12, 2))
```

## When this is wrong

The legal frame here is the GDPR and the EDPB's reading of it. Other jurisdictions set different deadlines, different exceptions and different definitions of personal data, and a product sold in several needs counsel's map, not this table.

Unlearning is not a deletion mechanism the opinion endorses. Paragraph 107 lists post-training techniques that attempt to remove or suppress personal data as mitigations, which is a weaker claim than erasure, and a team that reports a request as completed on the strength of one is reporting a rate as a guarantee.

The provider comparison is a snapshot of documentation pages checked on 28 October 2026, and Anthropic's Covered Models list names specific models that a later release will extend. The subprocessor register should record the date each row was checked and be rechecked when a provider ships a model or an endpoint you adopt.

Whether an embedding is personal data on its own is contested. The practical answer in most systems is that it does not matter, because the index stores the source chunk and a document id beside the vector; if a design truly stores vectors alone, the anonymity reasoning of the opinion applies and needs its own evidence.
