## Two budgets

A training step's memory splits into two budgets that scale with different things. Model states scale with parameter count and nothing else. Activations scale with layers, hidden size, micro-batch and sequence length, and not with the optimizer. Every technique in this module shrinks one of the two, and choosing among them starts with knowing which budget is over.

## Model states: 16 bytes per parameter

The ZeRO paper's accounting for mixed-precision Adam, with Psi parameters:

| Tensor | Bytes per parameter |
|---|---|
| BF16 (or FP16) parameters, used in forward and backward | 2 |
| BF16 gradients | 2 |
| FP32 master copy of the parameters | 4 |
| FP32 Adam first moment | 4 |
| FP32 Adam second moment | 4 |
| Total | 16 |

The optimizer's share, the last three rows, is the paper's K = 12. That is the piece ZeRO-1 shards first, because it is three quarters of the total and is touched once per step. The Playbook adds a fifth row that many stacks carry: an FP32 gradient accumulation buffer of 4 bytes, for 20 bytes per parameter. Llama 3's report says it accumulates gradients in FP32 across micro-batches, so a stack configured that way pays the 20.

The arithmetic is not optional. The ZeRO paper's own example: a 1.5B-parameter GPT-2 needs at least 1.5B x 16 = 24 GB for model states, before any activation, while its BF16 weights alone are 3 GB. The ratio of training memory to inference weights is 8 to 1 before activations.

## Activations: where sequence length bites

Korthikanti et al. count the bytes each transformer layer stores for the backward pass, with 16-bit activations, sequence length s, micro-batch b, hidden size h and a attention heads:

```
per layer = s * b * h * (34 + 5 * a * s / h)
```

The 34 is the sum of every intermediate kept by the attention block and the MLP: inputs to the QKV projection, the attention output, the MLP's 4h intermediate, layer-norm inputs, dropout masks. The 5as/h term is the attention score matrix, its softmax output and its dropout mask, each of size a x s x s per sequence. That term is quadratic in s; the 34 term is linear.

<Predict id="trn-dist-act-layer" question="For the same scenario model (h = 4096, a = 32) at s = 4096 and b = 1, how many GB of activations does one layer store, using sbh(34 + 5as/h)?" answer={3.25} tolerance={0.05} unit=" GB">
<p>sbh = 4096 x 1 x 4096 = 16,777,216. 5as/h = 5 x 32 x 4096 / 4096 = 160, so the bracket is 194, and one layer stores 16,777,216 x 194 = <b>3.25 GB</b>. Thirty-two layers store 104.2 GB. At s = 2048 the bracket falls to 34 + 80 = 114 and the total to 30.6 GB: halving the sequence cut activations by 3.4 times, because the attention term dominates at this size.</p>
</Predict>

So for this model at 4K tokens, activations are as large as the model states, and they scale with the micro-batch: b = 4 is 417 GB. The ZeRO paper's own figure lands in the same place: its 1.5B GPT-2 at sequence length 1K and batch 32 needs about 60 GB of activations against 24 GB of model states.

## The four levers on activations

**Full recomputation.** Store only each layer's input, 2sbh bytes, and recompute the layer in the backward pass. For the scenario model that is 2 x 16,777,216 = 33.5 MB per layer and 1.07 GB for all 32, a 97 times cut. The price is an extra forward pass: the Playbook puts it at up to 30 to 40% more compute and time, and the ZeRO paper cites about 33% for its example. Full recomputation is the fallback that always fits and always costs.

**Selective recomputation.** Recompute only what is cheap in FLOPs and expensive in bytes: the attention scores, softmax and dropout, the 5as/h term. The per-layer cost becomes sbh x 34, 570 MB for the scenario layer. The Playbook reports a 70% activation memory reduction at 2.7% compute cost for GPT-3 175B. FlashAttention never materialises the score matrix and recomputes it in the backward pass, so, as the Playbook notes, most stacks using it already have selective recomputation of attention without configuring it.

**Tensor parallelism.** Sharding the matmuls over t GPUs shards the activations inside them, but not the layer-norm and dropout regions between them. Korthikanti's formula with tensor parallelism alone is sbh(10 + 24/t + 5as/(ht)): the 10 is the part every rank still stores in full.

**Sequence parallelism.** Shard those layer-norm and dropout regions along the sequence dimension too, and the 10 joins the shard: sbh(34/t + 5as/(ht)). With selective recomputation on top, a layer stores sbh x 34/t. For the scenario model at t = 8, that is 71.3 MB per layer and 2.28 GB for 32 layers, against 104.2 GB with none of the levers. The communication is the same, because the all-reduces become pairs of reduce-scatter and all-gather.

Korthikanti et al. report that sequence parallelism plus selective recomputation cut activation memory by about 5 times and removed over 90% of the time overhead of full recomputation. On a 530B model on 2,240 A100s, that moved MFU from 42.1% with full recomputation to 54.2%, 29% faster. That number is the reason the default for large dense models moved from "recompute everything" to "recompute attention, shard the rest".

## What else is on the card

The two budgets do not exhaust the card. Communication buffers for bucketed gradients and all-gathered parameters, temporary workspace for kernels, the logits tensor at the output (s x b x vocabulary, large at 128K vocabularies) and allocator fragmentation all take memory that no formula above counts. Gradient accumulation adds its own term: the Playbook notes that accumulation keeps gradient buffers alive through the whole step, where a single micro-batch could free activations as gradients are produced.

Production reports show the remaining tricks are specific. DeepSeek-V3 recomputes RMSNorm and the MLA up-projections, keeps the exponential moving average of the parameters in CPU memory, and shares the embedding and output head between the multi-token prediction module and the main model, which together let it train without tensor parallelism.

## A planner in a dozen lines

```python
def step_memory_gb(psi, L, h, a, s, b, t=1, dp=1, zero=0, recompute="none", fp32_acc=False):
    """Per-GPU memory in GB for a dense GPT-style model; pipeline parallelism ignored."""
    psi_gpu = psi / t                                     # tensor parallelism shards weights
    grads = 4 if fp32_acc else 2
    p_b, g_b, o_b = 2 * psi_gpu, grads * psi_gpu, 12 * psi_gpu
    if zero >= 1: o_b /= dp
    if zero >= 2: g_b /= dp
    if zero >= 3: p_b /= dp
    sbh = s * b * h
    per_layer = {
        "none": sbh * (10 + 24 / t + 5 * a * s / (h * t)),    # TP without SP
        "sp": sbh * (34 / t + 5 * a * s / (h * t)),
        "selective_sp": sbh * 34 / t,
        "full": 2 * sbh,
    }[recompute]
    return (p_b + g_b + o_b + L * per_layer) / 1e9


psi = 12 * 32 * 4096**2 + 2 * 32000 * 4096
print(round(step_memory_gb(psi, 32, 4096, 32, 4096, 1), 1))                                      # 211.4
print(round(step_memory_gb(psi, 32, 4096, 32, 4096, 1, t=8, recompute="selective_sp"), 1))      # 15.7
print(round(step_memory_gb(psi, 32, 4096, 32, 4096, 1, dp=32, zero=1, recompute="full"), 1))    # 30.4
```

The [training memory planner lab](/labs/training-memory-planner) extends this to pipeline splits and a search over layouts.

The second and third lines are two ways to fit the same model on 80 GB cards: tensor parallelism over a node with selective recomputation, or ZeRO-1 over 32 GPUs with full recomputation. The first pays tensor-parallel communication inside every layer; the second pays a third more compute. Which is cheaper depends on the interconnect, which is the subject of the next three lessons.

## When this is wrong

The 34 in Korthikanti's formula is for a specific GPT block with a 4h MLP, layer norms, dropout and 16-bit activations. SwiGLU MLPs, RMSNorm without dropout, grouped-query attention, MLA and MoE layers each change the constant. Rederive it for the block you train, or measure peak memory on one layer and scale.

The formula counts what autograd saves, not what the allocator holds. Fragmentation, communication buffers and the output logits can add several gigabytes, and the peak usually occurs at a specific moment in the step, often the first backward layer, not at the sum of all terms. Plan with headroom and confirm with a memory profile.

FP8 training mostly changes the activation side. DeepSeek-V3 runs its matmuls in FP8 with fine-grained scaling and dispatches MoE activations in FP8; how it stores optimizer state changes the per-parameter row, so recount the 16 bytes from the report or the code of the stack you use rather than assuming them.

Pipeline parallelism adds a dimension this lesson ignored: a stage holds activations for several micro-batches in flight, and the count depends on the schedule. The pipeline lesson computes it.
