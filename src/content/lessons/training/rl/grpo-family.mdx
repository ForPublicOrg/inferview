## The problem these papers solve

GRPO, from DeepSeekMath, removed the value model by scoring G samples per prompt and normalising each reward by the group's mean and standard deviation. It then averaged each sample's per-token loss over that sample's length, averaged the samples, kept a symmetric clip of ε = 0.2 around the sampling policy, and added a KL term to a reference. Each of those choices looked neutral and each turned out to bias long reasoning runs. Dr. GRPO, DAPO and GSPO are three diagnoses of which choice broke what, published between March and July 2025. Read together, they are a configuration review of one algorithm.

## Zero-signal groups

A group where every sample gets the same reward has zero standard deviation and zero advantage for every sample. The prompt cost G full generations and contributes no gradient. As training succeeds, more prompts become all-correct, so the useful fraction of each batch shrinks exactly when the run is going well.

DAPO's fix is dynamic sampling: over-sample prompts and drop groups whose accuracy is 0 or 1 until the batch is full of groups with mixed outcomes. In DAPO's ablation it is the largest single step, from 42 to 50 on AIME 2024. The cost is generation that is thrown away, and a batch whose difficulty mix is set by the filter rather than by the dataset.

## Dr. GRPO: two normalisations that bias the update

**Length.** GRPO divides each sample's summed token loss by its own length |o_i|. A wrong answer with advantage −1 and 200 tokens pushes each token down by 1/200 = 0.005; the same advantage on a 2,000-token wrong answer pushes each token down by 0.0005, ten times less. The policy learns that long wrong answers are cheap. Liu et al. report that GRPO keeps lengthening responses after the reward gain slows, and that Dr. GRPO substantially reduces the length of incorrect responses on evaluation benchmarks.

**Difficulty.** Dividing by the group standard deviation up-weights questions where almost every sample agrees. In a group of 16 with one correct sample, p = 1/16, the standard deviation is √(0.0625 × 0.9375) = 0.242, and the correct sample's advantage is (1 − 0.0625) / 0.242 = 3.87. In a group with eight correct, the standard deviation is 0.5 and the advantages are ±1. One lucky sample on a nearly impossible question moves the policy almost four times as hard as a correct answer on a question the model solves half the time.

Dr. GRPO removes both terms: no standard-deviation division, and a constant in place of the per-sample token count, for example the generation budget. Their minimalist recipe on Qwen2.5-Math-7B reports 43.3% on AIME 2024. In TRL this is `loss_type="dr_grpo"`, whose docs recommend the max completion length as the constant, and `scale_rewards=False` turns off the standard-deviation scaling.

## DAPO: four changes and no KL

DAPO trains Qwen2.5-32B base on maths with 512 prompts per rollout step, 16 responses per prompt, and mini-batches of 512 responses, so each rollout step takes 8,192 / 512 = 16 gradient updates. With 16 updates per batch the ratio drifts from 1 and the clip binds, which is what makes the clip changes matter.

**Clip-higher.** ε_low stays at 0.2, ε_high rises to 0.28. A token sampled at probability 0.01 can rise to at most 0.012 per batch under a 0.2 clip, a gain of 0.002; a token at 0.6 can rise to 0.72, a gain of 0.12, sixty times larger in absolute probability. Raising ε_high to 0.28 lifts the first cap to 0.0128. The symmetric clip throttles exactly the low-probability tokens that carry exploration, and DAPO raises ε_high "to leave more room for the increase of low-probability tokens".

**Token-level loss.** Average the loss over all tokens in the batch instead of averaging per sample first. A long response then contributes in proportion to its tokens, and long wrong answers are no longer discounted. This addresses the same length bias as Dr. GRPO from a different normaliser.

**Overlong shaping.** Truncated responses are scored as if their content were wrong, which punishes a correct line of reasoning for being long. DAPO first masks truncated samples out of the loss, then replaces that with a soft penalty: none up to 16,384 tokens, linear down to −1 across a 4,096-token cache, and generation cut at 20,480. A 18,432-token response gets (18,432 − 16,384) / 4,096 = 0.5 subtracted.

**No KL.** DAPO excludes the KL term, arguing that for long chain-of-thought training "the model distribution can diverge significantly from the initial model", so the restriction is not necessary.

The paper reports 50 on AIME 2024 (avg@32) against 47 for DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B, using 50% of its training steps. The ablation, cumulative from naive GRPO at 30: overlong filtering 36, clip-higher 38, soft overlong punishment 41, token-level loss 42, dynamic sampling 50.

## GSPO: a sequence-level ratio

The token-level ratio π_θ / π_old for one token is an importance weight estimated from a single sample of that token's distribution. Zheng et al. argue it does not correct the distribution mismatch it is meant to correct, adds high-variance noise that accumulates over long responses, and can cause collapse that is "often irreversible". GSPO replaces it with one ratio per response, the length-normalised sequence likelihood ratio: (π_θ(y|x) / π_old(y|x)) raised to 1/|y|, a geometric mean of the token ratios, and clips that.

The normalisation is not cosmetic. If every token in a 1,000-token response has a ratio of 1.001, the raw sequence ratio is 1.001<sup>1000</sup> = e<sup>0.9995</sup> = 2.72, far outside any clip; the geometric mean is 1.001. GSPO's clip ranges are 3e-4 and 4e-4, against 0.2 and 0.27 in their GRPO baseline, and the fraction of clipped tokens differs by two orders of magnitude between the two.

The case that made it matter is mixture of experts. In the 48-layer Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, about 10% of the experts activated for a sample change after a single gradient update, so the token ratio compares log-probabilities computed through different experts. GRPO needed Routing Replay (replaying the old routing during the update) to converge on MoE; GSPO did not. The paper credits GSPO for the Qwen3 RL improvements. A second consequence for infrastructure: sequence likelihoods are robust enough that the inference engine's values can be used directly instead of recomputing them in the trainer.

## What changed in practice

TRL's GRPO trainer, on the main-branch docs checked for this lesson, defaults to β = 0.0 (no KL term, no reference model loaded), `loss_type="dapo"` (token-level normalisation), ε = 0.2 with `epsilon_high` unset (the docs point to DAPO's 0.28), 8 generations per prompt, `scale_rewards="group"`, `importance_sampling_level="token"` with `"sequence"` available for GSPO, and `mask_truncated_completions=False`. The default object is no longer the 2024 GRPO: it is DAPO's loss without DAPO's clip-higher, overlong masking or dynamic sampling, and with group standard-deviation scaling that Dr. GRPO argued against. Treat each flag as a decision to record in the run config, with the version you read the default from.

## Choosing for a stated task

**Maths or code with a verifier, dense model, long responses.** Token-level loss, ε_high 0.28, dynamic sampling, mask or softly penalise truncated samples, no KL. Watch response length and entropy; length growth on wrong answers is the bias Dr. GRPO describes.

**The same task on an MoE policy.** Sequence-level ratios (GSPO), or token-level with Routing Replay if the framework supports it. Without either, expect instability that a learning-rate cut will not fix.

**Chat with a learned reward model.** Keep β above zero. DAPO's argument for dropping the KL is about verifiable rewards where the correct answer is fixed; with a learned reward model the reference is the only limit on drift towards what the reward model over-scores.

**Small batches or few generations per prompt.** Dynamic sampling wastes a larger share of a small batch, and the group standard deviation of 4 samples is noisy. Remove the standard-deviation division before adding filters.

## When this is wrong

Each paper's headline number is one model family on maths benchmarks. DAPO's 50 and its ablation are Qwen2.5-32B base on AIME 2024 with avg@32; Dr. GRPO's 43.3 is a 7B maths model. The ordering of the ablation steps is cumulative, so the size of each step depends on what was already applied.

The zero-gradient argument assumes binary rewards. With graded or learned rewards, all-equal groups are rare, and the standard-deviation term behaves differently; Dr. GRPO's difficulty bias is clearest with 0/1 rewards.

Library defaults move. The TRL defaults above are from the main-branch docs on the date checked; a pinned release can differ, and a default in a trainer is a maintainer's choice for a general user, not a recommendation for your task.
