## The decision

Model selection is an eval run followed by a filter. The eval ranks candidates on the team's task at its latency and cost targets. The filter removes candidates whose terms the feature cannot live with: what the licence lets you do with the weights and their outputs, what the provider keeps and where, and when the model stops answering. The filter is cheaper to run than the eval, so run it first on the long list and evaluate only the survivors.

This playbook covers the filter. Building the eval is in the evals track; the price side of the comparison is in [Unit economics of an AI feature](/learn/llmops/economics-selection/unit-economics).

## Licence clauses that change the answer

"Open model" covers licences with very different obligations. Three families cover most open-weight candidates.

**Llama 4 Community License.** Three clauses matter in practice. A company whose products had more than 700 million monthly active users in the calendar month before the Llama 4 release date must request a separate licence, which Meta may grant at its discretion; the test is a snapshot at that date, so a later Llama version's licence is what catches a company that grows past the threshold afterwards. Anyone distributing Llama materials or a product containing them must prominently display "Built with Llama". And a distributed model created, trained or fine-tuned with Llama materials or their outputs must have a name beginning with "Llama". The Acceptable Use Policy is incorporated by reference, so its text is part of the licence, and the agreement is governed by California law.

**Gemma Terms of Use.** Google reserves the right to restrict usage, "remotely or otherwise", that it reasonably believes violates the terms or the Prohibited Use Policy. Anyone distributing Gemma or a Model Derivative must make those use restrictions an enforceable provision of their own agreement and pass on a copy of the terms. The definition of Model Derivative reaches further than most engineers assume: it includes a model trained by distillation from Gemma or on synthetic data Gemma generated, although Gemma's outputs themselves are not derivatives. A team that uses Gemma to generate training data for another model has made that model subject to the terms.

**Apache 2.0 and MIT.** Apache 2.0 grants perpetual, irrevocable copyright and patent licences with no field-of-use restriction and no user threshold. Its obligations apply on redistribution: include the licence, mark modified files, and carry the NOTICE file's attributions. Its patent licence ends for a licensee who sues claiming the work infringes a patent. MIT's one condition is that the copyright and permission notice travel with the copies. Neither has an acceptable use policy, a naming rule or a vendor right to restrict use.

**Open weights are not open source.** The Open Source Initiative's definition, version 1.0, requires freedom to use the system for any purpose without asking permission, and a preferred form for modification that includes three things: data information detailed enough for a skilled person to build a substantially equivalent system, the complete code for training, data processing and running, and the parameters. A licence with a use policy fails the first condition; weights released without training code and data information fail the second. Call these models open-weight in design documents, because legal review reads "open source" as a claim about the licence.

Which clause binds depends on what the team does with the model:

| Use | Llama 4 | Gemma | Apache 2.0 / MIT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serve internally, no distribution | Acceptable Use Policy; user threshold | Prohibited Use Policy; remote restriction right | none beyond the licence |
| Ship weights to customers (on-prem product) | Built with Llama; licence copy | Pass use restrictions on as enforceable terms; terms copy | Licence copy; NOTICE; change notices |
| Distribute a fine-tuned or distilled model | Name must begin with Llama | It is a Model Derivative, same obligations | Licence copy; NOTICE |
| Train another model on its outputs | Counts if the result is distributed (name rule) | Distillation and synthetic-data training make a derivative | No restriction |

Read the current text before deciding; both Meta's and Google's terms are versioned and dated, and the Gemma terms carry a last-modified date of 1 April 2026.

## Data retention and residency

Hosted models are filtered by what the provider keeps. OpenAI's data controls page states that API data is not used for training unless the customer opts in, and that abuse-monitoring logs are kept for up to 30 days by default. Zero Data Retention and Modified Abuse Monitoring need OpenAI's approval. Several endpoints keep application state until deleted and are not ZDR-eligible (Assistants, threads, vector stores, files, fine-tuning jobs, evals and batches), and the Responses API keeps state for 30 days by default. European data residency requires one of the two approved controls.

Two consequences for selection. The endpoint is part of the choice: a feature built on a stateful endpoint keeps data at the provider whatever the model. And residency has a price: on Claude 4.6 and later models, pinning inference to the US with `inference_geo` multiplies every token price by 1.1.

## Retirement schedules

Anthropic gives at least 60 days' notice before retiring a publicly released model, and requests to a retired model fail. Claude Sonnet 4.5 was deprecated on 30 September 2026 and retires on 30 November 2026. The schedule also differs by platform: the dates apply to Anthropic-operated platforms, while Amazon Bedrock and Google-managed Vertex set their own, which is why the pricing page lists Claude Opus 4.1 as retired except on those two.

The selection input is not the date but the gap between the notice period and the team's migration time.

<Predict id="ops-selection-migration-slack" question="A notice arrives on 30 September for a 30 November retirement. <p>30 September to 30 November is 31 + 30 = 61 days. The migration is 3 + 2 + 3 = 8 weeks, 56 days. The rollout ends <b>5 days</b> before retirement, with no room for a stage that fails. Under the 60-day minimum notice it would be 4. A team in this position either shortens the pipeline before the next notice or starts migration when a successor ships, not when the notice arrives.</p>

Self-hosted weights do not retire, but they age: the team carries the upgrade decision and every security fix in the serving stack.

## Worked decision

Scenario: a vendor of document-management software wants a summarisation model inside its on-premises product, installed in customers' data centres. Its customers include two that will not accept outbound calls from the product. The candidates after the eval are a hosted frontier model, a Llama 4 model, a Gemma model and an Apache 2.0 model, with the hosted model best on the eval and the three open-weight models within the agreed quality margin.

- The hosted model fails the filter for the two customers: their data cannot leave, and the product cannot depend on a provider's retirement schedule in a data centre the vendor does not operate.
- The Llama 4 model passes, with obligations: the product must display "Built with Llama", and a fine-tuned version shipped to customers must have a name beginning with "Llama". Marketing has to agree to that name before engineering starts the fine-tune.
- The Gemma model passes with a different cost: the vendor's customer agreement must carry Gemma's use restrictions as enforceable terms, which legal has to draft into every contract, and Google's right to restrict usage it believes violates the terms becomes a dependency of every installation.
- The Apache 2.0 model passes with a licence file and a NOTICE.

The decision: the Apache 2.0 model, fine-tuned on the vendor's labelled summaries. What the vendor gives up is the quality margin to the best open model, which the eval measured as acceptable, and a fine-tune on a weaker base. The decision note records the eval margin, the licence obligations of the rejected candidates and the condition for revisiting: a model with a permissive licence that closes the gap to the hosted model on the eval.

## What to measure after deciding

The eval margin to the next candidate, rerun when a new model ships. The retirement date of every hosted model in use, with the measured duration of the last migration beside it. Licence text versions in use, with their dates, stored next to the model artefacts so an audit can show which terms applied when. For hosted models, the retention settings per endpoint, checked after any change of endpoint or account.

<p>The check is the definition, not the model card: Gemma's terms define derivatives to include distillation and training on synthetic data it generated, while plain outputs are not derivatives. Using it as a teacher therefore carries its terms into the student. On a mobile app every install is a distribution, so the use restrictions go into the end-user agreement, and the app has to be able to comply with a restriction Google asserts.</p>
<p>The constraint conflict is calendar against quality. With one legal reviewer and ten weeks, I would ask legal for the time to add the clause and the copy of the terms in parallel with a second distillation run from an Apache 2.0 teacher. Both students go through the same eval; if the permissive one is within the agreed margin, it ships and the Gemma student is dropped, giving up some quality for terms that need no pass-through. ## When this is wrong

The table summarises clauses; it is not legal advice, and the licences are versioned. A model's licence can change between versions of the same family, so the filter runs per checkpoint, not per family.

Some organisations can accept clauses this playbook treats as costs. An internal tool that is never distributed meets none of the distribution obligations, and the use policies may never bind. For them the filter is shorter and the eval decides.

Hosted terms are negotiated, not only published. Enterprise agreements can change retention, residency and notice periods, so the published pages are the floor of what is available, and the agreement on file is what applies.
