# Gap research: what interview loops ask in 2026, by company and level

Angle: what the loops for AI engineer, applied AI, forward-deployed engineer (FDE), ML engineer, research engineer, ML infra and AI security roles look like in 2026, compared against the Inferview bank in `src/content/problems` (154 problems) and the rounds lessons in `src/content/lessons/rounds/`.

Every source below was opened on 2026-10-28 unless the entry says otherwise. "Checked" means the page was fetched and the fact was read on it. Three primary pages refused automated fetches on 2026-10-28 (openai.com/interview-guide, openai.com/index/harness-engineering, metacareers.com): facts from them are marked "via" the secondary page that quotes them, and a reviewer should open them in a browser before publishing.

Provenance labels, used throughout:

- **official**: the company's own careers page, job post, engineering blog or a named executive on the record.
- **press**: a news outlet reporting internal documents or an executive interview.
- **candidate**: a candidate's own report (Aced/Exponent experiences, PracHub experiences, Blind, Glassdoor).
- **vendor**: an interview-prep business describing a loop. Unverified.
- **synthesised**: a question bank that says its questions are written from public focus areas, not reported (ombharatiya/AI-Engineer-Interview-Questions says this on every company page). Never use these to tag a company.

### 1.3 What is missing

**Question themes with no coverage.**

1. Progressive-spec coding: in-memory database or KV store with transactions, then undo, then TTL or snapshots; in-memory file system; credit tracker with expirations. Reported at Anthropic, OpenAI, Ramp, Perplexity (sources in 1.1 and section 3). Zero problems.
2. Build-and-defend work samples: Sierra Plan/Build/Review, Cursor's day-long build in their codebase, Cognition's few-hour product build, OpenAI FDE take-home with walkthrough. Zero problems; `rnd-010` is the closest and is a RAG design, not a timed build with a review.
3. Codebase rounds: bug squash in a real multi-file library (Stripe), PR review where tests pass but production breaks (Netflix, Sierra pilot), code comprehension (Google pilot, Meta phase 1). The bank's debug problems are single snippets with a hypothesis pick.
4. Integration rounds: multi-part API exercises with idempotent payment reconciliation and auth headers collected while crawling (Stripe, Ramp). Only `api-001` (easy pagination) exists.
5. Harness engineering as a design topic at staff level: routing, planner and supervisor patterns, tool orchestration, termination, model and effort routing, regression gates on harness changes, cost per successful task (Decagon, Perplexity JDs). No problem asks for it.
6. FDE decomposition with deployment constraints: regulated-industry customer, hosting model, government contractor, a first-two-weeks plan (Anthropic report, OpenAI FDE report).
7. Project deep dive and presentation rounds: 30-minute project presentation (OpenAI), paper presentation (Cohere staff), 5-minute Loom (Cognition), 5 to 10 minute technical talk (Sierra).
8. Research-engineer content: post-training and distillation pipeline design (Cohere staff), PPO and RLHF mechanics (landed bank, Anthropic), DPO loss in code (vendor-reported for OpenAI research), rapid-fire quiz format (DeepMind).
9. ML infra at fleet scale: routing across accelerators, autoscaling across production and research workloads, model release pipelines (Anthropic Inference JD), credit and quota ledgers (OpenAI L5 coding title "Design a credit tracking service for user token balances"; Perplexity "CreditTracker with expirations").
10. Security of ML systems beyond the prompt: protecting model weights, a malicious dependency in a training pipeline, secrets incident response, hardening GPU Kubernetes, red-teaming AI-generated code, RL environment sandboxing, agent identity and delegated authority (Anthropic security report and JDs, Cursor JD).
11. Customer-agent operations: escalation rate doubles after a model upgrade; deterministic policy limits inside an LLM agent; chat to voice (Sierra-style themes; the Sierra question list in the ombharatiya bank is synthesised, so these are themes, not company tags).
12. Data-analysis notebook rounds: a Colab analysis (Ramp).
13. Staff behavioural: release criteria for a new model (OpenAI L5 list), an ethical concern raised under executive pressure (Anthropic report), "Did people actually adopt it?" (Anthropic report).

**Question types missing.** SPEC section 6 has seven types, all single-stage. Loops use:

- multi-stage code with requirements added per stage and hidden tests per stage (CodeSignal levels, "each round adding more functionality");
- repo labs: several files, a failing or passing test suite, find and fix or review a diff;
- timed work samples graded by a rubric and a review conversation (PLAN.md lists a `take-home` type; SPEC does not have it);
- follow-up chains where the interviewer adds a constraint after each answer (DeepMind ML screen; Hello Interview's OpenAI guide describes constraints added mid-conversation);
- presentations and deep dives with a probe list;
- rapid-fire timed quizzes;
- role-play beyond one problem.

**Existing problems below the level asked.**

- Easy items tagged senior: `fnd-002`, `fnd-003`, `fnd-005`, `api-001`, `api-007`, `evl-003`, `evl-016`, `evl-017`, `rag-001`, `rag-010`, `agt-007`, `sfe-007`, `inf-012`, `rnd-005`, `rnd-009`, `rnd-013`, `sys-003` (17 items). Loops do ask some of these as openers (Netflix: "What is a KV cache?"; Databricks L4: "What is a transformer?"), then go deeper. Either relabel them as warm-ups or turn each into the first stage of a chain.
- `sys-001`, `sys-017`, `sys-002`, `evl-015`: senior design prompts with no staff stage.
- The three rate limiters and two duplicated hand-writes noted in 1.2.
- `rnd-013`: recall of policies rather than performance in the round.
- No problem is staff-only, while staff loops (Cohere L6, OpenAI L5, Synthesia Principal/Staff, Decagon Staff) ask for post-training design, paper defence, production architecture and harness ownership.

## 2. Material to add

Each topic lists its format: lesson, teardown, lab, playbook or paper walkthrough. All sources checked 2026-10-28 unless marked.

**T1. The 2026 loop atlas (playbook).** One page per role family (applied AI and FDE, research engineer, ML infra, AI security, SWE at AI companies) showing rounds, AI policy per round and level signals, every row labelled official, press, candidate or vendor, with a last-checked date. Replaces the four-row table in `rounds/behavioural`. Sources: https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview; https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance; https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/AI-resistant-technical-evaluations; https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/meta-ai-enabled-coding; https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/google-is-testing-a-new-rule-transform-job-interviews; https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-ai-is-changing-software-engineering; https://itpro.com/business/careers-and-training/amazon-bans-ai-tools-during-job-interviews; https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/synthesia/6afd3236-71d3-4f67-92e6-b99a868a3260.

**T2. Progressive-spec coding lab (lab).** Four-stage Pyodide problems with hidden tests per stage and a clock: in-memory database (set, get, delete; then filtered scans; then TTL with an injected clock; then backup and restore); in-memory file system (mkdir, touch, ls; then cd and relative paths); credit ledger with expiring grants. Teaches state design that survives stage three. Sources: https://www.aced.io/experiences/ramp-ai-engineer-interview-13abb1; https://prachub.com/companies/perplexity-ai; https://www.hellointerview.com/guides/openai/l5; https://github.com/landedjobs/awesome-ai-engineer-interview/blob/main/company/anthropic.md.

**T3. Build-and-defend work sample (playbook plus lab).** How to scope a product in a Plan session, spend a 2-hour Build, and survive a Review that inspects data model, abstractions, path to production and AI use. Lab: a timed brief with a self-review rubric and the review questions reported at Cursor ("How did you scope the feature?", "If this is new, what are the benefits and trade-offs?"). Sources: https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview; https://www.aced.io/experiences/cursor-software-engineer-interview-a9c32f; https://officechai.com/ai/cognition-ceo-scott-wu-explains-why-they-let-people-use-as-much-ai-as-they-like-in-job-interviews; https://www.theforwarddeployed.io/interviews/openai.

**T4. Repo bug squash and PR review (lab).** A multi-file Python package in Pyodide with three seeded bugs of the kinds candidates report (a missing visitor method, an exists-but-not-a-file check, a resent request whose body stream was already consumed), written fresh rather than copied; plus a PR whose tests pass and which breaks on production data (freshness, nulls, unbounded memory). Sources: https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/stripe-new-grad-software-engineer-interview-experience-rejected-after-ai-coding-and-integration-plus-bug-squash-onsite; https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/netflix-machine-learning-engineer-interview-experience-ml-fundamentals-plus-a-production-pr-review-rejected; https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview.

**T5. Integration round lab (lab).** A deterministic mock HTTP service in the browser: paginate, collect auth headers while crawling, reconcile payments idempotently, handle retries and partial failure; parts unlock in order. Sources: https://www.aced.io/experiences/ramp-ai-engineer-interview-13abb1; https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/stripe-new-grad-software-engineer-interview-experience-rejected-after-ai-coding-and-integration-plus-bug-squash-onsite.

**T6. The AI-assisted round, scored (playbook plus lab).** Meta's four competencies and Snowflake's reported probes (algorithm choice, live direction of the AI, review, tests). Lab: an AI-written rate limiter with seeded race and clock bugs; the user writes boundary tests with an injectable clock and a concurrency test; follow-ups on multi-server atomicity and store outage. Merge `rnd-007`, `inf-009` and `sys-016` into this. Sources: https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/meta-ai-enabled-coding; https://prachub.com/interview-questions/design-and-implement-a-rate-limiter-with-an-ai-assistant-then-defend-and-test-it; https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-ai-is-changing-software-engineering.

**T7. Harness engineering for staff candidates (lesson plus teardown).** What a harness owns (routing, planner and supervisor patterns, tool orchestration, context, termination, permissions, model and effort routing), how teams measure it (task success, latency, cost per successful task), and how harness changes are gated. Teardown of two public JDs as specifications of the job. Sources: https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/decagon/7df0a996-5a9f-496f-9f8b-c302983765b2; https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/perplexity/f3814af2-55a3-49a5-b0cb-67199a2a45fb; https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents (listed in research/tracks, not reopened here); https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/ (dated 11 Feb 2026 per search result; direct fetch refused, open in a browser).

**T8. FDE decomposition case (playbook).** A vague enterprise brief turned into scope, success metric, data access plan, hosting choice, compliance questions for regulated and government customers, and a two-week plan. Sources: https://www.aced.io/experiences/anthropic-machine-learning-engineer-interview-27873a; https://www.theforwarddeployed.io/interviews/openai; https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/anthropic/jobs/5423029008.

## 5. Misconceptions a reviewer should check

1. "AI is allowed in interviews now" or "AI is banned". Both are wrong as general claims. Anthropic defaults to no AI unless stated; Amazon's quoted guidance bans it unless permitted; Meta has one AI round and one classic round; Google's pilot is junior and mid only; Sierra, Cognition, Shopify and Synthesia allow or expect it. Every claim needs a company, a round and a date.
2. "Meta scores your prompting." The vendor summary of Meta's materials says prompt engineering is not the focus; verification and communication are scored.
3. "LeetCode is gone at AI companies." Sierra removed coding and algorithms rounds, but candidates report CodeSignal-style rounds at Anthropic and Ramp, LeetCode-style rounds at OpenAI (Hello Interview), and a spreadsheet-cycle problem in an older Sierra loop.
4. Round counts and pass rates from vendor pages (DesignGurus, Dataford, techinterview.org, Interview Query) disagree with each other and cite nothing. Do not state a loop shape as fact from them. techinterview.org's "between FAANG senior and FAANG staff" and its "paid" Cursor onsite are unsourced.
5. Questions from ombharatiya/AI-Engineer-Interview-Questions company pages are synthesised by the author ("not leaked questions"). They must not populate `companies` fields.
6. Old reports presented as current: the Sierra agent engineer report is about two years old and predates the April 2026 loop; the DeepMind Blind post is from April 2021.
7. "OpenAI L5 equals staff." The Hello Interview page says L5 is "often compared with" staff scope elsewhere; it is a comparison, not a mapping.
8. "Applied AI Engineer at Anthropic is a fixed level because it says 4+ years." The JD says years "correlate with the internal job level"; the minimum does not set the level.
9. Meta's supported languages: the existing `rounds/ai-collab-coding` lesson says nine languages per Meta's careers page; Hello Interview lists six. Re-check metacareers.com in a browser and keep one number.
10. The "over 50% of candidates cheat in virtual interviews" figure in Google coverage has no primary source; do not use it.
11. Company tags around Cursor and xAI: Cursor's Ashby posting for Engineering Manager, Agent and Product Security opens with a SpaceX and "SpaceXSI" company description (checked 2026-10-28), and `inf-016` is tagged SpaceX. Confirm the corporate relationship from a primary announcement before writing company names on Cursor or xAI items.
12. Prompt-injection questions in security loops are not only app-layer: the reported Anthropic security questions ask about infrastructure-level detection, weights, supply chain and incident response. A safety track that stops at OWASP LLM01 does not prepare a security-role candidate.
13. Interview exercises reported in detail (Stripe bug squash libraries, Perplexity file system) may be under candidate NDA. Inferview should teach the skill with original material and cite the report only for the format.
14. Indian and European loops: the public primary material found here is limited to Synthesia's JD and thin candidate pages; do not generalise US lab loops to Bengaluru or Paris offices without a source.
